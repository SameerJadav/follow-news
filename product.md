# Follow — Product Idea

A personal news app, built for one person and read on a phone. It has one job:
help you know what's actually happening in the world and in India, and let you
follow the handful of stories you truly care about — without noise, without
guilt, and without ever making you doubt what you just read.

## Who this is for

One specific reader. Wakes up around 8:30 (earlier on workout days), out the door
to work, home around 9:30pm. Reads at breakfast, for about 10 minutes, or on a
break during the day — always on a phone, so the phone is the only screen this
is designed for. Non-native English reader — needs plain, easy language, not
newspaper English. Wants three things at once: to be able to **talk confidently**
about the big things happening, to actually **understand** things deeply,
and to be sure he **didn't miss** anything that matters. He does not want to be
personalized to by topic — just the biggest, most important things, presented well.

## Feature: the daily digest (the discovery feed)

Every morning, by a set time — ready and waiting before breakfast, no need to
check or refresh — a new digest is there.

- **Two sections you switch between: World and India.** Not one long mixed list.
  India means national news — big stories from any state — not local city news.
- **Only the genuinely biggest stories.** Not a fixed number. A quiet day might
  have two or three; a heavy news day might have ten. No padding, no filler
  stories added just to hit a quota, no "under-covered region" story included
  out of obligation if nothing big is actually happening there.
- **Each story is a full, self-contained read.** An informative headline and
  opening line give you the gist immediately — enough that skimming still means
  you don't miss anything. The rest of the story is complete but concise: real
  understanding, not a teaser. Why the story matters is woven naturally into the
  story itself — not split off into a separate section you skip. There's no
  "what to watch next" section either — that part of the old design almost never
  got read, so it's gone.
- **Every story stands alone.** You never need to remember yesterday's digest to
  understand today's. Nothing assumes you're a daily reader.
- **Words to know.** A short list of harder words used in the stories, with
  simple explanations and pronunciation, for a non-native reader.
- **Visible sources.** Small, tappable markers on claims let you see exactly
  which outlet a fact came from, without cluttering the writing itself.
- **A hard close.** Once you've read the day's stories, that's it — a clear,
  finite end, not an endless scroll pretending there's always more.

## Feature: Follow (the heart of the app)

This is the actual reason to open the app daily — not the digest.

When a story in the digest grabs you — the kind you find yourself wanting to
know _everything_ about — you tap **Follow**. From that point on:

- You get a **full-picture explainer**, built from scratch by researching the
  entire story, from wherever it actually began — even if that's months before
  it ever showed up in your digest. You're never dropped into the middle of
  something with missing backstory.
- As the story keeps developing, each day adds a new entry to a **timeline** —
  so the story keeps growing the fuller picture you already have, instead of
  repeating it.
- When the story is actually over, it **closes**, and that's the end of it.
- Following is always your choice. **Nothing follows itself.** No story is ever
  tracked, updated, or resurfaced for you unless you deliberately chose to
  follow it. The old idea of the app quietly remembering everything you've read
  and reminding you where you left off is gone entirely — replaced by this
  intentional, opt-in follow.

## Trust and correctness come first

Being right matters more than covering more. A smaller, fully true digest beats
a bigger one you have to second-guess.

- Never guess or blend numbers from different sources into a figure nobody
  actually reported.
- If a story is only thinly sourced, that's shown honestly — never quietly
  papered over.
- Real, short quotes and tappable sources make the truth visible, but the visible
  trust signals are never allowed to run ahead of the actual accuracy behind
  them — no false confidence, no trust theater.

## Deliberately left out

These were part of the old idea, and are intentionally not part of this one:

- **No notifications, no reminders, no nagging.** The digest is simply ready
  each morning at its set time — the app never pushes itself at you. It has to
  earn the daily visit by being worth opening, on its own.
- **No automatic memory or "since you read" updates.** Follow replaces this —
  tracking a story is something you choose, not something that happens to you.
- **No personalization by topic, no accounts, no onboarding, no email delivery.**
  One reader, one experience.
- **No five-layer story structure, no "why it happened" or "historical
  parallel" sections, no separate "genuine disagreement" or "solutions" layers.**
  Just what happened, written completely, with significance built in.
- **No small or low-consequence stories**, and no quota story added just to
  cover an under-reported region or topic.

## Constraint: free only

This is built for one person, so it must cost nothing to run. Everything used
to build and run it has to be either completely free or fall within a free
plan — no paid subscriptions or services, ever. Free plans come with usage
limits, and hitting one must never break the morning: wait for the limit to
lift and resume, rather than failing.

Free hosting has one consequence worth stating plainly rather than glossing
over: **the site is unlisted, not private.** It lives at an address that is
never shared and asks search engines not to index it, but anyone who had the
link could read it. Nothing on it is secret — it's rewritten public news — but
which stories are being followed would be visible too. That's an accepted
trade for costing nothing, not an oversight.

## Constraint: simple and reliable, built once

This is a one-person project, built once and then left alone — not something
that gets revisited, maintained, or fixed later. So it has to be built as
simply as possible, avoiding any complexity that isn't earning its keep, and it
has to be reliable from day one, since once it's done, no one's coming back to
patch it.
