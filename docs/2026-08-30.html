Russian strike on Kyiv arms depot kills dozens and sparks wide evacuations

Russian drones struck a Ukrainian military ammunition depot near Kyiv on Friday night1. The BBC reported that 37 people died1, while Al Jazeera reported 38 people died2. The BBC said that 42 people were injured, including four children1. Al Jazeera said that dozens of people were wounded2. The explosion happened in the Bucha district on the outskirts of Kyiv1. The warehouse was in Myla and belonged to the Ukrainian military1. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky confirmed that the depot stored shells, drones, and other munitions3. The strike caused stored shells, mines, and drones to explode late on Friday1. More than 370 people were evacuated from the surrounding area4. The BBC said almost 400 people were evacuated1.

The explosions damaged a care home for elderly and disabled people1. Tymur Tkachenko, head of the local regional military administration, said the strike started a fire that damaged at least 50 residential buildings5. Ukrainian media cited Tkachenko as saying 34 of the victims were residents at the care facility5. Taras Didych, head of the Dmytrivka community, died while trying to rescue others during the explosion4. Russia's Ministry of Defence said its forces targeted an ammunition depot containing launch boosters and drone parts1.

Ukrainian leaders criticized the decision to store weapons near homes1. President Zelensky said storing explosives next to residential areas was terrible negligence1. He said criminal proceedings had been opened1. Ukrainian Prime Minister Sergiy Koretsky said repeating storage mistakes in the fifth year of the full-scale invasion is criminal negligence1. This was the second strike on a Kyiv munitions depot in less than two months2. An earlier strike hit an ammunition warehouse in Vyshneve in July1. The BBC said that July explosion killed nine people, injured dozens, and damaged over 280 homes1. Prime Minister Koretsky said 22 people died in that July attack5. An investigation showed a state-owned company broke rules about storing explosives near homes4. President Zelensky said two officials from state-owned companies, including arms maker Ukroboronprom, would be held accountable for illegal storage5.

Attacks hit other regions as well1. Kyiv Mayor Vitaliy Klitschko said a two-year-old child died after a Russian drone crashed in Obolonskyi1. Regional administration head Oleksandr Prokudin said Russian strikes on Kherson on Saturday killed two people and wounded 27 others5. The Ukrainian army said it shot down 233 Russian drones overnight1. Pavlo Palisa, deputy head of Ukraine's presidential office, said Ukrainian interceptor drones are not yet effective enough against jet-powered Russian drones5. President Zelensky said Ukraine aims to launch 1,000 long-range drone strikes on Russia every day5.

Niger military moves to stop army mutiny in capital city Niamey

Niger's military moved to stop a mutiny by rebel soldiers in the capital city of Niamey1. Rebellious troops attacked military officers, held soldiers hostage at a barracks, and fired at key sites2. Witnesses reported heavy gunfire and explosions at Base 101 shortly after midnight on Saturday3. Rebel soldiers tried to enter the presidential palace and blocked the national television broadcaster Tele Sahel3. The channel went off the air for just over an hour before resuming broadcasts around 10:00 a.m. local time4.

Niger's defence ministry said armed forces were retaking control of targeted sites4. Diplomatic and security sources said the presidential guard, aided by Russian forces, recaptured an air base and killed several mutineers by Saturday evening3. But regional sources said rebelling troops still held two barracks around Niamey on Saturday3.

This mutiny was the third major incident at the capital's airport complex in 20261. Jihadists had previously attacked the airport twice earlier in the year3. General Abdourahamane Tiani seized power in Niger in 2023, cutting ties with Western partners and aligning with Russia1. Neighbouring Algeria condemned the mutiny and offered support to Niger's government3.

Rescuers search for thousands after Himalayan flood disaster kills hundreds

Rescuers searched for thousands of missing people across Nepal, India, and China after massive flash floods1. The US Geological Survey said a massive glacial collapse caused the high-altitude disaster, sending debris torrents downstream2. The confirmed death toll reached 682 people, including 675 in Nepal and seven in Tibet1. Disaster agencies said nearly 3,000 people are missing1. This includes 2,426 missing people in Nepal and 554 in Tibet1. The missing list includes at least 777 foreign nationals and 933 hydropower project workers1. The Red Cross estimated that the disaster affected at least 93,000 people, while over 3,700 people were rescued in Nepal1.

Rescue operations focused heavily on trapped workers1. The Nepali army pumped air through pipes into tunnels at the Trishuli 3A and 3B hydropower sites1. Nepalese Energy Minister Biraj Bhakta Shrestha said workers drilled a hole into a cable tunnel, but could not reach trapped staff1. Nepal asked for specialized foreign help, including tunnel rescue teams, tracking tech, DNA analysis tools, and mortuary facilities1. India sent an 11-member technical team for tunnel rescue, and a Chinese team was expected soon1.

Several countries took action to aid victims and locate citizens1. Indian authorities said they rescued 88 Indian nationals in Nepal, including four hydropower workers in Rasuwa3. India's Ministry of External Affairs reduced its figure of missing Indian citizens in Nepal to 287 on Saturday3. The ministry said another 128 uncontactable people in Nepal were of Indian origin with other nationalities3. On Saturday, 120 Indian nationals crossed from Tibet into Nepal3. In Tibet, Chinese authorities at Gyirong port evacuated 555 tourists and moved 499 local residents and workers to safety3. Chinese monitors confirmed that a dammed debris lake started draining naturally, reducing immediate flood risks3.

The international response and climate concerns drew attention1. The US State Department promised $500,000 in initial emergency aid for Nepal1. Nepalese Foreign Minister Shisir Khanal and Chinese Foreign Minister Wang Yi agreed to share satellite and water data in real time2. Khanal noted that Nepal produces only about 0.01 percent of global greenhouse gas emissions despite facing severe impacts2. UN climate chief Simon Stiell said burning fossil fuels warms the climate and makes mountain disasters more frequent and severe2. Meanwhile, flood survivors in Nuwakot district publicly criticized the Nepalese government for slow relief delivery and search efforts4.

RSS chief Mohan Bhagwat speaks at New York event amid local protests

RSS chief Mohan Bhagwat spoke at a diaspora event at Madison Square Garden in New York1. He delivered the keynote address at the Universal Oneness Celebration, supported by over 200 Hindu American groups1. Bhagwat said that if any Hindu believes Muslims should not live in India, that person is no longer a Hindu1. He said RSS volunteers run about 130,000 service projects and increased dialogue with Muslim and Christian communities starting in 20183. To highlight equal service, he cited volunteer work after the 1996 Charkhi Dadri plane crash in Haryana1.

Bhagwat arrived in the United States on Tuesday for a centenary outreach tour across North America and the UK4. He also spoke to leaders at the One World, One Humanity conference on August 293.

The visit drew strong pushback2. Civil rights, interfaith, and caste groups protested outside Madison Square Garden5. New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani opposed the event, calling the group's ideology exclusionary2. The US Commission on International Religious Freedom recommended revoking Bhagwat's visa and sanctioning RSS members2. Indian Prime Minister Narendra Modi previously spoke to 50,000 people at a 2019 event in Texas5.
