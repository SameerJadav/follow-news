Iran and Oman Discuss Strait of Hormuz Framework Amid Ongoing Conflict

Iranian and Omani foreign ministers discussed a framework agreement in Tehran1. They spoke about setting up a temporary shipping corridor in the Strait of Hormuz1. They also discussed clearing naval mines from the passage1.

Iran's Revolutionary Guards said Tehran and Oman agreed on arrangements for the strait2. They said the two sides agreed on sharing the waterway2. They also agreed on sharing its revenues2.

Iranian Deputy Foreign Minister Kazem Gharibabadi spoke about reopening the strait1. He said the Strait of Hormuz will only reopen in exchange for clear actions1. He said the war must end1. He said the blockade must be lifted1. He also said the situation in Yemen must be resolved1.

Qatar's prime minister is scheduled to travel to Tehran soon2. He aims to restart diplomatic negotiations between Iran and the United States2.

Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian commented on the war3. He said that the war must end at some point3. He added that Iran still negotiates from a position of strength3.

US President Donald Trump said that the war with Iran will continue4. He said it will carry on for as long as necessary4.

Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu expressed doubt about peace talks5. He said he doubted negotiations could produce an agreement5. He said this was due to Iran's current leadership5.

Shipping traffic in the Strait of Hormuz has dropped severely. Ship-tracking data showed that about 5 million barrels of oil per day passed through the strait on Monday2. Before February 28, roughly 20 million barrels per day passed through the passage2.

Data from ship tracker Kpler showed a similar slowdown3. Kpler reported that only 112 oil and gas tankers passed through the strait between August 1 and August 193.

Safety in the waterway has also worsened. The UN's International Maritime Organization recorded 70 incidents in the Strait of Hormuz2. These incidents occurred since February 282. The organization reported that these events resulted in the deaths of 19 seafarers2.

Hospital Fire in Islamabad Kills 14 Newborn Infants

A fire at a hospital maternity ward in Islamabad killed 14 newborn infants1. The disaster occurred in the mother and child unit at the Pakistan Institute of Medical Sciences1.

Rescue officials said the fire started around 6:45 a.m.2. An air-conditioning unit compressor exploded inside the nursery2. Federal Health Minister Mustafa Kamal said oxygen canisters inside the ward intensified the flames3.

Hospital spokesperson Dr. Aneeza Jalil said 15 babies were in the ward when the fire broke out3. She said a doctor saved one baby3. Kamal confirmed that at least 14 babies died in the fire2.

PIMS Executive Director Rana Imran Sikander spoke about safety at the facility1. He said the ward door was manned rather than locked to prevent baby theft1.

Pakistani Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif suspended Federal Health Secretary Aslam Ghauri after the hospital fire1.

Meta Reaches Multi-Billion Dollar Settlement With US States Over Teen Safety

Meta agreed to pay billions of dollars to settle lawsuits from US states over youth safety1. France 24 reported that Meta agreed to pay $17 billion to settle claims from 47 states2. Livemint reported a $16.68 billion settlement with 29 states3. Al Jazeera put the total settlement figure at up to $17.1 billion1.

The lawsuits accused Meta of causing social media addiction and harm to teenagers2. They also made claims regarding child data collection3.

Under the agreement, Meta will add daily time limits on Instagram and Facebook2. It will also pause notifications during daytime school hours2. Meta proposed a two-hour daily limit for users under 18 years old4.

Meta said 30 percent of the payment depends on other companies4. YouTube and TikTok must implement matching daily limits and safety features for Meta to pay that portion4.

Meta previously faced legal penalties in New Mexico3. A jury ordered Meta to pay $375 million in March3. A judge ordered Meta to pay $567 million on August 63.

India and China Announce Eight-Point Consensus on Border Management

India and China announced an eight-point consensus on border management following high-level talks in Beijing1. Indian National Security Adviser Ajit Doval and Chinese Foreign Minister Wang Yi co-chaired the special representatives meeting1. Doval also met Chinese Vice-President Han Zheng before delegation talks2.

Wang Yi used the phrase "Dragon-Elephant Dance" to describe cooperation despite differences2.

The two nations agreed to establish two new military meeting points1. They also agreed to open two new border military hotlines1. In addition, they agreed to reopen three designated border trading points3.

India thanked China for expanding batches for the Kailash Manasarovar Yatra pilgrimage3. Both sides agreed to hold the 26th round of talks in India in 20271.

The special talks mechanism began in 2003 to address the 3,488-kilometer border dispute3. Tensions rose after a 2020 clash in Galwan Valley killed 20 Indian and four Chinese soldiers4. In October 2024, both sides agreed on border patrolling4. That deal led to troop disengagement in eastern Ladakh4.

Indian Prime Minister Narendra Modi and Chinese President Xi Jinping met in October 2024 and in 20255. India will host the BRICS Summit on September 12, 20265. China is building a $137-billion dam on the Brahmaputra river3.
