Houthi forces capture Yemeni port city of Mokha as crude oil prices surge past $100

Houthi forces captured the Yemeni port city of Mokha from Saudi-backed forces1. The port city sits about 80 kilometers, or 50 miles, from the Bab el-Mandeb Strait2. Pro-government commander Tarek Salah said his forces moved their command center on the west coast to a safe location1. A Yemeni military source told AFP that Houthi forces were pushing towards the strategic Bab al-Mandab strait3. Fighting started again in Yemen as Houthi forces moved south towards the Bab al-Mandab Strait4.

A Yemeni military source told AFP that Houthi fighters also captured Zuqar Island in the Red Sea5. They took the island after rocket barrages and a boat assault5. Houthi spokesman Yahya Sarea said Saudi warplanes carried out 64 air strikes across four western Yemeni provinces within 24 hours5. Saudi authorities reported that Houthi missile and drone strikes on September 8 injured 73 civilians5. They said the strikes also temporarily stopped operations at multiple oil facilities5.

The fighting caused global oil prices to jump sharply. The benchmark US oil contract West Texas Intermediate rose above $100 a barrel on September 10 after Houthis seized Mocha3. Brent crude rose more than 6 percent past $107 a barrel on September 103. That was its highest level since May3. Rystad Energy analyst Jorge Leon said oil flow through the Strait of Hormuz dropped3. It fell from around 8 million barrels per day in late August to between 1.5 million and 2 million barrels per day3.

Financial markets reacted to the news as well. The 30-year US Treasury bond yield reached 5.36 percent3. This marked a new peak since 20073. US producer price inflation climbed to 5.4 percent in August3. That was up from 4.8 percent in July3. The European Central Bank increased its benchmark interest rate to 2.5 percent on September 103.

BRICS Summit begins in New Delhi as Vladimir Putin and Xi Jinping arrive

Russian President Vladimir Putin landed in New Delhi on Friday to attend the BRICS summit1. He arrived for a bilateral meeting with Prime Minister Narendra Modi1. The BRICS Summit 2026 marks Putin's first overseas participation in a BRICS summit since Russia invaded Ukraine in February 20222. PM Modi and President Putin previously met in Bishkek on August 31 on the sidelines of the Shanghai Cooperation Organisation summit2.

The 18th BRICS summit is taking place at Bharat Mandapam in New Delhi from September 12-133. Its theme is 'Building for Resilience, Innovation, Cooperation and Sustainability'4.

Many global leaders are expected to attend the BRICS summit in New Delhi3. Expected guests include Chinese President Xi Jinping, Egyptian President Abdel Fattah El-Sisi, Ethiopia's Prime Minister Abiy Ahmed Ali, and Indonesian President Prabowo Subianto3. Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian is scheduled to attend3. This marks his first visit to India since taking office after President Ebrahim Raisi died in a helicopter crash in 20243. UN Secretary-General Antonio Guterres, WHO head Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus, and WTO Director-General Ngozi Okonjo-Iweala are also attending3. But Bangladesh will not be represented at the summit3. Minister of State for Foreign Affairs Humayun Kobir confirmed this despite an invitation sent to Prime Minister Tarique Rahman3.

India and Russia are expanding economic and defense discussions. Russian presidential spokesperson Dmitry Peskov said Russia and India are exploring cooperation in rare earths2. Peskov said Russia is exploring the possible sale of Sukhoi Su-57 stealth fighter jets to India2. Union Civil Aviation Minister Kinjarapu Ram Mohan Naidu confirmed expanded talks on manufacturing aircraft in India2. These include the IL-114 turboprop and Sukhoi Superjet 1002. Union Commerce and Industry Minister Piyush Goyal said India and Russia aim to reach $100 billion in bilateral trade by 20304. Goyal said the countries also target $50 billion in two-way investment by 20304. Bilateral trade between India and Russia currently stands at around $60 billion4.

Delhi authorities put strict security measures in place. Special airspace restrictions apply from September 11 to September 13 within 300 kilometers of New Delhi5. Safdarjung Airport will remain closed, except for specified military and police helicopter operations5. Aircraft climbing near the restricted zone must reach around 29,000 feet by the time they are 200 kilometers from the capital5. Normal scheduled commercial flights at Delhi's main airport will continue taking off and landing5. Nearly 4,800 traffic personnel are deployed in Delhi for security and traffic management5.

Delhi authorities seal Chandni Chowk shops following fatal Satya Niketan building collapse

Municipal workers sealed 56 jewellery and bullion shops in Chandni Chowk's Kucha Mahajani on Thursday1. The Municipal Corporation of Delhi enforcement drive was intensified after a building collapse in Satya Niketan on September 6 left seven people dead1. The five-storey paying guest building collapsed, killing seven people and injuring 122. Five of the seven people killed were Delhi University students2.

Initial findings revealed the 50-year-old building in Satya Niketan had five floors despite only two floors being permitted2. Police arrested the building owner, his son, the operator, and a contractor2. Operator Shubham Tyagi was denied anticipatory bail by a Delhi court3.

The Municipal Corporation of Delhi suspended five municipal officials over alleged negligence2. Three additional engineers were suspended for negligence after the collapse4. The All India Bullion and Jewellers Association questioned whether only traders will bear the brunt or if action will also be taken against government departments and politicians1.

A Supreme Court Bench directed authorities across India to file affidavits on compliance regarding unauthorized constructions5. Amicus curiae Ajit Kumar Sinha brought the Satya Niketan collapse to the Supreme Court's attention5. The Delhi High Court scheduled further hearings on public interest litigation for September 255.
