Iran and Oman Draft Agreement on Strait of Hormuz Shipping Route

Iran and Oman are completing a joint agreement to establish a temporary shipping route through the Strait of Hormuz1. Iranian foreign ministry spokesman Esmail Baghaei said on Wednesday that the joint statement regarding the commercial passage is currently in its final review and drafting stage1. Donald Trump also commented on the negotiations late Tuesday, stating that an agreement regarding the Strait of Hormuz is imminent1.

The potential agreement follows months of conflict and maritime disruption in the region. Before the war, the Strait of Hormuz was freely open to all maritime traffic1. However, Tehran has largely blocked the narrow waterway since the United States and Israel launched military attacks on Iran in late February2. Under normal conditions, approximately one-fifth of the world's oil and liquefied natural gas passes through the strategic strait2.

The draft proposal currently under discussion outlines specific conditions for commercial traffic. Reuters reported that the agreement would place inbound commercial shipping through the Strait of Hormuz under Iranian control3. Iran's Deputy Foreign Minister Kazem Gharibabadi stated that the proposed route would be temporary, remaining open for two to four months2. Gharibabadi added that parts of the planned shipping route would cross through Iranian territorial waters, while other sections would pass through Omani waters4.

Despite the drafting of the proposal, significant political and military tensions persist between Washington and Tehran. Iranian spokesman Esmaeil Baqaei stated that the factors making the Strait of Hormuz insecure still exist on the part of the United States, particularly the naval blockade and other aggressive and threatening actions against Iran and its interests5. On the other hand, US officials have repeatedly insisted that they would never accept any agreement that permits Iran to control access to the Strait of Hormuz1.

Military forces continue to enforce measures in the region's waterways. US Central Command stated on Wednesday that it had redirected 48 commercial vessels, disabled two, and boarded two others as part of its ongoing naval blockade4. Meanwhile, Yemen's Iran-backed Houthis have maintained a blockade on Saudi Arabian Red Sea ports since July 202.

Abdul El-Sayed Wins Democratic Senate Primary in Michigan

Former public health official Abdul El-Sayed has won the Michigan Democratic primary election for the US Senate1. El-Sayed defeated Haley Stevens to secure the nomination2. During the campaign, outside political groups supported Stevens over El-Sayed by a nine-to-one financial margin3. Specifically, groups linked to AIPAC spent $30 million on negative advertisements targeting El-Sayed1. Opponents also attacked him politically, with Donald Trump stating during a Nevada campaign rally that El-Sayed does not love Israel, does not love Jewish people, and hates them1.

El-Sayed will now face former Republican congressman Mike Rogers in the general election for the US Senate in Michigan3. In another Michigan primary contest, State Representative Donavan McKinney defeated two-term Congressman Shri Thanedar in the 13th Congressional District Democratic primary, winning 51.9 percent of the vote to 48.1 percent4. McKinney received endorsements from Senator Bernie Sanders, Representative Rashida Tlaib, Justice Democrats, and Leaders We Deserve4. Elsewhere, in Missouri's Democratic congressional primary, Wesley Bell defeated incumbent representative Cori Bush3.

Russian Missile and Drone Barrage Kills 17 in Kyiv

An overnight Russian ballistic missile and drone attack killed at least 17 people in Kyiv and the surrounding region1. More than 40 people were injured across Kyiv and nearby areas during the strikes2. Russia launched a total of 115 drones and 24 ballistic missiles in the overnight attack targeting locations across Ukraine3. Ukrainian air defense forces intercepted 98 of the 115 drones, but they failed to shoot down any of the ballistic missiles4.

The attack damaged several key infrastructure sites in the capital. Seven sites were struck in Kyiv on Wednesday, including a brewing company, construction material warehouses, and a railway station4. The Kyiv military administration stated that the strike caused an ammonia leak, requiring emergency crews to manage the situation4.

The attack highlighted Ukraine's severe shortages in military supplies. Volodymyr Zelenskyy stated that air defense interceptor supplies delivered to Ukraine during the first half of 2026 fell to one-third of the levels supplied in 20254. As strikes intensify elsewhere, Donetsk region governor Vadym Filashkin ordered hundreds of families with children to evacuate the city of Kramatorsk due to escalating Russian attacks4.

Clashes and Strikes Continue in Southern Lebanon

Violence has continued in Lebanon following military strikes and casualties on both sides. An Israeli airstrike hit the roof of a cemetery prayer hall in Tibnin, Lebanon, killing one person and injuring 12 others1. The Israeli military also issued an evacuation order for residents of Mansouri, Lebanon, marking its first evacuation directive in over a month1. In another operational action, Israeli forces detonated 700 tons of explosives during a demolition operation around Beaufort Castle outside Nabatieh2.

On the ground, two Israeli soldiers were killed and another was critically injured when an explosive device detonated inside a booby-trapped building in Majdal Selem3. Lebanon's Health Ministry reported that Israeli attacks have killed at least 4,333 people and wounded 12,250 others in Lebanon since March 23. Conversely, at least 40 Israelis, including 38 soldiers, have been killed by Hezbollah since March 22.

Diplomatic and political efforts remain tense. Italian Foreign Minister Antonio Tajani contacted Iran's foreign minister to request that Tehran halt Hezbollah's military operations4. However, Hezbollah leader Naim Qassem stated that direct negotiations with Israel would bring nothing but shame, humiliation, and successive concessions for Lebanon4. Amid the conflict, the UN humanitarian office reported that over 820,000 displaced persons in Lebanon have returned home, while approximately 360,000 remain displaced1.

Explosives-Laden Drone Found at Leipzig Airport in Germany

An employee discovered an explosive-laden drone in the cargo operations area near the south runway at Leipzig/Halle Airport in Germany1. German police found up to 800 grams of plastic explosive attached to the drone2. Police subsequently removed the detonator from the device and executed a controlled explosion3. German state prosecutors, including special units focused on terrorism and extremism, have taken over the investigation into the incident4.

The airport plays a significant role in regional logistics. Leipzig/Halle Airport serves as a maintenance base for Ukraine's Antonov Airlines and functions as a transit hub for German military and NATO goods3. During the situation, a DHL cargo plane aborted its landing at Leipzig and subsequently collided mid-air with an unidentified object, sustaining minor damage before landing safely in Hanover1.

Government and diplomatic officials expressed serious concern over the discovery. German Interior Minister Alexander Dobrindt stated that authority forces are dealing with competition that may include foreign powers who want to contribute to uncertainty in Germany1. Commenting on the incident to Welt TV, Ukraine's ambassador to Germany Oleksii Makeiev questioned who else it could be but Russia1.

Ousted Bangladesh Prime Minister Sheikh Hasina Announces December Return

Deposed Bangladeshi Prime Minister Sheikh Hasina announced during a virtual press conference in New Delhi that she plans to return to Bangladesh in December1. Sheikh Hasina's son, Sajeeb Wazed, participated in the New Delhi press conference via video link from Washington, D.C.2. During the press interaction, Sheikh Hasina demanded that Bangladesh lift the ban on the Awami League party and release political prisoners3.

Her announcement drew a strong reaction from authorities in Bangladesh. Bangladesh's foreign ministry issued a statement expressing outrage that India permitted Sheikh Hasina to hold a live press interaction in New Delhi1. Hasina fled the country following widespread student-led demonstrations that led to her removal. According to the United Nations, nearly 1,400 people were killed during the 2024 youth-led uprising in Bangladesh2. Following her removal, a special tribunal in Dhaka sentenced Sheikh Hasina to death in absentia in 2025 for alleged crimes against humanity2.

Bangladesh has undergone major political changes since Hasina's exile. Following national elections in February, the Bangladesh Nationalist Party assumed power, with Tarique Rahman becoming prime minister4. Hasina's planned return comes amid ongoing political friction between her supporters and the new government in Dhaka.

Indian Student Protests Persist as Supreme Court Calls for Dialogue

Youth-led protests continue across India as systemic issues in education and employment remain unresolved. Graduate unemployment in India has remained between 35 percent and 40 percent over the past 25 years1. Education Minister Dharmendra Pradhan resigned on July 25 following sustained youth-led protests over leaked examination papers1. Activist Sonam Wangchuk completed a 26-day hunger strike in Delhi during June and July demanding examination and education reforms1.

New political initiatives have emerged from the protest movement. Cockroach Janta Party founder Abhijeet Dipke announced in Chhatrapati Sambhajinagar that the outfit will operate as a pressure group rather than contesting elections2. Cockroach Janta Party members announced plans to travel to Ranchi to back candidates protesting alleged exam irregularities in the Jharkhand Public Service Commission2. Meanwhile, Cockroach Janta Party chief spokesperson Saurav Das filed a police complaint alleging that 15 to 20 media persons and YouTubers filmed inside his home without consent3.

Government platforms and institutions have responded in various ways. Meta temporarily removed a video posted by Prime Minister Narendra Modi during student protests before restoring it and citing an automated moderation error4. Chief Justice of India Surya Kant observed during a Supreme Court hearing that the biggest powerful source is listening to what people are saying and why they are agitating5.

Meta Apologizes to India Over Restriction of Prime Minister's Video

Social media giant Meta has apologized to the Indian government after temporarily restricting a post by Prime Minister Narendra Modi. Prime Minister Narendra Modi's video post addressing students about paper leaks was temporarily restricted on Facebook on July 231. Meta attributed the restriction of PM Modi's Facebook post to a technical glitch within its automated AI content filtering system1. Following the restriction, Parliamentary Standing Committee chairman Nishikant Dubey issued a three-day deadline for Mark Zuckerberg to tender an apology2. Meta's Chief Global Affairs Officer Joel Kaplan stated that he apologized to the minister on behalf of Meta for the error restricting PM Modi's post2.

The incident has sparked legal and political questions regarding internet platform protections in India. Indian IT ministry officials informed Meta executives that algorithmic content curation disqualifies the company from safe harbour protection under Section 79 of the IT Act3. Meta representatives also admitted to Indian officials that paid promotion on its platforms resulted in illegal content being amplified2. In a separate issue, a BBC Eye investigation identified approximately 30 advertisements on Instagram promoting child sexual abuse material4.

Other technology companies face similar parliamentary scrutiny. The Parliamentary Standing Committee on Communications and IT demanded that Google India lose its safe harbour protection following cyberfraud complaints linked to Play Store apps3.
