United States forces hit Iranian military targets after attacks on shipping and American troops

United States forces began striking military targets inside Iran at noon on Tuesday1. US Central Command announced that the strikes targeted the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps1. It said the operation responded to attempted attacks on commercial shipping in the Strait of Hormuz1. US Central Command said forces also reacted to attacks against American service members1. About 20% of the world's oil passes through the Strait of Hormuz2.

Iranian state media reported explosions on Qeshm Island on the northern side of the Strait of Hormuz1. Iranian news agency Nour News reported explosions in the port city of Bandar Abbas1. Nour News also reported explosions in the port city of Chabahar1. Iranian state media said US strikes hit a fishmeal factory on Qeshm Island2. Strikes also hit a fishing pier in Sirik2. State media said a maritime tower in Sirik was hit too2. US strikes also hit an area outside Jiroft Airport2.

The Iranian Red Crescent reported that four people died in Sirik3. It said a four-year-old child was among those killed3. Missile shrapnel hit a wedding celebration in Sirik3. The Red Crescent said that more than 50 people were injured3. US Navy Captain Tim Hawkins said the US military never targets civilians3. Hawkins said that the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps does target civilians3.

The Revolutionary Guard launched a barrage of ballistic missiles at a US Marine base in Aqaba, Jordan3. Iranian forces launched the missiles in response to the attack in Sirik3. The Jordanian Armed Forces reported no deaths from the missile strike near Aqaba3. Jordan said no injuries resulted from the attack3.

US President Donald Trump posted a warning on social media3. Trump said Iran would be hit again at a much harder level if it retaliates3. He said the next strikes would also be at a higher level3.

A Memorandum of Understanding in June had declared an end to fighting1. That earlier fighting had killed thousands of people in Iran and Lebanon1. Fighting began after US and Israeli strikes started on February 281. Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian spoke in Bishkek about the agreement1. Pezeshkian said Iran would immediately reciprocate if the US returns to its commitments1. Iranian Parliament Speaker Mohammad Baqer Qalibaf also issued a warning1. Qalibaf said no one will export oil if enemies try to stop Iran's Persian Gulf oil exports1.

The strikes caused a reaction in global financial markets. Japan's 10-year bond yield reached 3%4. That yield reached 3% for the first time since 1996 after the market reaction4. US national government debt has risen above $40 trillion4. The US Embassy in Qatar urged Americans in the Middle East to exercise heightened vigilance5. The embassy warned about potential flight cancellations and travel disruptions5.

Russian Foreign Ministry spokesperson Maria Zakharova said Moscow would respond in a mirror-image fashion1.

Nepal flash flood death toll reaches 1,050 as rescuers search for missing workers

Flash floods in Nepal killed 1,050 people by Tuesday evening1. Around 4,000 people remain missing1. Nearly 600 foreigners from 39 countries are among the missing1.

The flash flood began in Tibet on August 262. A catastrophic collapse of rock and ice sent debris through two river systems into Nepal2. Debris flowed through the Bhote Koshi and Trishuli river systems2. Nepal's National Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Authority reported 987 bodies recovered by Tuesday morning2. The agency said 11,814 people were rescued2. It reported 3,916 people missing at that time2. Rescuers prioritized searching for at least 900 missing workers at 12 hydropower projects3. More than 600 hydropower workers were feared trapped in tunnels north of Bidur2.

An Indian tunnel rescue team established a foothold in tunnels in Chilime using improvised floats1. India's Ministry of External Affairs reported that 163 Indian nationals were rescued from Nepal1. It said 151 Indian nationals left China as of September 11. India sent a consignment of 37.5 tonnes of relief material, medicines, and food packets1. That delivery brought total Indian emergency aid to 47.5 tonnes1.

Five Australians were confirmed safe, reducing the number of unaccounted-for Australian citizens to 384. Australia's Department of Foreign Affairs and Trade received nearly 800 calls4. Australia dispatched 18 personnel to Nepal4. Africa Disaster Relief searched for six missing South Africans along the border between Nepal and China5.

Prime Minister Narendra Modi urges Shanghai Cooperation Organisation members to dismantle terror networks
