Death toll rises after powerful 7.4-magnitude earthquake hits Colombia

A powerful 7.4-magnitude earthquake struck western Colombia at 07:34 local time (12:34 GMT) on Monday1. Rescuers searched under rubble across the region following what has been called the country's strongest earthquake this century2. Official figures confirm that more than 180 people died in the earthquake, while aggregated figures from local officials indicate the death toll may exceed 2401. Meanwhile, the Colombian Association of Capital Cities reported that at least 132 people were killed and more than 570 were injured as of Monday afternoon3.

Colombian President Abelardo de la Espriella, who took office on the Friday preceding the earthquake3, declared a state of disaster and announced temporary rental subsidies for affected families4. The president reported that almost 2,600 people were injured, nearly 200 remain missing, over 1,100 homes were destroyed, and over 8,000 homes were damaged1. However, local authorities in western Colombia reported that more than 3,000 people remain missing and nearly 5,000 homes were damaged4. In international developments, Spanish Foreign Minister José Manuel Albares reported that 164 Spanish citizens were missing in Colombia following the earthquake1. To assist local rescue teams, the European Union activated its Copernicus satellite service to help Colombian authorities assess damage and carry out rescue operations4.

Major cities across western Colombia suffered heavy casualties and destruction. In Cali, Mayor Alejandro Eder reported 95 deaths, 239 people trapped, 949 injured, and 45 buildings partially or fully collapsed1. In the city's Calima neighbourhood, four children died and 30 were referred to care centres after a section of the Gimnasio Calima school collapsed1. To prevent looting, Mayor Eder ordered a curfew starting at 20:00 local time on Tuesday until 06:00 on Wednesday1.

In Pereira, 79 people were confirmed dead by the city's fire department, leading local authorities to implement a car-free day to facilitate search efforts1. In Manizales, Mayor Jorge Eduardo Rojas stated that five people were killed and 60 buildings partially or totally collapsed in the city1. Air travel in the region was severely disrupted, as airports in Quibdo, Pereira, Manizales, Armenia, Cartago, and Buenaventura were closed due to earthquake damage4.

The earthquake was followed by significant seismic activity. Colombia's geological service reported that more than 70 aftershocks occurred following Monday's earthquake, including two 3.6-magnitude quakes early on Tuesday1. This disaster comes after recent regional tragedy, as twin earthquakes struck the northern coast of Venezuela on June 24, killing more than 6,000 people3.

Syrian court sentences ousted president Bashar al-Assad to death in absentia

A court in Syria has sentenced ousted President Bashar al-Assad and his brother Maher al-Assad to death in absentia for crimes committed during the country's 14-year civil war, which claimed approximately 500,000 lives1. The Damascus court also sentenced four other former military and security officials to death in absentia, including former defense minister Fahd al-Freij2. Bashar al-Assad had become the designated heir to the presidency after his older brother, Basil, died in a car accident in 19941. He fled Damascus for Moscow with his family after his regime was overthrown in December 20242. Ahmed al-Sharaa subsequently became president of Syria following an 11-day campaign that ended the Assad family's rule2.

Syrians gathered outside the Justice Palace in Damascus following the rulings3. Former political security head in Daraa province, Atef Najib, was sentenced to death in person while standing in a cage wearing a prison uniform2. Judge Fakhareddine al-Aryan announced Najib's sentence during a court session broadcast live on state television4. Najib was sanctioned by the U.S. Department of the Treasury in April 20115 before being detained by Syrian security forces in early 20254. The detention and alleged torture of teenagers accused of writing anti-government graffiti in Daraa in March 2011 had ignited the nationwide protests that escalated into civil war2. The region has continued to see bloodshed, as about 1,500 mostly Alawite civilians were killed in retaliatory violence in March 2025, and over 1,700 people were killed in Suweida province in July 20251.

Russia frees former US Marine Robert Gilman after talks between Trump and Putin

Russia has released former U.S. Marine Robert Gilman from prison after more than four years following talks between U.S. President Donald Trump and Russian President Vladimir Putin1. Trump confirmed that Russia released Gilman on a humanitarian basis without demanding a prisoner exchange1. Gilman, 32, was scheduled to land at Andrews Air Force Base in Washington, D.C., on Tuesday night2, and U.S. officials stated he would then be flown to a rehabilitation centre in San Antonio, Texas, for medical treatment3.

Gilman served in the U.S. Marine Corps from August 2019 until August 20203. He was arrested in January 2022 on a train to Moscow for allegedly kicking a police officer while drunk, receiving an initial sentence of three and a half years that was extended to 10 years following alleged assaults on prison staff4. However, his father, Vladimir Gilman, stated in an October 2024 op-ed that the police officer dropped charges and sustained no injuries3.

Advocate Eric Lebson stated that Gilman was transferred in June from a prison hospital to a psychiatric ward in a dissociative stupor due to prison abuse2. U.S. Senator Ed Markey added that Gilman suffered from severe pneumonia and entered a near-death, catatonic state prior to his release2. András Baka elected Hungarian president as parliament enacts reforms

Hungary's parliament has elected former Supreme Court chief András Baka as head of state by a vote of 140 to 61. All 51 attending lawmakers from Viktor Orbán's Fidesz party walked out of the parliamentary chamber in protest before the vote1. Baka, who is scheduled to assume the presidency on August 192, previously served as a judge at the European Court of Human Rights for nearly 20 years before becoming president of Hungary's Supreme Court in 20093. Orbán's government removed him from that post in December 2011, three years before his term expired3, but Baka later won an unfair dismissal case against Hungary at the European Court of Human Rights4. Prime Minister Péter Magyar had initially offered the presidency to chess star Judit Polgár, who declined the role3.

The election comes as the government led by Prime Minister Magyar introduces major reforms, including constitutional amendments by the Tisza party that limit the prime minister to two terms in office and members of parliament to three terms1. The government abolished the Sovereignty Protection Office and established the National Asset Recovery and Protection Office1. In addition, public service radio and TV in Hungary have been off the air since July 9 while news programming undergoes restructuring1.

Pilot of turbulence-hit Air India flight tests positive for marijuana after 300-foot drop

The pilot-in-command of an Air India flight that suffered a sudden drop in altitude has tested positive for marijuana in a confirmatory drug test, an official confirmed1. The drug test returned positive for the banned substance following an investigation into the flight's dangerous drop1. Under regulations set by India's aviation regulator, the Directorate General of Civil Aviation (DGCA), a pilot who tests positive for psychoactive substances for the first time must undergo de-addiction treatment3. If a pilot tests positive a second time, DGCA regulations mandate a three-year licence suspension, and a third positive result leads to the permanent cancellation of the pilot's licence3.

The safety incident occurred on August 4 aboard Air India flight AI2379, which was traveling from Phuket to Delhi1. While operating in cruise, the aircraft experienced a sudden loss of altitude and dropped around 300 feet1. The sudden drop resulted in injuries to 20 passengers and four crew members1. Following the flight's arrival, 17 people were hospitalised due to the injuries sustained during the sudden drop3.

Preliminary findings have provided details about what occurred in the cockpit before and during the altitude loss. A preliminary defect report noted that the aircraft's autopilot disconnected2. The disconnect triggered a stall warning and a flight control Electronic Centralised Aircraft Monitor (ECAM) message prior to the loss of altitude2. During the actual loss of altitude on the Air India flight, the stall warning flashed in the cockpit4. In response, the first officer executed a stall-recovery response to regain control of the aircraft4.

The investigation into the incident involves international aviation authorities. France's Bureau of Enquiry and Analysis for Civil Aviation Safety (BEA) and technical representatives from Airbus are currently assisting India's Aircraft Accident Investigation Bureau2. The positive drug test results prompted immediate corporate and regulatory discussions. Air India's outgoing chief executive officer, Campbell Wilson, met with officials from the Civil Aviation Ministry and the DGCA on Tuesday evening following the test results1. In a separate leadership change for the airline, former Ethiopian Airlines chief executive Tewolde Gebremariam was appointed as Air India's new chief executive officer and managing director1.

India examines security impact of new Pakistan-Saudi-Turkey defence pact

India is examining the national security and regional stability implications of a new defence agreement signed by Pakistan, Saudi Arabia, and Turkey, according to External Affairs Ministry spokesperson Randhir Jaiswal1. The pact, known as the Makkah Joint Defence Agreement, was signed in Mecca on August 7 by Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman, Turkish President Recep Tayyip Erdogan, and Pakistani Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif1.

A central provision of the Makkah Joint Defence Agreement states that an armed attack against any one of the three signatory nations will be regarded as an attack against all three1. This trilateral pact builds upon a previous mutual defence agreement that was concluded between Pakistan and Saudi Arabia in September1.

In addition to addressing the new pact, External Affairs Ministry spokesperson Randhir Jaiswal called on the international community to hold Pakistan accountable following recent unrest2. His comments came after protests in Pakistan-occupied Kashmir caused more than 90 deaths2.
