Trump bans CNN, MS NOW, and Politico from White House press access

President Donald Trump banned CNN, MS NOW, and Politico from the White House on Friday1. He posted on Truth Social that the outlets report fake news2. Trump told reporters in the Oval Office that banned outlets purposely write negative news3. He said they do this to try to diminish Republicans3. Trump said he does not want them in his office4. He added that he wants the ban to go as far as possible4. Trump also said he intends to sue all three outlets4.

The banned organizations and rights groups quickly responded to the decision. CNN called the ban an illegal assault on constitutionally-protected press freedoms1. Politico said it will continue to report fairly on the White House1. It added that it will defend its First Amendment rights1. Bruce Brown of the Reporters Committee for Freedom of the Press called the move flatly unconstitutional1. Jameel Jaffer of the Knight First Amendment Institute said excluding journalists based on viewpoint is unconstitutional1. Amnesty International accused the administration of trying to stifle scrutiny3. Reporters Without Borders said it is exploring legal avenues to respond3.

US and Denmark reach security agreement regarding Greenland

The United States, Denmark, and Greenland agreed on Friday to sign a new Arctic security pact1. The three leaders announced they expect to sign the deal next week at the United Nations General Assembly1. Chairman of the Greenland Government Jens-Frederik Nielsen confirmed the upcoming agreement1. US President Donald Trump posted about the deal on Truth Social1. Trump said the agreement comes at no cost to the United States1. He called the security pact an "Infinite Life" agreement with no end date2.

Trump posted that plans will start immediately to build a large military presence in parts of Greenland3. US Secretary of State Marco Rubio called the agreement a historic deal1. Rubio said it is a huge win for the United States and the American people1. An anonymous US State Department official shared details about the terms4. The official said the deal explicitly guarantees complete ability for the US to defend the American continent4. This includes permanent military access across Greenland4. It also grants permanent basing rights and overflight rights4.

The US official said the agreement blocks other world powers4. The official said it guarantees China and Russia cannot set up military bases in Greenland4. The official added that neither country can send troops to Greenland4. Currently, the United States maintains a military installation at Pituffik Space Base in Greenland2. That facility operates under existing defense arrangements with Denmark2.

Denmark Prime Minister Mette Frederiksen spoke about the value of the pact1. Frederiksen said the deal strengthens common security in the Arctic and North Atlantic area1. She noted that the deal recognizes the sovereignty of the Kingdom1. She added that it respects Greenlandic self-determination1.

Protests erupt in Nigeria after 37 suspected illegal miners die in custody

Thirty-seven suspected illegal gold miners died in custody in Minna, Nigeria1. The men were held in a cell run by the Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps1. An intelligence report showed that overcrowding and poor ventilation likely caused the deaths1. The agency initially claimed a diphtheria outbreak might be responsible2. It later said the cause of death was unconfirmed2. The detainees were buried on Thursday without forensic examinations1.

The deaths triggered angry street protests in Minna. Security forces fired tear gas and opened fire on demonstrators1. Niger State Governor Umar Bago declared three days of mourning1. He imposed a 24-hour curfew following violent attacks on government buildings3. Governor Bago also postponed the start of election campaigning1.

Nigeria Interior Minister Olubunmi Tunji-Ojo ordered an investigation1. He immediately suspended Niger state agency commandant Suberu Siyaka Aniviye1. Agency spokesperson Babawale Afolabi said over 50 people were arrested in raids before the deaths2.

Bank of Japan raises interest rate to 1.25 percent

The Bank of Japan raised its target interest rate to 1.25 percent on Friday1. Policy makers voted 7-2 to increase the rate by 25 basis points1. The move brings borrowing costs in Japan to their highest level since 19952. Following the announcement, the Japanese yen fell against the US dollar1.

Bank of Japan Governor Kazuo Ueda discussed future policy after the decision2. Ueda said he would not rule out back-to-back interest rate hikes2. He noted that future decisions depend on how price conditions develop2. Inflation in Japan stood at 1.9 percent in August2.

US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent warned currency traders not to bet against the yen2. Bessent said he had insight into Japanese policy actions2. In late July, the US Treasury sold at least $10 billion in euros to buy yen2. That intervention aimed to support the Japanese currency2. The Bank of England voted to keep UK interest rates unchanged at 3.75 percent2.

Mamata Banerjee petitions Supreme Court after Election Commission freezes TMC symbol

Former West Bengal Chief Minister Mamata Banerjee challenged an Election Commission ruling in the Supreme Court1. Her Trinamool Congress faction filed the petition on September 18, 20261. The filing follows an interim order issued by the Election Commission of India on September 171. That order froze the All India Trinamool Congress name and symbol1. The decision applies to upcoming West Bengal Assembly byelections in Nandigram and Rejinagar1.

The Election Commission gave new names and symbols to both competing party factions1. Mamata Banerjee's group received the name 'Mamata All India Trinamool Congress'1. Her faction was given a 'football player' symbol1. A rival faction led by Ritabrata Banerjee received the name 'Democratic Trinamool Congress'1. The commission allotted an 'envelope' symbol to Ritabrata Banerjee's group1. Both factions are recognized as political parties in West Bengal, Meghalaya, and Tripura2.

Mamata Banerjee described losing her party's original name and symbol as a black day in democratic history2. She originally founded the All India Trinamool Congress in 19983. She created the party after splitting from the Indian National Congress3. She had personally hand-drew the original 'Jora Ghas Phool' symbol3. In her court filing, her faction highlighted long delays in disqualification proceedings1. Those proceedings against 10 rebel legislators remain pending before the West Bengal Assembly Speaker1.

The legal battle follows severe internal party rebellions. Nearly 60 out of 80 Trinamool Congress state lawmakers rebelled after May elections4. In June 2026, 20 out of 28 party members in the Lok Sabha merged with another party4. They joined the Tripura-based Nationalist Citizens Party of India4.

Following the symbol ruling, political plans for upcoming elections shifted. Sanchita Pradhan Dey was initially chosen as candidate for Mamata Banerjee's faction in Nandigram2. However, Dey withdrew her nomination2. Mamata Banerjee then announced her faction would not contest the Nandigram seat2. Instead, her group backed Congress candidate Milan Pradhan2. On September 18, 2026, police arrested Pradhan in connection with a 2007 murder case4.

US President Donald Trump signs Russia sanctions law targeting energy importers

US President Donald Trump signed a new sanctions bill into law on Friday1. The legislation is called the Lindsey O. Graham Sanctioning Russia and Iran Act of 20261. Republican Senator Lindsey Graham, who named the bill, died in July 20261. The US Senate passed the bill 86-11 in August1. The House of Representatives later approved it in a 262-159 vote1.

The law gives the US president authority to impose tariffs of up to 100 percent1. These tariffs apply to imports from the five largest buyers of Russian oil or natural gas1. The targets include India and China1. Countries importing under 15 percent of Russia's natural gas exports may get exemptions2. To qualify, they must take significant steps to reduce imports2.

India's Ministry of External Affairs issued a warning about the new law2. It said the sanctions could hurt India-US bilateral relations2. The ministry added that the law could impact global energy markets2. The bill also extends the Iran Sanctions Act for five more years1.

India rejects Pakistan's account of naval warship collision in Arabian Sea

India has rejected Pakistan's allegations regarding a collision between two warships in the Arabian Sea1. Foreign ministry spokesperson Randhir Jaiswal said PNS Hunain performed a dangerous overtaking manoeuvre1. He said this violated a 1991 bilateral agreement requiring warships to stay three nautical miles apart1.

INS Kolkata sustained no major damage and remained at sea1. PNS Hunain was observed returning to harbour after sustaining damage1. Pakistan gave a conflicting account of the collision. Pakistan summoned Indian Charge d'Affaires Satyanjal Pandey2. It alleged that aggressive manoeuvres by the Indian warship inside Pakistan's economic zone caused the crash2.

The last recorded collision between the two navies happened in 20112. In that incident, INS Godavari was marginally damaged by PNS Babur during anti-piracy operations2.
