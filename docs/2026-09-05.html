Russian drone strikes Ukraine's SBU headquarters in Kyiv as US envoys prepare peace mission

A Russian drone struck the headquarters of Ukraine's SBU security services in Kyiv on Friday1. The attack injured at least 12 people, according to Kyiv Mayor Vitali Klitschko1. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy accused Moscow of directly targeting the personal office of the SBU chief during the daytime strike2. Ukrainian Foreign Minister Andriy Sybiga described the strike on the SBU central building on Volodymyrska Street as a major escalation2.

The acting head of the SBU, Oleksandr Poklad, was not inside the building during the attack and was unharmed3. This was the first time the SBU headquarters had been hit since Russia began its full-scale invasion in February 20223. Russia currently controls about 20% of Ukrainian territory4.

The strike occurred as United States envoys prepared to travel to the region1. US President Donald Trump announced on Friday that special envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner are traveling to Kyiv and Moscow to present a proposal aimed at ending the war1. Russian state news agency TASS reported that Witkoff and Kushner will visit Moscow on Saturday and Sunday1. Zelenskyy said that the envoys will visit Ukraine on Sunday1. This trip marks their first journey to Ukraine since Trump returned to power1.

Zelenskyy proposed a temporary ceasefire during the visits of Witkoff and Kushner2. He said that Ukraine would conduct no airstrikes if Russia agrees to a ceasefire during the peace talks3. Zelenskyy also confirmed that Ukraine agreed to keep Russian airspace operating normally so the US envoys can travel safely5.

On Thursday, Russian President Vladimir Putin spoke at an international economic forum4. Putin said that there are chances for a peace deal4. But he maintained his demands for territorial concessions from Ukraine4. Trump attributed the war to a personality problem between Zelenskyy and Putin1. Trump said that both leaders truly hate each other1.

A July study by the Center for Strategic and International Studies estimates the war has cost Russia around 450,000 lives1. The same study estimates that Ukraine has suffered between 125,000 and 150,000 fatalities1. Russian strikes continued in other parts of Ukraine overnight5. An assault on the Odesa region killed one person and injured five others, including a child5. Overnight strikes in the Dnipro region killed two police officials5.

UN General Assembly votes to adopt new world map accurately representing Africa's size

The UN General Assembly voted 164 to 1 to adopt a resolution replacing traditional world maps with a new map1. The resolution, named Correct the Map, adopts a map projection that accurately represents the size of Africa1. Six countries abstained from the vote1. The United States was the only country to vote against the resolution1. US representative Yaryna Ferencevych criticized the resolution1. Ferencevych said that debating 16th-century map projects instead of international peace mocked the General Assembly's purpose1.

The traditional Mercator map shows Africa as similar in size to Greenland1. In reality, Africa is 14 times larger than Greenland1. African Union member states and the Bahamas sponsored the resolution2. The resolution is non-legally binding and does not challenge using the Mercator map for sea and air navigation2.

French Foreign Minister Jean-Noël Barrot announced that his ministry will stop using the Mercator map1. Barrot said at Sorbonne University that the Mercator map gives the US, Russia, and China disproportionate visual prominence over Africa, Latin America, or Southeast Asia3. France plans to switch to the Eckert IV map projection4. Togolese Foreign Minister Robert Dussey said that Togo hopes to transition school geography materials away from the Mercator map by the end of the year2.

US diesel prices hit record high amid energy market disruptions from Iran conflict

Average US diesel prices reached a record high of $5.85 per gallon on Friday1. According to AAA, this price surpassed the previous record of $5.81 set in June 20222. US diesel prices have increased by about 55% since the US-Israeli conflict with Iran began on February 281. On that date, the national average was $3.76 a gallon1. The average price of regular gasoline reached $4.15 per gallon, up from $2.98 before the war1.

Brent crude oil was trading above $95 a barrel on Friday, compared to around $70 before the conflict1. Distillate inventories on the US East Coast fell to a record low of 19.3 million barrels in late August1. About 90% of US school buses run on diesel fuel2. Russia also restricted diesel exports through September 30 after Ukrainian drones attacked Russian oil refineries1.

US President Donald Trump announced an oil agreement with Venezuela to lower fuel prices3. Venezuelan Interim President Delcy Rodríguez said the deal involves developing 17 oil fields3. A poll showed Trump's approval rating dropped to 33%, with 31% approving of the Iran war3.

Rescue operations continue in Nepal floods as Indian nationals remain missing and workers saved from tunnel

Rescuers pulled two workers alive from a flooded hydropower tunnel in Nepal after massive flash floods1. Preliminary investigations show a collapsed glacier and its bedrock triggered a flash flood along the Nepal-Tibet border3. Nepal's National Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Authority said the floods were caused by an ice-rock avalanche2.

Nepalese authorities reported at least 1,295 people were killed in the disasters2. More than 12,000 people have been rescued across Nepal, while almost 5,000 people remain missing2. Local authorities reported that floods damaged 35 motorable bridges, 45 suspension bridges, and 40 kilometers of black-topped roads in central Nepal2. A massive landslide occurred at Bhattar in Api Himal Rural Municipality4. The landslide partially blocked the Chaulani River in Darchula district4. The Chaulani River flows into the Mahakali River along the Nepal-India border4.

The flash floods severely impacted Nepal's power sector3. Almost 100% of Nepal's electricity comes from hydropower3. The floods severely damaged 12 hydropower plants in the Trishuli River basin3. The destruction knocked out more than 10% of Nepal's total power generation capacity3. The state-owned Nepal Electricity Authority owns six of the 12 damaged facilities3. The Upper Trishuli-1 hydropower project was valued at about $647 million before being nearly destroyed3. Overall, Nepal has 225 hydropower plants with over 3,900 megawatts of installed capacity3. The country earns nearly $200 million each year exporting hydroelectricity to India and Bangladesh3. In 2010, only two-thirds of Nepal's population had electricity access3.

Climate factors have contributed to increasing hazards in the region3. High-altitude Himalayan regions are warming 50% faster than the global average3. According to disaster authorities, more than nine in 10 recorded disaster events in Nepal between 2018 and 2024 were climate-related3.

The Indian government has engaged in relief and recovery efforts2. India's Ministry of External Affairs reported that 275 Indian nationals remain missing after the floods2. A total of 166 Indian nationals were rescued, and 1,907 Indian nationals safely crossed into Nepal from China2. India supplied about 95 tonnes of relief material using seven aircraft2. India also deployed 54 personnel to Nepal, including 30 tunnel rescue experts, 19 forensic experts, and a five-member medical team2. In India, the Karnataka State Disaster Management Authority designated Bowring and Lady Curzon Hospital in Bengaluru as the center to collect DNA samples from relatives of missing persons5.

Air India sacks pilot after drug test confirmation following total hydraulics failure on Phuket-Delhi flight

Air India dismissed a pilot who tested positive for a psychoactive substance after an inflight emergency1. The pilot commanded an August 4 flight from Phuket to Delhi carrying 137 passengers and crew members2. The eight-year-old Airbus A320neo aircraft suffered a near-simultaneous loss of all three hydraulic systems at 36,000 feet2. The flight control system detected the loss of the green hydraulic system at 9:32 am, followed four seconds later by the loss of the blue and yellow systems2.

During the altitude upset, the aircraft gained 372 feet and dropped by 292 feet2. The blue hydraulic system was restored shortly after 9:32 am, followed by the yellow and green systems4. The aircraft landed safely at Delhi airport at 11:00 am IST3. At least 24 people received medical assistance, with at least four suffering serious injuries1.

Both pilots passed alcohol breath analyzer tests in Delhi1. But a confirmatory blood test returned a non-negative result for a psychoactive substance for the pilot-in-command1. The co-pilot tested negative1. The pilot-in-command was monitoring controls rather than flying during the incident4. Under civil aviation rules, pilots testing positive must undergo rehabilitation before flying again1. Air India said safety is its highest priority4.
