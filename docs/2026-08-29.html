King Harald V of Norway dies at 89 as son Haakon VIII ascends throne

King Harald V of Norway died on August 28 at the age of 891. He ruled Norway for 35 years1. His 53-year-old son immediately became the new monarch as King Haakon VIII1. Norwegian Prime Minister Jonas Gahr Store said that an era has ended and a new one begins1. Store added that the king is dead and long live the king1. King Harald V stayed in an Oslo hospital from August 171. He suffered from haemolytic anaemia1. The Norwegian Armed Forces honoured the late king with a 21-shot funeral salute2. They fired the salute from Akershus Fortress in central Oslo2. King Haakon VIII's 22-year-old daughter Ingrid Alexandra became the new crown princess and next in line to the throne2. Britain's King Charles III paid tribute to King Harald V3. King Charles III called him a towering figure who guided Norway with warmth and humility3. Public support for the monarchy in Norway dropped to 54% in February from 72% in 20243. In June, Crown Princess Mette-Marit's son, Marius Borg Hoiby, received a four-year prison sentence after a rape conviction4.

US announces deal with Venezuela securing majority control of oil reserves

US President Donald Trump announced an agreement with Venezuela to secure American control over massive oil reserves1. The deal covers more than 65 billion barrels of proven oil reserves in Venezuela1. A US official said that the US government will retain 55% effective control of a joint venture under the deal1. That joint venture includes a private operator1. The private operator was granted a 100-year concession in Venezuela1. Venezuela's interim President Delcy Rodriguez said the agreement involves developing 17 strategic oil fields1. Rodriguez said the project will draw over $100 billion in investment1. She added that the operation will generate over $209 billion in state taxes1. US Secretary of State Marco Rubio said the Venezuela oil deal will bring nearly $100 billion in private investment2. Rubio said the project will support thousands of high-paying jobs2. The agreement comes after political changes in South America. On January 3, US special forces captured former Venezuelan President Nicolas Maduro1. US special forces also captured his wife, Cilia Flores, on that day1. Greenland creates truth commission to examine historic forced contraception campaign

Greenland announced the creation of a Truth and Reconciliation Commission to examine a historical forced contraception campaign1. The commission will examine actions taken against Indigenous Inuit women and girls1. A joint inquiry revealed that between the 1960s and 1990s, at least 4,000 Greenlandic Inuits were fitted with IUDs or received contraceptive injections without informed consent1. By the late 1970s, at least 4,070 women had been fitted with an IUD2. That figure represented one out of every two Greenlandic women of childbearing age2. The Danish parliament approved compensation payments of up to 300,000 Danish crowns, or $46,758, for each affected woman1. A four-member expert panel appointed to investigate the campaign split and submitted two conflicting reports on whether the actions constituted genocide3. Panel experts Dalee Sambo Dorough and Miriam Cullen determined that Inuit women's rights were violated3. However, they said they could not conclude genocide occurred based on contraceptive practices alone3.

Nepal and China report nearly 600 deaths after flash floods hit region

Nepal and Chinese authorities reported nearly 600 flood deaths after a glacier collapsed on Wednesday1,. The glacier collapse triggered flash floods across Nepal and Tibet, leaving over 2,400 people missing1. Nepal's official death toll reached 579, while Chinese state media reported seven deaths in Tibet2. Authorities reported at least 2,478 people missing after the flood2. That total included 1,924 missing in Nepal and 554 missing in Tibet2. More than 3,700 people have been rescued in Nepal2. The Red Cross said at least 93,000 people were affected by the disaster2. Chinese authorities evacuated 555 stranded tourists and 499 Chinese residents from Tibet2. Nepal Foreign Minister Shisir Khanal said Nepal needs foreign technical assistance for specialized tasks1. Khanal mentioned tunnel rescue, body identification, DNA testing, and body storage1. Nepal Finance Minister Swarnim Wagle said Nepal will require at least a few billion dollars to recover from the flood destruction3. Indian Prime Minister Narendra Modi spoke with Nepal Prime Minister Balendra Shah shortly after the disaster to offer humanitarian assistance4. India sent 62 tonnes of emergency relief assistance to Nepal across three airlifts4. The United Arab Emirates allocated $10 million in humanitarian aid to Nepal through the UAE Aid Agency4. Indian External Affairs Minister S. Jaishankar received 21 pilgrims from Tamil Nadu who were rescued from flood areas in Nepal5. At least 320 Indian nationals remained uncontactable in Nepal5. Official sources said 149 Indian nationals crossed over from China into Nepal on August 28, 20265.

Indian food authority proposes red warning labels on packaged food high in fat and sugar

The Food Safety and Standards Authority of India proposed red hexagonal warning labels on packaged foods1. The agency filed a compliance affidavit with the Supreme Court detailing the proposal1. The case stems from a public interest lawsuit filed by the NGO 3S and Our Health Society1. On August 13, a Supreme Court bench led by Justice J.B. Pardiwala issued an order giving the government two weeks to submit its final decision2. Proposed warning labels will display terms like High Fat, High Sugar, High Salt, or Highly Sweetened Beverage2. The label text will use a font size one point larger than the nutrition table on the back2. Single-ingredient foods such as ghee, edible oil, salt, sugar, jaggery, and honey are proposed to be exempted1. Proposed thresholds are based on the Dietary Guidelines for Indians 20243. The agency proposed implementing warning labels in two phases3. The first phase will start with foods high in two or more specified nutrients before extending to single nutrients3. A study in The Lancet estimated that roughly 450 million people in India could be overweight or obese by 20504. Senior Coca-Cola India executive Mili Bhattacharya said during a March 19 meeting that believing a symbol on a label will improve a diet is very simplistic5.

India signs agreement to buy Javelin anti-tank missiles from United States

India plans to purchase the Javelin anti-tank guided missile system from the United States1. US Ambassador to India Sergio Gor announced the purchasing plan1. India signed a Letter of Offer and Acceptance with the United States to buy the system through Foreign Military Sales2. The proposed sale is valued at $45.7 million3. The Javelin is a man-portable missile that uses fire-and-forget technology to track and hit targets automatically after launch1. The system has been in full-rate production since 1994 and reached its 50,000th delivery in 20212. India requested to buy 100 FGM-148 Javelin rounds, one fly-to-buy missile, and 25 Javelin Command Launch Units3. In November 2025, the US State Department approved a potential $93 million sale involving up to 100 Javelin systems and 216 Excalibur artillery projectiles3. Ambassador Gor said that US Secretary of War Pete Hegseth raised the possibility of joint co-production at the May 2026 Shangri-La Dialogue3.
