The United States put 50% tariffs on $20 billion of Canadian goods after trade talks collapsed

The United States put 50% tariffs on $20 billion worth of Canadian goods on Saturday1. US President Donald Trump first announced the 50% tariffs on July 202. Trump said the reason was Canadian bans on the sale of US alcohol in provincial stores2.

The tariffs affect about $20 billion of Canadian imports3. This amount represents about 5% of total Canadian exports3. Canadian exports to the US totaled $409 billion last year4. Those exports accounted for 73 percent of all Canadian exports4. Canadian Federation of Independent Business president Dan Kelly gave an estimate about the impact1. Kelly estimated that 40% of small Canadian exporters will be directly hit by the US tariffs1.

Trade talks between the two countries collapsed1. After the talks collapsed, Trump wrote a message on social media1. Trump wrote that Canada wants the benefits of being a US state without being one1. Trump also spoke on Fox News3. Trump said Canadians were foolish to engage in a trade war with the US3. He said Canadians are foolish to think they will win3.

US Trade Representative Jamieson Greer spoke on Saturday3. Greer said there are no plans to resume trade talks with Canada3. Canada and Mexico formally requested a 16-year renewal of the USMCA trade pact this summer1. But Trump refused to renew the USMCA trade agreement1.

Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney pledged to respond to the US tariffs1. Carney promised to match US tariffs dollar for dollar starting September 81. The Canadian tariffs will cover items including steel, dairy, appliances, and electronics1.

Carney said US negotiators made threats during trade talks5. Carney said they made threats against the French language, Quebec culture, and Canadian culture5. Carney also visited China in January5. During that visit, Carney agreed to reduce tariffs on a capped number of Chinese electric vehicles5. In exchange, China lowered its duties on Canadian canola5.

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy warned against holding national elections during the war

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy warned that holding elections during the war is a huge risk1. Zelenskyy said elections would be a tsunami for the state that will split Ukraine1. Ukraine suspended national elections under martial law after Russia's full-scale invasion in 20221. Zelenskyy's five-year presidential term formally expired in May 20241.

Ousted former Ukrainian defence minister Mykhailo Fedorov publicly called for Ukraine to organize fresh elections despite the ongoing war1. Fedorov spoke in a BBC interview2. Fedorov said Zelenskyy should face questions regarding his awareness of potential inner-circle corruption2. His comment came after a money laundering investigation2. Ukraine's anti-corruption bureau uncovered a scheme misappropriating more than $3 million in Ukrainian currency2. The money was used to pay bail for a former energy minister accused of money laundering2.

Zelenskyy also discussed military concerns1. Zelenskyy said Russia has a stockpile of between 600 and 630 high-speed ballistic missiles1. He said Russia seeks to produce over 100 a month1. Zelenskyy said he believes Russia will try to conscript an additional 300,000 soldiers after its parliamentary elections in September3.

Foreign allies announced plans to send aid to Ukraine3. Germany agreed to supply Ukraine with about 600 PAC-2 and PAC-3 interceptors in 2027 and 20283. Norway announced plans to give 85 billion Norwegian crowns in financial support in the 2027 fiscal year4. That support equals $9.2 billion4.

Iran announced a major natural gas discovery in Fars province containing recoverable energy reserves

Iran announced the discovery of more than 200 billion cubic metres of natural gas in Fars province1. Officials said over 160 billion cubic metres of the gas is potentially recoverable1. Iranian Oil Minister Mohsen Paknejad spoke about the discovery2. Paknejad said the discovery contains more than 7.5 trillion cubic feet of gas2. He said around 5.7 trillion cubic feet of that gas is recoverable2. Paknejad described the newly discovered gas as sweet3. He said low levels of hydrogen sulphide will lower development and operating costs3.

The discovery comes after damage to Iranian energy facilities2. Attacks on Iranian energy infrastructure started in late February2. The attacks reduced Iran's daily gas production capacity by around a quarter2. Iran's pre-war natural gas production was estimated at about 650 million cubic metres per day3.

Political tensions remain high in the region2. US President Donald Trump shared a graphic on Truth Social2. The graphic depicted the Strait of Hormuz as new US territory2. Iranian Supreme National Security Council secretary Mohsen Rezaei issued a warning to nearby countries2. Rezaei warned neighboring countries that Iran will consider them enemies if they join the US economic war2.

Panama-flagged cargo ship sinks in Bay of Bengal off Odisha coast triggering rescue operation

A Panama-flagged bulk carrier named M.V. Ocean Winner sank in the Bay of Bengal off the coast of Odisha1. M.V. Ocean Winner sank about 230 to 240 nautical miles off the coast2. The ship was carrying 72,100 metric tonnes of iron ore2. The vessel departed Paradip Port late on August 20, 20261. Specifically, the ship departed the Paradip coast at 10:42 pm on August 202. The vessel was carrying iron ore fines2.

Initial reports from Indian maritime authorities said M.V. Ocean Winner was bound for China2. But other government information indicated Singapore as its destination2. Communication with M.V. Ocean Winner was lost 36 hours after its departure1. The loss of communication prompted a search and rescue request to the Maritime Rescue Coordination Centre1. Victory Star Group Co Ltd requested search and rescue assistance after communication was lost late on August 223.

The Andaman & Nicobar Command reported that M.V. Ocean Winner sent a distress alert on Saturday3. The alert came from a position around 300 nautical miles north-northwest of Diglipur3. M.V. Ocean Winner had 24 crew members onboard1. The crew comprised 20 Chinese nationals, three Myanmarese nationals, and one Bangladeshi national1.

An Indian Navy P8I surveillance aircraft detected two floating liferafts near the reported position of M.V. Ocean Winner1. The aircraft also spotted an oil slick in the area1. Commercial vessel MT Aisopos rescued two Chinese crew members from liferafts by late evening on August 22, 20261. Union Shipping and Waterways Minister Sarbananda Sonowal confirmed that two survivors were rescued2. Sonowal said the rescued crew received medical care2.

The search operation for the missing crew members continues3. The Indian Coast Guard deployed vessels ICGS Varad, ICGS Anmol, and ICGS Vijit to assist in search operations3. Coast Guard spokesperson Commandant Amit Uniyal reported that rough sea conditions were slowing work4. Uniyal said rough sea conditions in the Bay of Bengal were hampering search operations4.

Database records from Equasis show M.V. Ocean Winner was a 28-year-old vessel built in 19981. The vessel measured 225 metres long and 32.26 metres wide1. It had a capacity of around 73,000 tonnes1. M.V. Ocean Winner's current owner is based in Fujian, China1. Protection and Indemnity insurance covered the vessel starting August 1, 20261. An inspection in Chittagong, Bangladesh, in June reported three deficiencies1. One deficiency involved its magnetic compass1.

Similar maritime incidents have happened in Indian waters before4. In September 2009, the Mongolian-flagged bulk carrier MV Black Rose sank near Paradip4. That vessel sank after developing a hull leak while carrying 23,847 tonnes of iron ore4. In 2009, the MV Asian Forest capsized off Mangalore Port on India's west coast4. That ship capsized due to cargo liquefaction after loading iron ore fines4.
