Powerful 7.4-magnitude earthquake strikes Colombia, killing at least 111 people

A powerful 7.4-magnitude earthquake has struck Colombia, killing at least 111 people, injuring 87 others, and causing 61 buildings to collapse across the country1. The earthquake was measured at magnitude 7.4 by both the US Geological Survey and Colombia's national authority2. Its epicenter was located near the Tatama National Nature Reserve on the eastern border of Choco province, approximately 30 kilometers from Cartago2. Less than an hour after the primary earthquake, the US Geological Survey recorded a 5.0-magnitude aftershock2.

The severe shaking caused extensive damage to public infrastructure and residential areas throughout the country2. Colombian President Abelardo de la Espriella reported that 1,575 homes were damaged, 37 homes were completely destroyed, 18 health centers were damaged, and 52 schools were damaged across Colombia2.

The metropolitan area of Pereira was particularly hard hit by the disaster1. Officials reported that 40 people died in the Pereira metropolitan area, 65 buildings collapsed, and people were trapped inside 15 of those collapsed structures1. In response to the crisis, the mayor of Pereira, Mauricio Salazar, declared a state of public calamity, manifest urgency, and a nighttime curfew in several districts1.

Damage was also reported in other towns and cities across the region3. In Manizales, one of the towers of the city's neo-Gothic cathedral collapsed during the earthquake and fell directly onto the nave3. Due to the widespread danger and destruction, civil aviation authorities suspended flight operations at multiple airports, including those in Pereira, Manizales, Quibdo, Armenia, Cartago, Buenaventura, and Cali2.

International assistance has begun to arrive as rescue operations continue1. Ecuador's President Daniel Noboa Azin offered Colombia 47 specialized rescuers, search dogs, and equipment to assist in response efforts1. Colombia has suffered deadly seismic events in its past, including a 6.2-magnitude earthquake near Armenia in 1999 that killed more than 1,100 people3.

Netanyahu rejects US Gaza peace plan and demands full Hamas disarmament

Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu has publicly rejected a US-backed peace proposal for Gaza, insisting that no military withdrawal can happen until Hamas is fully disarmed1. The 15-point peace plan, proposed by Donald Trump, called for Hamas to surrender its weapons, the creation of an international stabilization mechanism, and a progressive withdrawal of Israeli forces1.

Netanyahu's public rejection blindsided Israeli military commanders, who had discussed withdrawal plans in Rafah with him just days earlier2. The rejection came as US Central Command chief Adm Brad Cooper visited Israel to urge officials to work toward ending military operations in Gaza, Lebanon, and Iran2. Currently, Israeli forces control more than 60 percent of the territory in Gaza3, and more than 73,300 people have been killed in Israel's offensive since October 20234.

Despite his firm rhetoric, Netanyahu scaled back airstrikes in Gaza by requiring targeted killings to be authorized directly by the IDF chief of staff rather than regional commanders2. According to a senior US official quoted by journalist Barak Ravid, the White House understood Netanyahu's political needs ahead of Israel's upcoming election and was unbothered by his statements as long as attacks remained restrained3. Meanwhile, construction of the first base for an International Stabilization Force is scheduled to begin in Rafah at the end of August 20263.

Ukrainian drone strike on Russian oil refinery kills 13 people

A Ukrainian drone strike on the city of Nizhnekamsk in Tatarstan, Russia, located about 750 miles from the border, has killed at least 13 people1. Local officials reported that 75 people were injured and one child was killed in the attack2. The strike targeted the Taneco oil refinery, which is owned by the company Tatneft1. Tatarstan regional head Rustam Minnikhanov stated that nine people died when a drone hit a hostel building1. The Uzbek consulate in Tatarstan confirmed that seven of the victims killed in the attack were citizens of Uzbekistan1.

Russia's Defence Ministry claimed its air defenses intercepted 456 Ukrainian drones overnight across Russian territory and Crimea2.

In other international developments, the World Health Organization reported that a warehouse in Ukraine's Dnipro region was struck twice in 24 hours, destroying $500,000 worth of medical supplies3. In Sweden, security service Säpo shut down a Russian intelligence operation involving three people working under diplomatic cover1. Germany announced it will double personnel in its federal police anti-drone unit to 300 after finding an explosive drone at Leipzig-Halle airport4. In Estonia, the governing coalition lost its parliamentary majority when two MPs left their parties, leaving the government with 50 of 101 seats4.

Heatwaves and drought trigger widespread water restrictions in Western Europe

Severe heatwaves and intense drought have forced nations across Western Europe to implement strict water restrictions1. Nearly 70 percent of France, covering 67 out of 101 departments, has been placed under the highest crisis alert level for water-use restrictions1. Weather agency Météo-France warned that temperatures would reach nearly 40°C in southeastern France, following a national record high of 44.3°C set on June 232. July wildfires in southwestern France forced the evacuation of 220,000 people from their homes2.

The extreme weather extends across the region2. England and Wales recorded their driest July since 1836, with 19 counties in England receiving no rain for the entire month2. Western Europe experienced its hottest June and July on record, with average temperatures reaching 21.62°C, which is 2.79°C above normal3. European climate agency Copernicus reported that July for Europe as a whole was the 11th warmest on record4. Copernicus also noted that global extrapolar sea surface temperatures reached a record average of 20.96°C in July2.

In the United States, July was the hottest month ever recorded in 132 years of record-keeping, with temperatures 3.3°F above average5. Wyoming recorded its hottest July on record, at 5.1°F above normal5.

Russian court bars anti-war Yabloko party from State Duma elections

Russia's Supreme Court has upheld a lawsuit by the Rodina party to bar the liberal, anti-war Yabloko party from competing in upcoming State Duma parliamentary elections1. The Rodina party's lawsuit accused Yabloko of supporting an extremist organization and committing copyright infringement by quoting a Soviet-era song2. Outside the courthouse, Yabloko leader Nikolai Rybakov stated that the party intends to appeal the decision1. An earlier 2026 poll showed Yabloko had 3 percent public support, below the 5 percent threshold needed to win seats in the State Duma1.

The ruling comes amid a broader crackdown on Yabloko members and political opposition in Russia2. In June, senior Yabloko member Maxim Kruglov was sentenced to seven years in prison over social media posts about civilian casualties in Ukrainian cities2. In 2025, Kremlin critic and Yabloko politician Lev Shlosberg was charged with spreading false information about the Russian military3. Meanwhile, public support for Russia's ruling United Russia party has fallen to 33 percent2.

WHO reveals DR Congo Ebola outbreak began months before official declaration

The World Health Organization (WHO) revealed that the current Ebola outbreak in the Democratic Republic of Congo began in February, three months earlier than its official declaration on May 151. WHO Africa director Dr. Mohamed Janabi announced that genetic sequencing confirmed the earlier timeline1. The DR Congo health ministry has reported 4,294 confirmed cases and 1,960 deaths caused by the Bundibugyo species of Ebola1.

Dr. Janabi stated that health workers are reaching only about 30 percent of Ebola cases due to widespread insecurity in eastern DR Congo, leaving the remaining patients to die at home1. The current outbreak is the fastest-growing on record, reaching 1,000 deaths faster than the 2014-2016 West Africa outbreak2.

To help combat the disease, the WHO has recommended initiating full-scale human trials for the Ervebo vaccine to assess whether it provides cross-protection against the Bundibugyo strain2.

Typhoon Dolphin forces over 1 million evacuations in eastern China

Tropical storm Dolphin brought heavy rainfall and severe flooding across eastern China after making landfall as a typhoon on Sunday1. Dolphin hit land in Taizhou, Zhejiang province, with maximum sustained winds of 151 kilometers per hour2. Chinese authorities were forced to relocate more than 900,000 people in Wenzhou, 150,000 in Fujian, and 200,000 in Shanghai2. A central Shanghai weather station recorded 313 millimeters of rainfall in 24 hours, the highest 24-hour total in over 150 years of record-keeping3. On Monday, Shanghai's two main airports cancelled 943 flights after cancelling over 1,300 flights before landfall2.

The storm's broader system affected surrounding regions2. Typhoon Dolphin drove temperatures in Hong Kong to a record high of 36.9°C on Sunday3. In Okinawa, Japan, the storm injured six people and cut power to over 50,000 buildings3. In the Philippines, monsoon rains enhanced by Dolphin triggered a landslide in Baguio, bringing the death toll in northern Luzon to 152.

Trump signs executive order seeking changes to childhood vaccine schedule

US President Donald Trump has signed an executive order directing major changes to the CDC childhood immunization schedule and calling for the combined MMR vaccine to be split into three separate shots1. Trump claimed in the Oval Office that children should receive vaccines across multiple doctor visits instead of getting several shots on a single day2. He also claimed that receiving MMR vaccines together could be lethal and attributed high autism rates to vaccine volume1. Republican Senator Bill Cassidy criticized the order on social media, writing that it is wrong and stating that vaccines do not cause autism1.

The executive order retains full routine recommendations for only six of 17 childhood vaccines, protecting against 11 diseases1. Trump announced that his administration will no longer recommend hepatitis B, Covid-19, or influenza vaccines for children3. Currently, separate individual vaccines for measles, mumps, and rubella are not licensed or available for children in the US4. Trump previously issued a vaccine directive in December 2025, but subsequent policy changes were blocked by a federal court4.

Student protests over exam leaks spark violent clashes and political tension in Jharkhand

Widespread anger over recruitment exam irregularities has led to violent clashes between student protesters and police in Ranchi, Jharkhand, triggering political turmoil and demands for official investigations1. Thousands of job aspirants marched toward the Jharkhand state assembly to protest against reported exam leaks2. To prevent demonstrators from storming barricades near the state legislature, police used water cannons, tear gas, and batons2. Demonstrators emphasized the intense competition for employment in the state, pointing out that a recent recruitment drive had attracted nearly 640,000 applications for just 2,025 open posts2.

The exam scandal has already led to official action within the state's testing authority2. The former chair of the Jharkhand Public Service Commission was arrested over exam irregularities, and three commission members subsequently resigned2. However, student leaders rejected government claims that 98 percent of their demands had been met, stating that officials had cancelled only three of the 13 requested exams3.

The crisis quickly escalated into a national political confrontation1. Outside the chief minister's residence, Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP) leaders Babulal Marandi and Aditya Sahu led a protest demanding a Central Bureau of Investigation (CBI) inquiry into the leaks before being detained by police1. In Delhi, Congress leader Rahul Gandhi held a press conference condemning police violence against the peacefully protesting students1. In response, Union Minister JP Nadda accused Rahul Gandhi of spreading misinformation regarding claims that pellet guns were used against student protesters at Jantar Mantar4.

Meanwhile, Jharkhand Chief Minister Hemant Soren released an open letter regarding the situation3. Soren thanked security agencies for handling the student protests with restraint and urged students not to fall into political traps3.

Indian Parliament passes Tribunals Reforms Bill to create new national commission

The Lok Sabha has passed the Tribunals Reforms Bill, 2026, by voice vote without debate amid persistent protests from Opposition members1. The legislation establishes a New Delhi-based National Tribunals Commission composed of a chairperson and four members, including two judicial members and two technical members1. The bill repeals the previous Tribunals Reforms Act, 2021, which was struck down by the Supreme Court for violating judicial independence1.

Under the new law, tribunal chairpersons will serve five-year terms up to age 70, while members will serve five-year terms up to age 672. Establishing and operating the new commission and its secretariat is estimated to cost ₹27.14 crore2. The bill places 16 tribunals under commission oversight3. While it excludes the National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT), it includes the NCLT's appellate forum3.

The reform comes as Indian commercial tribunals struggle with severe operational delays3. An analysis by think tank DAKSH showed that commercial tribunals in India face a backlog of more than 356,000 cases worth ₹24.72 trillion, managed by just 350 members3.
