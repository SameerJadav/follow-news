US envoys meet Putin in Moscow as Russia orders three-day pause in Kyiv strikes

US envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner met Russian President Vladimir Putin in Moscow on Saturday1.

Kirill Dmitriev, who is Putin's investment envoy, received the two American envoys in the Russian capital3. US President Donald Trump said on Friday that Witkoff and Kushner were bringing a proposal to end the war1. Russian state media reported that the meeting between Putin and the envoys at the Kremlin lasted three hours1. At the start of the meeting, Putin said it is convenient for Russia to work with Witkoff and Kushner4. But Putin also told the envoys that the current situation is not so easy1.

During the meeting, Witkoff thanked Putin for agreeing with Ukraine to stop strikes on each other's capital cities for three days1. Putin ordered a three-day halt to strikes on Kyiv because of the visit by the US envoys1. Kremlin spokesperson Dmitry Peskov said Putin gave the order to stop strikes starting from midnight Saturday5. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky said Ukraine is ready to refrain from strikes on Moscow through the end of Saturday, as well as on Sunday and Monday1. Witkoff and Kushner are scheduled to travel to Kyiv on Sunday for diplomatic engagements1. CIA Director John Ratcliffe made a rare visit to Moscow a little over a week before Witkoff and Kushner arrived1.

Putin said in televised remarks that Russia has not agreed to a ceasefire in areas of Ukraine outside of Kyiv1. Ground troops in Ukraine remain locked along a 775-mile frontline5. Zelensky said Russia fired demonstrative strikes on airports in Kyiv and Boryspil overnight before his planned meeting with the envoys1. On Friday, a Russian drone hit the headquarters of the Security Service of Ukraine5. Russian strikes killed at least seven people across Ukraine on Saturday, including two in the Bucha district outside Kyiv5. Zelensky said an overnight Russian strike on a civilian building in the Dnipro region killed four people and injured five others1. Oleksandr Hanzha, head of the local military administration, said an overnight Russian strike at a factory in the Dnipropetrovsk region killed five people and wounded five others5.

US military disables three Iranian oil tankers following attacks on warships

US Central Command said its forces permanently disabled two Iran-linked oil tankers and completely destroyed a third in the Gulf of Oman1. US Central Command said the strikes retaliated after Tehran targeted a US aircraft carrier and a guided-missile destroyer1. US Defence Secretary Pete Hegseth posted on social media that if Iran shoots at US ships, the US will destroy and sink their oil tankers1. US Central Command commander Admiral Brad Cooper said the US military would destroy Iran's oil fleet if necessary to defend American forces2.

US Central Command identified the disabled tankers as M/T Downy, struck off Kharg Island, and M/T Stark 1, struck near Jask3. Kharg Island hosts Iran's main oil export terminal, handling about 90% of the country's crude oil exports via pipes from the mainland1. US Central Command identified the destroyed third tanker as M/T Kylo, which is also known as Noxen3.

The US resumed attacks after striking two rocket launchers to stop Iran from laying naval mines in the Strait of Hormuz1. Iranian state media reported that Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps retaliated against six ships1. These included three oil tankers in the Strait of Hormuz and three US-affiliated vessels1.

US Vice President JD Vance said he was extremely sceptical about Iranian claims that US strikes killed four people at a wedding in Sirik1. A preliminary US military inquiry found a February 28 strike on a school compound in Minab killed at least 156 people2. The inquiry found outdated coordinates had identified the school as part of a naval base2. The average price for a gallon of diesel in the US reached $5.85, up from $3.71 a year prior1.

Rescuers pull survivors from wreckage days after flood disaster in Nepal and China

Rescue teams pulled survivors from flood wreckage in Nepal and China days after a massive disaster1. Rescuers pulled Chandika Kumari Shrestha, who is 64 years old, semi-conscious from a buried house in Betrawati 10 days after floods hit Nepal1. Local officials in Betrawati said only five out of about 300 houses remained standing after the flood1. A joint team of Nepal army personnel and South Korean experts rescued Chinese worker Luhaitao from a tunnel1. Luhaitao was rescued 225 metres inside a tunnel at the Upper Trishuli-1 hydropower project and flown to a military hospital1. The Upper Trishuli-1 project is valued at about $647m3.

A collapse of ice, rock, and mud swept through a Himalayan valley in Nepal4. Nepal's national disaster authority reported that the flood killed at least 1,331 people2. Nepal's disaster management agency reported that at least 1,375 people died5. The disaster left about 5,000 people missing, including 589 foreign nationals2. Officials in Tibet recorded 31 deaths and 531 people missing across the border2.

About 900 workers remain unaccounted for at hydropower construction sites in Nepal, with roughly 500 believed trapped inside tunnels2. The floods severely damaged 12 hydropower plants in the Trishuli River basin and knocked out over 10% of Nepal's power capacity3. Nearly 100% of Nepal's electricity comes from hydropower3.

UN adopts resolution approving world map that shows true size of countries

The United Nations voted on Friday to support moving away from the 16th-century Mercator map toward a map showing true country sizes1. The UN General Assembly adopted the Correct the Map resolution to fix discrepancies in the Mercator map projection2. Flemish cartographer Gerardus Mercator created the Mercator map in 1569 to aid European sailors in ocean navigation2. The African continent is about 14 times larger than Greenland, despite looking similar in size on the Mercator map2.

Togo introduced the resolution at the UN on behalf of 54 African member states, supported by the African Union3. Togo's Foreign Minister Robert Dussey said a fair map does not change geography, but changes how the world is seen4. A team of cartographers created the Equal Earth projection in 2018, and African Union members adopted it in February2.

The UN resolution passed with 164 countries voting in favour, one voting against, and six abstaining5. India voted in favour of the resolution, while the United States was the only country to vote against it5. US representative Yaryna Ferencevych said resolutions like Correct the Map cause the UN to lose credibility3. The US delegation claimed the resolution was part of a larger radical political project4. Ukraine abstained over concerns about how some Equal Earth maps depict Ukrainian territories occupied by Russia5.

PM Modi addresses SRCC centenary, defends GDP growth and warns Pakistan

Indian Prime Minister Narendra Modi addressed the centenary celebrations at Shri Ram College of Commerce in Delhi on Saturday evening1. Modi previously spoke at the college in February 2013 while serving as Chief Minister of Gujarat1. Modi recalled his 2013 glass analogy, saying he sees the glass as full, with half water and half air1. Modi is serving his third consecutive term as Prime Minister and completes 25 years as head of government next month1.

Modi cited India's first-quarter GDP growth rate of 7.8 per cent as evidence of economic strength1. Modi described critics of the GDP growth numbers as grumpy uncles who focus only on negativity1. Modi said those who placed India among the fragile five economies cannot digest India's 7.8 per cent growth2.

Former senior Finance Ministry official Subhash Chandra Garg claimed the 7.8 per cent growth rate was overstated due to base revisions2. Garg said nominal GDP growth would be 2.6 per cent if the previous year's base was not revised down2. Former RBI Governor Raghuram Rajan asked why strong growth has not led to higher private investment and job creation2. India's Ministry of Statistics held a press briefing to defend the revised GDP methodology implemented in February2.

Congress leader Kanhaiya Kumar called Modi's address a public relations campaign before student elections3. Kumar said Modi focused on 2013 and 2047 while failing to address student or public issues1. Congress MP Pawan Khera criticized Modi's speech on social media, writing that even onions would burst into tears1.

Modi criticized the Congress party's student outreach campaign, called Chhatron Ki Goonj, describing it as an echo of lies4. The campaign focuses on student issues including exam paper leaks, rising education costs, and unemployment4. Modi said if Pakistan indulges in misadventure, Indian armed forces enter their territory and strike3. Modi said his Independence Day reference to urban Naxals exposed political opponents like a homeopathic dose3.

Indian government advances Census enumeration in four poll-bound states

The Indian government advanced the population enumeration phase of Census 2027 in four states1. The enumeration in Uttar Pradesh, Goa, Punjab, and non-snow-bound Uttarakhand will run from December 1, 2026, to January 4, 20271. The reference date for the advanced Census enumeration in these four states is set for January 5, 20271. A revisional round for Census enumeration in these four states will take place from January 5 to January 9, 20272. A 15-day self-enumeration window for Census 2027 will open in the four states from November 16 to November 30, 20261.

Assembly elections are scheduled to take place in Uttar Pradesh, Uttarakhand, Punjab, Goa, and Manipur in February-March 20271. Government officials said dates were advanced to prevent staffing conflicts between census duties and elections2.

The Census phase in Manipur was deferred following local demands to conduct the National Register of Citizens exercise first1. The Ministry of Home Affairs deferred the Manipur census on August 31 amid ongoing state violence2.

Census 2027 will be India's first digital census and will collect caste data for the first time since 19311. The national population census was originally scheduled for 2021 but was delayed due to the COVID-19 pandemic2.
