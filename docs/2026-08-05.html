Mass Funeral Held in Gaza for 112 People as Ceasefire Negotiations Stall

Thousands of mourners gathered in the Sabra neighbourhood of Gaza City for a mass funeral for 112 members of the Abu Shreia and al-Hassaina families1. Rescue teams in Gaza recovered the 112 bodies from an Israeli airstrike that flattened a residential block in November 20232. To assist in the recovery efforts from destroyed buildings, the International Committee of the Red Cross provided two excavators to Gaza Civil Defence teams3. Civil Defence spokesperson Mahmoud Basal estimated that more than 8,000 bodies remain buried beneath the rubble across Gaza1.

The mass burial comes as ceasefire talks remain deadlocked. Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu publicly rejected key elements of a US-backed ceasefire proposal for Gaza2. Netanyahu stated that Israeli troops will not withdraw from their positions until Hamas is completely disarmed2. In July, Hamas had agreed to disarm and hand over power to the National Committee for the Administration of Gaza4.

The war began after a Hamas-led attack on October 7, 2023. Reports differ on the toll of that attack: one source notes over 1,000 deaths in Israel4, while another states that about 1,200 people were killed and 251 taken hostage5. Gaza's Health Ministry reported that over 73,377 Palestinians have been killed since the war began2.

US and Iran Report Progress on Reopening the Strait of Hormuz

Global oil prices dropped sharply following announcements that negotiators are making progress toward reopening the Strait of Hormuz1. Brent crude prices fell by almost 5 percent to under $80 a barrel after reports of positive movement in the talks were released2. The shipping channel is vital for global energy trade, as the Strait of Hormuz handled about one-fifth of global daily oil and liquefied natural gas supplies prior to the conflict2.

Diplomats and government officials from several nations have indicated that a agreement may be near. US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent stated that there is a chance a deal could be reached imminently to reopen the Strait of Hormuz1. Iranian Foreign Ministry spokesperson Esmaeil Baghaei stated that talks with Oman regarding shipping lanes in the Strait of Hormuz have been positive3. Meanwhile, US Secretary of State Marco Rubio stated that progress has been made in talks with Iran and Oman, though he noted that no final agreement has been reached4.

Details of the proposed transit framework have begun to emerge. Under an emerging agreement, ships would enter the Persian Gulf through an Iranian-controlled route and exit through an Omani-controlled route4. High-level diplomatic discussions are also taking place across the region to reduce tensions. Qatar's Emir Sheikh Tamim bin Hamad Al Thani held a phone call with US President Donald Trump to discuss de-escalation efforts3.

The negotiations come as military pressures remain high in the region. Sources reported that the US military has used nearly 80 percent of its THAAD missile interceptor inventory during the conflict with Iran4.

EU and Spain Coordinate Response Following Mass Migrant Influx into Ceuta

Around 72,000 migrants entered Ceuta between July 30 and July 31, with approximately 70,000 subsequently returned to Morocco1. The border crisis resulted in casualties on both sides of the border. At least 75 people died on the Spanish side during the Ceuta migrant crisis, while 11 deaths were reported in Morocco1.

In response to the surge, Spain installed a maritime barrier off the coast of Ceuta following a ruling by its Supreme Court regarding migrant deportations2. Neighbouring European nations also reacted quickly. Italy temporarily suspended its Schengen free-movement agreement with Spain following the migrant surge into Ceuta1.

European officials commented on the causes and political implications of the crisis. European Commissioner Magnus Brunner stated that the migrant influx into Ceuta was driven by criminal smuggling networks and social media misinformation2. Brunner also stated that Spain's plan to regularize undocumented migrants is not a good signal for the rest of Europe3. Following the crisis, twenty-two EU member states signed a letter regarding border security and migration policies3. Additionally, Italy proposed that the European Union adopt new external processing hubs for asylum procedures in third countries4.

Opposition Leader Udhayanidhi Stalin Arrested and Granted Station Bail

Tamil Nadu Leader of the Opposition Udhayanidhi Stalin was arrested in Chennai and transported to Sengipatti Police Station in Thanjavur for questioning1. Udhayanidhi Stalin was booked under provisions of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, the Information Technology Act, and the Tamil Nadu Women Harassment Prevention Act2.

The police questioning lasted several hours before his release. Udhayanidhi Stalin was released from police custody around 8 p.m. on Tuesday following questioning3. His release came after the Madras High Court directed the police to release Udhayanidhi Stalin on station bail after questioning1.

The legal action followed controversy surrounding a recent public speech. Udhayanidhi Stalin denied making double-meaning or derogatory remarks against actor Trisha Krishnan during a rally in Thanjavur2. Udhayanidhi Stalin asserted that his speech focused entirely on farmers' demands regarding the release of Cauvery water4.

The arrest drew political reactions from national leaders. Bharatiya Janata Party MP Kangana Ranaut stated on social media that Udhayanidhi Stalin's arrest sets a positive precedent against vulgar public language5.

Indian Ship Sinks Off Yemen Coast Following Projectile Attack

An Indian-flagged vessel, the MSV Faize Noore Oliya, capsized and sank off the coast of Yemen after being struck by a projectile1. All 14 people on board the MSV Faize Noore Oliya, including 13 Indian nationals, were safely rescued1. The Yemeni Navy and Coast Guard rescued the crew of MSV Faize Noore Oliya and brought them safely to the Port of Mokha2.

The attack occurred during heightened maritime threats in regional waters. Yemen's Houthi movement declared a naval blockade of Saudi Arabia's Red Sea ports on July 202. Earlier, on July 14, two UAE-flagged vessels carrying Indian crew members were attacked in the Strait of Hormuz, resulting in one Indian fatality3.

In response to these incidents, India's Ministry of External Affairs stated that repeated attacks on commercial shipping in West Asia are deeply worrisome4. The Indian government established real-time tracking for ships operating in high-risk Gulf waters through an integrated digital platform called eNavik4.

Prashant Kishor's Jan Suraaj Party Wins Bankipur Bypoll in Bihar

Jan Suraaj Party founder Prashant Kishor won the Bankipur Assembly byelection in Bihar, defeating the Bharatiya Janata Party candidate by over 19,000 votes1. Prashant Kishor secured 64,151 votes in the Bankipur bypoll, while BJP candidate Neeraj Kumar Sinha received 44,827 votes2. The victory is notable because the Bharatiya Janata Party had held the Bankipur Assembly seat in Bihar for more than three decades prior to the byelection1. The Bankipur seat became vacant after BJP leader Nitin Nabin was elected to the Rajya Sabha2. In his election affidavit, Prashant Kishor declared combined family movable and immovable assets valued at ₹198.07 crore3.

Following the result, Prashant Kishor stated that his victory in Bankipur serves as a message for the NDA leadership to replace Bihar Chief Minister Samrat Choudhary2. The election outcome led to immediate high-level political meetings. Prime Minister Narendra Modi met former Bihar Chief Minister Nitish Kumar and JDU leaders in New Delhi following the Bankipur bypoll result4.
