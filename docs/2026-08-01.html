Hamas Agrees to US-Brokered Disarmament Framework for Gaza

US President Donald Trump has declared a major breakthrough in international negotiations regarding the disarmament of Hamas in Gaza and the full withdrawal of Israeli military forces1. The preliminary framework for this Gaza agreement was reached following a high-level meeting involving mediators from Egypt, Qatar, Turkiye, and the United States2.

The proposed agreement relies on a detailed governance and security plan created by an international body. Under a 15-point roadmap published by the Board of Peace, a body named the National Committee for the Administration of Gaza will take charge of local oversight3. This national committee will oversee the decommissioning and secure storage of heavy weapons, production sites, and underground tunnels throughout Gaza3.

As part of the proposed agreement, Hamas negotiator Ghazi Hamad stated that Hamas will hand over its weapons to the National Committee for the Administration of Gaza1. However, Hamad emphasized that this transfer of arms is strictly on the condition of an Israeli troop withdrawal1. An annex attached to the proposed plan requires Israeli forces to withdraw to a boundary known as the Yellow Line within 24 hours of approval by the Israeli government4.

Despite the diplomatic progress, the agreement faces immediate political opposition inside Israel. Far-right Israeli National Security Minister Itamar Ben-Gvir publicly dismissed the proposed Gaza deal, calling it unacceptable1.

At the same time, violence has continued to affect civilians in the enclave. Medical authorities in Gaza reported that one Palestinian was killed and several others were wounded in Israeli airstrikes and gunfire shortly after Trump made his announcement3. The human cost of the wider war remains severe, as at least 73,341 Palestinians have been killed in Gaza during the conflict2. Furthermore, at least 1,214 Palestinians have been killed in Gaza since the October ceasefire agreement took effect2.

In addition to his statements on Gaza, US President Donald Trump addressed wider regional security. Trump stated that the United States would be hitting Iran very hard in the coming weeks1.

Migrant Deaths in Ceuta Trigger European Political Debate

Spanish authorities reported that at least 57 people died while attempting to reach the Spanish enclave of Ceuta from Morocco1. Spanish Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez strongly criticized the arrival of tens of thousands of migrants into Ceuta, describing the entry as a violation of Spain's territorial integrity2.

Following the influx, Spain's interior ministry stated that over 48,300 migrants had voluntarily returned to Morocco by 6pm on Friday2. The crisis comes alongside legal rulings regarding how border officials handle incoming migrants. Spain's Supreme Court recently ruled that migrants intercepted at sea while trying to enter Ceuta or Melilla cannot be summarily returned without processing2.

The border situation in North Africa also drew political comments from abroad. US President Donald Trump told Fox News that the border crossing in Ceuta demonstrates what will happen in the US in three years if Democrats win3.

Football Organizations Oppose FIFA Plan to Sell Tournament Stakes

A controversial proposal by FIFA to sell private stakes in its business has triggered widespread backlash across global football governing bodies. FIFA proposed creating a commercial subsidiary named FIFA Forward Enterprise, in which private investors could buy minority stakes1.

In response to the plan, European football governing body UEFA voted to boycott World Cups if FIFA proceeds with selling stakes to private investors1. Meanwhile, South American football governing body CONMEBOL requested further information from FIFA regarding its plan to sell private stakes in its tournament business2.

The initiative has also caused internal fallout within FIFA itself. Carlos Cordeiro, a senior advisor to FIFA President Gianni Infantino, resigned in protest against the private investment plan3. Additionally, FIFA Chief Operating Officer Kevin Lamour stated that FIFA staff were deceived by Gianni Infantino regarding the World Cup sell-off proposal4.

Bodies Recovered After Fatal Avalanche on Broad Peak

Rescuers in Pakistan have recovered bodies after a devastating avalanche struck a 10-person climbing expedition on Broad Peak1. The climbing team was led by Nirmal Purja, who set a world record in 2019 by climbing all 14 of the world's 8,000-metre-plus peaks in just over six months2.

According to GPS tracking data, the avalanche occurred at an elevation of 6,579 metres on the west ridge of Broad Peak2. Following the slide, recovery teams retrieved the remains of 39-year-old American climber Mallory Geis, who was from San Antonio, Texas1.

Search teams also identified the bodies of two other victims of the avalanche1. They were identified as Omani climber Nadhira Ahmed Abdullah Al Harthy and Nepali climber Pur Bahadur Gurung1.

PM Modi Offers Forgiveness to Student Protesters Over Exam Leaks

Indian Prime Minister Narendra Modi addressed recent student demonstrations across the country by releasing a video message stating he forgives young protesters who hurled abuses at him and his late mother1. The Prime Minister's public comments come after widespread student unrest related to national medical entrance examinations.

The youth-led protests were organized by the Cockroach Janta Party (CJP)2. These CJP demonstrations focused on paper leaks involving the NEET medical exam2. The public anger and widespread rallies over the NEET paper leaks led directly to the resignation of Union Education Minister Dharmendra Pradhan2.

During one CJP demonstration at Jantar Mantar, protesters allegedly directed abusive language at the Prime Minister2. In response, authorities initially registered a Zero FIR in Noida over the abusive remarks made against PM Modi during the Jantar Mantar protest2. That case was later transferred to the Delhi Police on July 312.

Despite PM Modi's video message offering forgiveness, political opponents sharply criticized his statement. Congress leader Pawan Khera questioned the message, stating that the country asked Modi to apologize, but Modi instead forgave the country3.

Former AAP Councillor Tahir Hussain Sentenced to Life Imprisonment

A Delhi court sentenced former AAP councillor Tahir Hussain and four others to life imprisonment for the 2020 murder of Intelligence Bureau staffer Ankit Sharma1.

During the trial, prosecutors had formally requested the death penalty1. However, Additional Sessions Judge Parveen Kumar Singh rejected the prosecution's plea for execution, ruling that the state failed to prove specific individual acts during the murder of Sharma1.

Tahir Hussain later joined AIMIM in December 2024 and finished third in the 2025 Delhi Assembly election in the Mustafabad constituency2. Following his sentencing, Tahir Hussain stated to reporters that he would get justice from the High Court2.

IMD Predicts Below-Normal Monsoon Rainfall Due to El Nino

The India Meteorological Department forecast below-normal average rainfall in India for the August-September period due to El Niño risks1. IMD chief Mrutyunjay Mohapatra stated that 24 days in July were under the influence of low pressure systems, compared to the normal average of 13.5 days1.

The climate impacts have drawn warning statements from global bodies. UN Secretary General António Guterres stated that a strengthening El Niño is adding fuel to a planet already on fire and pushing climate conditions into uncharted territory2.

In response to local water conditions, the Bhakra Beas Management Board increased total water discharge from Pandoh Dam into the Beas River to nearly 36,000 cusecs3.
