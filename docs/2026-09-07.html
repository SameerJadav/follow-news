US Envoys Kushner and Witkoff Visit Kyiv for Peace Talks

US envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner visited Kyiv for peace talks1. This was their first visit to Kyiv since Russia launched its full-scale invasion of Ukraine1. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky greeted Witkoff and Kushner at Saint Sophia Cathedral in Kyiv1. On Friday, Russian drones hit the headquarters of Ukraine's security services near that cathedral1. Russian strikes also bombed the two main airports in Kyiv on Friday2. Those strikes forced Kushner and Witkoff to travel to the capital city by train2. They travelled by train because of the risk of Russian drone strikes1.

Russia and Ukraine agreed to a short-term pause in air strikes during the visit1. Both countries pledged not to attack each other's capitals over the weekend2. They made this pledge to ensure safe passage for the US envoys2. Before arriving in Ukraine, Kushner and Witkoff spent time in Moscow3. They held three hours of talks with Russian President Vladimir Putin on Saturday3. Putin told the envoys that Russia was making tangible advances on the battlefield1.

After the talks in Moscow, the envoys went to Kyiv1. Witkoff said that peace talks in Ukraine have been very meaningful4. UK National Security Adviser Jonathan Powell joined the talks in Kyiv1. Other European national security advisers also participated in the discussions1. The talks have focused on a 20-point peace plan2. Washington and Kyiv put forward this peace plan in December2.

Zelensky said that Ukraine is counting on a winter package from the US and Europe2. He said this package needs to include air defence2. He said it must also include energy support2. He added that the package should include American LNG2.

While diplomats met, attacks continued in other areas2. Ukrainian authorities reported on Sunday that at least eight people were killed across the country in the past day2. They reported that 69 people were injured during the same period2. Ukraine also reported that its forces struck the Ryazan oil refinery overnight3. That refinery is about 480 kilometres inside Russia3. The strike caused a fire at the refinery3.

Ukrainian officials also made claims about the war in August1. They claimed that Russia suffered 42,000 dead or wounded casualties during August1. They also claimed that Ukraine gained more territory than it lost that month1.

US Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said the agency issued a full ground stop in Miami after the crash3. All runways and taxiways at the airport were closed by 3 pm2. The Miami-Dade Sheriff's Office closed NW 67th Avenue between NW 25th Street and NW 36th Street2. Miami Sheriff Rosie Cordero-Stutz said there was no apparent threat to public safety1.

Far-Right AfD Wins Record Share in Saxony-Anhalt Election

The far-right Alternative for Germany party won a record vote share in the Saxony-Anhalt state election1. Incomplete results show the party, known as AfD, set to win 44% of the vote1. Voter turnout in the state was estimated at 78%1. Saxony-Anhalt has a population of 2.1 million people1. Domestic intelligence classifies the state branch of AfD as right-wing extremist1. AfD state leader Ulrich Siegmund called the result sensational1.

The conservative CDU finished second with just over 17% of the vote1. CDU national general secretary Franziska Hoppermann called the result very difficult for her party2. She rejected any cooperation with the AfD2. Exit polls showed Die Linke won 9.3%3. The Social Democrats won 8.4%, and the Greens won 8.7%3. The BSW party reached the 5% threshold needed to enter parliament3.

Modern Germany has never had a far-right party lead a state government since World War II2. Siegmund promised to launch a remigration and deportation offensive if AfD forms a government3. He also promised to ban rainbow flags in schools3. In addition, he promised to stop new windfarm construction3.

Anak Krakatau Eruption Disrupts Flights Across Indonesia

Volcanic ash from Mount Anak Krakatau forced officials to suspend over 300 flights at Jakarta's main airport on Sunday1. That airport was Soekarno-Hatta International Airport1. Two new eruptions occurred as the volcano spewed ash across the region2. Indonesian authorities suspended operations at eight airports3. The closures left about 170,000 passengers stranded3. The Indonesian transport ministry reported that 1,558 flights were delayed3.

Volcanic ash reached up to 20,000 feet on the Java side3. The ash reached 50,000 feet on the Sumatra side3. Booming sounds from the eruption were heard up to 435 miles or 700 kilometres away4. Indonesia's disaster agency BNPB announced plans for cloud-seeding to wash away the ash4. Port authorities banned ships in the Sunda Strait from going within two miles of the volcano4.

Indonesian authorities confirmed that no tsunami warning is active for this eruption3. A previous eruption and collapse of Anak Krakatau in 2018 triggered a tsunami that killed more than 400 people1.

Building Collapse Kills At Least Five in Delhi's Satya Niketan

A five-storey building housing college students collapsed in South West Delhi on September 6, 20261. The building was located in the Satya Niketan area1. Satya Niketan is a dense hub for paying guest accommodation near Delhi University's South Campus1. Eyewitnesses told local media that up to 50 people were inside the building when it collapsed at around 13:30 local time on Sunday2. At least five people were killed in the disaster1. Reports differed on the number of survivors rescued from the site1. The Hindu reported that 11 people were pulled out and taken to the hospital1. Al Jazeera reported that nine people were rescued3.

Residents said the collapsed building was at least 50 years old1. They said the structure comprised a basement plus five upper floors1. The Municipal Corporation of Delhi said the building violated building norms1. Municipal rules permitted only a ground floor plus a first floor1. Repair work was underway in the basement when the collapse happened1. A local shop owner said the leaseholder was conducting excavation work in the basement to expand the hostel2.

Police launched legal action against multiple individuals associated with the property1. Delhi Police said they are registering a first information report against Anand Bansal1. Bansal operates the Hostel Daze paying guest accommodation1. Deputy Commissioner of Police Amit Goel said an FIR was registered against building owner Hariram and others at the South Campus police station4.

Authorities also took action regarding neighboring properties and ordered an official inquiry1. The Municipal Corporation of Delhi evacuated tenants from an adjoining girls' paying guest building and sealed that property1. Delhi Chief Minister Rekha Gupta ordered a magisterial probe into the collapse1.

The collapse sparked student demonstrations in the city4. Around 150 to 200 members of the ABVP student organization protested at Delhi's Civic Centre4. They demonstrated against the Municipal Corporation of Delhi4. A security guard was injured when protesters tried to force their way into the main Civic Centre building4. Delhi Police detained around 15 students after the protest4. Police initiated legal proceedings against them under the Delhi Police Act4.

Building safety remains a persistent issue in the Indian capital2. Fire department figures show that more than 1,000 building collapses occurred in Delhi over the past three years2. In April, 11 people were killed when a residential building collapsed in north-eastern Delhi2.

ISRO and IN-SPACe Reject Reports of Space Agency Privatisation

The Indian Space Research Organisation rejected reports that it will be privatised or its role reduced1. The agency, known as ISRO, called those reports baseless and incorrect1. IN-SPACe chairman Pawan Goenka said ISRO will remain the bedrock of the space sector and lead next-generation programmes1. ISRO said strategic and nationally critical capabilities will remain under government ownership2.

Nine ISRO employee associations sent a joint letter on September 4 to ISRO Chairperson V. Narayanan3. They asked for official clarification on plans to transfer manufacturing to private companies3. The letter was signed by groups including the ISRO Staff Association, SHAR Employees Association, and ISAC Employees Association2. The associations cited remarks by Goenka that ISRO would eventually not manufacture launch vehicles3. They wrote that reducing ISRO to a residual research unit cannot be accepted without debate4. Department of Space employees are organized as service associations rather than trade unions3.

India aims to grow its space economy from about $8.4 billion to $44 billion by 20333. The country currently has more than 450 space startups1. India targets 50 space launches a year by 20301. Space goals include establishing the Bharatiya Antariksh Station by 2035 and sending a crewed mission to the Moon by 20404. On September 4, ISRO launched its EOS-05 Earth imaging satellite aboard the GSLV-F17 rocket3. Private company Skyroot launched its Vikram-1 rocket on July 181.
