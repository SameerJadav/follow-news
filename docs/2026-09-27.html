A powerful nor'easter storm battered the US Northeast, causing widespread blackouts and flooding

A powerful nor'easter storm brought flooding and power outages across the northeastern United States on Saturday1. Travel suffered immediate disruption as airlines cancelled more than 400 flights by Saturday afternoon1. Electricity grids also failed across several states1. Power outages affected over 88,000 homes and businesses as of Saturday afternoon1. Poweroutage.com reported that 101,674 homes and businesses were without power as of 1:15 p.m. Saturday2. This outage covered areas from Massachusetts to Maryland2. In New York City, estimated rainfall forecasts doubled to six inches on Saturday2. The heavy rain caused power outages for about 22,000 customers in the city2. The governors of New York and New Jersey declared states of emergency in counties expected to suffer the worst impact, including New York City1. New York Governor Kathy Hochul issued a strong warning to residents1. Hochul said, "Anyone who goes in or near the water this weekend is literally gambling with their life"1. Officials in New York also warned of wind gusts reaching up to 55 mph, or 86 km/h, throughout the weekend3. Coastal regions prepared for high waters and rising seas2. Senior forecaster Bob Oravec of the US Weather Prediction Center said storm surges were expected to raise ocean levels by three feet along the coasts of New Jersey and New York2. In Sea Bright, Mayor Brian Kelly said he expected street flooding to reach up to 6ft4. Kelly compared the threat to an "Irene-scale flooding event"4. In Manasquan, New Jersey, officials issued a voluntary evacuation order for residents living in low-lying areas5. Private property also suffered damage from the strong winds4. Kenny Formica, a marina owner in Surf City, New Jersey, reported that a customer's 25ft boat worth $150,000 blew off its lift during the storm4. Transit networks and major events faced widespread cancellations1. The Long Island Rail Road suspended service on its Long Beach Branch due to flooding on Saturday2. Musician Ed Sheeran cancelled scheduled concerts in Massachusetts on Friday and Saturday because of the weather1. A professional football match between Red Bull New York and St Louis City SC was also cancelled due to the storm3. In sports, the Boston Red Sox rescheduled the last game of their series against the Chicago Cubs3. The game was moved to Sunday afternoon in Florida3.

US President Donald Trump rejected an Iranian proposal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz to shipping

US President Donald Trump rejected an Iranian proposal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz to commercial shipping within seven days1. The strategic waterway has been effectively closed to shipping since war broke out between the US, Israel, and Iran in late February1. Strategic stretches of Yemen's Red Sea coast, including the port of Mokha, were seized by Yemen's Houthis1. Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi had proposed a seven-day plan3. The plan required the US to lift its naval blockade, waive oil sanctions, and agree to a ceasefire covering Lebanon in exchange for reopening the strait3. Araghchi said that Tehran would wait for the definitive views of mediators regarding Trump's comments before making a decision1. Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian told CBS that Iran would permit UN nuclear inspectors to enter the country if the US accepts its proposal4. Pezeshkian also told Fox News that Iran signed an agreement based on a June memorandum of understanding and remains willing to move forward3. Under that June agreement, Iran promised not to seek a nuclear weapon and to reopen the strait in return for US blockade relief and eased sanctions4. But Trump posted an image on Truth Social showing the Strait of Hormuz labelled as "Trump Strait"3. The Wall Street Journal reported that Trump told aides he expects to resume a bombing campaign against Iran after US midterm elections in November5. Saudi Arabia expressed concern that Iran's deal was a bilateral agreement between Iran and Oman rather than involving Gulf states as joint signatories5.

US and China agreed to selective tariff relief and an AI communication channel after Washington summit

Chinese President Xi Jinping concluded a three-day summit visit to Washington with US President Donald Trump on Friday1. Following the meeting, the United States and China agreed to reduce tariffs on $30 billion of non-sensitive goods in each direction2. The tariff reductions cover US exports such as agricultural goods, wood, and cosmetics, as well as US imports including small appliances, toys, and decorations2. Both countries also agreed to extend their trade truce by two months beyond its scheduled November 10 expiration date1. On technology, the White House said on Friday that both nations agreed to establish a bilateral communication channel for artificial intelligence incidents3. Trump announced that his administration would rename artificial intelligence as "super intelligence" or SI in official communications3. China's Foreign Ministry said that Beijing respects Washington's position and phrasing switch3. But Trump told reporters at the White House that the US should not integrate AI initiatives with China because the US leads China by a lot2. Trump said upon departing that the US is not going to put brakes on AI development3. During broader diplomatic exchanges, Chinese Vice President Han Zheng indirectly asked the US at the UN General Assembly to respect the sovereignty of Iran and Cuba4. Trump had previously warned Xi against supplying shoulder-mounted air-defence missile launchers to Iran and helping Iran evade oil sanctions4.

A Pakistani Taliban suicide attack killed at least 11 people near a police checkpost in Dera Ismail Khan

A suicide bomber rammed an explosives-laden tractor-trolley into the Aman Mela police checkpost in Pakistan's Dera Ismail Khan district1. At least 11 to 12 people were killed in the suicide attack1. Two customs and excise officials were among those killed in the bombing1. Around 30 people, mostly security personnel and passersby, were injured in the blast2. The Tehreek-e-Taliban Pakistan claimed responsibility for the suicide attack in a statement2. The militant group had ended its ceasefire with the Pakistani government in November 20221. Pakistan has repeatedly accused Kabul of allowing Tehreek-e-Taliban Pakistan members to operate from Afghan soil, an accusation Afghan authorities deny2. The bombing occurred two days after border clashes between Pakistan and Afghanistan killed at least 13 Afghan Taliban fighters1. At the same time, international powers are participating in peace efforts. China, Turkey, Qatar, and Saudi Arabia are participating in mediation efforts to secure peace between Pakistan and Afghanistan1.

Pope Leo XIV celebrated a massive open-air Mass in central Paris during a historic papal visit

Pope Leo XIV celebrated an outdoor Mass in Paris during the first papal visit to France in two decades1. The four-day trip marks the first official papal visit to the country in nearly two decades2. The Pope held Mass along the Avenue des Champs-Elysees with an estimated 800,000 people attending1. The Vatican estimated that 700,000 people turned out for the outdoor Mass at Place de la Concorde in Paris3. About 14,500 police officers, gendarmes, military personnel, and firefighters were deployed to secure the event3. Attendees included Brigitte Macron, Prime Minister Sebastien Lecornu, far-right leader Marine Le Pen, and LVMH chairman Bernard Arnault1. French President Emmanuel Macron did not attend, represented instead by his wife Brigitte2. Born Robert Prevost in Chicago in 1955, Pope Leo XIV is the first US-born pontiff4. During his visit, he visited the Maison Bakhita center to meet migrants and volunteers supporting refugees4. He also cautioned against "losing our humanity amid a paradise of machines" regarding artificial intelligence4. The visit comes after a 2021 inquiry found clergy or lay members abused about 330,000 minors over seven decades2. An INSEE report showed only 25% of French 18-to-49-year-olds identify as Catholic, down from 43% a decade earlier5.

Election Commission of India announced revision rule changes amid political protests and misconduct claims

The Election Commission of India faced growing political backlash over its Special Intensive Revision of electoral rolls1. Political opposition mounted as Maharashtra Navnirman Sena chief Raj Thackeray announced a joint public march with Shiv Sena (UBT) chief Uddhav Thackeray1. The protest is planned in Mumbai on October 4, 2026, against the Special Intensive Revision process1. Shiv Sena (UBT) leader Aaditya Thackeray also backed calls for protests in Mumbai against Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar beginning October 22. Raj Thackeray called the Election Commission of India a wing of the BJP1. He accused Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar of committing fraud under the SIR process1. Serious allegations against Kumar also emerged from a former official3. Former Kerala Chief Secretary Jiji Thomson alleged that Kumar asked him on a flight in 2016 whether he was interested in contesting the 2019 Lok Sabha election as a BJP candidate3. Following this allegation, Cockroach Janta Party leader Abhijeet Dipke called for Kumar's resignation2. The Cockroach Janta Party launched its 'Election Commission Thik Karo' campaign2. The party cited the exclusion of more than 13 crore names from draft electoral rolls across 30 states and Union Territories during the Special Intensive Revision2. Kumar became the 26th Chief Election Commissioner of India in February 20253. Internal tensions within the commission were also reported3. The Indian Express reported that Election Commissioners Sukhbir Singh Sandhu and Vivek Joshi raised objections on record at least 14 times over 10 months3. Their objections concerned decisions made regarding the Special Intensive Revision without their knowledge3. But the Election Commission stated on Saturday that decisions concerning the revision had received unanimous approval from Kumar, Sandhu, and Joshi3. In response to concerns, the Election Commission announced operational changes4. The commission announced measures including doorstep collection of documents from voters who have been issued notices during the revision4. The Election Commission also announced that a committee including an independent expert from an IIT or IIIT will review ECINET4. Meanwhile, government allies defended the election body5. Andhra Pradesh Chief Minister N Chandrababu Naidu said that the Election Commission's clarification reaffirmed the integrity and credibility of India's constitutional institutions5. LJP (Ram Vilas) chief Chirag Paswan questioned why opposition parties remain silent about electoral processes in states where they have formed governments5.
