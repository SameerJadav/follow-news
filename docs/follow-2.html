PM Modi creates exam reform task force after minister resigns over leaks

Closed Mon 27 Jul · 0 updates

The full picture

India has faced a long history of examination paper leaks and irregularities, with over 90 reported incidents at both national and state levels in the past two decades. At least 15 of these involved examinations conducted by central government agencies. The National Testing Agency, or NTA, an autonomous body under the Ministry of Education, has also been criticized for issues such as paper leaks, unfair grace marks, and technical problems during exams.1

The immediate cause for the current crisis began on May 3, 2026, when more than 2.2 million students took the National Eligibility cum Entrance Test, known as NEET-UG. This exam is for admission to undergraduate medical and dental courses across India.7 Nine days later, on May 12, 2026, the government cancelled the examination. Investigations showed that a "guess paper" with questions similar to the actual exam had been circulated before the test. This led to arrests, including some people connected to the NTA.7

The cancellation of the NEET-UG 2026 exam sparked widespread protests across the country. Students and parents took to the streets, demanding accountability and reforms. A new youth movement, the "Cockroach Janta Party," or CJP, began physical demonstrations in New Delhi on June 6, 2026. The CJP demanded the resignation of the Union Education Minister, a judicial inquiry into the leaks, financial compensation for affected families, and the release of arrested protesters. These protests grew into a nationwide movement.7

In response to the growing public anger, the National Testing Agency took action. On July 24, 2026, the NTA terminated 47 of its officials. This was described as a major step to restructure the agency. The NTA also announced plans to change the NEET exam format from pen-and-paper to computer-based starting in 2027.11

The sustained pressure from the protests led to a significant political development. On July 25, 2026, Union Education Minister Dharmendra Pradhan resigned from his post. Pradhan stated his resignation was to prevent "anti-national forces" from exploiting the situation and to maintain national unity. His resignation marked the first time a mass public uprising had forced a minister in Prime Minister Narendra Modi's government to step down.7

As of July 26, 2026, Prime Minister Narendra Modi announced the creation of a high-powered task force. This task force will recommend reforms to make India's examination system more transparent, secure, and driven by technology.15

On July 27, 2026, the details of the six-member task force were released. It is chaired by Nandan Nilekani, co-founder and chairman of Infosys, known for his work on India's Aadhaar digital identity program. Other members include S. Somanath, a space scientist and former chairman of the Indian Space Research Organisation; Tapan Deka, a security expert and former director of the Intelligence Bureau; Anita Karwal, an education expert and former Education Secretary; Amrit Lal Meena, a logistics expert; and Dr. V. Kamakoti, an artificial intelligence and computing expert and director of IIT Chennai. The task force is mandated to suggest structural and technology-led reforms, review the NTA's operations, and propose measures to prevent future leaks and malpractices. The government also plans to introduce new legislation in Parliament to strengthen legal protections against exam-related offenses, including stricter penalties.15
