Ukraine targets Moscow with massive drone strike as Russian election concludes

Ukraine launched a massive drone attack against targets in Russia as voting ended in Russia's parliamentary election1. The vote was the first parliamentary election in Russia since its full-scale invasion of Ukraine began in 20221.

The political party United Russia received nearly 58 percent of the party-list vote1. That total came with about 58 percent of precincts counted across the country1. Official election figures showed that total voter turnout stood at 56.66 percent when the polls closed1.

Government authorities in Kyiv denounced voting in Russian-occupied areas of Ukraine1. Kyiv called the voting illegal1. Ukrainian officials urged foreign governments not to recognize the election results1. Russian election chief Ella Pamfilova said cyberattacks targeted Russia's electronic voting system during the election process2.

Russia's Defence Ministry reported that its forces shot down 1,110 Ukrainian drones across Russia, Crimea, and the Black Sea2. Moscow Mayor Sergey Sobyanin gave a different figure for intercepted weapons3. Sobyanin said more than 1,600 drones were intercepted and shot down in total3. He said 450 of those drones were aimed at Moscow3.

A drone strike caused damage to a facility at the Moscow oil refinery3. In the town of Sofyino, a drone struck a three-storey residential building4. A 44-year-old woman was killed in Sofyino during that strike4. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said Ukraine used domestically produced Flamingo cruise missiles to strike targets in Moscow3.

Attacks also took place inside Ukraine. Ukrainian officials said nine people were killed in Russian attacks in the Kyiv and Sumy regions on Saturday4. Three children were among the dead in those attacks4.

German Chancellor Merz cancels UN trip following state election defeats

German Chancellor Friedrich Merz called his party's state election performance in Mecklenburg-Vorpommern a disaster1. Merz said he would remain in office despite the result1.

In Mecklenburg-Vorpommern, public broadcasters projected the far-right AfD party to win 38% of the vote1. The SPD followed in second place on 35%1. Merz's party, the CDU, secured 4.9 percent of the vote in Mecklenburg-Western Pomerania2. That result marked the party's worst state election performance in that region since 19492.

In Berlin's state election, political party Die Linke led projections with nearly 26% of the vote1. The CDU placed second in Berlin on 19%1. After the election losses, Merz cleared his calendar3. Merz skipped a planned trip to New York for the UN General Assembly3.

Personal approval ratings for Merz hover just above 10 percent3. Across Germany, the far-right AfD leads national opinion polls with about 30 percent support4.

US and China discuss AI safety mechanisms before White House summit

US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent and Chinese Vice Premier He Lifeng led talks in New York1. The meeting took place at JPMorgan Chase's headquarters and lasted around eight hours1.

The US proposed creating a US-China AI dialogue1. This proposal includes a notification mechanism for high-level national security incidents resulting from AI1. US Trade Representative Jamieson Greer said export controls on advanced AI chips and semiconductor manufacturing equipment were not part of the mechanism2.

The talks took place before a scheduled summit meeting between US President Donald Trump and Chinese President Xi Jinping1. The leaders are scheduled to hold a summit meeting at the White House on Thursday1. A trade truce agreed by both nations in Busan, South Korea, in October 2025 is scheduled to expire on November 102.

In related developments, Anthropic chief executive Dario Amodei called for a worldwide pacing of frontier AI development requiring cooperation with China3. China released the third iteration of its AI safety governance framework3. The framework addresses risks from AI agents, cybersecurity threats from frontier models, and recursive self-improvement3.

Delhi voter roll revision leads to widespread notices and political criticism

The Election Commission of India launched a voter roll review in Delhi on May 141. The Special Intensive Revision exercise has led to millions of notices sent to voters2. Delhi is among 19 states and Union Territories included in the third phase of the process3.

Officials issued notices to 34.5% of men on Delhi's draft electoral roll2. Notices went to 33.4% of women and 50% of transgender individuals on the draft roll2. In total, 59,791 notices had no reason listed against them2.

Not being mapped to the previous review accounted for 42% of the notices issued2. Name mismatches with the previous electoral roll accounted for 41% of notices2. Parent name mismatches comprised 24% of notices, and self-name mismatches comprised 17%2. Discrepancies in age differences accounted for 16% of notices2.

Prominent political and official figures received notices under the process4. Served individuals included LK Advani, Jagdeep Dhankhar, S Jaishankar, Sukhbir Singh Sandhu, Manish Sisodia, and Delhi Chief Minister Rekha Gupta4. Delhi Chief Minister Rekha Gupta received a notice because the age difference between her and her parents appeared under 15 years3. Former Supreme Court judge Justice Madan B Lokur received a notice because the recorded age gap between him and his grandfather was 40 years or less3.

Shyam Mukhija, aged 125 from Old Delhi's Sadar Bazar assembly constituency, was the oldest person served a notice due to a self-name mismatch2. Deceased former official Bibek Debroy also received a notice because his name appeared on the flagged draft rolls4. Debroy was the former chairman of the Economic Advisory Council to the Prime Minister4.

More than 80.62 lakh voter names were deleted from draft rolls after being marked as shifted or absent5. Only 6.66 lakh Form-6 applications were submitted for inclusion5. Authorities received 84,263 Form-6 applications between August 31 and September 19, after receiving 2,12,400 applications up to August 301. In Bihar, a similar review saw the total electorate decrease from 7.89 crore to about 7.42 crore5. That decrease occurred even though 21.53 lakh electors were added through Form-6 applications5.

Lawyer Kapil Sibal described the exercise as a special intensive deletion aimed at removing valid voters4. The Delhi Chief Electoral Officer's office said disposal of notices will be completed by October 29, 20264. Authorities will publish the final electoral roll on November 4, 20264.

Crime Branch takes over probe into IIT Bombay student death after protests

Mumbai Police transferred an investigation into the death of an IIT Bombay student to the Crime Branch1. An Assistant Commissioner of Police will lead the investigation1.

Sahil Wakode, a 20-year-old second-year student of energy science and engineering, died by suicide in his hostel room1. His death was the third suicide on the campus this year2. An invigilating professor reported Sahil after finding him with a phone during an examination1. IIT Bombay said Sahil was caught using a mobile phone to upload an exam paper onto the ChatGPT chatbot3.

Sahil's father filed an FIR against Professor Suryanarayana Doolla, alleging three months of harassment1. Police charged Doolla and unidentified persons with abetment to suicide and violations of the SC/ST Act3. Sahil's family refused to claim his body from Rajawadi Hospital for seven hours until police assured them the case went to the Crime Branch3.

The Faculty Forum of IIT Bombay defended Doolla, saying he executed approved exam procedures1. IIT Bombay Director Shireesh Kedare denied caste discrimination, saying student caste information remains confidential1. Kedare agreed to 18 demands from protesting students, including replacing ceiling fans with wall-mounted fans in hostels2. Student demands included Doolla's suspension and an independent probe committee2.

IIT Kharagpur Director sent a message reminding students that admission serves as evidence of sustained effort4.
