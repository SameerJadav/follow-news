Tensions Rise in Middle East as US and Iran Trade Threats and Strait of Hormuz Shipping Drops

Tensions between the United States and Iran escalated sharply after military moves, harsh statements, and a reduction in maritime traffic in the Middle East1. US President Donald Trump stated on August 18, 2026, that the United States has no talks planned with Iran1. On social media, President Trump posted a map depicting the Strait of Hormuz as US territory1. In response, Iran's security chief Mohsen Rezaei mocked President Trump's claims of controlling the critical waterway on August 18, 20263.

Naval security in the area remains highly unstable1. Crossings through the Strait of Hormuz fell 19.5% to 95 transits last week, according to data from shipping platform Kpler1. An unidentified projectile struck and damaged the engine room of a vessel near Oman in the Strait of Hormuz, causing a casualty among the crew1. Iranian negotiator Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf stated that the Strait of Hormuz will remain shut until the United States lifts its port blockade and oil sanctions, releases frozen assets, and ends all military operations2.

Military actions and threats have expanded across the region4. The United Arab Emirates Defence Ministry reported that its air defence systems detected two Iranian ballistic missiles, both of which fell harmlessly into the sea2. Meanwhile, Major General Amir Hatami, who commands Iran's Artesh army, announced a $30,000 reward for the killing or capture of any US soldier4. Unrelated military activity was also reported in Syria, where eight air strikes hit the Abu al-Duhur military base in the eastern countryside of Idlib, causing physical damage but no reported casualties2.

Inside Iran, political tensions and domestic crackdowns continue alongside the international standoff4. Mahmood Amiry-Moghaddam, the founder of Iran Human Rights, reported that Iran has executed 28 political prisoners so far in 20264.

US Imposes Sanctions on International Criminal Court Officials

US Secretary of State Marco Rubio announced new sanctions targeting International Criminal Court President Tomoko Akane and Senior Trial Lawyer Abdoulaye Seye1. The sanctions freeze their assets in the United States and restrict US entities from engaging in financial transactions with them starting September 172. To facilitate a transition, the US Treasury Department issued a general licence permitting the wind-down of transactions involving Akane through September 173.

The court previously drew US anger when it issued arrest warrants in 2024 for Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu and former Israeli Defence Minister Yoav Gallant2.

The measures face legal challenges inside the United States1. Four US human rights groups filed a lawsuit against the Trump administration in a New York court, alleging that its campaign to dismantle the ICC violates the US Constitution1. That lawsuit follows an earlier case filed in June in a New York federal court by three ICC judges—Kimberly Prost, Solomy Balungi Bossa, and Reine Alapini-Gansou—who sued the US administration over its actions1.

South Korea Reassesses Alliance After Trump Orders Cut in Military Drills

US President Donald Trump ordered a substantial reduction in joint military exercises with South Korea just before the start of the scheduled Ulchi Freedom Shield exercises1. The joint drills were originally scheduled to involve approximately 18,000 South Korean troops alongside US forces2. The United States currently maintains about 28,500 troops stationed in South Korea1.

In response to the sudden policy change, South Korean President Lee Jae Myung stated on August 18, 2026, that a firm military alliance with the United States continues to underpin South Korea's national security1.

However, President Lee also renewed calls for South Korea to gain greater military independence2. He urged accelerated efforts to transfer wartime operational control of South Korean forces from the United States back to South Korea2. In addition, President Lee called for faster progress on South Korea's nuclear-powered submarine program2.

Supreme Court and Education Ministry Intervene Following Mass Student Protests

The Supreme Court of India and the Union Education Ministry have stepped in following mass student protests over competitive examinations1.

Delhi Police submitted a 42-page affidavit to the Supreme Court late on August 17, 2026, detailing a major demonstration on July 201. Police reported that over 30,000 protesters were met by 5,000 uniformed officers on that day1. Conflicting reports emerged regarding injuries4. Union Parliamentary Affairs Minister Kiren Rijiju claimed that no protester was seriously injured or suffered broken bones4. However, a Right to Information response revealed that 10 people were wounded by pellets during the protest march4.

Politicians are now demanding relief for students3. Cockroach Janta Party spokesperson Saurav Das issued a two-day ultimatum to the Union government to honor commitments to withdraw police cases (FIRs) against protesters3. The Supreme Court requested Solicitor General Tushar Mehta to provide details of FIRs against student protesters so the court can consider quashing them under Article 1423.

Meanwhile, ministry leadership has changed after Dharmendra Pradhan resigned on July 25, 2026, and Pralhad Joshi took over5. Joshi directed a four-tier question paper checking system and new security infrastructure at the National Testing Agency, which also removed 600 experts in an team overhaul2.

Audit Shows Most of PM CARES Fund Corpus Held in Fixed Bank Deposits

Newly published financial accounts show that the vast majority of the PM CARES Fund is held in bank fixed deposits while spending remains extremely low1.

Audited financial statements for FY 2023-24 and FY 2024-25 were signed by auditors on August 7, 2026, and officially published on August 173. By the end of FY 2024-25, the closing balance of the fund reached ₹8,452.06 crore1. Audited accounts reveal that ₹7,846 crore of that total corpus was held in fixed deposits with scheduled banks2.

Donations to the fund dropped by 30% compared to the previous year, with PM CARES receiving ₹480 crore in FY 2024-251. The fund also received ₹324.66 crore in refunds from implementing agencies during the same financial year2. Despite the massive balance, PM CARES spent just ₹87.85 lakh in FY 2024-25, which amounted to 0.01% of its available closing balance1.
