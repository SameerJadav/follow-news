US and Saudi Arabia launch joint airstrikes on Iran-backed militia bases in Iraq

United States and Saudi Arabian military forces have carried out coordinated airstrikes targeting bases of the Popular Mobilisation Forces in Iraq1. The military action follows allegations that the Iran-backed group had previously launched 30 drone strikes against Saudi Arabia2. According to reports, the joint US-Saudi air raids killed at least 20 fighters associated with Iraq's Popular Mobilisation Forces3. The strikes mark a significant escalation in regional conflict across the Middle East.

Hostilities have escalated across several borders in recent days. Satellite imagery from Monday showed that the major Abqaiq oil facility in Saudi Arabia was hit by drones launched by Iranian proxy militias operating from Iraq2. Earlier, on 13 July, Saudi Arabia bombed Sanaa airport outside the capital of Yemen to prevent an Iranian aircraft from landing2. That airport bombing triggered retaliatory strikes by Houthi forces against Saudi airports in Abha and Jizan2. In another major incident, Iran launched missiles at US military installations in Jordan3. This was Iran's first attack in the region following a brief pause in fighting with the US3. Meanwhile, a drone strike hit a US-owned floating storage tanker on Wednesday at a natural gas loading port in Damietta, Egypt3. Commenting on the situation, US President Donald Trump stated that the United States would be "hitting [Iran] hard"3.

The air attacks triggered immediate diplomatic consequences in the region. Iraqi Prime Minister Ali al-Zaidi cancelled a scheduled meeting in Jeddah with Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman following the US-Saudi military raids3. Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman, who is the de facto ruler of Saudi Arabia, previously launched the country's Vision 2030 initiative to diversify the national economy away from its dependence on oil revenues2.

The broader regional conflict has also caused severe damage to international shipping and local economies. Egyptian President Abdel-Fattah el-Sissi stated that Egypt lost around $7 billion in 2024 because of a steep decline in shipping traffic through the Suez Canal4. Figures show that annual transit ship counts through the canal halved from over 26,000 vessels down to just over 13,0004. As a result, annual revenues from the Suez Canal dropped sharply from $10.25 billion in 2023 to around $4 billion in 20244.

Wildfires rage across southern Europe during intense summer heatwave

Firefighters across southern Europe are battling severe wildfires driven by high temperatures and strong winds1. In southwest France, a massive wildfire west of Bordeaux burned approximately 420 square kilometers of the Gironde region2. More than 200,000 residents and tourists were forced to flee the flames in the region2, though about 57,000 residents from three towns were later allowed to return to their homes2. Local authorities placed French firefighters on high alert1, and the Gironde prefecture reported that 126 firefighters have been injured since the blaze began3. The fires have also killed or stranded thousands of wild animals, prompting rescue efforts by volunteers and firefighters4.

The crisis extends across the Mediterranean region. In Spain, wildfires have burned over 170 square kilometers, which is six times more area than burned during the same period last year2. Spanish Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez announced that the government raised the fire near Madrid to "operational level 3" for the first time in Spanish history3, noting that the upcoming 12 hours would be critical for containment efforts3. Víctor Resco de Dios, a professor of forestry engineering, warned that the region has entered an era of unextinguishable fires2. In Greece, three firefighters died while fighting blazes on Crete and the southern mainland3. Across southern Europe, over 330,000 people have been evacuated from homes and hotels3.

Powerful earthquake in Japan leaves 18 dead as search operations continue

A powerful earthquake struck the island of Kyushu in southwestern Japan, leaving at least 18 people dead and triggering major emergency responses1,. The Japan Meteorological Agency recorded the Kumamoto quake at a magnitude of 7.1, while the US Geological Survey measured it at 6.81. Japanese Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi stated that emergency teams face a race against time to rescue people trapped in damaged structures1. Prime Minister Takaichi deployed over 4,600 personnel to aid search and rescue operations2.

The region experienced over 100 aftershocks following the initial quake on Tuesday2, including a 5.8 magnitude aftershock that hit the Amakusa and Ashikita areas around 10:20 pm1. Retail company president Akio Yoshida reported that three employees died and four were hospitalized when an explosion struck the Aeon Mall in Kashima an hour after the earthquake2. Defense Minister Shinjiro Koizumi announced that about 300 air conditioning units would be flown to Kumamoto to support evacuees facing severe heat2. Widespread structural damage was reported across Kumamoto prefecture, including the collapse of the worship hall at the 19th-century Yatsushiro Shrine2. Industrial operations were also affected; Toyota and Honda suspended factory operations in Kumamoto until Friday1. However, semiconductor manufacturer TSMC resumed work at its local plant on Tuesday evening after passing safety checks3. TSMC established the facility in 2021 with Sony, Denso, and Toyota, targeting over $20 billion in total investment3.

Lok Sabha passes new anti-paper leak bill following nationwide student demonstrations

The Lok Sabha has passed the Public Examinations (Prevention of Unfair Means) Amendment Bill, 2026, by voice vote amid ongoing opposition protests1. The passage of the bill comes after widespread public outrage and nationwide student demonstrations over exam paper leaks and irregularities in the NEET examination process1,. Opposition members protested in Parliament against recent police actions taken against student protesters during the nationwide demonstrations1.

Under the strict provisions of the new legislation, individuals convicted of involvement in public exam paper leaks face 5 to 10 years in prison and fines up to Rs 50 lakh1. Furthermore, individuals found guilty of participating in organized paper-leak crimes will face a minimum sentence of 7 years in prison alongside fines reaching up to Rs 10 crore1. Minister of State Jitendra Singh informed lawmakers that 52 First Information Reports (FIRs) have been registered under anti-paper leak legislation since the original framework took effect in 20241.

The legislative move follows significant political fallout and civil unrest surrounding national entrance tests. Union Education Minister Dharmendra Pradhan submitted his resignation on July 25 after intense public pressure and student marches2. Demonstrations were spearheaded by youth groups, including the Cockroach Janta Party. Founder Abhijeet Dipke publicly criticized Prime Minister Narendra Modi's social media outreach to youth, suggesting the Prime Minister should leave his post to become an online influencer2. Dipke warned that students would initiate an even larger nationwide protest if the government refused to withdraw criminal FIRs filed against student demonstrators2.

Key figures involved in the student protests have reported facing political heat and threats. Cockroach Janta Party spokesperson Ratna Singh disclosed on social media that she received abusive messages and death threats following the rallies3. Meanwhile, Abhijeet Dipke returned to his hometown of Sambhajinagar in Maharashtra on July 29 after the conclusion of a 36-day student agitation3.

Madhya Pradesh raises moong procurement cap as protesting farmers march on Bhopal

The Madhya Pradesh state government increased its summer moong procurement cap from 25% to 60% of total crop output after nearly 2,000 protesting farmers marched into Bhopal1. Farmers affiliated with the Samyukta Kisan Morcha broke through police barricades to gather at Roshanpura Square, setting up camp within one kilometer of Chief Minister Mohan Yadav's residence2. The protesters arrived in tractor-trolleys stocked with five days of food and water, pledging to stay until all demands were addressed3. In response, Chief Minister Yadav appointed a four-member ministerial committee to conduct formal negotiations2.

Farmer leaders pointed out that while the Minimum Support Price (MSP) for summer moong was set at ₹8,768 per quintal for 2026–27, excess crop was selling in open markets for only ₹4,000 to ₹5,000 per quintal4. State Agriculture Minister Aidal Singh Kansana announced the extension of the procurement deadline to August 20 and suspended the MSP e-token system for fertilizer distribution1. Union Agriculture Minister Shivraj Singh Chouhan confirmed that Madhya Pradesh had procured about 60,000 metric tonnes of moong as of July 291. The central government agreed to purchase 4.54 lakh metric tonnes at ₹8,780 per quintal1.

Supreme Court accepts CBI closure report clearing former PM Manmohan Singh

A Supreme Court bench led by Chief Justice of India Surya Kant has accepted the Central Bureau of Investigation's (CBI) closure report clearing former Prime Minister Manmohan Singh in the long-standing coal block allocation case1. The ruling brings a formal end to legal proceedings regarding allegations connected to his time overseeing coal allocations. Former Prime Minister Singh passed away in December 2024 while an appeal against a trial court summons was still pending1.

During the hearing, Senior Advocates Kapil Sibal and A.M. Singhvi requested that the Supreme Court set aside adverse remarks made by the trial court against Singh despite his death2. The Supreme Court determined that the trial court special judge lacked valid legal justification when rejecting the CBI's 2014 closure reports, which had originally cleared the former prime minister2. The coal allocation case dates back several years; in August 2014, the Supreme Court had quashed 214 coal block allocations made between 1993 and 2010 after ruling that the allocation processes were arbitrary and illegal1.

Assam flood death toll rises to 75 as state announces enhanced relief grants

The death toll from severe flooding in Assam has reached 75 following seven new reported fatalities in Sivasagar district1. Despite the rising death toll, overall flood conditions showed slight improvement, with the total number of affected residents falling to 3.32 lakh across 622 villages and 21 revenue circles1. Charaideo remains the hardest-hit district, with approximately 1.42 lakh people impacted by high water levels1. Floodwaters submerged over 45,300 hectares of cropland statewide, while the Dhansiri river continued to flow above its danger mark in Numaligarh district2.

Assam Chief Minister Himanta Biswa Sarma announced several emergency relief measures for affected populations. Families in Sivasagar, Charaideo, Jorhat, and Golaghat districts will receive an interim grant of ₹15,000 for immediate household needs1. The state government also increased financial ex-gratia compensation for families of flood victims from ₹4 lakh to ₹9 lakh, adding ₹5 lakh directly from the Chief Minister's Relief Fund2. Rules were simplified by waiving post-mortem requirements, accepting certificates from local circle officers instead2. To maintain communication, telecom authorities activated Intra Circle Roaming in Sivasagar1.
