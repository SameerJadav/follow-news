Swedish voters leave election in tight deadlock between political blocs

Swedish voters cast ballots in a closely fought election that could bring the far-right into government1.

Sweden is a Nordic nation2. The country has a population of 10.6 million people2.

Public broadcaster SVT said the two main political blocs were separated by only 20,000 votes3. This gap was recorded with 50% of the votes counted3.

Projected results from Sweden's election authority gave Magdalena Andersson's left-wing bloc a one-seat majority3. Her Social Democrat-led group led over Prime Minister Ulf Kristersson's coalition3.

At 9:45pm local time, SVT projected the opposition bloc leading by 175 seats to 174 seats4.

Christian Democrats leader Ebba Busch said the election result may remain uncertain until Wednesday4. Swedish Foreign Minister Maria Malmer Stenergard also said results might not be clear until Wednesday2. She explained that election officials are waiting for overseas votes to arrive2.

Under Prime Minister Kristersson, Sweden made several major policy changes2. Sweden abolished permanent residency for refugees2. The government also tightened welfare rules2. In addition, authorities increased expulsions2.

Kristersson promised cabinet jobs to the hard-right Sweden Democrats if he remains in office3. The Sweden Democrats were founded by Nazi sympathisers in the 1980s3.

Partial election results suggest the Sweden Democrats lost support for the first time since 20103. The party first entered parliament in 20103. Party leader Jimmie Åkesson acknowledged his party lost percentage points compared to 20224. He said the party likely lost its status as Sweden's second-largest party4. While voting in the suburb of Rinkeby, Åkesson remarked that it "doesn't look like Sweden"2.

Andersson questioned voters about the future of the nation3. She asked if Sweden should have a government entirely dominated by the Sweden Democrats3. She said such a outcome has never happened in Sweden before3. She asked if voters wanted a government led by her instead3. She said her government would steer Sweden in a new spirit of co-operation3.

Projected vote shares showed the Social Democrats leading with 27.6%4. The Moderates followed with 20.0%4. The Sweden Democrats received 17.5%4. The Left Party won 8.2%4. The Centre Party reached 7.1%4. The Christian Democrats achieved 6.3%4. The Green Party received 6.2%4. The Liberals got 5.5%4.

Liberal Party leader Simona Mohamsson called her party's election results a historic comeback4.
