Massive Drone and Missile Attacks Hit Russia and Ukraine as Regional Tensions Rise

Authorities stated that recent strikes across Russia and Ukraine killed 19 people1. Regional governor Yuri Slusar said swarms of Ukrainian drones killed five people in a residential area of Russia's southern Rostov region1. Meanwhile, a Russian drone and missile strike killed two people and wounded 14 at ArcelorMittal's steel plant in Kryvyi Rih1. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky stated that Ukrainian cities were struck by more than 1,550 attack drones, nearly 1,560 guided aerial bombs, and 62 missiles over the past week1.

Both sides reported widespread drone activity across the region. Russia's defence ministry claimed it intercepted 822 drones across the country overnight1. In another statement, the Russian defence ministry claimed it shot down 1,478 Ukrainian drones over a 24-hour period2. However, Serhii Kuzan, chair of the Ukrainian Security and Cooperation Center, said Russian authorities deliberately inflate drone interception figures to exaggerate air defence effectiveness2.

Attacks also struck commercial infrastructure near Russia's capital. Moscow Mayor Sergei Sobyanin said a Wildberries warehouse in Koledino was struck in an attack, injuring three people3. Wildberries is a major retail operator. Consultancy firm Data Insight estimated that Wildberries lost up to 480 billion rubles worth of goods and approximately one-third of its warehouse space due to strikes4. Wildberries and Ozon together handle goods and services equivalent to about 8.5 percent of Russia's gross domestic product4.

The air conflict extended beyond the immediate war zone and raised concerns of future strikes. Romania's defence ministry said a Spanish F-18 fighter jet shot down a drone that entered Romanian airspace near the borders with Ukraine and Moldova1. Meanwhile, former Ukrainian Defence Minister Mykhailo Fedorov told CBS News that Ukraine will be able to strike inside Russia using domestically-made ballistic missiles within three to six months3.

U.S. Negotiators Meet Hamas in Egypt as Arab Nations Condemn Israel

U.S. negotiator Jared Kushner met with Hamas chief Khalil al-Hayya in Egypt on Sunday to discuss progress on a U.S.-backed 15-point roadmap1. Axios reported that U.S. Board of Peace high representative Nickolay Mladenov and former British Prime Minister Tony Blair participated in the meeting with Hamas leaders in Egypt2.

However, Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu rejected the 15-point Gaza peace roadmap1. Netanyahu stated that Israeli forces will not retreat from any position in Gaza until Hamas is fully disarmed1. Israeli forces currently control approximately 60 percent of Gaza1. Foreign ministers from regional powers, including Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Jordan, Pakistan, Indonesia, Egypt, Qatar, and Turkey, issued a joint statement condemning Israel's rejection of the roadmap1. Hamas stated on July 30 that it would hand over its weapons to a new Palestinian governing body overseeing Gaza under the supervision of the Board of Peace3.

A Gaza ceasefire agreement brokered by international mediators came into effect on October 10 of last year4. More than 1,250 Palestinians in Gaza have been killed by Israeli fire since the October 2025 ceasefire took effect, according to Palestinian official sources2. More than 73,000 people in Gaza have been killed since the start of the war on October 7, 2023, according to Gaza's health ministry4.

Wildfires Burn Across Belgium, Greece, and France Forcing Mass Evacuations

A wildfire in Belgium's High Fens nature reserve expanded to consume 3,000 hectares of land by early Sunday1. The High Fens fire is the largest recorded wildfire in Belgium's modern history, burning more than twice the area destroyed during the country's previous record fire in 20112. Approximately 600 residents in the Belgian municipalities of Waimes and Bütgenbach were instructed to evacuate due to shifting winds and spreading smoke3. Officials in Monschau, Germany, reported that the Belgian wildfire advanced within approximately 3 kilometres of the German border4.

Belgian Interior Minister Bernard Quintin announced that two fire fronts advancing toward Jalhay and Monschau had been brought under control1. Sweden sent two water-bombing aircraft and Sweden and the Netherlands dispatched helicopters to help Belgian crews battle the High Fens fire1.

Wildfires also caused damage and deaths elsewhere in Europe. An elderly couple died outside their home in the Peristeria beach area on the Greek island of Salamis due to forest fires3. In France, a wildfire in the Landes region burned 1,700 hectares of forest and remained out of control despite halting its spread5. Around 100 French firefighters travelled to Spain to assist in controlling a wildfire in Aragon that destroyed roughly 16,600 hectares2.

Powerful 7.7-Magnitude Earthquake Hits Indonesia, Killing Dozens

A magnitude-7.7 earthquake struck off Indonesia's Flores island, killing at least 53 people and injuring over 130 others1. Hours after the Flores quake, a magnitude-6.9 earthquake struck northern Sumatra, with no immediate casualties reported1.

Authorities issued a tsunami warning following the Flores earthquake, which was lifted approximately three hours later after no significant sea level changes were detected2. Indonesian authorities declared a state of emergency in East Nusa Tenggara province after roughly 5,000 people were evacuated1. Indonesia's National Disaster Management Agency reported that over 900 homes and hundreds of buildings were damaged alongside 341 recorded aftershocks2.

Damage was recorded across infrastructure and public facilities. Ceilings collapsed in the operating theatres and clinics at Ruteng Regional Hospital in Manggarai, forcing medical staff to treat patients in outdoor tents2. Emergency access was also disrupted, but rescue teams cleared landslide debris to reopen access along sections of the 700-kilometre Trans-Flores Highway3.

BJP and Congress Clash Over Alleged Disrespect to National Song Vande Mataram

Union Home Minister Amit Shah accused Congress leaders of disrespecting the National Song Vande Mataram during Independence Day celebrations at the AICC headquarters1. Amit Shah alleged that Sonia Gandhi gestured to Congress president Mallikarjun Kharge to stop the singing of Vande Mataram midway1.

Following the event, political actions were initiated against Congress leaders. BJP leaders lodged formal police complaints against Sonia Gandhi in New Delhi and Karnataka over the Vande Mataram incident1. A legal complaint filed in Delhi cited video footage where Rahul Gandhi is allegedly heard saying 'We are not singing Vande Mataram'2.

In response, Congress representatives rejected the allegations and defended their leaders' actions. Congress general secretary K.C. Venugopal stated that the incident at the AICC headquarters was a communication misunderstanding and denied any intention to insult the song1. The Congress party stated that Sonia Gandhi was only asking for a chair for Mallikarjun Kharge during the event, not attempting to interrupt the song3. Congress leader Jairam Ramesh criticized Amit Shah, alleging he was using the controversy to divert attention from government failures3.

This dispute follows recent legal changes regarding national symbols. In July 2026, Parliament passed the Prevention of Insults to National Honour (Amendment) Act, making attempts to prevent or stop the singing of Vande Mataram a legal offence1.

Student Protests Over Exam Irregularities Escalate in Jharkhand

Protesting job aspirants burned effigies of Jharkhand Chief Minister Hemant Soren and Congress leader Rahul Gandhi at Jaipal Singh Munda Stadium in Ranchi1. A protester named Baha Linda shaved his head in Ranchi to symbolically perform last rites for Hemant Soren and Rahul Gandhi, calling them 'as good as dead'2.

Students organized under the JPSC-JSSC Reform Manch are demanding a Central Bureau of Investigation probe into alleged paper leaks and exam irregularities1. In response to the growing movement, Jharkhand government officials invited the student movement leaders for a fourth round of talks on August 17 at the State Guest House in Ranchi1. Student representatives agreed to attend the August 17 talks provided their legal adviser is allowed to participate3.

Chief Minister Hemant Soren addressed the issue during a public national event. In his Independence Day speech, Chief Minister Hemant Soren promised an impartial investigation into recruitment exam irregularities and introduced a time-bound examination calendar3. Testing Agency Orders UGC-NET Re-tests Following Extensive Exam Errors

The National Testing Agency announced that the UGC-NET June 2026 exam will be reconducted for English, Commerce, and Sociology due to extensive errors1. An NTA inquiry committee found factual, typographical, translation, scholar name, and punctuation errors, alongside repeated questions from previous years1.

Specific errors were raised by candidates across subjects. Sociology candidates flagged multiple spelling mistakes in scholar names, including 'Ritzer' printed as 'Putzer' and 'Parsons' printed as 'Parsow'2. English candidates alleged that 67 out of 150 questions in the June exam were identical to questions administered in 20242.

The NTA released provisional answer keys for 84 UGC-NET subjects while withholding the answer keys for English, Commerce, and Sociology2. The re-tests are scheduled for September 9 for English and Commerce, and September 10 for Sociology, with no additional exam fee charged to candidates1. The UGC-NET re-examination marks the second major national examination cancelled by the NTA in 2026 following the scrapping of the May 3 NEET-UG exam3.

Bangladesh Conditions PM's India Visit on Extradition of Sheikh Hasina

Bangladesh foreign ministry official A.K.M. Shahidul Karim stated Prime Minister Tarique Rahman will not visit India until New Delhi extradites ousted leader Sheikh Hasina1. Tarique Rahman assumed office as Bangladesh's Prime Minister in February and conducted his initial foreign visits to Malaysia and China2. India's High Commissioner to Bangladesh, Dinesh Trivedi, met with Prime Minister Tarique Rahman in Dhaka2.

Sheikh Hasina has been convicted by court decisions in Bangladesh. Bangladesh's International Crimes Tribunal sentenced Sheikh Hasina to death in November for crimes against humanity1. In February, a court sentenced Sheikh Hasina to 10 years' imprisonment across two corruption cases involving land allocation in Dhaka1.

Despite these rulings, Sheikh Hasina held an audio virtual press conference at the Foreign Correspondents' Club in Delhi on August 5, announcing plans to return to Bangladesh in December1. India's Ministry of External Affairs clarified on August 7 that the Indian government had no role in organizing and did not endorse Sheikh Hasina's press conference1.
