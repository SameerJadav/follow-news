Netanyahu Rejects US Peace Plan and Demands Complete Hamas Disarmament

Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu rejected a US-backed 15-point peace plan for Gaza on August 9, 20261. Netanyahu announced at a cabinet meeting that Israeli forces will not withdraw from Gaza until Hamas is genuinely disarmed2. He stated that the Israeli military will not carry out any withdrawal until Hamas is completely disarmed of all heavy and light weaponry1.

Palestinians in Gaza stated that Netanyahu's rejection of the US-backed 15-point peace plan has deepened uncertainty over an end to the war3. In contrast, Hamas stated that it remains committed to the 15-point peace agreement3. A Hamas official told the BBC that the group reaffirms its commitment to the plan and is ready to engage seriously in implementing it4. Bassem Naim, a member of Hamas's political bureau, urged mediators and the United States to pressure Netanyahu to adhere to the agreed roadmap5. Previously, a senior Hamas official stated that the group would only hand over its weapons if Israel withdrew from parts of Gaza first2.

The 15-point proposal was created in July by US President Donald Trump's Board of Peace1. President Trump announced in late July 2026 that his Board of Peace had reached an agreement for the complete disarmament of Hamas and other armed groups in Gaza2. Under the proposal from the Board of Peace, Hamas would disarm in exchange for a full Israeli military withdrawal from Gaza and a transition of power to a Palestinian National Committee1. Nickolay Mladenov, high representative for Gaza for the Board of Peace, stated that the 15-point plan is the only way forward to prevent a recurrence of the conflict1.

Political pressure inside Israel also influenced the decision. Far-right members of Netanyahu's cabinet urged him to reject the US-led Gaza plan ahead of Israel's October 27, 2026 elections5.

The rejection comes as fighting and civilian suffering continue in the region. Palestinian officials and aid agencies report that Israel controls roughly 70 percent of Gaza2. About 1.9 million people, representing 90% of Gaza's population, have been displaced since the war began4. More than 73,000 people have been killed in Gaza during Israel's military campaign, according to the territory's health ministry4. The ministry also reported that Israeli operations in Gaza have killed 1,258 Palestinians since the October ceasefire5.

Iran Demands War Compensation to Reopen Strait of Hormuz as US Eases Military Stance

Iran's Revolutionary Guards stated on August 9, 2026, that they will not reopen the Strait of Hormuz until the United States complies with demands including war damage compensation1. The Revolutionary Guards declared the strait a theatre of war that will remain closed until all conditions are accepted2.

Iran's Supreme National Security Council set conditions for reopening the waterway, including ending the war, lifting the US blockade of Iranian ports, ending sanctions, releasing frozen assets, and receiving compensation1. Negotiations involving Iran, Oman, and the US regarding reopening the strait remain stalled3.

Meanwhile, US President Donald Trump told Axios that he is "low-keying it" regarding his administration's military approach to Iran1. Trump signaled that his administration is easing off potential new military strikes against Iran in favor of economic pressure2. Trump also stated that lower oil prices slightly above $75 a barrel have reduced economic pressure on American consumers3. A June 2026 memorandum between the US and Iran had included a proposal for a $300 billion reconstruction fund for Iran2.

In regional leadership developments, Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian met Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei to discuss military developments, economic challenges, and livelihoods as Pezeshkian entered his third year in office4. Mojtaba Khamenei became supreme leader following the death of his father, Ayatollah Ali Khamenei, in February 28 US-Israeli strikes4. However, Israeli media reported that Mojtaba Khamenei was in an extremely critical health condition4. Elsewhere, Yemen's Houthis announced they struck a Saudi oil facility on Saudi Arabia's Red Sea coast1. Houthi strikes on the Yemeni port city of Mokha killed three civilians and eight military personnel and injured 32 people1.

Syria and Russia Agree to Convert Russian Bases into Joint Training Facilities

Syria and Russia agreed on August 9, 2026, to end Moscow's military use of Hmeimim air base and Tartous port as military outposts1. Under the new agreement, Damascus will take control of Hmeimim air base and part of Tartous port, while remaining facilities will become joint training centers1.

A memorandum of understanding sets a deadline of no more than three months to complete the transition of the military bases2. Syrian Foreign Minister Asaad al-Shaibani inspected Hmeimim airbase and Tartus naval base on August 9, 20262. Omar al-Hasri, head of Syria's Civil Aviation Authority, announced that the agency is taking control of Latakia's international airport adjacent to Hmeimim2.

Russia has gradually reduced its military presence in Syria from over 100 military bases down to two coastal locations3. Earlier in 2026, the Kremlin withdrew its military forces from Qamishli airport in northeastern Syria2. Syrian President Ahmed al-Sharaa has been received twice by Russian President Vladimir Putin in Moscow since taking power2. Meanwhile, the Syrian government in Damascus has repeatedly demanded that Russia extradite ousted leader Bashar al-Assad, who is living in Moscow3.

Jharkhand Student Recruitment Protests Continue Over Exam Investigation Demands

Student recruitment protests in Jharkhand entered their 16th day as demonstrators continued to demand accountability over examination irregularities, with six demonstrators on an indefinite hunger strike1. Ranchi Police deployed security measures for a planned student protest march to the Jharkhand Assembly on August 10, 20262.

The state government has offered concessions to address student concerns. Student leader Ravindra Paswan stated that the state government agreed to consider cancelling the 14th JPSC Civil Services Examination, JPSC Backlog 2023, and JPSC Backlog 2025 exams2. Jharkhand Governor Santosh Kumar Gangwar accepted the resignations of three JPSC Commission members: Ajita Bhattacharya, Anima Hansda, and Jamal Ahmed3.

Jharkhand Education Minister Sudivya Kumar stated that the government accepted 98% of the student protesters' demands1. The Jharkhand government proposed a judicial inquiry headed by a retired high court judge and a CID investigation into criminal cases linked to exam irregularities1. The government promised fast-track courts and stated chargesheets against accused persons would be filed within 90 days1. The government also proposed establishing an expert panel with representatives from IIT-ISM, IIM Ranchi, and XLRI to recommend examination system reforms4.

Despite these offers, disagreement remains over specific demands. Student leader Kunal stated that student protesters insist on a CBI inquiry into the JSSC-CGL recruitment examination2. The state government rejected a CBI probe into the JSSC-CGL exam1. Minister Sudivya Kumar stated the state government lacks authority to cancel the CGL examination because it was conducted under High Court and Supreme Court supervision4.

Police actions during the protests also drew attention. Ranchi City SP Paras Rana clarified that security personnel carried paintball guns to identify troublemakers, not pellet guns as rumoured3.

TMC Accuses BJP of Attack on Mamata Banerjee's Vehicle in West Bengal

Demonstrators surrounded former West Bengal Chief Minister Mamata Banerjee's vehicle in Halisahar, North 24 Parganas district, throwing shoes, mud, and stones1. Local agitators raised slogans of "go back" and "thief" at Banerjee during her visit on Sunday afternoon2. Rajya Sabha MP Dola Sen stated that thrown stones and half-bricks cracked the glass of Banerjee's car3.

Banerjee stated that her car was attacked with stones, shoes, and mud, accusing the police of protecting BJP lumpens1. Trinamool Congress (TMC) MPs Kalyan Banerjee and Abhishek Banerjee accused the BJP of orchestrating an attempt to murder Mamata Banerjee during her visit3. TMC MP Kalyan Banerjee alleged that West Bengal Chief Minister Suvendu Adhikari directed the attack3 and demanded the immediate resignation of West Bengal's Director General of Police2.

West Bengal BJP state president Samik Bhattacharya denied BJP involvement in the attack on Banerjee's car and condemned the throwing of stones1.

The protest followed a recent custody death in the area. Birju Keot, husband of former municipal councillor Jeni Sharma Keot, died in police custody following his arrest by Halisahar police on extortion allegations1. A senior Barrackpore city police officer confirmed that a judicial magistrate enquiry was ordered into Keot's death in custody2.
