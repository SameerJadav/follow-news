Hackers stole $6.3 billion from crypto exchange Binance in massive cyber heist

Hackers stole $6.3 billion from cryptocurrency exchange Binance in a record cyber heist1. The company froze around half of the stolen funds after the attack occurred1. Binance secured $3 billion of its stolen digital assets1. Unidentified hackers held the remaining funds1.

Blockchain intelligence firm TRM reported that an attacker moved about $6.36 billion out of Binance wallets2. The funds were moved across ten blockchains, mostly within 20 minutes2. NyxLab CEO Kok Tin Gan said the attack appeared to involve one signature2. That signature granted a never-expiring, full-access token to a Binance hot wallet2.

Binance co-CEO Richard Teng said hackers used a self-destruct mechanism1. The mechanism destroyed tooling and traces as they went1. Analytics firms linked the Binance hack to a North Korean hacking unit1. Experts including TRM Labs attributed the theft to TraderTraitor, an elite North Korean unit also called Lazarus Group3. Chainalysis co-founder and CEO Jonathan Levin said on X that the group likely did the attack2.

Cryptocurrency theft generates an estimated half of North Korea's foreign currency income3. A UN panel of experts reported in spring 2024 that it was reviewing 58 suspected North Korean cyber-attacks3. Those attacks targeted cryptocurrency companies between 2017 and 2023, and they were worth $3 billion3. In February 2016, North Korean hackers tried to move $951 million from Bangladesh Bank's account at the Federal Reserve Bank of New York3. They ultimately stole $81 million in that attack3.

The Binance heist eclipsed a $1.5 billion hack on the Bybit exchange in 20251. Binance set aside more than $6.34 billion in company funds to cover the stolen assets1. The US Secret Service said it was working around the clock to identify, freeze, trace, and recover stolen funds1. The price of Bitcoin fell 5% to $76,800 in Friday afternoon trading in New York after news of the hack1.

Binance was founded in China in 20171. It has an estimated total of over 300 million registered users worldwide1. Former Binance CEO Changpeng Zhao pleaded guilty in 2023 to failing to prevent money laundering on the platform2. US President Donald Trump pardoned Zhao in 20252.

Israeli general election ends in deadlock as Benjamin Netanyahu falls short of majority

Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu failed to win a parliamentary majority in the national election1. His Likud party won 27 seats, remaining the largest party in the Knesset1. But his right-wing coalition won 53 seats, short of the 61 needed for a majority1. A rival bloc led by Gadi Eisenkot secured 48 seats2.

Netanyahu accused Palestinian Israeli parties of stealing the election and receiving money from Qatar3. Israel's Electoral Commission found no evidence of fraud regarding Palestinian Israeli parties3. Qatar also denied involvement3. Acting Central Elections Committee Director General Dean Livne said there was no basis for allegations of systematic violations2. Israeli security agency Shin Bet investigated whether Arab-led parties received illegal foreign funding from Qatar and Turkey2.

Arab Israelis make up about one-fifth of Israel's population2. The Arab party lists The Joint List and Ra'am won a record 19 seats following high turnout2. US President Donald Trump endorsed Netanyahu before the election1. In Israel, a candidate given a mandate to form a government has 28 days to get support from 61 lawmakers, with a possible 14-day extension1.

Civilians leave Russian-occupied Oleshky during rare localized ceasefire

Hundreds of residents left the Russian-occupied city of Oleshky during a four-hour ceasefire on Friday1. US First Lady Melania Trump brokered the localized humanitarian ceasefire in the city1. The population of Oleshky fell from a pre-war total of 24,000 to about 2,0002.

Ukrainian negotiator David Arakhamia said evacuated residents traveled to Anapa in Russia2. From there, they will fly to Belarus and travel by bus to Ukraine2. Russia's human rights ombudsman Yana Lantratova said 25 buses and nine ambulances helped with evacuations2. The ceasefire allowed workers to de-mine roads leading out of Oleshky3.

Ukrainian human rights ombudsman Dmytro Lubinets said this was the first long frontline ceasefire maintained since 20223. The Kremlin described the Oleshky ceasefire as a special case that is hard to apply to broader peace talks3. Russian forces captured Oleshky in February 20223. The city suffered severe flooding after the Kakhovka dam was destroyed in June 20233. In another attack, a Russian drone strike on a train station in Dnipro killed at least 13 people on Thursday2.

Polish police enter Constitutional Tribunal as head judge faces suspension

Polish police entered the Constitutional Tribunal building in Warsaw after demonstrators threw objects at officers1. Demonstrators gathered outside the court during the protest1. Supporters of the opposition Law and Justice party chanted that Tusk was a traitor and prayed outside2.

Authorities suspended Constitutional Tribunal head Bogdan Swieczkowski1. He had used his power to block judges elected by the parliamentary majority from working1. Justice Minister Waldemar Zurek requested that legal immunity be removed for Swieczkowski and his deputy so they could face criminal charges1. Judge Anna Korwin-Piotrowska was put in charge of the court after the suspension1.

The Constitutional Court stated on social media that the actions against its top judges were a coup3. It insisted that the judges remain legally appointed3. Polish President Karol Nawrocki turned his plane around while flying to Senegal1. He said the president must be in Poland during the court crisis1. Nawrocki has refused to swear in five court judges elected by parliament since March3. Polish Prime Minister Donald Tusk said restoring the rule of law after years of Law and Justice party lawlessness is a difficult process1.

ISRO successfully launches NVS-03 navigation satellite aboard GSLV-F18 rocket

The Indian Space Research Organisation successfully launched the NVS-03 navigation satellite on Friday1. A GSLV-F18 rocket carried the satellite into space from the Satish Dhawan Space Centre in Sriharikota1. Liftoff occurred at 11:51 pm Indian Standard Time on October 30, 2026, from the Second Launch Pad2. The rocket measured 51.7 metres tall2.

The GSLV-F18 rocket placed the 2,250 kg NVS-03 satellite into geosynchronous transfer orbit 18 minutes after liftoff1. ISRO accomplished the GSLV-F18 mission with a turnaround time of 56 days after the earlier GSLV-F17 mission1.

ISRO Chairman Dr. V. Narayanan stated that the mission restored the NavIC constellation to its minimum operational configuration of four satellites1. NavIC was originally designed with a constellation of seven satellites placed in geostationary and geosynchronous orbits3. But the last functioning atomic clock aboard the IRNSS-1F satellite stopped working on March 13, 20261. That left only three operational satellites in the NavIC system1.

India's armed forces rely on foreign navigation systems like US GPS and Russian GLONASS when NavIC has fewer than four operational satellites3. The success of NVS-03 restores that minimum requirement1. The replacement satellite NVS-02 failed to reach its intended orbit on January 29, 2025, because of a pyro valve signal loss in its engine3. For this launch, ISRO kept specific valves open before liftoff to prevent the same technical problem from happening again2.

The NVS-03 satellite carries an indigenously manufactured atomic clock alongside imported atomic clocks3. The satellite transmits signals in the L1, L5, and S bands4. These signals make NavIC compatible with modern civilian smartphone chipsets4. In June 2025, the Indian government announced plans to replace US GPS with five atomic clocks located across India to maintain Indian Standard Time4.

Oil tankers carrying Indian seafarers targeted in Strait of Hormuz attacks

Explosive devices damaged an oil tanker carrying Indian seafarers in the Strait of Hormuz on Friday1. The Panama-flagged oil tanker MT Louise 1 was carrying 27 Indian seafarers when it was targeted with limpet mines2. The attack damaged the ship's engine room and temporarily disabled the vessel before crew members controlled an onboard fire3.

On October 29, the Liberia-flagged MT Arzanah was hit in a projectile attack in the Strait of Hormuz while carrying two Indian nationals3. Union Minister Sarbananda Sonowal confirmed that all 29 Indian seafarers across both ships were safe2. External Affairs Minister S. Jaishankar raised concerns about attacks on civilian shipping during the G20 foreign ministers' meeting on Friday2.

The UK Maritime Trade Operations reported that a small boat attached explosive devices to a tanker on Friday1. Limpet mines attach to a ship's hull using magnets to damage machinery3. Meanwhile, the Omani Maritime Security Centre evacuated 25 crew members from the burning Panamanian-flagged oil tanker RITI on Friday4. December Brent crude futures rose $2.76, or 2.4 percent, to settle at $117.64 a barrel on Friday1.
