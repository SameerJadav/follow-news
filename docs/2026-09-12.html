Houthis seize Yemen coast as drone strikes force Saudi oil pipeline shutdown

Houthi forces seized control of the entire Red Sea coast of Yemen on September 111. The fighters also captured three strategic islands1. The UN migration agency said the offensive killed more than 500 people1. The agency added that the fighting displaced around 46,000 people1.

Global energy markets reacted quickly to the conflict. Oil prices rose past $100 per barrel1. Average US diesel prices topped $6 per gallon1.

Attacks near Riyadh and Medina targeted energy routes1. Saudi Arabia's energy ministry temporarily shut down its East-West oil pipeline after the attacks1. Saudi Arabia had been transporting around 5 million barrels of oil daily through that pipeline2. The pipeline flows to Yanbu2. This pathway helped the nation bypass the Strait of Hormuz2.

Saudi Arabia's foreign ministry said drones from Iraq struck the pipeline3. Iraq confirmed the drone attack originated from its Maysan governorate3. The Iraqi government then removed its military operations commander in that region3. The Iraqi Prime Minister asked Saudi Arabia not to hit back4. Saudi Arabia chose not to retaliate against Iraq after the request4.

The International Energy Agency released data on Saudi oil output. The agency said Saudi crude oil supply dropped by 2.3 million barrels per day in August5. That reduction brought total supply down to 6 million barrels per day5.

Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman spoke with US President Donald Trump3. Prince Mohammed urged President Trump to take military action against the Houthis3. President Trump declined direct US military involvement against the Houthis3. President Trump offered intelligence assistance instead3. He also offered targeting assistance to Saudi Arabia3.

Diplomats are attempting to secure water passages. Iran, Iraq, and Gulf Arab states scheduled a meeting in Oman5. They plan to discuss safe commercial shipping routes through the Strait of Hormuz5.

Indian Prime Minister Narendra Modi hosts BRICS leaders in New Delhi

Leaders of BRICS member states gathered in New Delhi, India, for an 11-nation BRICS summit1. The meeting occurred while a conflict involving the United States, Israel, and Iran had been ongoing for seven months1. BRICS members the United Arab Emirates and Saudi Arabia have faced attacks from Iran1.

Indian Minister of Ports, Shipping and Waterways Shantanu Thakur greeted Pezeshkian at the airport in New Delhi3. Prime Minister Modi met with President Pezeshkian on the sidelines of the summit4. India's Ministry of External Affairs said Modi stressed the need to protect freedom of navigation and commerce2. Modi also emphasized seafarer safety in his meeting with Pezeshkian2.
