Houthis Target Saudi Oil Sites as US Pauses Strikes on Iran

Widespread Wildfires Force Evacuations Across France and Spain

More than 250,000 people across France and Spain have been evacuated due to widespread wildfires1. The severe blazes have swept through regions in southern Europe, causing casualties and extensive damage. In Spain, firefighters found a man in his 70s dead inside his vehicle in Manises, near Valencia1. Spanish Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez stated that 130,000 hectares of land have been burned by wildfires in Spain so far in 20261. Facing unprecedented devastation, Spain declared a national emergency due to wildfires for the first time in its history2. Authorities also issued movement restrictions, instructing more than 28,000 residents in the autonomous region of Madrid and Ávila municipality to remain indoors2.

France has also suffered heavy destruction from the spreading fires. French Interior Minister Laurent Nuñez stated that 98,000 hectares of land have burned in France since the start of 20261. Wildfires near Arcachon Bay and Biscarrosse destroyed more than 36,000 hectares of forest and forced nearly 200,000 people to evacuate3. In response, the French armed forces deployed 1,500 soldiers to south-west France to assist local firefighters and support evacuations2. The French military also deployed an Airbus A400M Atlas cargo aircraft adapted for aerial firefighting in the Gironde region2.

The ongoing fire crisis disrupted major national sporting events in France. Organizers and police shortened the final stage of the Tour de France from 133 kilometers to 89 kilometers to redeploy security forces to wildfire zones3. Stage 21 of the Tour de France was modified to begin with laps on the Champs-Élysées rather than starting in Thoiry3. Prior to these emergency changes, Slovenian cyclist Tadej Pogačar maintained a strong lead going into Stage 20 of the Tour de France4.

Wildfires also threatened communities further north in the United Kingdom. More than 500 firefighters worked to contain a wildfire in Scotland's Cairngorms National Park that has burned for 10 days5. Authorities evacuated residents of Nethy Bridge in Scotland to a respite center in Aviemore as the Cairngorms fire advanced5. Scottish First Minister John Swinney submitted a request to the UK government for military aerial support to battle the Cairngorms wildfire5.

Drone Strikes Target Siberian Refinery and Russian Retail Warehouses

Ukrainian drones struck an oil refinery in the Siberian region of Tyumen on July 25, 2026, starting a fire1. Due to domestic fuel shortages caused by drone strikes on refineries, Russia extended its ban on fuel exports until the end of 20261. Ukrainian drones also carried out a tenth attack this week against warehouses belonging to Russian online retailer Wildberries2. At least nine employees were killed and dozens injured in Ukrainian drone strikes targeting Wildberries warehouses2. Wildberries founder Tatyana Kim holds an estimated net worth of approximately $8 billion2. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy stated that Wildberries facilities were being used to store and distribute drone components and military goods for Russian forces2. Threats from Ukrainian drones also forced the suspension of an all-Russian athletics competition in Yekaterinburg1.

Meanwhile, Russian-appointed officials stated that a Ukrainian drone strike on a seaside tourist camp in Kyrylivka killed 12 people, including four children1. A Russian drone attack on a Nova Poshta delivery service facility in Ukraine's Sumy region killed three drivers3. The conflict spread near international borders when a Romanian F-16 fighter jet intercepted and shot down a drone that entered Romanian airspace near the border with Ukraine3.

Amid the fighting, Kazakh President Kassym-Jomart Tokayev publicly told Russian President Vladimir Putin in Omsk that the war in Ukraine should be frozen and ended1. The White House announced that US President Donald Trump will meet Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy in Washington on Tuesday3.

Indian Education Minister Resigns Following Weeks of Student Protests

Dharmendra Pradhan resigned from his role as India's Education Minister following widespread student protests over exam paper leaks1. His resignation came after 36 days of continuous protests led by a student group called the Cockroach Janta Party2. Following Dharmendra Pradhan's decision to step down, Union Home Minister Amit Shah and other senior cabinet members visited his residence to offer support1.

The student demonstrations eventually led to an agreement with authorities. The Cockroach Janta Party ended its continuous sit-in protest at Jantar Mantar on July 25, 2026, after reaching an agreement with the Indian government3. As part of the resolution, Union Health Minister J.P. Nadda assured student representatives that no criminal charges or new cases would be filed against protesters3. To restrict communications around the gathering site during the demonstrations, the Ministry of Home Affairs authorized eight separate internet shutdown orders around Jantar Mantar between July 20 and July 25, 20263.

The youth movement behind the demonstrations gained widespread national attention. The Cockroach Janta Party adopted its name in response to a courtroom comment that referred to protesting students as 'cockroaches'4. The group experienced massive growth online during the period of public unrest. The Cockroach Janta Party accumulated 22 million Instagram followers over a period of several days during the student protests4. Following the resolution of the sit-in, an Instagram celebration reel published by the Cockroach Janta Party received 120 million views within 10 hours of posting4.
