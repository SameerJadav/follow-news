Massive Himalayan floods kill hundreds across Nepal and Tibet

Floods caused by a glacier collapse killed hundreds of people across Nepal and Tibet1. As of Sunday, 810 people died in the disaster1. Nepalese authorities counted 794 dead in Nepal1. Chinese authorities reported 16 dead in Tibet1. NDTV reported a different count of 734 recovered bodies2. Search teams reported 3,048 people missing across both regions1. In Nepal, 2,502 people remain missing1. In Tibet, 546 people are missing1.

Nepal's disaster management authority NDRRMA said 933 workers from hydropower projects are untraced1. The Independent Power Producers' Association Nepal listed 537 project workers as unaccounted for1. The Nepalese Army used helicopters to airlift heavy earth-moving machinery1. Machinery went to the Upper Trishuli-3A hydropower project1. Rescuers needed to reach a mud-filled entry point of a tunnel1. Army spokesperson Brig Gen Rajaram Basnet said rescuers found the crown head of Trishuli 3A1. Teams dug a hole into the tunnel to search for survivors inside1.

India's Ministry of External Affairs said rescuers saved 149 Indian nationals in Nepal1. Another 275 Indian citizens remain missing in Nepal1. Chinese authorities said 546 people were missing in Gyirong in Tibet1. That number included 261 foreign citizens from 23 countries1. Among those missing in Gyirong were 49 Indians and 104 Nepalese citizens1.

India sent four relief flights carrying 68.5 tonnes of supplies to Kathmandu1. India also sent a 13-member army tunnel-rescue team to Nepal1. Six forensic teams from India joined the relief efforts1. Recovering from the disaster could cost one-tenth of Nepal's annual economic output3.

Officials and experts from Nepal and China met in Kathmandu on May 273. They discussed monitoring floods, weather hazards, and glacier threats3. Twelve days before the disaster, China's World Meteorological Centre emailed Nepal3. The centre warned of a monsoon depression3. It also warned of potential flash floods between August 14 and 193.

Icelandic voters reject proposal to restart EU membership talks

Icelandic voters rejected a proposal to restart talks to join the European Union in a national referendum1. Results showed 52.8% voted against resuming talks, while 47.2% voted in favor1. The BBC reported a margin of about 12,600 votes out of over 225,000 cast1. France 24 reported that around 9,500 votes separated the two sides2. Voter turnout reached 82.5%, the highest in any Icelandic election since 20091.

Iceland first applied to join the EU in 2009 after a banking collapse1. Talks were put on hold in 20131. Prime Minister Kristrún Frostadóttir said her government will not revisit EU membership1. Frostadóttir previously said Iceland already follows 75% of EU rules3. Capital city voters supported joining the EU, while rural regions voted against1. Fish and marine industries make up nearly 40% of Iceland's exports1. AFP reported that about 90% of fisheries companies opposed EU membership4. Iceland fought the Cod Wars over fishing rights with Britain in the 1970s4. European Council President António Costa said the EU respects the choice5. Reform UK leader Nigel Farage congratulated voters for keeping their independence1.

NASA launches Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope aboard SpaceX rocket

NASA launched the Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope aboard a SpaceX rocket on August 301. SpaceX launched the $4.3 billion telescope from the Kennedy Space Center in Florida using a Falcon Heavy rocket1. The space telescope reached orbit 10 minutes after liftoff3. It is traveling on a 100-day journey to Lagrange Point 21. That point is located about 1 million miles, or 1.6 million kilometers, from Earth1. The telescope will join the James Webb Space Telescope at that location2.

The telescope is named after physicist Nancy Grace Roman1. She served as NASA's first chief astronomer in the 1960s and its first female executive1. The telescope features a field of vision 100 times larger than the Hubble and James Webb telescopes1. NASA administrator Nicky Fox expects the telescope to find tens of thousands or up to 100,000 exoplanets1. Its primary survey will cover more than 2 billion galaxies over more than one year2. The mission has a five-year primary lifespan and could run for 10 years1.
