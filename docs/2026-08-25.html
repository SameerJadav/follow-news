US launches Operation Economic Outcast to isolate Iran with sweeping sanctions

US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent announced a major sanctions campaign against Iran on August 24, 20261. President Donald Trump directed the launch of Operation Economic Outcast1. The operation targets Iran and its foreign enablers1. Bessent said the objective is to cut off every economic lifeline that sustains the Iranian regime1. He said Tehran must be left standing completely alone1.

Bessent announced an unprecedented global economic war against all Iranian revenue streams2. The effort directly targets Iran's oil revenue2. He named five key sectors supporting the Iranian economy3. These sectors are digital assets, technology, gold, aviation, and shipping3. They will all face heightened scrutiny from the United States3. Bessent warned that any entity helping Iran wash money will face strict penalties3. They will be removed from the US dollar financial system3.

Bessent said Trump is personally calling world leaders3. Trump is asking them to stop all economic dealings with Tehran3. Bessent pointed to recent actions by the United Arab Emirates as proof of diplomatic success3. The country suspended trade, commercial exchanges, and financial transactions with Iran3.

Iran warned it would stop all regional oil exports if the conflict with the US continues4. The regime also warned ships not to cross the Strait of Hormuz without permission4. Economist David Oxley said about 90% of Iran's oil exports go to China4. Oxley works as chief climate and commodities economist at Capital Economics4. On August 24, 2026, Brent crude oil reached $92 per barrel4. Gasoline prices in the United States went over $4 per gallon4.

The US dollar index rose 0.17 percent to 98.99 on August 24, 20265. This increase came after the US announced expanded sanctions5. CNBC reported that Bessent could use the Treasury General Account5. The account holds near $1 trillion at the Federal Reserve5. He could use it to fund bond buybacks5.

The market actions also affected Canada5. The Canadian dollar fell 0.61 percent to C$1.385 per US dollar on August 24, 20265. Trump threatened to raise tariffs on Canadian goods to 50% on January 1, 20271. These goods include cars, trucks, auto parts, and steel1. Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney said this threat was expected1. Carney asked what message it sends to US workers who rely on Canadian demand1. He mentioned workers in states like Michigan and Ohio1.

Public support for the war has dropped among Americans1. A Reuters/Ipsos poll showed support fell to 31%1. This was down from 34% earlier in August 20261. Among Republicans, 69% support the war with Iran1. That is down from 77% in March1. Recent polling showed Trump's approval rating at a low of 33%1.

US-Canada trade negotiations collapse as Trump threatens 50 percent auto tariffs

Trade negotiations between the United States and Canada collapsed after both sides failed to reach an agreement1. Donald Trump announced a 50% tariff on Canadian cars, trucks, auto parts, and steel2. The new tax starts on January 1, 20272.

Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney suspended negotiations and recalled his team to Ottawa3. Carney said Canada rejected the US terms because of last-minute demands4. He said those demands limited tariff relief and restricted Canada's trade independence4. Carney said Canada will return to talks only when the US acts like a true partner2. An Angus Reid Institute poll showed three in four Canadians supported his decision to walk away5.

Ontario Premier Doug Ford said Trump declared economic war on Canada5. Ford warned Canada could cut off electricity and critical minerals to the US5. Industry leader Flavio Volpe warned that tariffs on auto parts would stop US car assembly5.

US Trade Representative Jamieson Greer said the US had to act after Canadian retaliation4. Annual trade between the two nations reached about $909 billion2. On August 24, shares of Ford fell 3.2%, Stellantis fell 4.7%, and General Motors fell 1.1%5. Trump claimed on Truth Social that Canadian farm tariffs created a $60 billion trade deficit3.

Britain expands Ukraine military support with missile technology and AI partnership

UK Prime Minister Andy Burnham visited Kyiv on August 24, 20261. This was his first official trip abroad as prime minister1. Burnham and Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy signed a new defense AI partnership2. Britain became the first foreign partner to access Ukraine's Avengers AI Labs2.

The deal allows the UK to use AI models trained on Ukrainian battlefield data3. This technology will protect British military sites and infrastructure3. The Avengers database contains 5 million battlefield images from the DELTA combat management system2. These AI systems analyze over 100,000 drone video feeds monthly and spot about 70 percent of targets in real time2. Burnham said this work will speed up security efforts3. The UK Ministry of Defence approved AI projects with British tech companies Sintela, Mind Foundry, and Skyral3.

The UK also allowed defense firm MBDA to share declassified details on Scalp cruise missiles1. This enables Ukraine to make missile components locally1. The missile measures 5.10 metres long and weighs about 1,300 kg4. Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov said the transfer undermines peace efforts1. He said Russian forces are searching for production sites1.

Britain has given over £25 billion in military and civilian help to Ukraine since 20224. Meanwhile, the European Union released €6.1 billion in military aid1. Former intelligence officer Dr. Frank Ledwidge said European commitments fail to solve Kyiv's need for air defence interceptors5.

US Department of Homeland Security proposes 103,265 dollar H-1B visa fee

The US Department of Homeland Security published a new rule in the Federal Register on Monday1. The rule proposes a new $103,265 fee for cap-subject H-1B visa petitions2. This proposal opens a 30-day public comment period1. Officials could finalize the new rule by the end of 20263.

The H-1B program allows American employers to hire foreign workers for specialty jobs3. The visa lasts up to three years initially3. It can be extended up to six years total3. The government sets an annual cap of 85,000 new visas2. This total includes 65,000 regular visas and 20,000 visas reserved under the Master's cap2. US Citizenship and Immigration Services already received enough petitions to reach both caps for fiscal year 20274.

The proposed $103,265 charge is an extra payment2. It comes on top of existing filing fees and statutory costs2. Before recent increases, government fees for H-1B visas usually ranged from $2,000 to $5,0001. In August, officials introduced extra fees of up to $4,500 for extension or transfer requests1.

An earlier $100,000 fee was introduced in September 2025 through a presidential proclamation2. That fee expires in September 2026 unless extended2. However, legal battles have blocked that requirement4. On June 8, 2026, a federal judge in Massachusetts struck down guidance implementing the $100,000 fee4. On July 24, 2026, an appeals court refused to pause that ruling4. As of February 15, only 70 employers had paid the $100,000 fee for 85 workers1.

Legal expert Mitch Wexler said the proposed $103,265 fee depends on whether a petition is cap-subject, not worker location2. Higher education institutions and affiliated non-profit groups will not pay the fee2. Research organizations are also exempt2. New York immigration lawyer Cyrus D Mehta called the fee preposterous2. He said the government killed the goose that laid the golden eggs2.

Government officials estimate the fee will raise $8.78 billion each year2. They estimate 14,541 small businesses will be affected2. For 76% of those businesses, costs will rise by more than 1% of annual revenue2.

Indian workers rely heavily on the program2. In fiscal year 2025, Indian citizens received 283,000 H-1B visas including extensions2. That was 70% of all visas given2. Indian workers also got nearly 58,000 new visas for initial employment2.

Overall interest in the program has dropped1. Employers registered for about 344,000 visas last year1. That was down over 25 percent from 20241. It was far below the 794,000 registrations in 20231. Boundless Immigration CEO Xiao Wang said top sponsors now include Amazon, Meta, Microsoft, and Google4. Company data showed senior Level III candidates had a 68% selection rate in March 20264. Entry-level Level I candidates had a 40% rate4.

Lawmakers have also introduced the End H-1B Visa Abuse Act of 20264. It proposes a three-year pause on new visas, a cap of 25,000, and a $200,000 minimum salary threshold4.
