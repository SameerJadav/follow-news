French President Emmanuel Macron described the situation as "completely unprecedented" and called it the country's "toughest since World War Two"1. French Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu reported that 116,085 hectares have burned and 162 arrests related to fire starts have been made since July 61. In Cestas, Mayor Jerome Steffe said 17,000 residents were evacuated overnight after a wildfire burned 42,000 hectares and destroyed 240 houses1. In Spain, wildfires have forced more than 100,000 people to evacuate1. A fire in Ávila, west of Madrid, burned 50,000 hectares, making it the largest wildfire in Spain's history3. Student protests over exam paper leaks trigger political fallout and parliamentary disruption in India

Massive student-led protests over national exam paper leaks have sparked political disruption and official investigations across India1. Indian Education Minister Dharmendra Pradhan resigned from his post following widespread demonstrations organized by students over the exam leaks2.

The protests in New Delhi led to violent clashes outside Parliament. Nearly 47 personnel from the Rapid Action Force were injured during student demonstrations outside Parliament, including six who suffered head injuries and four who suffered bone fractures1. Following the events of July 20, the Central Reserve Police Force launched an inquiry to determine whether the Rapid Action Force was authorized to use pump-action pellet guns against the protesting students1.

Police response and surveillance methods have also become major points of dispute. Delhi Police alleged that 2,873 individuals present at protests in Jantar Mantar had criminal antecedents3. Police stated that they identified 989 of these individuals by using a Facial Recognition System3. In response to this tracking, student activist Aishe Ghosh filed a petition in the Delhi High Court seeking to declare mass facial recognition surveillance of protesters unconstitutional2. Meanwhile, political groups have entered discussions; the Cockroach Janta Party demanded the withdrawal of all criminal cases filed against demonstrators as part of a negotiated settlement with the Indian government3.

The fallout from the police response has caused severe disruption in the Indian parliament. The Rajya Sabha was adjourned for a sixth consecutive day after opposition members demanded that Home Minister Amit Shah apologize for police action against protesting students4.

In a separate national security update, Prime Minister Narendra Modi and Home Minister Amit Shah commended the Central Reserve Police Force on its Raising Day1. They praised the force for eliminating Left-wing extremism within a set deadline of March 31, 20261.
