Russian missile and drone barrage strikes Kyiv, killing at least 16 and injuring dozens

Russian forces launched a heavy missile and drone attack on Kyiv overnight into Thursday, causing widespread destruction and multiple casualties1. Official reports presented differing numbers for the casualties across the capital1. Kyiv officials reported that at least 16 people were killed in and around the city1, while the State Emergency Service of Ukraine reported that at least 17 people were killed across Kyiv2. Official sources also reported different figures for the number of injured people1. Authorities reported that 33 people were injured in Kyiv1, whereas the State Emergency Service of Ukraine reported that more than 40 people were injured across the capital2.

The aerial attack hit several civilian locations. Kyiv Mayor Vitali Klitschko stated that homes, a school, and a children's medical center were damaged in the attack1. Specific neighborhoods suffered severe losses, with eight people reported killed in Kyiv's Solomianskyi district during the attack1. Outside the main city, Tymur Tkachenko, governor of the Kyiv region, said a strike in Brovary district killed one man and wounded another1.

Ukraine's air force provided details regarding the scale of the Russian bombardment. The air force reported that Russia launched 168 drones alongside dozens of ballistic, cruise, and hypersonic missiles1. Air defense forces managed to intercept many incoming weapons. Ukraine's air force reported shooting down 41 out of 46 cruise missiles and 145 drones launched by Russia1. However, defense systems could not halt every threat. Ukraine's air force stated that none of the high-speed ballistic missiles launched by Russia were intercepted1. To counter these weapons, Ukraine relies on American supplies of Patriot interceptor missiles to defend against Russian ballistic missiles2.

The attack also created international effects. Romania's defence ministry stated a drone entered Romanian airspace near Galați and crashed in Tulcea county1. Across the border, Russia's defence ministry claimed that 726 Ukrainian drones were shot down over various regions in Russia2.

Chinese court sentences Evergrande founder Hui Ka Yan to life in prison

The Shenzhen Intermediate People's Court has sentenced Evergrande founder Hui Ka Yan to life in prison and confiscated all of his personal property1. Hui Ka Yan pleaded guilty in April to eight charges including misuse of funds, fundraising fraud, and bribery1. In March 2024, Hui Ka Yan was fined $6.5 million and banned for life from China's capital market2.

The court also levied heavy fines against the corporation, fining Evergrande Group 8.8 billion yuan and its property arm 7 billion yuan1. Regulators in Hong Kong and mainland Chinese authorities found Evergrande overstated revenues by roughly $80 billion over 2019 and 20203. Evergrande defaulted on most of its $300 billion in liabilities after the Chinese government introduced regulations limiting developer debt in 20201.

Legal penalties reached dozens of other company officials. More than 50 individuals linked to Evergrande were sentenced on Thursday, receiving prison terms ranging from 20 months to 18 years1. Hui Ka Yan's sons, Xu Zhijian and Xu Tenghe, were among the Evergrande executives sentenced to jail terms ranging from 22 months to 18 years2.

Mirza Fakhrul Islam Alamgir elected President of Bangladesh in historic vote

Mirza Fakhrul Islam Alamgir was elected President of Bangladesh after defeating challenger Oli Ahmed by 255 votes to 881. Out of 349 registered voting lawmakers, 343 participated in the parliamentary vote2. This election marked the first contested presidential vote in Bangladesh since 19912. Alamgir will take office as Bangladesh's 23rd President2.

Alamgir previously served as secretary-general of the ruling Bangladesh Nationalist Party1. The presidential vote occurred after former Bangladesh President Mohammed Shahabuddin resigned halfway through his term last month1. Parliamentary Speaker Hafiz Uddin Ahmad served as acting president following Shahabuddin's resignation2.

The political transition comes during legal proceedings against former leaders. Former Bangladesh Prime Minister Sheikh Hasina was sentenced to be hanged after being found guilty in absentia of crimes against humanity3.

Former Pakistani Prime Minister Imran Khan was transferred from Adiala jail to Shifa International Hospital in Islamabad under heavy security1. The movement followed an order from a three-judge panel of Pakistan's Supreme Court requiring that Imran Khan be taken to a hospital within 48 hours for a medical examination1.

Imran Khan has been imprisoned since August 2023 on corruption charges1. His health condition has caused concern; in February, Imran Khan's lawyer stated he had only 15% vision left in his right eye1. Imran Khan's next court hearing is scheduled for September 16, and his lawyer stated he should remain hospitalized until at least then2.

Government ministers committed to respecting the judicial order. Parliamentary Affairs Minister Tariq Fazal Chaudhry affirmed the Pakistani government would fully comply with the Supreme Court ruling regarding Imran Khan3. Supreme Court holds 1978 landmark definition of industry applies to pending labor disputes

A nine-judge Supreme Court bench ruled that pending labor disputes under the Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, will continue to be decided based on a landmark 1978 judgment1. At the same time, the nine-judge bench held that the 1978 Bangalore Water Supply judgment's definition of 'industry' will not apply to Section 2(p) of the Industrial Relations Code, 20201.

Chief Justice of India Surya Kant authored the majority opinion for the nine-judge bench1. The ruling addresses how legal standards function alongside newer statutory frameworks. The Industrial Relations Code, 2020, came into force on November 21, 20252.

The 1978 Bangalore Water Supply decision defined legal standards for workplaces through three specific conditions1. The 1978 triple test for defining an industry requires systematic activity, functional cooperation between employer and employees, and production of goods or services2. Under the court's decision, this triple test remains the governing standard for resolving all pending disputes filed under the Industrial Disputes Act, 19471.

The ruling was not unanimous. Justice B.V. Nagarathna, joined by Justices Dipankar Datta and Ujjal Bhuyan, dissented1. In their dissenting opinion, the judges held that reconsidering the 1978 judgment was unwarranted1.

India permits duty-free raw sugar imports to help reduce rising domestic prices

The Indian government has allowed duty-free imports of 10 lakh (1 million) tonnes of raw sugar for the first time in a decade1. The Directorate General of Foreign Trade set October 31, 2026, as the deadline for duty-free raw sugar imports under the Tariff Rate Quota2.

The decision comes after domestic sugar prices rose significantly. Average retail sugar prices in India reached Rs 52.3 per kg, representing a 13% increase from a year earlier1. Similarly, the all-India average ex-mill sugar price rose to Rs 5,400-5,500 per quintal, up from Rs 3,900 a year earlier2.

In addition to allowing imports, the government placed limits on major domestic buyers. The food ministry capped sugar inventories for bulk consumers using over 10 tonnes a month to a maximum of 15 days of consumption1. The stockholding restrictions for bulk sugar consumers will apply from September 1 to November 301.
