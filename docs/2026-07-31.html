US launches heavy wave of strikes on Iranian targets as Middle East conflict spreads

US Central Command announced that it has completed a heavy wave of strikes on dozens of Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) targets located in Iran1. The military operation comes as conflict intensifies across multiple countries in the region. US Central Command stated that joint US-Saudi strikes in eastern Iraq were carried out in response to over 30 IRGC-directed aerial drone attacks during the preceding 72 hours1. The US-Saudi attacks in Iraq resulted in the deaths of at least 20 members of the Iran-backed Popular Mobilisation Forces2.

The strikes inside Iraq have drawn official criticism from leaders in Baghdad. Iraqi spokesperson Haider al-Aboudi stated that Baghdad has not granted any approval for attacks on specific sites or groups within Iraqi territory2.

Military actions and missile exchanges extended into neighboring countries. Jordan's armed forces reported shooting down five Iranian missiles that were targeting its territory on Thursday1. On the other side, Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps stated that it struck the US-run Azraq base situated in Jordan2. Furthermore, Iranian state media claimed that its forces destroyed several American F-35 stealth fighters operating from bases in Jordan3.

Losses of life were also recorded within Iranian territories during the flare-up. Iranian state media reported that three people, including a toddler and the child's parents, were killed on Qeshm island2. Iranian state media added that three IRGC members were killed in Zanjan province2.

Maritime security and energy infrastructure have also been hit by the expanding violence. The government of Egypt announced that it was analysing drone fragments collected after a strike on two gas vessels in the Mediterranean port of Damietta2. Meanwhile, Yemen's Houthi movement announced a blockade on Saudi ships passing through the Bab el-Mandeb Strait on July 204.

Amid the rising tensions, high-level diplomatic discussions were held in an effort to contain the fighting. Saudi Defense Minister Prince Khalid bin Salman travelled to Washington to urge restraint3. During his visit, he warned against allowing the current situation to escalate into a broader regional war3.

UEFA threatens World Cup boycott over FIFA commercial investment plan

European football's governing body UEFA stated on Thursday that it would boycott the World Cup if FIFA presses ahead with its private investor plan1. FIFA seeks to raise up to $4.2 billion from private investors through a newly proposed commercial entity named FIFA Forward Enterprise2. Under FIFA's proposal, private investors would own a 20 percent stake in FIFA Forward Enterprise3. The proposed commercial subsidiary would be valued at around $20 billion, with Thrive Eternal expected to lead the proposed investor group4.

To gain support, FIFA President Gianni Infantino offered member associations an increase in basic funding from $10 million to $20 million over four years if the investment proposal is approved3. Infantino defended the plan by stating that FIFA Forward Enterprise is an offer, part of a democratic process, and an opportunity rather than an obligation2.

However, other football bodies raised concerns. Concacaf expressed deep concerns regarding the lack of due process in FIFA's proposal, though it stopped short of calling for a boycott5.

Separately, the 2030 FIFA World Cup is scheduled to be staged primarily in Spain, Portugal, and Morocco, with centenary matches in Uruguay, Argentina, and Paraguay5. The Women's Under-20 World Cup in Poland is scheduled to begin on September 53.

Spain sends military to Ceuta enclave following influx of migrants from Morocco

Spain announced it would deploy military personnel to Ceuta to support border security after hundreds of migrants entered from Morocco1. Representatives from the Guardia Civil stated that between 1,500 and 2,000 migrants arrived in Ceuta over a 10-day period, representing about 2 percent of the territory's population2. Ceuta regional government head Juan Jesús Vivas stated that the small city was facing a humanitarian and social emergency of national proportions2. Vivas also stated that 60 bodies had been recovered at sea in recent months3.

Tragedy accompanied the influx, as at least nine deaths were reported among migrants attempting to reach the Spanish enclave of Ceuta from Morocco1. Earlier in the month, Spain's Supreme Court ruled that migrants intercepted at sea attempting to reach Ceuta or Melilla cannot be summarily returned to Morocco3.

The situation triggered international political reactions. Italian Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni called for Spain to be suspended from the Schengen zone following the migrant influx in Ceuta3.

Indian Parliament passes strict Anti-Paper Leak Bill despite opposition walkout

The Parliament of India has passed the Public Examinations (Prevention of Unfair Means) Amendment Bill, 20261. The Rajya Sabha passed the Anti-Paper Leak Bill on Thursday, following its clearance by the Lok Sabha on Wednesday1. The landmark legislation establishes strict new legal measures and severe penalties aimed at preventing fraudulent practices in public examinations across India.

During parliamentary proceedings on Thursday, the opposition demonstrated its dissatisfaction with the debate process. Opposition members belonging to the INDIA alliance walked out of the Rajya Sabha prior to the government's official reply on the Anti-Paper Leak Bill1. Highlighting the extent of the issue, Congress leader Mallikarjun Kharge stated that question papers for 152 state and central examinations were leaked over the past 10 years without offenders being punished1.

The amended law introduces significantly harsher prison terms and fines for convicted individuals. Under the amended law, individuals convicted of examination fraud face between five and 10 years in prison, compared with three to five years previously2. Additionally, the maximum financial penalty for individual offenders under the Anti-Paper Leak Bill was increased from ₹10 lakh to ₹50 lakh2.

The legislation also mandates strict penalties for institutions and organized groups involved in leaks. Service providers involved in examination fraud face fines of up to ₹5 crore and can be barred from conducting examinations for 8 years2. Persons involved in organized examination crime face a minimum prison sentence of seven years and fines of up to ₹10 crore2.

Prime Minister Narendra Modi welcomed the passage of the new law and issued a strong warning to lawbreakers. In a video address, Prime Minister Narendra Modi stated that no paper-leak mafia or gang seeking to play with the future of the nation's children will be spared2.

PM Modi chairs high-level security meeting on West Asia crisis and supply chains

Prime Minister Narendra Modi chaired the fourth special meeting of the Cabinet Committee on Security to review the West Asia crisis and supply chains1. During the meeting, PM Modi directed the establishment of a mechanism to provide emergency assistance, timely information, and counselling to Indian seafarers and their families1.

The decision comes amid growing risks to Indian citizens in the region. The Indian government informed Parliament that 16 Indian nationals, including 10 seafarers, have lost their lives in the Middle East conflict this year2. An additional 12 Indian nationals died in an accidental explosion at a gas facility in Qatar in June3. More than 10 million Indian nationals reside and work in West Asia, primarily in Saudi Arabia and the United Arab Emirates3.

The conflict also threatens energy security and shipping. Prior to recent conflict escalations, 40 percent of India's energy imports passed through the Strait of Hormuz4. Furthermore, India relies on foreign imports to meet over 88 percent of its total energy requirements4. Ship crossings through the Bab al-Mandeb Strait fell from an average of 43 per day in early July to 31 per day following Houthi targeting threats4.
