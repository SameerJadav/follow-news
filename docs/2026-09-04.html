UN and WMO Warn of Unprecedented Threats from Supersized El Niño Event

The World Meteorological Organization warned that the current El Niño could be the strongest recorded since records began four decades ago2. The disappearance of anchovies along the South American Pacific coast is signaling a major El Niño event3.

Ocean temperatures at the center of El Niño measured 2.6 degrees Celsius above the 30-year average1. Models forecast a peak of 4 degrees Celsius in November1. UK Met Office projections indicate equatorial Pacific ocean temperatures could spike by 3 degrees Celsius when El Niño peaks in late 2026 or early 20273.

WMO Secretary-General Celeste Saulo said the agency has launched its largest mobilization in its 50-year history to save lives3. The World Food Programme estimated that El Niño is likely to push about 50 million people into acute hunger1. The British government is planning a public campaign advising citizens to stock up on tinned food and water for national emergencies1.

In Indonesia, Professor Bambang Hero Saharjo said research indicates this year's El Niño could cause wildfires to burn up to 11 million hectares4. Indonesia's Health Ministry recorded over 50,000 cases of respiratory infections linked to wildfires between July and August4. That figure included more than 12,000 cases in children under five4.

ISRO Successfully Launches EOS-05 Earth Observation Satellite into Orbit

The Indian Space Research Organisation successfully launched the EOS-05 Earth observation satellite on September 41. The satellite was launched aboard a Geosynchronous Satellite Launch Vehicle rocket1. The GSLV-F17 rocket lifted off following a 27.5-hour countdown from the Satish Dhawan Space Centre in Sriharikota2. Lift off took place at 2.55 a.m. on September 41. The GSLV placed EOS-05 into a Sub-Geosynchronous Transfer Orbit 18 minutes after lifting off1.

EOS-05 is India's first imaging satellite designed to monitor Earth from a geosynchronous orbit approximately 36,000 km high1. The EOS-05 satellite is also designated as Gisat-1A3. EOS-05 carries two multispectral imagers3. One of these imagers features a spatial resolution of 42 metres per pixel3. ISRO Chairman Dr. V. Narayanan said that EOS-05 is the heaviest satellite ever launched by a GSLV rocket1. The satellite weighs 2,367 kg1.

The rocket carrying the satellite had specific physical dimensions. The GSLV-F17 rocket stands 51.7 metres tall4. It has a liftoff mass of 420.5 tonnes4.

This successful launch replaces an earlier failed attempt. EOS-05 replaces the EOS-03 mission1. The EOS-03 mission failed in August 2021 due to a cryogenic upper stage ignition anomaly1. Prior to this launch, ISRO had experienced another setback. ISRO had not conducted any launch missions since the failure of the PSLV-C62 mission on January 12, 20261.

The new satellite adds to India's existing capabilities in space. India had 21 operational Earth observation satellites as of February 20264. ISRO plans to expand its space operations further this year. Narayanan announced that ISRO plans six more launches in the current financial year4. These future missions include the navigation satellite NVS-034. They also include the first uncrewed Gaganyaan mission4.
