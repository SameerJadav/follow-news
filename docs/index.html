Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva wins fourth term as President of Brazil

Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva won a fourth term as President of Brazil1. He defeated Flávio Bolsonaro in the election runoff1. Lula won the runoff vote with 51.79% of the total vote1. He defeated Flávio Bolsonaro by 4.4 million votes1.

Flávio Bolsonaro had won the first round of voting on October 41. He received 47% of the vote in that round1. Lula received 45% in the first round1.

Brazil's Supreme Court ordered voting hours extended nationwide by two hours on Sunday2. Both presidential campaigns requested this extension2. Lula's campaign asked for extended hours because of bus transportation issues in nine states2. Flávio Bolsonaro's campaign cited heavy rain in southern Brazil2. Supreme Electoral Court President Kássio Nunes Marques confirmed Lula's victory1. He said the voting time extension did not change the outcome of the race1.

US President Donald Trump claimed without providing evidence that Brazil's election looked rigged2. Trump also criticized the decision to extend voting hours2. Flávio Bolsonaro's political party issued a statement3. The party recognized the presidential result announced by the electoral court3. But the party called for political debate on questions raised during the election3.

Supreme Court Justice Alexandre de Moraes banned Flávio Bolsonaro from leaving Brazil3. Flávio Bolsonaro is a 45-year-old senator3. Justice Moraes ordered him to give up his passports within 24 hours3. This Supreme Court ruling relates to a criminal investigation3. The investigation involves alleged money laundering with lawyer Willer Tomaz and betting companies3.

Flávio Bolsonaro said federal police gave him the travel ban order in Brasília3. Police served the order outside the home of his father, Jair Bolsonaro3. Former President Jair Bolsonaro is currently under house arrest3. He is serving a 27-year prison sentence1. He was convicted for plotting a coup after his 2022 election loss1.

The election result was unexpected1. After the result, the São Paulo stock exchange opened 15% lower1. The stock exchange later partially recovered1.

Student List movement wins majority in Serbia parliamentary election

The opposition Student List movement won Serbia's parliamentary election with 52.45% of the vote1. The Student List won 138 seats in the National Assembly1. The Aleksandar Vučić – United Serbia list received 39.2% of the vote1. That list secured 103 seats1.

Outgoing President Aleksandar Vučić conceded defeat late on Sunday2. His concession ended 14 years of rule by his Serbian Progressive Party2. Vučić alleged the election result verified a revolution supported by European countries and the US deep state3.

The Student List movement formed after a railway station canopy collapsed in Novi Sad on November 1, 20241. That collapse killed 16 people1.

Voter turnout reached 69% as over 4.4 million citizens voted1. Turnout was 55.5% in December 20231.

The Student List trained at least 16,000 official controllers and mobile teams1. They monitored for fraud like carousel voting and parallel lists1.

The movement is leaderless and has no hierarchy2. Its candidates include academics, doctors, actors, and farmers without prior political experience2. Prominent figures include cardiologist Ilija Srdanović, former central bank governor Dejan Šoškić, and chemistry professor Radomir Saičić2.

European Commissioner for Enlargement Marta Kos said a new chapter can begin for Serbia4. She said the European Union door is open4. Kremlin spokesperson Dmitry Peskov said Russia hopes Serbia's new leadership sees cooperation with Russia as beneficial2.

Israel prepares for national election as West Bank displacements and regional tensions rise

Israel will hold a parliamentary election on October 27 to choose 120 members of the Knesset1. A party or coalition needs 61 seats to establish a governing coalition1. Polls show Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's far-right coalition trailing an opposition bloc2. Retired general Gadi Eisenkot leads that opposition bloc2. Neither side is projected to reach 61 seats2.

Netanyahu posted an AI-generated image of a nuclear cloud destroying Tel Aviv2. He warned voters to elect him or face nuclear destruction from Iran2. Israeli media reported a clash between Netanyahu and IDF Chief of Staff Lt Gen Eyal Zamir2. Zamir requested a written military directive concerning Iran2. Eisenkot said he would not allow the military to become a pawn for Netanyahu's political survival2.

Reports indicated Netanyahu tried to persuade US President Donald Trump to attack Iran2. But Trump ruled out action until after the US midterm elections2.

Israeli forces detained an international delegation of 23 people at the border for nine hours3. The group included UK MP Clive Lewis and Scottish MP Lara Bird3. Israeli forces barred them from entering the West Bank3.

UN rights chief Volker Türk warned of ethnic cleansing after Israeli forces displaced over 3,000 Palestinians in Hebron4. The displacement followed a checkpoint attack where two Palestinian gunmen killed two Israeli soldiers4. The UN aid office recorded at least 160 Israeli settler attacks linked to the olive harvest in early October3.

Jordan launches airstrikes in Syria and arrests suspects after church attack

Jordanian security forces arrested three men following a shootout in the city of Karak1. The shootout occurred at an abandoned house at dawn1. Security forces arrested brothers Hazzah Zaidan Mohammad and Mishal Zaidan Mohammad, alongside Ahmad Sami Hussein1. All three suspects were wounded in the shootout1. Authorities seized a large cache of munitions1.

The arrests followed an attack during a funeral at St George's Church in Hammoud village1. Nine people died and over two dozen were wounded in that Friday attack1. Public Security chief Maj Gen Obaidallah Abd Rabbo Al-Maayta described the attackers as extremist fighters acting independently1. Jordanian Interior Minister Mazen al-Faraya said all suspects were Jordanians with no foreign collaboration2.

King Abdullah II declared three days of national mourning1. He vowed to respond with an iron fist against terrorist ideologies1.

The Jordanian air force launched retaliatory airstrikes against armed groups in southern Syria1. The health director in Syria's Daraa province reported that at least five civilians died in those strikes1. But Syria's SANA news agency quoted the same official saying at least eight civilians were killed2.

Eight church attack victims were buried beside the church on Sunday2. The ninth victim was buried in south Amman on Monday2.

Election Commission defends voter roll revision process in Supreme Court

The Election Commission of India filed an affidavit in the Supreme Court to defend voter roll revisions1. Deputy Election Commissioner Bhanu Prakash Yeturu filed the affidavit1. He declared that the Chief Election Commissioner and two Election Commissioners unanimously decided on June 24, 2025, to conduct a nationwide voter roll revision1.

Opposition leader Rahul Gandhi and others filed petitions challenging the process1. Their challenge followed a news report alleging differences among the commissioners1. The Indian Express reported that Commissioners Sukhbir Singh Sandhu and Vivek Joshi objected to decisions 14 times2.

The Election Commission stated that letters sent by Sandhu and Joshi pertained to internal work allocation1. An internal order issued on July 28 was never implemented1.

The commission denied amending Form 6 in its revision orders1. It noted that only the Central Government can amend Form 61. The new process requires voters to link their details with parents or grandparents in earlier rolls2.

Karnataka's final electoral roll will be published on October 273. The state's voter count dropped from 5.54 crore to 4.54 crore after 1.07 crore names were deleted3. Opposition parties protested to demand the removal of Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar4.

Andhra Pradesh and Odisha prepare emergency response as Cyclone Arnab approaches

The Andhra Pradesh government released ₹27 crore to 20 districts to prepare for Cyclone Arnab1. The money will support relief operations ahead of the cyclone's expected landfall on October 281.

The India Meteorological Department forecast that Cyclone Arnab will cross the coast between Bapatla and Visakhapatnam1. The cyclone will also affect the Yanam coast1. Winds are expected to reach sustained speeds of 115 to 125 kilometers per hour1. Wind gusts could reach up to 140 kilometers per hour1.

At 5:30 am on October 26, Cyclone Arnab was 590 kilometers northwest of Sri Vijaya Puram2. It was 610 kilometers southeast of Gopalpur2. The storm was also 650 kilometers east-southeast of Visakhapatnam2.

Officials in Andhra Pradesh scheduled 4,711 pregnant women to move to safer healthcare facilities1. A total of 30,043 fishing boats returned to harbours across the state1.

The Andhra Pradesh Department of Health prepared 6,431 emergency beds for the storm3. The department readied 630 medical teams and 716 vehicles3. Health authorities also stocked 13,101 doses of anti-snake venom across facilities3.

The Real Time Governance Society urged residents to report emergencies through WhatsApp3. Residents can use the Mana Mitra platform for cyclone help3.

State Disaster Management Authority Director Prakhar Jain reported that 7 NDRF teams and 8 SDRF teams deployed to vulnerable districts4. Fifteen NDRF teams deployed across vulnerable locations in Andhra Pradesh and Puducherry's Yanam district5.

In New Delhi, Cabinet Secretary T V Somanathan chaired a National Crisis Management Committee meeting5. The committee reviewed cyclone preparedness across several states5.

The Andaman and Nicobar Islands ordered all schools to close until Tuesday5. The Odisha government ordered schools, colleges, and Anganwadi centres to close on October 28 and 29 in six southern districts5.

Odisha Special Relief Commissioner Rajesh Prabhakar Patil said 135 cyclone shelters were prepared4. The state also prepared 1,600 temporary accommodation centres4. Odisha Chief Minister Mohan Majhi assigned eight state ministers to monitor preparations in different locations4.
