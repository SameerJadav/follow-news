EU calls for border action after mass migrant crossings in Ceuta

European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen has called for united action on border security following mass migrant crossings into Ceuta1. Ceuta and Melilla represent the European Union's only land borders with Africa2.

Spain estimates that approximately 69,500 migrants have returned to Morocco after entering Ceuta1. However, Ceuta's top government official Juan Jesus Vivas estimated that between 3,000 and 5,000 migrants remained in the city after initial returns3. Following the crossings, Spanish officials began installing a 500-metre floating barrier at the border with Morocco2.

Casualties have been reported on both sides of the border. The official death toll stands at 72 on the Spanish side and 11 on the Moroccan side1. Meanwhile, the Moroccan Association for Human Rights reported nearly 130 victims and dozens missing from the border rush4.

The crisis has caused political reactions across Europe. Italy temporarily suspended the Schengen border-free arrangement with Spain following the crossings1. Additionally, Italy and Denmark organised a letter signed by 22 EU member states criticizing Spain's regularisation of undocumented migrants2.

In contrast, Iranian foreign ministry spokesman Esmaeil Baqaei explicitly denied direct talks with Washington1. The conflict comes during an active war between the United States and Iran, which began on February 28, 2026, following surprise strikes by the United States and Israel1. Following Tehran's public denial of direct discussions, Donald Trump called the Iranian leadership unbelievably duplicitous on Truth Social for denying negotiations2.

At the same time, Donald Trump stated that he called off planned military strikes on Iran3. According to Trump, he made this decision at the request of Saudi Arabia, the United Arab Emirates, and Qatar3.

Brent Crude oil fell by 4.5% to $83.98 per barrel following announcements regarding potential negotiations3. Meanwhile, shipping through the region has dropped sharply. Europe heatwave and low river levels strain energy output and force evacuations

Severe weather conditions are creating energy and safety challenges across Europe. England and Wales recorded their driest July in nearly 200 years of recordkeeping, according to the Met Office1. Water levels on the River Rhine dropped to historic record lows at Cologne in Germany and Lobith in the Netherlands2.

Low water levels on the Danube River have severely affected power generation. Hungary reduced output at the Paks nuclear power plant to little more than 10% capacity due to low Danube water levels3. In response, the Romanian military detonated a rocky outcrop in the Danube to direct cooling water flow to the Cernavoda nuclear power plant3. Romanian naval forces used 180 kg of explosives in a controlled underwater explosion to restore water flow near Cernavoda4. Due to the national energy crisis, carmakers Dacia and Ford agreed to suspend automotive production in Romania until August 193.

Wildfires and firefighting operations have also turned deadly. In France, authorities evacuated over 224,000 people due to a massive wildfire burning 420 square kilometres in the country's largest civilian evacuation outside wartime4. West of Athens, two crew members died in a midair helicopter collision during firefighting operations1.

US and Japan perform joint currency intervention to support the yen

Japan and the United States have executed a joint currency intervention to strengthen the Japanese yen1. Following the intervention, the Japanese yen strengthened to 155 to the US dollar1. Prior to the market intervention, the Japanese yen had fallen to a 40-year low of nearly 164 to the US dollar1.

Bank of Japan data indicated that Tokyo spent as much as $36.58 billion on Friday to support the yen1. In Washington, US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent noted on his cabinet meeting pad a task to buy $5 billion to $10 billion worth of Japanese yen1.

This market intervention was the first joint foreign exchange action conducted by the United States and Japan since March 20112. At the time of the action, the Bank of Japan maintained its main interest rate at 1% while the US Federal Reserve's benchmark rate stood between 3.50% and 3.75%2.

Prashant Kishor's Jan Suraaj wins Bihar byelection in Bankipur

Jan Suraaj Party founder Prashant Kishor has won the Bankipur Assembly byelection in Bihar, defeating BJP candidate Neeraj Kumar Sinha by 19,324 votes1. The victory ends a long political tenure, as the BJP had held the Bankipur Assembly seat in Bihar for over three decades since 1995 prior to Prashant Kishor's victory2. The Bankipur seat became vacant after BJP leader Nitin Nabin resigned upon his election to the Rajya Sabha and elevation to BJP national president3.

In the Bankipur race, independent candidates named Lalu Prasad Yadav and Nitish Kumar received 81 and 149 votes respectively, losing their security deposits4.

Results were also announced for assembly byelections in other Indian states. In Gujarat, BJP candidate Satish Govindbhai Patel won the Manjalpur Assembly seat by a margin of 30,630 votes1. Meanwhile, in Madhya Pradesh, Congress candidate Ghanshyam Singh won the Datia Assembly byelection by 6,016 votes1.

Delhi court acquits former wrestling chief Brij Bhushan Sharan Singh in harassment case

A New Delhi court has acquitted former Wrestling Federation of India chief Brij Bhushan Sharan Singh in a sexual harassment case filed by six female wrestlers1. In the same ruling, former Wrestling Federation of India assistant secretary Vinod Tomar was acquitted of criminal intimidation charges by Additional Chief Judicial Magistrate Ashwini Panwar2.

The verdict follows years of legal proceedings and public protests. Delhi Police registered two FIRs against Brij Bhushan Sharan Singh in April 2023 following complaints by female wrestlers3. Prominent wrestlers including Vinesh Phogat, Sakshi Malik, and Bajrang Punia launched a sit-in protest against Singh at Jantar Mantar in April 20233. In May 2025, a Delhi court accepted a police closure report in a POCSO case against Singh after a minor complainant withdrew her allegations3.

Following the court's verdict, wrestler Vinesh Phogat stated that the complainants will appeal Brij Bhushan Sharan Singh's acquittal in a higher court1.
