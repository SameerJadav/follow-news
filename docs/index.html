Donald Trump Vows to Claim Strait of Hormuz as US Territory Amid Escalating War

United States President Donald Trump has stated at a rally in Garden City, New York, that he will soon declare the Strait of Hormuz as US territory1. Speaking to attendees, Trump claimed that no ships can pass through the US blockade of the strategic waterway unless the United States wants them to1. His declaration comes amid an escalating conflict in the region1.

Iran immediately rejected the American president's claims regarding the critical shipping route1. Iranian Deputy Foreign Minister Kazem Gharibabadi stated that the Strait of Hormuz will remain under Iranian control1. Furthermore, Iranian officials declared that Tehran will not reopen the waterway unless the United States meets several conditions2. These demands include lifting economic sanctions, paying compensation for war damage, and releasing frozen Iranian assets2.

The dispute follows the outbreak of military conflict earlier this year1. The United States and Israel launched a war against Iran on February 281. Before the conflict began, the Strait of Hormuz served as a vital global energy route, with one-fifth of the world's oil and liquefied natural gas passing through it1.

Efforts to manage the war have faced serious challenges3. In June, the United States and Iran signed a memorandum of understanding to halt military operations during the war3. However, violence around the strait has continued4. Drone attacks recently struck two oil tankers operated by Abu Dhabi's state-owned oil company, ADNOC, inside the Strait of Hormuz4. Following the strikes, the United Arab Emirates accused Iran of committing piracy4.

Public support for the military action appears low in the United States3. A Quinnipiac University poll released in late July found that 60 percent of US voters oppose American military action against Iran3.

Luigi Mangione has pleaded guilty in federal court to two charges of stalking UnitedHealthcare CEO Brian Thompson with the intent to kill him1. Mangione entered the plea without a plea deal1. Speaking in court, Mangione stated: 'I shot Mr. Thompson in Manhattan and he died'1.

Thompson was shot and killed on December 4, 2024, outside a Midtown Manhattan hotel that was hosting an investor conference1. Mangione was arrested five days after the shooting at a McDonald's restaurant in Altoona, Pennsylvania2.

Federal prosecutors stated they will seek a sentence of life in prison at Mangione's sentencing scheduled for December 181. The legal proceedings follow complex courtroom rulings1. In September 2025, State Judge Gregory Carro dismissed state terrorism charges2. Later, in January 2026, US District Judge Margaret Garnett dismissed federal murder and weapons charges on legal technicalities1. Mangione's lawyers have now filed a motion to dismiss separate New York state murder and weapons charges, citing double jeopardy principles1.

Wildfires Fuel Mass Evacuations Across Europe Amid Extreme Heat

Massive wildfires driven by heatwaves and extreme drought have burned approximately 500,000 hectares of land across Europe1. The fires have threatened towns and forced thousands of people to flee their homes in multiple countries2.

In Croatia, a fire near Omis killed one person, injured 40 others, and forced 1,200 people to evacuate2. Emergency crews in Greece used boats to evacuate more than 300 people from the coastal town of Siviri as flames approached2. In France, authorities evacuated 525 people from the village of Luglon in the Landes region2. French officials have also detained 474 people, including 183 minors, on suspicion of starting wildfires since July 14.

In Spain, a wildfire in Huelva burned 31,000 hectares and required the evacuation of 700 residents2. A Spanish army unit was deployed to remove the historical remains of three 11th-century Aragonese kings from the San Juan de la Peña monastery to protect them from the advancing blaze4. Meanwhile, in western Germany, more than 2,000 people were evacuated from the village of Gey as a forest fire moved toward the area3.

Trump Administration Asks Supreme Court to Allow White House Ballroom Construction

The Trump administration has submitted an emergency petition to the US Supreme Court seeking permission to continue construction on a $400 million White House ballroom1. The emergency legal request comes after a federal appeals court stepped in to pause the project1.

On August 7, the US Court of Appeals for the DC Circuit ruled 2-1 to halt above-ground construction of the ballroom, stating that the project required congressional approval1. The original lawsuit against the construction was initiated by the National Trust for Historic Preservation after the White House East Wing was demolished1.

In response to the administration's emergency petition, Chief Justice John Roberts ordered the plaintiffs challenging the ballroom project to file a formal response by Tuesday1. In a Supreme Court filing, US Solicitor General D. John Sauer stated that construction on the project is already 65 percent complete2. Sauer added that a crew of 250 workers is currently working on the ballroom 20 hours a day, seven days a week3.

American Missionary Kevin Rideout Freed After Nine Months of Captivity in Niger

American missionary pilot Kevin Rideout has been released after being held captive in Niger for more than nine months1. Rideout was abducted in the capital city of Niamey on October 21 by three armed individuals1. Before his kidnapping, Rideout had worked as a missionary in Niger for 19 years1.

Following his release, the Christian non-governmental organization SIM International announced that Rideout is in good health and currently under the care of US officials2. Reports also indicate that he was transferred to FBI custody after being freed3.

President Droupadi Murmu Addresses Nation on Eve of India's 80th Independence Day

President Droupadi Murmu addressed the nation on the eve of India's 80th Independence Day1. During her formal address, the full version of the national song Vande Mataram was played both before and after her speech1.

In her message, President Murmu highlighted efforts to reform public testing systems1. She stated that the government is taking comprehensive steps to reform the public examination system in order to eliminate unfair practices1. The address comes after the Centre enacted the Public Examinations (Prevention of Unfair Means) Amendment Act, 2026, on July 312. The law imposes strict penalties for individual exam cheating, including up to 10 years of imprisonment and a fine of ₹50 lakh2.

The national celebrations will continue with major events led by top leaders3. Prime Minister Narendra Modi will lead the 80th Independence Day celebrations from the iconic Red Fort3.

As part of the holiday honors, several police officers across the country were recognized for their service4. Twenty Tamil Nadu police personnel were awarded the President's Medal for Distinguished Service and the Medal for Meritorious Service4. Additionally, seventeen Delhi Police personnel were named to receive Independence Day service medals5.

Tensions Rise Between India and Pakistan Over Indus Waters Treaty Remarks

A diplomatic dispute has broken out after Pakistan Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif warned that Pakistan's water rights under the Indus Waters Treaty represent a 'red line'1.

Jammu and Kashmir Chief Minister Omar Abdullah strongly criticized Sharif's remarks, stating that the Indus Waters Treaty has 'sucked our blood'1. Meanwhile, Ministry of External Affairs spokesperson Randhir Jaiswal stated that India's position on the treaty remains completely unchanged2.

The dispute follows a severe strain in bilateral relations last year3. India placed the 1960 Indus Waters Treaty in abeyance following an April 22, 2025 terror attack in Pahalgam that killed 26 people3. In response to that attack, India launched Operation Sindoor, targeting terror infrastructure located in Pakistan and Pakistan-occupied Jammu and Kashmir3.

India Notifies 40 Questions for Census 2027 Phase 2 Including Caste Data

The Indian government has notified 40 questions for Phase 2 of Census 20271. Phase 2 population enumeration will begin on August 17 in Ladakh as well as snow-bound areas of Jammu and Kashmir, Uttarakhand, and Himachal Pradesh1.

Under the notified rules, Phase 2 of Census 2027 will record caste as declared by the respondent1. This marks the first time in independent India that caste across all communities will be enumerated2. India last conducted a comprehensive population count of all caste groups in 19312.

Along with caste, the Census 2027 questionnaire includes several new fields1. Enumerators will collect data on the number of bank accounts, place of Covid-19 vaccination, mobile phone number, Aadhaar number, and voter card number1.
