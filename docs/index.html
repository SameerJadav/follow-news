Houthi missile strike on Riyadh airport kills 12 and injures hundreds

Houthi forces struck King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh with a missile on Saturday1. At least 12 people died in the attack1. Over 300 people suffered injuries at the airport1. Saudi Arabia's General Authority of Civil Aviation reported that 309 people were injured1. The aviation authority said some of the injured are in critical condition1.

Four Saudi citizens and two Bangladeshis died in the strike1. Citizens from the United States, Egypt, Jordan, Syria, Palestine, and Sudan were also killed1. An unnamed regional official said a missile hit a terminal complex2. The complex houses terminals 3 and 4 for domestic flights2. Regional officials said earlier Houthi attacks on the airport on Thursday damaged three planes2.

Flight operations at the Riyadh airport were temporarily stopped after the attack1. Planes flying to Riyadh were sent to other airports1. FlightAware reported that at least 225 flights were cancelled over 24 hours1. The US and UK embassies in Riyadh warned their citizens to stay away1. They told citizens to avoid the airport and the surrounding area1.

Another strike occurred in eastern Saudi Arabia3. A military coalition fighting the Houthis said a projectile fell near King Fahd International Airport in Dammam3.

The Houthis warned airline companies to stop flying in Saudi airspace4. They said any plane in Saudi airspace except over Mecca and Medina is a target4. The Houthis also warned Pakistan against fighting them for Saudi Arabia4. They said they would treat Pakistan like Saudi Arabia if Pakistan gets involved4.

US President Donald Trump spoke to reporters at the White House4. Reporters asked if the United States would join strikes against the Houthis4. Trump said he will make a decision soon4. Regional officials told the AP last month that Saudi Arabia was running low on missile interceptors2. Saudi Arabia asked France, Britain, Pakistan, and Egypt for air defense help2.

Earlier strikes hit King Khalid International Airport and Abha International Airport on October 6 and 71. The aviation authority said those earlier attacks killed three people1. Those attacks also injured 36 people1.

In Yemen, pro-government forces secured the port of Mokha3. Mokha sits on the Bab al-Mandab Strait near the Red Sea3. Spokesman Colonel Majed Abdullah al-Nazili gave details about the fighting4. He said government forces killed at least 1,150 Houthis in operation 'Dawn of Yemen'4. Forces completed almost 2,000 targeted operations during that mission4.

Trump claimed Ukraine lost 30 percent of its land and 2 million people under Zelensky2. Ukrainian Foreign Minister Andriy Sybiga defended Zelensky2. Union Home Secretary Govind Mohan approved internet cuts near eight temporary jail sites3. The Delhi government converted four sports stadiums into temporary jails3. The Supreme Court said on Friday that authorities cannot use a sledgehammer to kill a fly2.
