US and Iran Exchange Military Strikes as Houthis Target Saudi Arabia

Daily military strikes between the United States and Iran have resumed after a fragile ceasefire collapsed earlier in July 2026. Prior to that, the two nations had signed a 14-point Memorandum of Understanding in June to halt military operations and reopen the Strait of Hormuz. However, fighting escalated again as the US military carried out 13 consecutive nights of attacks on Iran prior to Saturday. During the conflict, US Navy Captain Tim Hawkins stated that American forces disabled a tanker ship in the Gulf of Oman that was trying to evade a blockade on Iranian ports. In response to the strikes, US Secretary of State Marco Rubio said that pressure on Tehran would continue until Iran changes its actions. Meanwhile, US Secretary of Defense Pete Hegseth stated that the cost of the ongoing conflict with Iran has reached $37.5 billion. The economic fallout from the war has also hit global energy markets, where Brent crude oil price rose roughly 27% in two weeks to close at $96.78 a barrel on Friday.

The conflict has also drawn in regional allies. Yemen's Iran-backed Houthi rebels announced an immediate maritime blockade against Saudi Arabia in the Bab al-Mandeb Strait. Ansarollah media reported on Telegram early Saturday that a Yemeni missile strike caused fires in the Saudi city of Jizan. Saudi authorities issued emergency warnings for Jazan and Yanbu provinces around 6:10 a.m. local time on Saturday. On Friday, Saudi Arabia launched air strikes on Houthi targets in the Yemeni port city of Hodeida and a nearby island.

At the same time, controversy has grown inside the United States over how the military reports casualties. The total number of US troops killed in the war on Iran rose to 18, while roughly 430 US service members have been injured. The Pentagon stated on Monday that 100 service members had been injured since July 7, but 96% of them had returned to duty. Former defense secretary and CIA director Leon Panetta criticized the Pentagon, calling its handling of casualty reports a shameful approach. Public information has been restricted, as the US Defence Department has not held a press briefing for two-and-a-half months. In October, most news organizations turned in their badges and walked out of the Pentagon over new rules requiring pre-approval from Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth. Questions about data accuracy also arose when a website update lowered the official death count from 18 to 14. Pentagon spokesperson Sean Parnell blamed this temporary change on a data disruption. Media claims have also added to the confusion, such as a video posted on X by the account Iran Army Media on July 19 claiming an Iranian missile shot down an American B-52 bomber. However, fact-checking group Lead Stories reported that the viral video was likely created using a military flight simulator or a video game.

Uncontrolled Wildfires Cause Mass Evacuations in France and Spain

Massive wildfires burning out of control across Southern Europe have forced more than 250,000 people to evacuate their homes in France and Spain. The Spanish government declared a national emergency as blazes spread rapidly. Near Madrid, 25,000 additional residents were evacuated from nearby villages, bringing the total number of people evacuated in the capital region to 70,000. Spain's interior ministry reported that wildfires burned nearly 10,000 hectares in the Madrid region and between 13,000 and 15,000 hectares in Ávila. In Ávila, the Spanish government ordered the complete evacuation of the Tiétar Valley, affecting 30,000 people. Emergency management chief Carlos Novillo stated that the wildfire near Madrid reached its peak and went beyond the capacity of firefighters to contain.

In France, Interior Minister Laurent Nunez stated that wildfires nationwide have burned nearly 98,000 hectares. According to EFFIS data, 69,473 hectares have burned in France since the start of the year, marking a record high for a single year. The French government stated that 197,000 people were evacuated near Bordeaux. Another source noted that wildfires in southwest France forced around 200,000 people to leave. About 44,000 people fled the Cap Ferret peninsula, where over 50 homes were destroyed. European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen sent 5 planes and 2 helicopters from the rescEU fleet to assist France. Meanwhile, a wildfire in Scotland's Cairngorms national park has been burning for 11 days.

Russian Missile Attack on Defense Event Near Kyiv Kills 10

A Russian missile strike on a venue hosting a defense industry event near Kyiv killed at least 10 people and wounded nearly 100 others. According to Ukrainian air force spokesman Yuri Ihnat, Russian forces launched three ballistic missiles in the attack, with Ukrainian defenses intercepting only one. Acting regional military administration head Ruslan Oliinyk stated that the strike hit a private military training site in the Bucha district, located 20 to 30 kilometers from central Kyiv. Elsewhere in Ukraine, Russian glide bombs killed five people in the city of Sloviansk. EU foreign policy chief Kaja Kallas reported that the attack in Sloviansk hit Latvia's Honorary Consulate. In the Sumy region, a Russian drone struck a facility belonging to the postal service Nova Poshta, killing three drivers. In the Chernihiv region, energy company Ukrenergo stated that an attack on an energy facility left around 150,000 people without power.

Cross-border strikes and border incidents also occurred. A Ukrainian strike killed six people and wounded 26 in the Russian city of Kirov. Ukrainian forces also attacked warehouses belonging to Wildberries, Russia's largest online retailer, in St Petersburg. In Russian-held Zaporizhia, regional governor Yevgeny Balitsky stated that a suspected Ukrainian attack on holiday camps killed at least eight people, including two children. In neighboring Romania, President Nicusor Dan stated that a Romanian F-16 pilot shot down a drone that breached Romanian airspace near the border.

Violent Clashes Between Israeli Settlers and Palestinians Leave Six Dead

Deadly clashes broke out between Israeli settlers and Palestinian residents in the West Bank village of Tell, also reported as Tal [c45, c50]. The Israeli military stated that the violence began when a Palestinian villager seized a weapon from an Israeli and shot one settler dead. The resulting exchange of gunfire left four Palestinians and a second Israeli dead [c46, c50]. The Israeli military also reported that a 27-year-old Israeli soldier fell during operational activity in the area. Following the shooting, Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu and Defence Minister Israel Katz confirmed that the shooter's home in Tal would be demolished. Israeli forces later arrested nearly 50 Palestinians and raided about 70 homes in the village.

Other violent incidents were reported across the region. Farata local council head Abdel Moneim Shanneh said settlers set fire to olive groves and soldiers shot at villagers trying to put out the flames. In another incident, a family rescued a baby from a house set on fire by settlers following a shootout. Palestinians in Tell held a funeral early Saturday for the four killed villagers, though the Israeli army limited attendance to around 30 people. Netanyahu ordered the setup of new settler outposts and accelerated legal approval for farm outposts. UN Special Rapporteur Francesca Albanese called settler attacks on Palestinian civilians pogroms and requested an immediate arms embargo on Israel. Palestinian Health Ministry data shows Israeli soldiers or settlers have killed at least 1,094 Palestinians since the Gaza war began, including 87 killed in the West Bank in 2026.

International Criminal Court Members Vote to Remove Chief Prosecutor Karim Khan

Member states of the International Criminal Court voted by a large majority to remove chief prosecutor Karim Khan from his post following sexual misconduct allegations. Assembly of States Parties president Päivi Kaukoranta announced that Khan had committed serious misconduct and a serious breach of duty. During a Special Session of the Assembly of States Parties in New York, the decision to remove Khan passed with 82 votes in favor. This vote makes Khan the first chief prosecutor ever to be removed from office in the court's history. An internal investigation conducted by the UN Office of Internal Oversight Services ran from November 2024 to December 2025, generating over 5,000 pages of evidence. Khan was unable to attend the vote at UN headquarters because Trump administration sanctions bar him from entering the United States. Khan's lawyer stated that he will challenge the lawfulness and fairness of the removal through all legal mechanisms.

International reactions followed the vote. US Secretary of State Marco Rubio announced that the United States is launching a campaign to dismantle the court. Foreign Affairs Minister Félix Plasencia announced that Venezuela will withdraw from the court entirely. Meanwhile, Israel's UN ambassador Danny Danon claimed Khan launched a political witch hunt against Israel to hide the misconduct allegations.

Zelensky Faces Political Crisis After Dismissing Ukrainian Defense Minister

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky is facing a growing political crisis following his decision on July 15 to remove Mykhailo Fedorov as defense minister during a cabinet reshuffle. In response to the dismissal, protesters gathered in Kyiv to announce an open-ended rally demanding that Zelensky reappoint Fedorov to his post. Meanwhile, Ukrainian MP Oleksandr Merezhko stated that Fedorov's public approval rating soared after he was removed from office.

Fedorov revealed that he turned down offers from Zelensky for other cabinet positions. Explaining his decision, Fedorov stated that there were only three positions worth having to influence the course of the war: minister of defense, commander-in-chief of the military, or president of Ukraine. Alongside the cabinet changes, President Zelensky appointed 43-year-old Maj Gen Mykhailo Drapatyi as the new commander-in-chief of Ukraine's military. Drapatyi replaces Oleksandr Syrskyi in the top military position.

SpaceX Starship Successfully Completes Test Flight and Indian Ocean Splashdown

SpaceX launched its 13th Starship test flight from Texas on Friday evening after being rescheduled twice earlier this month. The 122-metre-tall rocket blasted off at around 6:50 p.m. Eastern Time from the Starbase facility [c71, c76]. During the flight, Starship deployed 20 upgraded Starlink V3 internet satellites into suborbital space. Starship successfully completed its test flight and landed intact in the Indian Ocean. SpaceX head Elon Musk confirmed the result on X, posting that Starship was floating in the ocean and transmitting telemetry data.

However, the mission experienced a minor issue during landing preparations. Five engines on the Super Heavy booster failed to reignite during descent, causing the booster to hit the Gulf of Mexico with more force than intended. Despite that issue, officials praised the overall mission. NASA Administrator Jared Isaacman stated that Starship's capabilities will be game-changing and ensure humanity never gives up the Moon again. SpaceX plans to use Starship to expand its satellite network with V3 satellites by the end of the year, which will allow standard cell phones to connect directly to satellite service.

Trump Threatens EU With Tariffs Over Fines Imposed on US Tech Companies

US President Donald Trump has threatened the European Union with steep tariffs and an official trade investigation after Brussels fined Google $1 billion over antitrust violations. The European Commission imposed the penalty because Google breached the Digital Markets Act by favoring its own services in search results and placing restrictions on app developers. European Commission Executive Vice President Teresa Ribera described the decision as decisive yet balanced. In contrast, Google executive Kent Walker criticized the ruling as product degradation driven by a small group of complainants. Separate EU court rulings recently upheld a previous fine of over $4 billion against Google following an eight-year review.

President Trump strongly condemned the EU's actions on social media, writing that financial penalties against US technology firms are highly unethical and that the US is not a piggybank for Europe. Trump posted on Truth Social that the European Union will pay a very big price and anticipated placing a substantial tariff on the trade bloc. Trump claimed that Apple received $15 billion in EU fines, Meta received $3 billion, and Amazon received $2.5 billion. He announced that the US will immediately start a Section 301 investigation into European regulators.

Education Minister Dharmendra Pradhan Resigns After Nationwide Student Protests

India's Union Education Minister Dharmendra Pradhan resigned from his post on Saturday following weeks of intense nationwide protests over alleged paper leaks and irregularities in competitive examinations [c88, c89]. Pradhan stated that he submitted his resignation to Prime Minister Narendra Modi to prevent anti-national forces from taking advantage of the ongoing student demonstrations. Following the announcement, student protesters gathered at Jantar Mantar to celebrate the minister's exit. Abhijeet Dipke, founder of the Cockroach Janata Party which helped lead the student movement, called Pradhan's resignation a victory for democracy and a victory for the Constitution of India. Dipke's group announced that it was calling off its agitation in good faith after the minister stepped down and key terms were agreed upon.

The resignation comes after weeks of growing anger and public demonstrations across the country. On Monday, a march toward Parliament turned violent when Delhi Police used lathis, tear gas, and pellet guns against demonstrators, injuring dozens of young people. Activist Sonam Wangchuk also joined the movement for accountability, holding a 26-day hunger strike to protest against the examination leaks. Tragedy also surrounded the crisis, prompting student leader Dipke to demand one crore rupees in financial compensation for families of students who committed suicide due to exam irregularities.

In response to the crisis, the government announced significant administrative changes and concessions. Before stepping down, Pradhan announced that future NEET examinations will be conducted as Computer-Based Tests to increase transparency and prevent leaks. Union Minister J.P. Nadda later confirmed that the government will take official steps to withdraw police FIRs filed against peaceful protesters. Nadda also confirmed that the government will provide financial compensation to the families of NEET suicide victims.
