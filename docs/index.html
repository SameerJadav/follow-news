US holds off on planned Iran strikes amid talks on nuclear deal and shipping routes

United States President Donald Trump posted on Truth Social on Saturday that he cancelled planned military strikes against Iran1. He stated that calling off the attacks was contingent on rapidly reaching a deal regarding Iran's nuclear programme and reopening the Strait of Hormuz1.

However, Iranian officials and news outlets challenged the US narrative. The Iranian news agency Mehr stated on Sunday that Donald Trump's assertion that Tehran requested a pause on strikes was a lie1. Iran's acting defence minister Majid Ebn-e-Reza posted on X that Iranian forces would remain on alert and would not be taken by surprise1. In addition, Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi informed Saudi Foreign Minister Prince Faisal bin Farhan that any military attacks by the US or Israel would be met with a proportionate response2.

Despite the tense statements, diplomatic discussions appear to be making progress through regional mediators. Hassan Ghashghavi, spokesperson for the Iranian parliament's national security commission, stated that mediators were attempting to revive a June agreement brokered by Pakistan1. On Telegram, Foreign Minister Araghchi posted that negotiations between Tehran and Muscat over the Strait of Hormuz are entering their final stages3. Iranian Foreign Ministry spokesman Esmail Baghaei also stated that Iran and Oman were close to an understanding on a new maritime route through the Strait of Hormuz3. Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian posted on X that a memorandum of understanding with the US would become the cornerstone of Tehran's foreign relations3. Regional leaders have also engaged directly; Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman spoke with Donald Trump by phone on Saturday to urge de-escalation regarding Iran1.

The sudden shift follows recent military actions and caught key allies unawares. On February 28, the US and Israel launched strikes aimed at destroying Iran's missile programme3. Channel 12 reported that Israeli officials and US Central Command first learned of Donald Trump's decision to call off the new strikes via his Truth Social post2.

The situation remains critical for international trade. Approximately 20 percent of global oil consumption normally passes through the Strait of Hormuz3. Maritime trade tracking firm Kpler reported on Friday that vessel traffic through the Strait of Hormuz had dropped sharply4.

Meanwhile, conflict persists across the wider region. Israeli airstrikes on Gaza on Sunday killed at least 18 Palestinians across Gaza City, Deir al-Balah, and Khan Younis1.

Two dead in helicopter collision during wildfire firefighting efforts in Greece

A mid-air collision between two leased Bell helicopters during firefighting operations in Greece killed a Danish pilot and a Greek fire service liaison officer on Sunday1. A British pilot and a Greek coordinator survived the crash, which occurred near the Attica-Boeotia border1 during water-dropping operations near Psatha in western Attica2.

Following the fatal accident, authorities grounded all Bell helicopters across Greece1. Both aircraft involved had been leased from Australia's McDermott Aviation3. Greek Prime Minister Kyriakos Mitsotakis expressed his deepest sorrow on X over the loss of the Greek coordinator and Danish pilot1.

The collision took place as over 500 firefighters fought blazes for 72 hours across regions including Porto Germeno, Psatha, Fokida, Megara, and Kefalonia1. More than 100 homes were destroyed overnight around the coastal village of Porto Germeno, located about 45 miles northwest of Athens3. A new wildfire also broke out overnight on the Ionian island of Kefalonia, prompting evacuations in the Pastra area2. These events follow the deaths of three Greek firefighters last week from injuries sustained battling wildfires3. Elsewhere, a mega-fire in Gironde, France, was contained after forcing the evacuation of 224,000 people3.

Sergey Goncharov, a veteran of Russia's Alpha counter-terrorism unit, suggested the female carrier might have been an unwitting courier delivered money to carry the package4.

Massive migrant crossing in Ceuta triggers political fallout across Europe

A major surge of migrants into the Spanish enclave of Ceuta has sparked political reactions across Europe1. Around 60,000 migrants crossed the border from Morocco into Ceuta over several days starting Thursday2.

The surge resulted in tragic loss of life. At least 72 people died attempting to cross into Ceuta, drowning or being crushed near the border fence1. Morocco's Interior Ministry reported 11 deaths, mostly from drowning2, while Ceuta leader Juan Jesus Vivas stated the city's morgue received 88 corpses, including victims from earlier crossing attempts2. On Saturday, Spanish police installed a 500-metre floating barrier off the border1. Spanish Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez assured British Prime Minister Andy Burnham that roughly 98% of the estimated 50,000 migrants who entered Ceuta had been returned to Morocco1. Burnham pledged to be relentless in tackling small boat Channel crossings following the crisis1.

Political tension heightened in response to the crisis. Far-right European Parliament member Alvise Pérez was confronted by counterprotesters in Ceuta on Saturday and sheltered inside a bar until police intervened4. On Sunday, Vox party leader Santiago Abascal visited Ceuta and criticized the government, labeling the influx an invasion4. Italian Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni suspended Italy's Schengen agreement with Spain, citing security concerns3.

Suicide bombing at peace rally in Pakistan's Swat district kills 14

A suicide attack outside a police station in Kabal, located in Pakistan's Swat district, killed at least 14 people and injured more than two dozen on Sunday1. Demonstrators had gathered near the police station for the Olasi Pasoon peace gathering to demand an end to militant violence in the Swat Valley1. District police chief Omar Khan stated that the bomber detonated explosives while demonstrators were chanting anti-militant slogans1.

Authorities confirmed that nine civilians and five police officers were among those killed1. Swat Deputy Inspector General of Police Fida Hussain reported that at least 22 people were wounded in the explosion3. The bombing follows an attack three days prior, in which an ambush using explosive-laden drones killed 11 police officers at a checkpoint in Hangu district3.

The deadly bombing reflects a wider wave of violence in the region. Military spokesperson Ahmad Sharif Chaudhry stated that Pakistan has recorded 3,145 terrorism-related incidents since January, resulting in 819 security and civilian deaths1. Furthermore, the Pakistan Institute for Conflict and Security Studies recorded 154 terrorism-related deaths across Khyber Pakhtunkhwa in July3.

Heavy monsoon rains and landslides in Kerala leave eight dead and eight missing

Torrential monsoon rains and multiple landslides across Kerala have resulted in significant casualties and destruction1. Kerala Chief Minister V.D. Satheesan confirmed that eight people died, eight remain missing, and 13 were injured following the severe weather1.

The India Meteorological Department issued an orange alert for 12 districts in Kerala on Sunday, warning residents of potential flash floods and mudslides1. Rain measurements showed extreme levels in several areas. Peerumade and Thodupuzha taluks in Idukki received over 200 mm of rainfall on Saturday morning, which triggered multiple landslides1. The Kerala State Disaster Management Authority recorded the highest rainfall of 320 mm at Ayyankunnu in Kannur district on Saturday2.

The deluge caused severe local accidents and stream overflows. In Malappuram, three-year-old Abdul Muhaini and four-year-old Adam Ayman drowned after falling into a swollen stream3. In response to the dangerous conditions, educational institutions in Pathanamthitta, Wayanad, Kannur, and Kasaragod districts were declared closed on August 32.

Heavy rainfall and flooding extended beyond Kerala into neighboring regions. In Gujarat's Vadodara district, rescue teams evacuated around 150 people, including 140 students, from a flooded hostel near Waghodia3.

Karnataka began releasing Cauvery water from the Kabini Dam on Saturday, raising discharge from 500 cusecs to 25,000 cusecs by Saturday night1. The release follows a directive from the Cauvery Water Management Authority ordering Karnataka to release 3,500 cusecs of water daily to Tamil Nadu for 15 days2.

The water dispute comes amidst low water levels and poor rainfall. On Sunday, the water level at Mettur Dam stood at 73.33 feet against its full capacity of 120 feet1. Karnataka Chief Minister D.K. Shivakumar stated that Karnataka has received only about 35 percent of its normal rainfall so far this year2.

Political developments continue to unfold over the water sharing issue. D.K. Shivakumar stated that Tamil Nadu Chief Minister C. Joseph Vijay agreed to postpone his planned visit to Bengaluru regarding the Cauvery dispute2. However, Kannada Chaluvali leader Vatal Nagaraj stated that a proposed state-wide bandh in Karnataka will proceed on August 13 despite government appeals to withdraw it3. In Tamil Nadu, BJP state president Nainar Nagenthran accused the Congress party of cheating Tamil Nadu farmers whenever it comes to power in Karnataka4.
