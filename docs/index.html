UAE Oil Tanker Attacked in Strait of Hormuz as Iran Outlines Conditions to Reopen Waterway

An oil tanker owned by Abu Dhabi state-owned energy firm Adnoc was hit by an Iranian missile in the Strait of Hormuz on Saturday1. The United Kingdom Maritime Trade Operations reported that a vessel east of Khasab, Oman, caught fire after being struck by a projectile1. Following the attack, the UAE Ministry of Foreign Affairs characterized the missile strike on the Adnoc vessel as an act of piracy by the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps2. Statements condemning the missile strike were quickly issued by Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Jordan, and the Gulf Cooperation Council2. This strike highlights the physical danger that tanker sailors continue to face while transiting the Strait of Hormuz as of August 20263. Adnoc stated that more than a dozen of its vessels have been targeted by missile and drone attacks in the waterway since February 2026, leaving one person dead and 20 injured1. The ongoing unrest poses severe risks to global energy security, as approximately one-fifth of global oil and natural gas supplies normally pass through the Strait of Hormuz4. Tensions in the region remain elevated further south as well, where Yemen's Iran-backed Houthis imposed a blockade on Saudi Arabian ports in the Red Sea starting on July 204. Iran's Supreme National Security Council announced that the Strait of Hormuz will remain closed until the United States changes its military and political conduct1. Mohammad Bagher Zolghadr, who serves as the secretary of Iran's Supreme National Security Council and is a commander in the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps, outlined Tehran's stance1. Iran's explicit demands for reopening the Strait of Hormuz include the lifting of the US naval blockade of Iranian ports, full compensation for war damage, and the unconditional release of frozen assets1. Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps spokesperson Hossein Mohebbi reiterated that the Strait of Hormuz will certainly reopen whenever the United States accepts Tehran's conditions5. Despite the standoff, some communication and partial transit continue. Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi stated that Iran and Oman were close to agreeing on a transit route through the Strait of Hormuz1. Araghchi also stated that maritime traffic had restored to 60 percent of normal levels within two weeks of implementing Clause 5 of the June Memorandum of Understanding5. In addition, US Vice President JD Vance told Fox News that Iran informed Washington it has no intentions to charge tolls on ships in the Strait of Hormuz4.

Colombia's President Abelardo de la Espriella Takes Office Promising Anti-Terror Campaign

Abelardo de la Espriella has taken office as President of Colombia after defeating leftist candidate Ivan Cepeda in June's run-off election by less than 1 percent of the vote1. De la Espriella, a former defence lawyer who represented drug-trafficking paramilitary figures2 and nicknames himself 'The Tiger'3, broke with tradition by holding his presidential inauguration ceremony in Cali instead of the capital city of Bogota2. Acting US Attorney General Todd Blanche headed the official United States delegation at the event4. In his maiden speech at a military base in Cali, President de la Espriella vowed to defeat narco-terrorists3. He announced plans to join the US-led 'Shield of the Americas' security partnership2 and resume aerial spraying of herbicides to eradicate illicit coca crops4. Following his swearing-in, the US State Department pledged $1 billion in security assistance to Colombia2. Espriella also campaigned on promises to reduce the size of the Colombian state by up to 40 percent4. Internationally, he plans to restore diplomatic relations with Israel while cutting ties with Cuba and Nicaragua5. Outgoing President Gustavo Petro, who pursued a 'Total Peace' strategy starting in 2022 emphasizing dialogue with criminal and rebel groups1, refused to attend the inauguration and called for mass public protests over unproven election fraud allegations5. Violence persisted nearby as a car bomb attributed by the military to two FARC dissident factions exploded at a Pan-American Highway toll plaza in Cauca, injuring two security guards1.

Saudi Arabia, Pakistan, and Turkey Sign Mutual Defence Pact in Mecca

Saudi Arabia, Turkey, and Pakistan signed a trilateral mutual defence agreement during a meeting in Mecca on Friday1. The joint statement stipulates that an armed attack against any one of the three member nations will be considered an attack against all three1. A Turkish official stated that the agreement is purely defensive and does not replace existing security commitments2. Turkey maintains the second-largest standing military force within the 32-member NATO alliance1. Pakistan Defence Minister Khawaja Asif advocated expanding the pact into a broader NATO-like military alliance for Islamic nations2. The agreement expands existing bilateral ties. Saudi Arabia and Pakistan previously established a bilateral mutual defence agreement containing a similar collective defence clause in September 20251. Historically, Pakistan has stationed military personnel, fighter aircraft, and air-defence systems inside Saudi Arabia3. Turkey also provided military support, including uncrewed aerial vehicles and electronic warfare equipment, to Pakistan during India's Operation Sindoor in May 20253. The announcement of the pact coincided with a new military offensive by Yemen's Saudi-backed government against the Houthis4.

US Senate Passes Bill Imposing 100% Tariffs on Key Russian Oil Buyers Including India

The United States Senate has approved a major trade bill aimed at penalizing countries that purchase Russian crude oil, passing the Lindsey Graham-named bill in an 86-11 vote1. The legislation authorizes tariffs of up to 500 percent on Russian imports, as well as 100 percent tariffs on five key Russian oil buyers, including India and China1. However, the measure will not take effect immediately, as the House of Representatives is not expected to consider the Senate-passed tariff legislation until September following its summer recess1. The legislative move comes after a period of fluctuating economic measures and negotiations between Washington and New Delhi. The United States previously raised overall tariff rates on Indian goods to 50 percent in August 2025 due to India's continued imports of Russian crude oil2. In February, President Donald Trump issued an executive order removing a 25 percent punitive tariff on Indian goods after reaching an interim trade agreement with New Delhi1. Later, Prime Minister Narendra Modi and US President Donald Trump met in France in June 2026, where they instructed officials to negotiate a bilateral trade agreement2. Diplomatic dialogue between the two nations has continued alongside the legislative actions in Washington. US Vice President JD Vance placed a phone call to Indian Prime Minister Narendra Modi to discuss expanding bilateral cooperation across key sectors including trade, defence, energy, and technology3. During the conversation, Prime Minister Modi extended personal congratulations to JD Vance and Second Lady Usha Vance following the birth of their son4. Vice President JD Vance and Usha Vance announced the birth of their fourth child, Alec Neel Vance, making him the first child born to a sitting US Vice President in over 150 years5.
