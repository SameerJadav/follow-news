Spain Restores Border Checks On Travel From Italy Over Ceuta Migrant Disagreements

Spain has imposed border controls on all air and sea travel arriving from Italy for a duration of one month starting at midnight, or 22:00 GMT1. The move follows a growing diplomatic dispute between the two nations after Italy previously introduced its own border restrictions1. Italian authorities put those measures in place after an influx of migrants entered the Spanish exclave of Ceuta from Morocco1. According to the Italian government, its border controls will remain in place until at least August 151. Spanish Foreign Minister José Manuel Albares strongly criticized the Italian measures, describing Italy's border checks as a torpedo fired at the hull of European unity2. Differing reports exist regarding the number of migrants involved in the movement into Ceuta. Italy enacted its restrictions after approximately 78,000 migrants entered the exclave1. However, official Spanish figures present different data, indicating that 72,000 people entered Ceuta and that 70,000 of them have since returned to Morocco2. Amid these movements, nearly 1,400 minors remain in Ceuta following the influx from Morocco1. Important legal and human rights developments have also emerged during the situation. Spain's Supreme Court ruled that migrants stopped at sea while attempting to reach Ceuta or Melilla cannot be summarily returned to Morocco1. At the same time, rights groups and officials have reported deaths linked to the sea and border crossings. The Moroccan Association for Human Rights together with the European Coordination for Human Rights in Morocco reported at least 141 deaths linked to the Ceuta crossings2. In contrast, Morocco's interior ministry stated that 11 people died on its side of the border, adding that all but one of those deaths were caused by drowning2. Law enforcement actions have also targeted illicit transport networks active in the region. On June 16, an international law enforcement operation dismantled a Western Mediterranean smuggling network, resulting in 78 arrests3. Officials stated that the dismantled network had generated over 24 million euros, equivalent to $27 million, in profits while bringing more than 2,000 people into Spain3.

Teenage Gunman Kills Seven People In Shooting At Home And School In Thailand

A 14-year-old student fatally shot his grandparents at home and opened fire at Debsirin Nonthaburi School before taking his own life1. The youth shot his 73-year-old grandfather and 74-year-old grandmother at their flat around 05:00 local time2. Later, around 10:00 a.m., he launched the attack at the school3. Five school staff members, including teachers and administrators, were killed at Debsirin Nonthaburi School3. Thai media later reported that a student died from injuries, raising the total death toll to eight including the gunman2. The Thai health department reported that nine wounded victims were in critical condition following the school attack4. Investigators found that the shooter fired at least 26 bullets using a gun that belonged to his grandfather, and he was found with 34 additional rounds of ammunition1. Following the tragedy, Debsirin Nonthaburi School ordered all classes suspended from August 10 to August 143. In response to the shooting, Thai Prime Minister Anutin Charnvirakul announced plans to introduce legislation that would allow only on-duty government officials to carry firearms in public2. Mass shootings have occurred previously in the country; a 2022 attack by a former policeman in northeastern Thailand killed 37 people, including 22 children4. In 2017, the Small Arms Survey estimated that Thailand had 10.3 million civilian-owned firearms, representing about 15 guns per 100 residents1.

Ebola Outbreak In Democratic Republic Of Congo Passes 4,000 Cases

Confirmed Ebola cases in the Democratic Republic of the Congo have reached 4,053, with 1,850 deaths reported since mid-May1. Earlier official health data recorded 3,973 Ebola cases and 1,801 deaths as of August 42. Geographically, Ituri province accounts for 87 percent of the ongoing cases, while North Kivu accounts for 11 percent1. More than two-thirds of Ebola deaths are occurring in local communities rather than in treatment centres2. To contain the virus, the Africa CDC announced plans to deploy health workers door-to-door for active case searching in affected areas2. Health officials also intercepted a riverboat in Maluku to screen over 300 passengers after a passenger died while showing Ebola-like symptoms3. Medical agencies are studying vaccines and monitoring the virus for changes. World Health Organization vaccine experts recommended a clinical trial of the Ervebo vaccine to evaluate potential protection against the Bundibugyo Ebola strain1. The vaccine alliance Gavi maintains a global stockpile of 500,000 Ervebo vaccine doses1. Meanwhile, the Africa CDC and WHO plan to launch studies to check whether the Bundibugyo Ebola virus strain is mutating due to its rapid spread3. The ongoing outbreak has also strained other public health efforts; UNICEF reported that first-dose measles vaccinations among children in Mongbwalu dropped by 69 percent amid the Ebola crisis2.

Saudi Arabia, Turkey, And Pakistan Sign Mecca Defence Pact As India Monitors Situation

Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman, Turkish President Recep Tayyip Erdogan, and Pakistani Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif signed the Mecca Joint Defence Agreement in Mecca1. The newly established pact sets up a mutual defense commitment among the three signatory countries1. Under the explicit terms of the agreement, an armed attack against any one of the three participating nations shall be regarded as an attack against them all1. The agreement comes at a time of severe security tensions in the region. Yemen's Houthi rebels have declared a maritime embargo against Saudi Arabia1. Houthi forces have repeatedly targeted Saudi airports, oil facilities, and tankers in the Red Sea1. The creation of the new alliance has drawn attention and responses from neighboring countries and international observers. India announced that it is closely following the newly signed defence agreement between Pakistan, Saudi Arabia, and Turkey2. An unidentified Turkish official stated that the Mecca Joint Defence Agreement is purely defensive in nature and is not directed at any specific nation2. However, the treaty met with criticism from Iranian officials. Iranian parliament member Ebrahim Rezaei stated that a paper agreement with Turkey and Pakistan would not bring security to Saudi Arabia1.

Heavy Rains Cause Severe Flooding And Travel Delays Across Delhi-NCR

Heavy monsoon weather has caused severe waterlogging and travel disruptions across Delhi and the surrounding areas1. The India Meteorological Department issued a red alert for Delhi-NCR on August 7, forecasting moderate to heavy rain, thunderstorms, and lightning2. The department also issued a yellow alert for Delhi following widespread heavy rain1. Rainfall measurements showed unusually high totals. Delhi recorded 127 mm of rainfall during the first seven days of August, marking its wettest first week of August in 13 years3. In the 24 hours up to 8:30 a.m. on August 7, Lodhi Road recorded 60.4 mm of rainfall, Safdarjung recorded 59.2 mm, and Ridge recorded 57 mm2. Sector 17 in Faridabad recorded 119.4 mm of rainfall between midnight and 5:00 p.m. on August 73. The heavy downpours severely impacted local infrastructure. Rainwater flooded the subway near Badarpur Border Metro Station, severely restricting movement for commuters2. Rainwater also entered the grounds of Safdarjung Hospital, causing movement difficulties for patients and attendants3. Despite the warnings, authorities confirmed that schools in Delhi-NCR will remain open on August 84. However, educational institutions in Ghaziabad, Meerut, and Baghpat were ordered closed until August 12 due to heavy movement of pilgrims during the Kanwar Yatra4.
