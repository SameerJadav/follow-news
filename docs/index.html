Canada proposes closer European Union ties to protect trade and sovereignty

Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney delivered a speech to the European Parliament in Strasbourg1. He called for closer cooperation on defence and energy between Canada and the European Union1.

Carney told European lawmakers that closer ties would protect Canada2. He said deeper cooperation would stop any external power from controlling Canadian markets2. He added that it would prevent outside powers from undermining Canadian sovereignty2. Earlier in 2026, Carney spoke at the World Economic Forum2. He urged middle powers to act together2. He said smaller nations must limit coercive actions by more powerful nations2.

Carney proposed new ways to connect citizens across the Atlantic Ocean1. He suggested deepening people-to-people ties with the European Union1. This proposal would allow young people to live, work, and study on either side of the Atlantic1.

Many Canadians share historical links with Europe. According to Canada's 2021 census, over half of the Canadian population reported having European origins1. Public opinion also shows support for closer cooperation. An Abacus Data survey conducted before the speech measured public views1. It found that 80 percent of Canadians support deepening strategic cooperation with the European Union1.

Economic experts view the proposed relationship positively. Fabian Zuleeg, Chief Economist at the European Policy Center, spoke about the economic relationship3. Zuleeg said the partnership between Canada and the European Union is a classic win-win situation3.

Trade with the United States remains central to the Canadian economy. Up to 72 percent of Canadian exports are destined for the United States1.

The proposal faces legal hurdles in Europe. The European Union currently does not have an associate member status1. Creating such a status would require approval from all 27 member states1.

Political opponents in Canada raised doubts about the initiative. Conservative Member of Parliament Adam Chambers questioned the prime minister's speech1. Chambers asked whether Carney's proposal was a concrete plan or just words and vibes1.

Biologists identify new wild cat species in cloud forests of Bolivia

Biologists named a new wild cat species discovered in Bolivia's Yungas cloud forest1. The cat is formally named Leopardus tilcayo and locally called tilcayo1. The tilcayo measures about 18 inches in length and weighs about 3 pounds1.

DNA analysis of 38 specimens showed South American tiger cats consist of five distinct species1. Genetic evidence indicates the tilcayo lineage separated from other tiger cats 1.4 million years ago1. Biologists also identified a distinct tiger cat subspecies in Peru's Yungas region1. The tilcayo is the first newly described living cat species identified since 19232.

Bolivian biologist Paola Nogales-Ascarrunz examined a wild kitten brought to a wildlife sanctuary in 20162. A 10-year-old male tilcayo lives at the Senda Verde sanctuary near La Paz3. Local camera traps show the cat is active at night3. Scat samples revealed the species eats small rodents3. The International Union for Conservation of Nature classifies tiger cats as vulnerable3.

US House approves bill allowing up to 100% tariffs on Indian and Chinese energy imports from Russia

The United States House of Representatives passed legislation granting US President Donald Trump authority to impose high tariffs1. The bill allows tariffs of up to 100% on countries buying Russian oil and gas, including India and China1. The bill is titled the Lindsey O. Graham Sanctioning Russia and Iran Act 20262. The House approved the measure by a vote of 262 to 159 after it cleared the Senate1.

Under the draft law, 100% tariffs could apply to the five largest importers of Russian oil or gas3. The duties would target countries that knowingly continue purchases 30 days after the bill becomes law3.

Indian officials defended their energy purchases. India's Ambassador to the US Vinay Mohan Kwatra said energy is a vital strategic necessity for 1.4 billion Indians1. Kwatra called energy the foundation of India's development1. India imports over 85% of its crude oil and about half of its natural gas1.

Trade relations between the US and India have faced past friction. In April 2025, the US announced a 26% tariff on several Indian imports4. The US paused that rate for 90 days before setting a temporary 10% duty4.

China also criticized the US legislation. Chinese Foreign Ministry spokesperson Guo Jiakun said China opposes unilateral sanctions that lack a UN Security Council mandate2. Chinese President Xi Jinping is scheduled to visit Washington on September 24-25 for a summit with Trump2. US and Chinese officials are consulting on a tariff reduction framework covering $30 billion in products from each side2.

The Department of Financial Services said 5% of fee collections will fund a dedicated fund to promote UPI adoption among small merchants1.
