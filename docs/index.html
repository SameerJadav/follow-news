Israeli election ends in deadlock as Netanyahu and opposition stall while Arab parties gain

Israeli political leaders faced a deadlock on Wednesday after election results left neither major group with a majority1.

With nearly 90 percent of the votes counted by Wednesday evening, neither Benjamin Netanyahu nor opposition leader Gadi Eisenkot had enough seats to form a government on his own1. A majority in the 120-seat Israeli parliament, called the Knesset, requires 61 seats1. Early results put the coalition bloc of Benjamin Netanyahu at about 53 to 54 seats1. The bloc led by Gadi Eisenkot stood at around 49 seats1. Netanyahu's Likud party remains the largest single party in the parliament, with an estimated 26 seats1. Eisenkot formed his political party, called Yashar, one year before the 2026 election2.

Arab citizens of Israel turned out to vote in record numbers during this election1. Overall voter turnout on October 27 was the highest recorded in Israel since 19992. Arab citizens make up approximately 20 percent of the population in Israel, which is roughly 2.1 million people3. The two main Arab parties in the country are projected to win around 19 of the 120 seats in the Knesset4. These two main Arab political parties are the Joint List, led by Yousef Jabareen, and Ra'am, led by Mansour Abbas3.

Yousef Jabareen said that the Joint List would seek to use its projected 12 seats to help remove the government of Benjamin Netanyahu3. Mansour Abbas said that senior members of Eisenkot's bloc had discussed a potential coalition deal with him before the election took place3. Eisenkot said he wants an Israeli government that is Zionist and committed to universal military service1. Yair Golan, the leader of the centre-left Democrats party, called for creating a change government in partnership with Arab citizens of Israel5. In Tel Aviv, Israelis held rallies demanding a Jewish-Arab government after Arab parties made major gains in Tuesday's election4.

Likud spokesman Guy Levy confirmed that Likud would not negotiate with any Arab party to form a government3. In addition, the Central Elections Committee received a complaint from the Likud party5. The complaint alleged that Arab parties compromised election integrity5.

United Nations rights chief Volker Turk expressed his views on the political outcome5. Turk said he hopes the next Israeli government will change course massively and respect international law5. The Israeli Knesset is scheduled to open on November 101.

Defeated Brazilian candidate Flávio Bolsonaro arrives in Florida after leaving Brazil

Brazilian senator Flávio Bolsonaro landed at Miami-Opa Locka Executive Airport in Florida after leaving Brazil in secret1. President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva, who won the runoff election by over four million votes, called the departure a shameful act1. Bolsonaro's political party acknowledged the official election results2.

Bolsonaro wrote in a letter that he did not flee Brazil but left for the sake of Brazil1. He posted on social media calling himself the elected president before changing the word to leader3. In the letter, he thanked Argentine President Javier Milei and US President Donald Trump4. Trump said he would not get involved, calling the election very strange2.

Bolsonaro traveled by land into Argentina, took a private jet from Puerto Iguazú, stopped in Ecuador, and flew to Miami1. Brazilian authorities canceled his passports after he missed a 24-hour deadline to hand them in1. Federal police faced criticism for failing to stop him4.

Bolsonaro faces criminal investigation in Brazil over financial crimes, corruption, and money laundering linked to an unreleased film called Dark Horse3. He loses his parliamentary immunity in February2. He has held Italian nationality since 20233. His father Jair Bolsonaro is in jail for conspiring to stay in power2.

Cyclone Arnab hits eastern India, killing several people and displacing thousands

Severe cyclonic storm Arnab hit the eastern coast of India, causing deaths, heavy rain, and damage across several states1.

The storm crossed the coasts of Andhra Pradesh and Yanam early on Wednesday2. It made landfall about 20 kilometers east-northeast of Narsapur as a very severe cyclonic storm2. At its peak, sustained wind speeds reached 125 to 135 kilometers per hour, with gusts up to 150 kilometers per hour3. The India Meteorological Department stated that the storm later weakened into a deep depression1. Forecasters expect it to weaken further into a depression on Thursday morning, October 29, 20261.

Reports differed on the exact number of deaths caused by the storm in Andhra Pradesh. The Times of India reported that eight people died in parts of the state after the storm crossed the coast3. NDTV reported five deaths from fallen trees and electrocution, with single deaths in Visakhapatnam, Anakapalle, Kakinada, Konaseema, and West Godavari districts2. Andhra Pradesh Chief Minister N. Chandrababu Naidu also stated that five people died1. Scroll.in reported that three people died in the state as the storm made landfall4. Scroll.in added that the storm damaged 31 houses4.

Before the cyclone arrived, authorities moved more than 20,000 people out of coastal districts in Andhra Pradesh3. Heavy rain fell across the region. Garividi in Vizianagaram district recorded 26 centimeters of rain between Tuesday morning and Wednesday morning3. This was the highest rainfall recorded in Andhra Pradesh3. The India Meteorological Department issued a flash flood warning for 13 districts in Andhra Pradesh and Yanam until Thursday morning2.

Chief Minister Naidu announced a relief and compensation package for affected residents1. He spoke to Union Home Minister Amit Shah to ask for central help for damaged crops and infrastructure1. The neighboring Telangana government offered full support and emergency personnel to help Andhra Pradesh restore power and deliver relief2.

Neighboring states also felt the impact of the cyclone. Odisha Chief Minister Mohan Charan Majhi said heavy rain affected at least 17 districts in southern Odisha1. Ahead of the storm, Odisha authorities moved about 28,000 people to relief camps2. They also relocated 2,200 pregnant women to safe places2. In Chhattisgarh, officials ordered all educational institutions in Raipur district to close on Thursday due to heavy rain and strong wind forecasts5.

At sea, three Indian seafarers named Aditya Sharma, Shivanand Chaurashiya, and Patnala Suresh died on June 92. A United States strike hit their Palau-flagged tanker, the MT Settebello, off Oman2. Supreme Court scrutinises Election Commission over voter list removals across India

The Supreme Court of India urged the Election Commission not to force removed Delhi voters to reapply as new voters1. Judges said nearly 4.3 million removed electors should not use Form 6 if they were on the 2026 rolls1. Out of about 14.5 million electors at the start of Delhi's revision, around 4.7 million did not submit forms1.

The court stated it had not approved the commission's logical discrepancy label1. That classification previously removed 2.71 million people from voter rolls in West Bengal1. In a May 27 judgment, the court ruled the revision process met proportionality rules and obeyed election law2.

The national voter revision began in Bihar in June 20253. By September 1, about 13.37 crore names were missing from draft rolls across India3. Election Commissioners Sukhbir Singh Sandhu and Vivek Joshi formally objected 14 times in 10 months to actions taken during the exercise3.

In Karnataka, about 64.5 lakh voters were missing from final rolls, lowering the total to 45,500,0114. Young adults aged 18 to 29 made up 59.5 percent, or 38.41 lakh, of those missing Karnataka voters4.
