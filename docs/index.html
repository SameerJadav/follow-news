Powerful Earthquake Strikes Kumamoto in Japan Causing Severe Damage and Building Collapses

A powerful earthquake struck Kumamoto prefecture on the southern Japanese island of Kyushu at 16:27 local time (07:27 GMT) on Tuesday1. News outlets reported differing initial figures for the magnitude of the main tremor. Al Jazeera and early estimates from the US Geological Survey put the magnitude at 7.13. However, the US Geological Survey later revised its figure to 6.81, and the Guardian World also reported a preliminary magnitude of 6.81. The Japan Meteorological Agency reported more than 60 aftershocks ranging in magnitude from 1 to 4 following the main quake1.

The earthquake caused widespread damage, injuries, and structural collapses across the area3. In Kumamoto, the outer walls of Kumamoto Castle were damaged3. Severe destruction was also reported at the local Aeon shopping mall, which had reopened on June 13, 2026, following extensive rebuilding after a prior earthquake in 20164. Before the building collapsed, mall staff helped evacuate about 200 visitors outside4. Shortly after the tremor, an explosion occurred inside the mall, destroying its walls and structure and trapping people inside1.

Elsewhere in the region, a chimney collapsed at Nippon Paper's Yatsushiro Plant, leaving two workers in cardiac arrest and nine others missing2. Japanese Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi stated that power outages, fires, damaged roads, damaged bridges, and collapsed buildings have occurred in several areas1. She pledged a full government response to the disaster3. As of 01:30am local time, more than 40,000 households across Kumamoto Prefecture were experiencing power outages1.

In response to the emergency, Japan's disaster management agency instructed approximately 300,000 people to move to evacuation centres1. A tsunami warning issued immediately after the earthquake was lifted within two hours1. The disaster follows a series of powerful earthquakes in Kumamoto in 2016, which killed 277 people either directly or from disaster-related injuries1.

Wildfires Across France and Spain Force Mass Evacuations Amid High Heat

Wildfires driven by severe heatwaves and drought are causing widespread destruction across southern Europe. In southwestern France, advancing fires near Bordeaux are threatening vineyards across the country's largest wine region1. Thomas Duroux, director general of Chateau Palmer, described the situation as a wake-up call, stating that the region is facing something never seen before1. The fires come as the Liv-ex Bordeaux 500 Index shows the Bordeaux fine wine market has lost nearly 20 percent of its value over the past five years1. Elsewhere in the region, a wildfire damaged parts of the commercial district in Biscarrosse2. Near Lacanau, west of Bordeaux, nearly 4,000 people were evacuated from tourist campsites and villages3. French President Emmanuel Macron stated that the current wildfire situation is the toughest crisis France has dealt with since World War II3.

In Spain, wildfires have forced tens of thousands of people from their homes4. In the Madrid region, more than 56,500 people have been evacuated4. Madrid regional leader Isabel Díaz Ayuso described the wildfires as the biggest natural catastrophe in the history of Spain4. A fire in Ávila burned 50,000 hectares, becoming the largest recorded wildfire in Spain's history3. Spanish Minister for Ecological Transition Sara Aagesen reported that around 77,000 hectares have been affected across the country4. Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez declared affected areas official disaster zones4. Across France, Spain, and Italy, approximately 330,000 people were evacuated over a single week3.

Oman Proposes Navigation Fee Plan to Iran for Strait of Hormuz

Oman has submitted a proposal to Iran to manage the Strait of Hormuz using a voluntary fee structure backed by regional nations1. The proposal is modeled after the Strait of Malacca system where ships voluntarily contribute to fund safety and environmental care2. However, Iranian Deputy Foreign Minister Kazem Gharibabadi stated that Tehran rejected an Omani proposal for an equal division of transit lanes in the strait1. Prior to US-Israel military action on February 28, the strategic waterway carried one-fifth of global energy supplies1. Foreign ministers of the Gulf Cooperation Council held a video conference on Tuesday to discuss maritime navigation security1.

Regional diplomatic and military tensions remain high. Iranian Foreign Ministry spokesperson Esmaeil Baqaei stated that Iran is not engaged in any negotiations with the United States3. In contrast, US President Donald Trump told Axios that the US is in very deep talks with Iran, warning that the US will return to strong military action if they fail3. Trump had paused a 13-night campaign of airstrikes against Iranian targets following recommendations from military commanders2. Meanwhile, Iran's joint military command rejected Trump's proposal to pay damages to commercial vessels using frozen Iranian assets2. In related maritime actions, Yemen's Houthi forces declared a blockade on Saudi Red Sea ports on July 20 and later claimed to fire ballistic missiles at a Saudi oil tanker2.

Lok Sabha Debates Anti-Paper Leak Bill as Protests and Legal Challenges Continue

The Lok Sabha has opened debate on the Public Examinations (Prevention of Unfair Means) Amendment Bill, 20261. The legislation comes in the wake of severe national scandals involving competitive examination paper leaks, which have contributed to at least 22 student suicides in India this year2. During the debate, Congress MP Gaurav Gogoi called the anti-paper leak bill a cosmetic exercise that reflected the government's admission of failure1. Congress MP Shashi Tharoor urged Parliament to completely overhaul the examination framework, moving from single high-stakes tests to continuous testing to prevent paper leaks3. Tharoor stated in the Lok Sabha that student suicides in India have increased from 8,000 in 2014 to 14,600 currently3.

The parliamentary proceedings follow weeks of intense student demonstrations across the country. Union Education Minister Dharmendra Pradhan resigned on July 25 following sustained youth demonstrations led by the Cockroach Janta Party2. Violent clashes occurred during protests in New Delhi on July 20, leaving 47 Rapid Action Force personnel and between 3 and 5 protesters injured4. An internal review by the Central Reserve Police Force concluded that Rapid Action Force personnel followed standard operating procedures when using pellet guns during those July 20 anti-NEET protests4.

Meanwhile, legal battles related to the unrest have reached the country's highest court. Cockroach Janta Party volunteer Junaid Malik filed a petition with the Supreme Court alleging unlawful detention and police harassment5.

India Coordinates Response for Seafarers Trapped on Black Sea Vessel

The Indian government is addressing growing dangers facing its maritime workers in international conflict zones. The Forward Seamen's Union of India reported that 13 Indian seafarers among 15 total crew members are trapped aboard the vessel MV AMIR1 at the Ukrainian port of Chornomorsk1. On July 25, two Indian crew members went missing after jumping overboard when the cargo ship AGN RAGNAR was attacked by drones near the Danube river outlet1. These incidents follow a July 18 attack on the merchant vessel MV OMORFI that killed Chief Officer Sagar Gupta2, as well as a July 19 strike on the MV Golden Leo near Odesa that killed four Indian nationals and critically injured another2.

In response to repeated attacks on commercial ships carrying Indian crews, the Indian Ministry of External Affairs summoned Ukrainian Ambassador Oleksandr Polishchuk to lodge a protest2. Indian Shipping Minister Sarbananda Sonowal instructed officials during a high-level review meeting to prevent Indian seafarers from being caught in geopolitical conflicts3. External affairs ministry spokesperson Randhir Jaiswal also stated that six Indian vessels carrying 125 Indian seafarers are stationed west of the Strait of Hormuz4. India represents the second-largest community of seafarers worldwide, making up 12.16 percent of the global maritime workforce with over 320,000 professionals4.

Assam Monsoon Floods Affect Over 300,000 as Death Toll Rises

Severe monsoon flooding in Assam caused seven additional deaths over a 24-hour period, leaving more than 300,000 people affected1. Overall, monsoon floods in Assam have caused 75 deaths and displaced more than 7.5 lakh people2. Assam Assembly Speaker Ranjeet Kumar Dass, Deputy Speaker Habbey Teron, and most Assam state legislators pledged one month's salary to flood relief2. The All Assam Students' Union sent a letter to Prime Minister Narendra Modi requesting that Assam's flood and erosion problem be classified as a national problem2.

To aid victims, Assam Chief Minister Himanta Biswa Sarma announced an additional ex-gratia payment of ₹5 lakh from the CM's Relief Fund, raising total ex-gratia to ₹9 lakh per deceased person3. The Assam government also allocated interim cash relief of ₹15,000 per family for approximately 100,000 severely affected households in Sivasagar, Charaideo, Jorhat, and Golaghat districts3. In neighboring regions, the Odisha state government initiated evacuation operations in low-lying areas as floods affected over 100,000 residents across four northern districts4.
