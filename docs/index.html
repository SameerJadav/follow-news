Israeli election exit polls show tight deadlock between Benjamin Netanyahu and opposition

Israeli television networks reported exit polls showing a tight deadlock between Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu and his opponents. Broadcasters Kan 11 and Channel 13 predicted Netanyahu's political bloc and Gadi Eisenkot's opposition group would win 51 seats each1. Channel 12 reported that Netanyahu's allies won 50 seats while the opposition earned 52 seats1. Channel 14 projected a stronger performance for Netanyahu with 60 seats2. The BBC reported that Eisenkot's opposition bloc won the most seats but lacked an outright majority3. A party or coalition needs 61 seats in the 120-seat Knesset to form a government1.

Netanyahu spoke to supporters in Jerusalem after 3 a.m. local time1. He said official results will show his Likud party as the largest group in Israel1. He also said a government that depends on Palestinian parties must not be formed1. Meanwhile, opposition leader Yair Lapid said Netanyahu's bloc suffered a total collapse and crushing defeat2. Eisenkot is a 66-year-old former military chief who entered politics four years ago2. He lost a son and two nephews in the Gaza war2.

Giant screens at the Likud event showed videos of Netanyahu alongside United States President Donald Trump4. The screens also displayed footage of military strikes4. Reports indicate the United States and Israel plan major attacks against Iran after their national elections4. The two countries previously conducted joint attacks on Iran in June 2025 and February 20264.

Election officials said overall voter turnout reached 77.7 percent5. This was the highest voter participation rate since 19995. Turnout among Palestinian citizens of Israel reached about 80 percent5. This figure broke previous records from 19995. Palestinian citizens make up about 21 percent of Israel's 10 million residents5. Exit polls showed Arab-led parties Joint List and Ra'am winning a record 18 seats5. Ra'am leader Mansour Abbas said both major Israeli political blocs contacted his party before and after the vote5. During voting, witnesses saw Likud supporters driving through Jerusalem with party flags5. The supporters blared the Islamic call to prayer to warn voters that Arab citizens were casting ballots5.

Final results could take time to confirm. Israel's Central Elections Committee expects 600,000 to 650,000 people to vote at special polling places1. These votes account for nearly 10 percent of all eligible voters1. Yisrael Beitenu leader Avigdor Lieberman warned that final results could change once military absentee ballots are counted1.

Brazilian Senator Flávio Bolsonaro leaves country for the United States after election loss

Brazilian Senator Flávio Bolsonaro left Brazil for the United States after losing the presidential runoff election1. Incumbent President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva won a fourth term with 51.8 percent of the vote1. Liberal Party leader Valdemar Costa Neto said the party accepts the election results1. President Lula called the departure shameful1. He said anyone who sneaks out of their country must have something to hide1. Former President Jair Bolsonaro is currently serving a 27-year prison sentence for planning a coup1.

Brazil's Supreme Court ordered Flávio Bolsonaro to surrender his passports during a money laundering investigation2. Sources said authorities cancelled his passports after he failed to surrender them within 24 hours3. A police official said he did not pass through any official migration checkpoint3. Todo Noticias reported he walked into Argentina before changing plans to take a private jet4. But Argentinian authorities denied he entered their country5. A US official confirmed entry visas would be given to him and his family3. Flávio Bolsonaro has also held Italian citizenship since 20234. Investigators are looking into a 680 million pound scheme linked to betting companies4.

Ceasefire holds in Russian-occupied Ukrainian city of Oleshky following international talks

A local ceasefire in the Russian-occupied city of Oleshky was extended across the broader Kherson region1. US First Lady Melania Trump and Pope Leo XIV helped negotiate the humanitarian deal1. Melania Trump led talks with civilian representatives after receiving information from UN officials2. Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov confirmed the truce was following plans and recognized US mediation1. Ukrainian official David Arakhamia said the truce will continue until Sunday evening1.

Between 1,700 and 2,000 residents remain trapped in Oleshky without food, water, or electricity1. The truce allows humanitarian aid deliveries before civilian evacuations begin on Friday1. Oleshky had 24,000 residents before Russian forces captured it in February 20221. Cable network Newsmax reported exclusive access to send reporters alongside observers entering the city3.

While the local truce held, fighting continued elsewhere. Russian drone and missile strikes on Kyiv killed one person and wounded 15 others overnight4. Strikes in Kharkiv killed two people on Monday night2. Meanwhile, leaders from Finland and Norway held a phone call with Vladimir Putin about peace talks4. Denmark's prime minister criticized that call4.
