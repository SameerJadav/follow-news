Massive unrest and clashes erupt across France over living costs

French authorities said about 550,000 demonstrators joined cost-of-living protests across the country on Saturday1. The nationwide demonstrations brought together trade unions, school students, and the anti-Macron Yellow Vests movement2.

Clashes between protesters and police led to two deaths during the unrest1. A 22-year-old police officer died in Nantes after being struck by a vehicle1. A protester also died near Paris1. French authorities reported around 2,400 arrests across the country after the violence1. Police in Paris reported a record 855 arrests in the capital alone3. France's interior ministry said almost 1,000 police officers and gendarmes were injured during Saturday's unrest2. Seven of those injured officers were hurt seriously2. On Sunday, police fired tear gas and stun grenades at hundreds of people gathered at Place de la République in Paris2.

Violent incidents occurred in several other parts of France during the demonstrations. An apparent arson attack damaged a 19th-century town hall in the city of Limoges on Saturday4. Firefighters fought the town hall blaze through Saturday night and into Sunday morning4. Limoges Mayor Guillaume Guérin said municipal police ran out of ammunition around 3:30 p.m. while trying to hold off rioters3. Guérin called the incident a colossal failure of the state3.

French President Emmanuel Macron gave a televised address on Sunday1. Macron blamed radical far-left factions and criminal elements for the destruction during the protests1. Macron said that those who destroyed must pay1. He said that those who looted must pay back1. He added that those who sought to bring down the Republic will be held accountable1.

International figures also commented on the situation in France. US President Donald Trump posted on social media and blamed immigration for the unrest2. Trump said that France is burning2. Protests over the cost of living and public services were not limited to France3. Similar demonstrations also took place in Spain, Italy, and Portugal3.

Deadly fire at Canadian military base kills four during training

Four Canadian service members died on Saturday during a military training exercise in Alberta1. A fast-moving fire and explosion occurred at Canadian Forces Base Wainwright1. Soldiers were conducting live-fire combat exercises when a grass fire started about 1 kilometer away2. Major-General Wade Rutland said uncommonly strong winds pushed the fire toward the range administrative area2.

The four deceased service members were identified as Sgt Morgan Wong, Pte Shaun Farrell, Pte Aidan Goulding, and Pte Hayden Lykkemark1. Hospitals admitted 39 patients, with at least 19 in critical condition1. Internal military communications said 31 people were very seriously injured, according to the Globe and Mail3.

Alberta's health agency sent 30 units to Wainwright, including 22 ground ambulances, six planes, and two helicopters2. Military medical personnel parachuted into Wainwright shortly before midnight to assist4. Ontario mayoral candidate Cathy Keane said on Facebook that her eldest son was among the critically injured personnel5.

Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney called the incident one of the worst tragedies for the forces on Canadian soil in peacetime1. Carney ordered flags on all federal buildings across Canada to be flown at half-mast1. The Department of National Defence said there were no indications that the fire was a terrorist attack1. Canadian Forces Base Wainwright usually has about 690 military personnel and 300 civilian workers on site daily2.

Israeli settlers attack European diplomats in occupied West Bank

Masked Israeli settlers attacked European diplomats and Palestinian civilians near Taybeh on Saturday1. The targeted delegation included Belgian, French, and Polish diplomats, along with a French mayor1. Eyewitnesses said an attacker aggressively shouted "France, France" before pushing French Mayor Bernard Baudoux to the ground2. Settlers smashed a reinforced window of an SUV carrying European diplomats as the vehicle drove away2.

Israeli Foreign Minister Gideon Saar and the Israeli military blamed Israeli rioters for the attack1. The office of Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu posted a statement titled "Serious accusations require serious evidence" casting doubt on the accounts2. On Friday, Washington Post photojournalist Heidi Levine was hospitalized after an assault while covering a Palestinian olive harvest1. US Ambassador to Israel Mike Huckabee called for Levine's attackers to be jailed before attending a settler conference on Sunday1.

US diplomats including Assistant Secretary of State Riley Barnes and US Ambassador to the Holy See Brian Burch visited Taybeh on Sunday1. US Secretary of State Marco Rubio warned against a brewing crisis in the West Bank3. Human rights group Yesh Din recorded at least 170 settler attacks on Palestinians since September 271. The West Bank is home to about 500,000 Israeli settlers and at least 3 million Palestinians4.

Trump pauses tariffs on nations buying Russian crude energy

US President Donald Trump paused sanctions linked to Russia two days before they were scheduled to take effect1. Trump invoked Section 115 of the Graham Act to certify that waiving sanctions was in the US national interest2. The Graham Act duties included tariffs of up to 500% on Russian goods2. They also included tariffs of up to 100% on the five largest importers of Russian crude oil2. According to the Centre for Research on Energy and Clean Air, China receives about 50% of Russia's crude exports, while India receives about 37%1.

US Secretary of State Marco Rubio defended Trump's 60-day waiver decision2. Rubio said India has voluntarily diversified its energy portfolio but still depends substantially on Russian energy2. Trump posted on Truth Social that his decision was aimed at avoiding driving Russia away from peace negotiations3. Trump also temporarily waived sanctions on Russian diesel to allow fuel into global markets and lower prices3.

Russian envoy Kirill Dmitriev called the order an important and truly historic moment1. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky told NBC News that siding with Russian President Vladimir Putin would be a historic mistake1.

Protests erupt in India over voter roll revisions and election official

Protesters staged widespread demonstrations in India against Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar after a voter roll investigation1. Protests began following a September 23 investigation regarding Election Commissioners' objections to roll revisions1. Civil society organisations including Jan Sarokar, MKSS, Voter Adhikar Manch, and NFIW held a Jan Sunwai public hearing in Batla House2.

A public audit of 258 households in Delhi's Okhla constituency found over 100 voters wrongly classified2. The audit showed these voters were marked as absent, shifted, dead, or duplicate2. In the Okhla constituency, over 67,000 names were placed on the notice list out of 1.85 lakh draft roll voters2. Nearly 1.6 lakh names in Okhla were placed on the ASDD list2.

Political groups and police clashed during related demonstrations. Delhi Police registered three FIRs against protesters for violating prohibitory orders, conducting a flag march, and obstructing police officers3. CRPF Director General GP Singh commended a female CRPF officer for demonstrating restraint after being slapped during a Cockroach Janta Party march3. Mumbai Police detained several people at Carter Road in Bandra who gathered for a Tiranga March called by the Cockroach Janta Party1.

Cockroach Janta Party leaders Saurav Das and Ashutosh Ranka were remanded to five days of judicial custody on October 144. Das and Ranka were detained after scaling locked gates at Jantar Mantar4. Cockroach Janta Party founder Abhijeet Dipke accused authorities of using delaying tactics to prolong the detention of Das and Ranka4.

In a related digital rule development, the Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology issued an advisory on October 10 asking Meta to review its content recommendation systems5. Apar Gupta of the Internet Freedom Foundation criticized the MeitY advisory, calling it a continuing censorship directive5.

Tragic stampede at Kolkata Durga Puja pandal kills at least 17

A deadly stampede occurred at a Durga Puja pandal in Kolkata on Saturday evening1. Reports gave different casualty figures for the incident. The Hindustan Times reported that at least 19 people were killed and 25 were injured1. West Bengal Chief Minister Suvendu Adhikari reported that at least 17 people were killed, including a seven-year-old girl2. The stampede happened at around 7:40 PM during Saptami celebrations amid sudden rainfall3.

Police are examining video footage capturing a microphone warning that the Chorbagan pandal was in poor condition and might collapse4. Local residents in Chorebagan started rescue efforts and carried injured victims to cabs to reach Calcutta Medical College and Hospital5.

Chief Minister Adhikari announced a Special Investigation Team headed by a sitting or retired High Court judge to probe the stampede1. Police arrested seven people under charges dealing with culpable homicide not amounting to murder1. The West Bengal government announced a ₹25 lakh ex-gratia payment and a Group D job for the next of kin of each deceased victim1. Prime Minister Narendra Modi announced ₹2 lakh compensation from the PMNRF for families of the deceased and ₹50,000 for each injured person3. Kolkata Police Commissioner Ajay Kumar Nand imposed Section 163 prohibitory orders on roads adjoining the pandal until October 204. Prohibitory orders under Section 163 of the BNSS were also imposed in parts of Bowbazar until Tuesday3.
