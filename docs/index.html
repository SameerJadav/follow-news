Police shoot dead suspect after fatal Berlin Pride vehicle and knife attack

German police have shot and killed a suspect after a fatal vehicle and knife attack at a Berlin Pride event on Saturday night1. One woman was killed and at least 29 other people were injured when a privately owned van was driven into a crowd near Tiergarten park1. Police spokesperson Florian Nath said the van drove parallel to a main road through the park and hit several people before crashing into a tree2.

The suspect was identified as Abdul Ballout, a 21-year-old German citizen of Lebanese background who was previously radicalised and known to police1. In May 2026, Ballout received a sentence of one year and 10 months in prison for preparing a serious act of violence endangering the state, but his sentence was deferred under parole supervision1. His father, Tawfik, told Der Spiegel that his son was brainwashed and that their family did not support Islamic State3. Around 6 p.m., police shot Ballout at an allotment garden complex in Spandau after he approached officers carrying a bladed weapon and rushed at them1.

German Chancellor Friedrich Merz called the event a "heinous" attack1. German President Frank-Walter Steinmeier described it as an attack on Germany's open society and way of life2. The flag at the German Bundestag was lowered to half-mast3. However, Alternative for Germany lawmaker Martin Hess blamed the attack on a complete failure of government security policies5. In 2016, a hijacked truck killed 13 people at a Berlin Christmas market4.

US and Iran pause military strikes as diplomatic efforts resume

The United States and Iran have paused military strikes for a second consecutive day after two weeks of nightly attacks, opening a potential space for diplomacy1. US Ambassador to the United Nations Mike Waltz told Fox News that President Donald Trump was giving potential diplomatic talks a little bit of room1.

Reports indicate that several military and strategic factors contributed to the pause in fighting. The New York Times reported that President Trump paused his escalation plans because of concerns over diminishing stockpiles of United States missile interceptors and air defence munitions1. Additionally, news outlet Axios reported that Admiral Bradley Cooper informed Donald Trump that the US military strike campaign against Iran had reached the limit of its effectiveness2. During a meeting in the Oval Office, Vice-President JD Vance and Joint Chiefs Chair Dan Caine also warned President Trump about the risks of launching a renewed military campaign against Iran2.

At the same time, regional tensions and maritime disruptions continue to create serious challenges. Iranian media reported that an oil tanker exploded in the Strait of Hormuz after it left an Iranian-approved navigation route and struck a naval mine1. In response to diplomatic efforts around the waterway, Iranian Foreign Ministry spokesperson Esmaeil Baqaei said deputy foreign ministers from Iran and Oman held multiple rounds of talks in Tehran regarding the Strait of Hormuz3. Meanwhile, Iranian-backed Houthi rebels in Yemen threatened a blockade of Saudi shipping in the Bab al-Mandeb strait4. After Houthi forces announced a blockade on Saudi oil in the Red Sea, benchmark Brent crude oil prices rose above $100 per barrel5.

Further complicating international relations, Iran summoned Ukraine's chargé d'affaires after accusing Ukrainian forces of striking an Iranian merchant ship in the Caspian Sea and killing one sailor3. Looking toward upcoming diplomatic meetings, Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu scheduled a security cabinet meeting ahead of his planned visit to the White House on Tuesday2.

Wildfires force evacuations of over 300,000 people across France and Spain

Massive wildfires have forced the evacuation of more than 330,000 people across France and Spain1. In south-west France, a wildfire has burned 42,000 hectares, creating one of the largest forest fires in the country since World War Two1. At least 240 homes in the Gironde department have been completely destroyed2, and about 55,000 people were evacuated overnight into Sunday from villages south of Bordeaux1. Bordeaux Airport faced extensive delays and cancellations on Sunday afternoon because air traffic control put restrictions in place to assist emergency firefighting operations1. During the evacuations, police in Eysines arrested a man on Saturday for allegedly stealing 44 bottles of grand cru wine from an evacuated home3.

In Spain, more than 90,000 people have been evacuated from regions surrounding Madrid and Ávila1. One civilian was killed on Saturday in a wildfire in Spain's Valencia region1. Spanish Prime Minister Pedro Sanchez stated that approximately 130,000 hectares of land have burned in Spain so far this year4. Spain received international firefighting aid, including two Canadair aircraft from Greece, two from Italy, and over 100 military personnel from Portugal5. Madrid regional president Isabel Díaz Ayuso announced a €1m emergency fund for affected livestock farmers2. Pope Leo IV offered prayers and expressed solidarity during his Sunday Angelus prayer5.

Typhoon Noul brings torrential rains and fatal flash floods to China

Typhoon Noul has struck southern China, causing massive travel disruptions and severe weather warnings1. The typhoon made landfall in Huidong County, Guangdong province, with maximum sustained winds of 45 metres per second, or 162 kilometres per hour1. Authorities evacuated more than 715,000 people across Guangdong province ahead of the storm's arrival1.

The typhoon caused widespread transport cancellations across the region. More than 700 flights were cancelled at airports in Guangzhou and Shenzhen1. In Hong Kong, authorities cancelled more than 410 flights and 150 high-speed train services1. Additionally, 20 flights connecting Singapore, Hong Kong, and Shenzhen were cancelled on Sunday because of weather conditions2.

Severe weather also brought deadly water hazards. In Weiyuan County, Gansu province, a sudden flash flood triggered by a cloudburst killed 10 tourists and injured 23 others on Sunday afternoon1. China's Ministry of Water Resources and Meteorological Administration jointly issued a red warning for flash floods across parts of several provinces, including Jiangxi, Hunan, and Sichuan2. To help recovery, China's NDRC allocated 100 million yuan, or 14.7 million US dollars, from the central budget for post-disaster infrastructure restoration1.

PM Modi creates exam reform task force after minister resigns over leaks

Indian Prime Minister Narendra Modi has announced major steps to reform the country's national examination system following widespread political fallout and public protests over exam paper leaks1. On Saturday, Dharmendra Pradhan resigned as Union Education Minister following student protests over the NEET UG 2026 paper leak1. Union Minister Pralhad Joshi officially took charge of the Ministry of Education at Kartavya Bhavan on Sunday1.

To rebuild public trust, Prime Minister Modi announced the creation of a high-powered task force led by Nandan Nilekani to make India's examination system leak-proof1. Prime Minister Modi also stated that new legislation tightening laws and penalties for exam paper leaks will be introduced in Parliament on Monday2. Following these official commitments, the youth activist group Cockroach Janta Party called off its protests on Saturday after the government accepted its demands2.

However, anger remains high regarding the police response to student demonstrations. Opposition leader Rahul Gandhi sent a letter to Home Minister Amit Shah demanding accountability for police actions against student protesters in Delhi on July 203. During those student demonstrations, 19-year-old student Sahil Lochab was hit by pellet guns and now faces the potential loss of an eye3.

In a separate political reaction following campaigns over paper leaks, the newly formed E20 Janta Party demanded that the government provide consumers an option to buy 100 percent petrol4. The political party also called for the resignation of minister Nitin Gadkari4. Currently, standard E20 ethanol-blended fuel costs Rs 102.12 per litre in Delhi, while ethanol-free 100-octane fuel costs Rs 169 per litre4.

Meanwhile, other youth-focused political proposals are emerging in state politics. Telangana Chief Minister A Revanth Reddy announced that the state assembly will pass a resolution in August to recommend lowering the minimum Lok Sabha candidacy age to 21 from 255.
