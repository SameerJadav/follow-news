Iran offers seven-day diplomatic plan to US to reopen Strait of Hormuz

Iran offered the United States a seven-day plan to reopen the Strait of Hormuz and resume nuclear talks1.

Iran sent its diplomatic proposal to Washington through Qatari mediation1. Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian hosted the prime minister of Qatar early on Friday2.

Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi outlined the seven-day proposal in New York on Thursday, September 242. He spoke during a private gathering on the sidelines of the UN General Assembly2. Araghchi said that the choice now rests with the US to approve the plan3.

Under the Iranian proposal, initial steps would take four to five days1. The Strait of Hormuz would then reopen on day six1. Talks between Iran and the US on a final deal would begin on day seven1.

The plan requires the US to meet several conditions before the waterway opens1. The US must lift its naval blockade of Iranian ports1. The US must also waive sanctions on Iranian oil sales1. In addition, the US must observe a ceasefire that includes Lebanon1.

The US and Israel launched air strikes on Iran on February 284. Iran retaliated against Israel and against US bases in the Gulf4. Iran also attacked US Arab allies and shipping in the Strait of Hormuz4. Before the war started, about a fifth of the world's traded oil and gas passed through the Strait of Hormuz1.

Recent shipping data from analytics firm Kpler showed a ten-day average of about 18 transits per day through the strait4. Daily ship numbers dropped as low as nine vessels4.

US President Donald Trump said he is in no rush to reach a deal with Iran1. Earlier in the week, Trump threatened to reach a deal or annihilate the Islamic Republic4.

Oil prices fell more than 2 per cent on Friday as investors hoped for a deal5. The S&P 500 stock index rose 0.5 per cent5.

He warned against genetic manipulation, surrogacy, the organ trade, and assisted dying1. France recently passed legislation allowing adults with incurable illnesses to receive lethal medication4. France also enshrined the right to abortion in its constitution in 20244.

Supreme Court permits Trump administration to use voter database

The US Supreme Court allowed the Trump administration to use a federal database to check voter citizenship status1.

The ruling lifted lower court restrictions on the Systematic Alien Verification for Entitlements program, known as SAVE1. The database was created in 1986 for public benefit checks1. The Trump administration expanded SAVE last year to perform bulk checks using Social Security records1. Currently, 27 US states use the system2.

US Attorney General Todd Blanche called the decision a huge victory for election integrity3. But Justice Ketanji Brown Jackson dissented from the ruling1. She wrote that disenfranchising lawful voters causes real harm while the government suffers no harm1.

In Travis County, Texas, officials checked nearly 100 voters flagged as non-citizens by SAVE1. They found that at least 10 per cent were actually US citizens1. In June, US District Judge Sparkle Sooknanan ruled that the expanded program violated privacy rights4.

The Supreme Court noted that federal law prohibits broad voter purges right before an election1. Legal expert Rick Hasen said the ruling will have little impact on upcoming midterm elections3. A 2024 study found 68 cases of non-citizen voting since 20005.

Protests intensify across India over electoral roll revision

The Indian National Congress staged nationwide protests demanding the resignation of Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar1.

Demonstrators joined widespread political actions across India on September 25, 20261. Police detained student leaders and activists during a protest at Jantar Mantar against Gyanesh Kumar2. India's youth-led Cockroach Janta Party also demanded that the Chief Election Commissioner resign3. The party threatened to launch a nationwide protest if Kumar does not resign within 48 hours2.

Opposition leaders sharply criticized the Special Intensive Revision of electoral rolls1. Maharashtra Navnirman Sena chief Raj Thackeray called for the complete cancellation of the revision process on September 251. He described the revision process as a new weapon in the hands of the Bharatiya Janata Party1. Shiv Sena (UBT) chief Uddhav Thackeray said that re-elections should be held in some states because of voter list anomalies1.

In contrast, the West Bengal BJP chief defended the poll chief4. He said that Gyanesh Kumar deserves the Padma Bhushan award for conducting the revision process4.

Three allies of the ruling National Democratic Alliance urged the Election Commission of India to clarify its position5. The LJP(RV), RLM, and TDP requested clear answers on the electoral process controversy5. LJP(RV) chief Chirag Paswan said that the Election Commission and Gyanesh Kumar must present their side to dispel public doubts5. RLM president Upendra Kushwaha urged the commission to immediately address public confusion over the voter list revision5. TDP parliamentary leader Lavu Sri Krishna Devarayalu said that transparency will strengthen public confidence in the commission5.

The controversy widened after media reports appeared in The Indian Express5. The newspaper revealed that two top election commissioners raised objections multiple times5. They objected to official decisions made without their knowledge or consent5. In response, the Election Commission said that differing internal views are normal5. The commission emphasized that its final decisions were unanimous5.

Questions have also arisen regarding the appointment process of election commissioners2. Under a 2023 law, the selection committee consists of the prime minister, a Union Cabinet minister, and the leader of the Opposition in the Lok Sabha2. Supreme Court Justice Dipankar Datta said during a hearing that the law did not appear to ensure an independent selection process2. Justice Satish Chandra Sharma disagreed with Justice Datta on the bench2.

Court records show links involving Justice Sharma and the Election Commission2. Justice Sharma's son, Siddharth Sharma, works as standing counsel for the Election Commission in Madhya Pradesh2. Justice Sharma also headed a bench that overturned a compensation order against District Magistrate Medha Roopam2. Roopam is the daughter of Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar2.
