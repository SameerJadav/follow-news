Death Toll Rises Past 260 Following Major 7.4 Earthquake in Western Colombia

A magnitude 7.4 earthquake struck western Colombia shortly after 7:30am local time on Monday1. The epicenter of the powerful quake was located in the Choco region near San Jose del Palmar at a depth of 100 kilometers2. Colombian authorities reported on Thursday that more than 150 aftershocks occurred following the main earthquake1.

The casualty count has grown rapidly as search teams assess damage across the impacted zones. Government figures indicate that at least 273 people were killed, at least 3,800 were injured, and 377 are listed as missing following the earthquake3.

Healthcare infrastructure suffered severe damage in major urban areas. Approximately 50 percent of the University Hospital of the Valle Evaristo Garcia in Cali was structurally damaged by the earthquake3. City authorities in Cali declared a red alert for hospitals as public hospital capacity reached 100 percent on Monday3.

International aid efforts have begun, alongside diplomatic disagreements regarding rescue teams. Colombian President Abelardo de la Espriella announced that Colombia will receive a $10m donation from the United Arab Emirates4. Colombia also accepted 58.5 tonnes of aid from Mexico following the earthquake4. However, Mexican President Claudia Sheinbaum stated that Colombia refused entry to a Mexican military rescue team by requesting additional certification4.

This deadly quake follows another major regional disaster, as an earthquake double-event in Venezuela on June 24 killed more than 6,300 people in 20262.

Despite the frequent halts to business, Parliament passed 12 bills during the Monsoon session, including the Public Examination (Prevention of Unfair Means) Amendment Bill1. The legislative session was heavily impacted by fallout from national examination controversies. Dharmendra Pradhan resigned as Union Education Minister following student protests over the NEET-UG 2026 paper leak2.
