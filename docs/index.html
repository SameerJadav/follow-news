Eritrea cuts diplomatic ties with Ethiopia after diplomat expulsions and rebel support claims

Eritrea cut all diplomatic ties with Ethiopia on Thursday following a growing political dispute1.

The decision came after Ethiopia expelled Eritrean diplomats from its capital, Addis Ababa1. Ethiopia declared 10 Eritrean diplomats persona non grata1. These diplomats included the charge d'affaires and officials accredited to the African Union1. Ethiopia gave the 10 diplomats 48 hours to leave the country1.

The official break in diplomatic ties occurred on October 1, 20262. Both countries accused each other of stoking internal conflicts2. Ethiopia's Ministry of Foreign Affairs accused Eritrea, Sudan, and Egypt of backing a new rebel alliance1. Ethiopia said these countries are supporting an opposition group led by the Tigray People's Liberation Front1.

Eritrea denied these claims3. Eritrean Information Minister Yemane Gebremeskel said Eritrea is not backing the newly formed rebel alliance3. The opposition coalition against Ethiopia's federal government includes several armed groups4. It includes the Tigray People's Liberation Front and the Oromo Liberation Army4. It also includes a faction of the Ogaden National Liberation Front4. Militant groups from Benishangul-Gumuz and Afar have joined the alliance as well4.

Fighting has continued to cause civilian deaths in the region3. A medic in the southern Tigrayan town of Alamata reported that 52 civilians died during three days of heavy fighting3. In response to rising security concerns, Ethiopian security services took new measures3. Ethiopia's National Intelligence and Security Service announced an indefinite ban on drone flights3. This ban applies to Addis Ababa and surrounding areas3.

The conflict comes after a history of long tension and changing relations between the two nations1. Ethiopia annexed Eritrea in 19621. Eritrea secured its independence in 19931. President Isaias Afwerki led an armed struggle that won independence for Eritrea1. Relations improved when Ethiopian Prime Minister Abiy Ahmed came to power in 20181. Abiy moved to normalise diplomatic relations with President Afwerki1.

Later, a major civil war broke out inside Ethiopia4. The civil war lasted two years between the Ethiopian federal government and the Tigray People's Liberation Front4. An African Union mediator estimated that 600,000 lives were lost during the civil war between 2020 and 20223. The 2022 Pretoria Peace Agreement ended that civil war4. Now, new fighting and accusations have broken the peace between the two neighboring countries2.

French prime minister holds crisis meeting as student protests cause widespread violence

Interior Minister Laurent Nunez launched an internal police investigation over allegations of excessive force against three students4.
