European Union Faces Border Crisis as Thousands Enter Spanish Enclave of Ceuta

A massive influx of migrants into the Spanish enclave of Ceuta has triggered a political crisis across Europe and led to strict border controls1. Ceuta and Melilla are autonomous Spanish cities located on the northern coast of Africa3. Together, they form the only land borders that the European Union shares on the African mainland3. Since July 29, nearly 60,000 migrants breached the border into Ceuta1. At least 67 people died while attempting to reach the enclave from Morocco during the mass surge3.

By August 1, the Spanish Interior Ministry reported that nearly all of the 60,000 migrants who crossed the border had departed Ceuta1. More than 48,000 of them returned to Morocco within 48 hours of crossing4. Despite these departures, Juan Jesús Vivas, the president of Ceuta, stated that several thousand people still need to be returned5. Vivas added that the situation inside the enclave has not fully returned to normal5.

To stop people from swimming into the enclave, Spanish Civil Guard officers began laying a 500-metre floating pneumatic barrier along the Tarajal breakwater in Ceuta4. However, Spanish authorities faced legal limits on immediate returns. A Spanish Supreme Court ruling published on July 8 barred authorities from immediately returning migrants who arrive by sea3.

The events in Ceuta quickly spread political tension across Europe. Leaders from 22 of the 27 EU member states signed an open letter demanding an urgent video meeting of interior ministers to coordinate a joint response1. Italian Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni posted on X that defending the Union's external borders is a shared European responsibility1. Italy also temporarily suspended its Schengen-area border-free agreement with Spain for one month following the migrant surge into Ceuta2.

Spanish Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez strongly criticized the border decision made by other EU members. Sánchez described the Schengen suspension as a selfish, polarising, and unlawful reaction2.

Russian Missiles Hit Kyiv as Ukraine Sinks Russian Ship in Black Sea

An overnight Russian missile and drone barrage against Kyiv killed nine people and injured over 30 others on August 11. In Kyiv's Darnytskyi district, seven people were killed and 14 others were injured after strikes caused widespread fires2. The attack also caused damage to Lithuania's embassy in Kyiv when Russian missiles struck near the compound1. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky reported that Russia launched 35 missiles and 185 attack drones during the raid1. He noted that Ukrainian forces intercepted only one of 27 ballistic missiles1. Zelensky later appealed on social media, writing that every package of ballistic missile interceptors saves lives2.

Meanwhile, Ukrainian forces launched strikes against Russian targets far from the frontline. A sanctioned Russian container ship was sunk in the Black Sea following an attack by Ukrainian drones1. Russian state nuclear operator Rosatom confirmed that its 100,000-tonne container ship, the Yanina, was sunk 130 miles from Novorossiysk3. Additionally, Ukrainian drone strikes hit three oil refineries in Russia's Bashkortostan region, roughly 1,000 miles from the front lines1. In the United States, President Donald Trump stated during a Cabinet meeting at Camp David that the US had not yet agreed to allow Ukraine to produce Patriot missiles under license3.

Famed Mountaineer Nirmal Purja Among 10 Killed in Pakistan Avalanche

Renowned British-Nepali mountaineer Nirmal Purja and nine other expedition members died in an avalanche on Broad Peak in Pakistan1. Broad Peak in Northern Pakistan is 26,414 feet high, making it the 12th-highest mountain in the world1. Purja had served in the British military for 16 years, including 10 years in the Special Boat Service, and was awarded an MBE in 20182. In 2019, Purja became the first person to climb all 14 world mountains above 8,000 meters in six months and six days1.

Pakistani rescue officials recovered the bodies of three climbers following the disaster2. The recovered victims were identified as American mountaineer Mallory Geis, 39, of San Antonio, Texas; Nadhira Ahmed Abdullah Al Harthy of Oman; and Pur Bahadur Gurung of Nepal1. Nepal's Prime Minister Balendra Shah expressed shock at the tragedy in the Karakoram range, which claimed the lives of six Nepalese and four foreign climbers2.

Football Governing Bodies Express No Confidence in FIFA President Infantino

European and international football governing bodies have declared a loss of confidence in FIFA President Gianni Infantino following a controversial World Cup investment proposal1. Infantino proposed FIFA Forward Enterprise, a plan to value a new commercial subsidiary at $20 billion and raise up to $4.2 billion from private investors1. Infantino withdrew the proposal to allow private equity investment following widespread backlash1.

Despite the withdrawal of the plan, major football bodies condemned FIFA's leadership. UEFA declared that FIFA leadership under Infantino had lost the confidence of European football and other governing bodies1. The Dutch Football Association (KNVB) declared a fundamental breach of trust and stated it no longer has confidence in Infantino3. CONCACAF condemned the proposal as a unilateral act of poor governance and called for a full review of Infantino's leadership2. Carlos Cordeiro, special adviser to Infantino and senior adviser to the White House Task Force on the 2026 World Cup, resigned amid the backlash4. However, Qatar Football Association President Sheikh Hamad bin Khalifa bin Ahmed Al Thani applauded Infantino's decision to scrap the plan to prioritize unity among member associations2.

Sightseeing Plane Crashes Near Nazca Lines in Peru, Killing All 13 Aboard

A tourist sightseeing plane crashed near the Nazca Lines in southern Peru on August 1, killing all 13 people on board1. The aircraft carried 11 passengers and two pilots when it went down in Pueblo Viejo after departing from Pisco1. The Cessna Grand Caravan aircraft crashed at 1:20 pm local time after losing contact with the control tower minutes after departure2. The plane belonged to local Peruvian sightseeing airline Aerodiana1.

Local authorities identified the crash victims as seven Italians, two Germans, two Spanish citizens, and two Peruvian crew members2. Flight safety in the region has been a recurring concern, as previous fatal crashes over the Nazca Lines occurred in 2022, 2010, and 2008 due to safety concerns surrounding small tourist flights2.

Political Row Grows in India Over Youth Protests and Parliament Skit

Political dispute has intensified in India following recent student protests, allegations of police violence, and a controversial political skit performed inside Parliament1. The tensions involve opposition political leaders, independent lawmakers, and student party organizers1.

One part of the dispute stems from student demonstrations and police action during a march on July 201. Cockroach Janta Party founder Abhijeet Dipke demanded a public apology from Prime Minister Narendra Modi over alleged police violence against student protesters during that July 20 march1. Further protests took place at Jantar Mantar, where some demonstrators targeted the Prime Minister3. Prime Minister Narendra Modi released a video message addressing the demonstrations3. In his statement, Modi said he forgave students who used abusive language against him during protests at Jantar Mantar, calling them misguided children3.

At the same time, severe political pushback emerged over an opposition protest in Parliament2. During the protest near Makar Dwar in Parliament, Independent MP Pappu Yadav wore saffron robes and performed a skit portraying a priest stealing money from donation boxes4. The skit mocked Ram temple donation theft2.

The performance prompted legal action from authorities and complaints from ruling party leaders2. Police in Varanasi registered an FIR against Opposition leader Rahul Gandhi, as well as MPs Pappu Yadav and Awadhesh Prasad, over the skit mocking Ram temple donation theft in Parliament2. Additionally, BJP MP Bansuri Swaraj filed a police complaint in Delhi against Opposition MPs2. Swaraj accused the lawmakers of making a mockery of Sanatan culture during their Parliament protest2.

Torrential Rain Triggers Landslides and Severe Flooding in Kerala

Incessant heavy rain has caused deadly landslides and severe flooding across Kerala, killing at least six people1. Four of the recorded deaths occurred in Idukki and Kottayam districts1. Two people, including a swimming instructor, were reported missing in rain-related incidents in Kannur district2.

The extreme weather caused severe disruption across multiple areas. In Kozhikode district, flooding and landslips forced the evacuation of 214 people into eight relief camps3. Authorities in Kozhikode also imposed traffic restrictions on heavy and non-essential vehicles along the Thamarassery ghat road due to landslide risks3. In Pala, located in Kottayam district, floodwaters submerged a poultry farm and killed approximately 2,500 chickens1. Kerala Chief Minister V.D. Satheesan confirmed that 17 houses were completely destroyed and 127 suffered partial damage across the state4.

Heavy rainfall is expected to continue across the region. The India Meteorological Department issued a red alert forecasting extremely heavy rainfall for eight districts in Kerala, including Pathanamthitta, Idukki, and Kozhikode1.
