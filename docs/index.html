United States and Canada pause 50 percent tariff threat after reaching trade deal

The United States and Canada have reached a temporary agreement to pause a major tariff threat against Canadian imports1. US President Donald Trump announced late on Tuesday on Truth Social that he paused proposed 50% tariffs against Canada for a three-day period2. The new 50% US tariffs threatened by the administration would have covered approximately $20 billion worth of Canadian goods2.

Following the announcement, US Trade Representative Jamieson Greer held a 45-minute meeting with his Canadian counterpart on Wednesday1. Greer expressed optimism about the outcome of the discussions1. He stated that officials feel confident they reached an agreement that will protect American workers, jobs, and supply chains while strengthening the North American economy1. On Wednesday afternoon, Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney met with his cabinet and Canadian provincial leaders to brief them on the trade deal1.

The pause comes after months of trade friction between the two neighboring nations. In February 2025, the White House hit Canada with a 25% tariff3. US officials cited inadequate progress by Canada in curbing cross-border illegal immigration and drug trafficking as the reason for those tariffs3. Trade between Canada and the United States is extensive, reaching an estimated $909 billion in 2024 according to the Office of the US Trade Representative3. Canada is heavily reliant on this economic relationship, as nearly 72% of Canada's goods exports went to the United States last year2.

Canadian public opinion has shown strong support for standing firm against American economic pressure. A poll by the firm Leger found that 56% of surveyed Canadians want Canada to take a hardline approach in trade negotiations with the United States1. Canadian authorities have previously taken direct action against US trade measures1. Last year, most Canadian provinces imposed a ban on US alcohol sales in retaliation to US tariffs1. Cross-border energy projects have also seen past cancellations3. Owner TC Energy halted the 1,200-mile Keystone XL pipeline project in 2021 after US President Joe Biden revoked a key permit3.

Israeli military opens criminal probe after admitting forces shot at car carrying Hind Rajab

The Israeli military admitted on Wednesday that its troops opened fire in 2024 on a car carrying five-year-old Hind Rajab and six of her family members in Gaza1. Hind Rajab died in January 2024 after making an emergency phone call to the Palestinian Red Crescent while trapped inside the vehicle2. Palestine Red Crescent operator Rana Faqih stayed on the line for hours with the child while appealing to the Israeli army to allow an ambulance to reach her3.

A 2024 Al Jazeera investigation indicated an Israeli tank was likely 13 to 23 metres away from the vehicle4. Research agency Forensic Architecture and Earshot mapped 335 bullet holes on the body of the car5. Her story drew international attention, and the docudrama 'The Voice of Hind Rajab', directed by Kaouther Ben Hania, premiered at the 82nd Venice International Film Festival on September 3, 20252.

The Israel Defense Forces stated that it has been conducting intensive combat across numerous fronts in an exceptionally complex operational reality for more than a thousand days3. In a separate decision, the IDF ordered a military police criminal investigation into the March 2025 killing of 15 Palestinian medics and humanitarian personnel near Rafah1. However, the military decided not to open a criminal investigation into April 2024 airstrikes that killed seven World Central Kitchen aid workers1. In June, a UN commission reported that Israeli forces have killed more than 20,000 children and injured 44,000 in Gaza since October 20235.

Helicopter crash in Kenya kills seven people including five American citizens

A Eurocopter EC130 B4 helicopter crashed in Samburu county, Kenya, at approximately 09:13 local time on Wednesday, killing all seven people on board1. The crash occurred near Mount Ololokwe2. Emergency teams from the Kenya Red Cross participated in a multiagency response effort at the site3. Tropic Air Kenya dispatched two of its helicopters to assist in search and rescue operations2. By Wednesday evening, three bodies remained trapped in the wreckage due to ongoing flames3.

A US State Department spokesperson confirmed that five of the seven people killed in the crash were US citizens2. Among the victims was 55-year-old Jose Suarez, the president and general manager of several Telemundo stations in Florida2. Ecuador's Strategic Intelligence Center Director General Michele Sensi-Contugi and his wife Stephany Hollihan were also killed in the incident1.

Helicopter crashes have previously claimed high-profile lives in the region. In April 2024, Kenyan military chief Gen Francis Omondi Ogolla died in an army helicopter crash in western Kenya1.

Former Ukrainian defence minister calls for elections as official is dismissed in corruption probe

Former Ukrainian defence minister Mykhailo Fedorov posted a video on YouTube on Tuesday calling for wartime presidential elections in Ukraine1. Fedorov was fired as defence minister in mid-July, just six months after his appointment2. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy confirmed Yevhenii Khmara as the new defence minister following Fedorov's dismissal1. Elections have been suspended in Ukraine under martial law, which has been in effect since Russia launched its full-scale invasion in February 20222.

Fedorov's call comes during ongoing government anti-corruption investigations. On Wednesday, Ukraine's deputy head of the presidential office Iryna Mudra was dismissed amid anti-corruption searches1. Ukraine's anti-corruption agency NABU reported investigating a conspiracy involving the laundering of 150 million hryvnias through a state-owned bank to post bail for former energy minister Herman Haluschenko3. In late 2025, former presidential chief aide Andriy Yermak resigned following a search of his home, and he was arrested in May on suspicion of money laundering3.

Meanwhile, violent fighting continues in the country. On Wednesday, a Russian drone strike hit a minibus in the southern city of Kherson, killing four people1.

Fire at Kolkata hotel building leaves nine people dead including four Bangladeshi citizens

A major fire broke out at around 1:45 am on Wednesday in a five-storey building housing several hotels on Mirza Ghalib Street in Kolkata, killing nine people1. West Bengal Fire and Emergency Services Minister Kaushik Chowdhury stated that firefighters saved around 80 lives during the blaze1. Kolkata Police reported that 60 people were rescued from the hotel building2.

Four of the Bangladeshi nationals who died in the fire were identified as journalist Debashish Dutta, his wife Afsana Shimmin, their three-year-old son Sharnashish Dutta, and Md Akram Ali3.

The tragedy in Kolkata comes just days after another fatal hotel fire in the state. Two days prior to the Kolkata fire, a hotel fire in Tarapith, located in the Birbhum district, also killed nine people2.

Court sends former Delhi minister Satyendar Jain to 14-day judicial custody in corruption case

The Rouse Avenue Court remanded former Delhi minister Satyendar Jain and four other accused individuals to 14 days of judicial custody on Wednesday1. The court order was issued in connection with an ongoing corruption investigation involving the Delhi Jal Board1. In the same court proceeding, accused DJB employee Ankit Shrivastava was remanded to two days of Anti-Corruption Branch custody1.

The arrests occurred a day prior to the court ruling. On Tuesday, the Anti-Corruption Branch arrested former Delhi Jal Board Chief Executive Officer Udit Prakash Rai along with Satyendar Jain and four others2. The Anti-Corruption Branch alleged that Udit Prakash Rai and his family received Rs 1.52 crore in bribes linked to sewage treatment plant tenders2.

The legal actions triggered political responses in the capital. AAP national convenor Arvind Kejriwal alleged that Union Home Minister Amit Shah directly ordered the arrest of Satyendar Jain1.
