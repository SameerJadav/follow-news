Iran prepares terms to reopen Strait of Hormuz during US conflict

Iranian official Mohsen Rezaei announced that Iran is drawing up conditions to reopen the Strait of Hormuz1. Mediators asked Tehran to set out its terms1. Rezaei serves as secretary of Iran's Supreme National Security Council2. He said Iran exported 80 million barrels of oil during a ceasefire period3.

Under a June memorandum of understanding, Iran agreed to clear mines1. Iran also agreed to permit free vessel passage in the Strait of Hormuz for 60 days1. In return, the United States agreed to lift its blockade1. The US also agreed to ease sanctions1.

US President Donald Trump rejected re-establishing the terms of that June agreement4. Oil prices rose after reports of his decision4. Trump declared in the Oval Office that US forces had cleared mines from the Strait of Hormuz1. He said that the waterway was open1. Trump later told Al Jazeera that he has no time schedule for negotiations with Iran1. He said he is in no hurry to resume talks1.

US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent announced sanctions named Operation Economic Outcast2. The sanctions target nearly 60 Iran-linked individuals, entities, and vessels2. The measures aim to pressure Iran during the war with the US2. Rezaei warned that Iran will target US economic interests 100 percent if the US blockade continues2. He said Iran will act with intensity and force2.

Iranian Foreign Ministry spokesperson Esmail Baghaei said Iran has the legitimate right to target the origin of any act of aggression2. He pointed specifically to US refueling aircraft departing from Bulgaria2. Qatari Prime Minister Mohammed bin Abdulrahman bin Jassim Al Thani met Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian in Tehran3. They met to discuss regional security and conditions for dialogue3.

The conflict has affected military readiness elsewhere. US defense and NATO officials said the US military faces a critical shortage of Patriot missile interceptors in Europe5. The shortage happened because interceptor stocks were redeployed to the Middle East for the war with Iran5. The Wall Street Journal reported that CIA Director John Ratcliffe traveled to Moscow5. He went to warn Russia not to attack NATO countries5.

In Australia, environmental authorities face avian health issues. A common dolphin found near Goolwa in South Australia tested positive for H5 bird flu4. It is the second native mammal species in Australia confirmed with the virus4. The Australian government decided to withdraw 24 expeditioners from its Macquarie Island research station4. The government took action because of psychosocial risks from an expected H5 avian influenza mortality event4.

Trump orders federal agencies to rename Lake Ontario as Lake America

US President Donald Trump signed an executive order directing the Department of the Interior to rename Lake Ontario as Lake America1. Lake Ontario lies along the border separating New York from the Canadian province of Ontario2. The name originates from the Huron Indigenous word oniatari:io, meaning lake of shining waters3.

The US Geological Survey updated the name in its Geographic Names Information System1. Democratic Representative Debbie Dingell of Michigan announced plans to introduce legislation in Congress to nullify the order4.

Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney said on social media that Canadians know things must be called by their name4. Carney said the lake is called Lake Ontario today and forever4.

Trump previously issued an executive order renaming the Gulf of Mexico as the Gulf of America4. That order led to a legal dispute with Mexico and a lawsuit by the Associated Press4. Trump also posted an altered 1948 Life magazine graphic on social media1. The graphic placed him at the top under a new category named greatest1.

Trade tensions between the countries remain high. The US put 50% tariffs on $20 billion worth of Canadian goods3. The tariffs were imposed after trade negotiations collapsed between Washington and Ottawa3. Canadian trade minister Dominic LeBlanc said on X that the US withdrew demands on French language labeling4.

Meta agrees to multi-billion settlements over youth safety claims

Tech company Meta agreed to pay major financial settlements to resolve state and federal child safety lawsuits in the United States1. One report stated Meta agreed to pay $18 billion to settle a lawsuit from 29 US states1. Another report put the 29-state settlement figure at $16.68 billion3.

Meta also reached a broader deal covering 47 states and territories for up to $17.1 billion in penalties4. That deal includes a separate $1 billion settlement in Texas4. Meta agreed to pay up to $17 billion to settle a federal lawsuit brought by California, Colorado, Kentucky, and New Jersey2. Judge Yvonne Gonzalez Rogers approved the multi-state agreement in federal court in Oakland, California4.

As part of the settlement, Meta agreed to implement a default two-hour daily time limit for users under 182. Meta will also set a default block on nighttime use2. It will ban displaying like and reaction counts2.

British minister Pat McFadden said the UK does not want American children receiving higher protection than British children4.

In 2025, Meta generated $198.8 billion in revenue and $102.5 billion in operating profit3.

Glacial collapse triggers fatal flood in Nepal and Tibet region

A massive glacial collapse triggered sudden flash floods along the Nepal-Tibet border, killing hundreds of people and leaving many missing1. The US Geological Survey clarified that a collapsed glacier caused the disaster2. The impact of the falling glacier hitting the valley floor registered a seismic magnitude of 5.22.

An analysis by ICIMOD found that an ice-rock avalanche entered the Lende Khola tributary2. The flood caused downstream river levels to rise by 7 to 9 metres within 30 minutes2. The disaster occurred in Nepal's northern Rasuwa district without earlier rainfall in the immediate area to warn residents3. Flooding struck along the Bhotekoshi and Trishuli river systems1.

Nepal Police reported that 469 people died as of Friday1. Police reported that 1,545 people were rescued or injured1. Authorities deployed 4,348 police personnel for search and rescue operations across affected districts1. Nepali army helicopter rescue teams worked to rescue about 100 people trapped inside a tunnel at the Trishuli 3A hydropower project4.

Hundreds of foreign citizens remain missing in Nepal. Nepal Police reported that missing foreigners include 178 Indians, 65 Americans, 51 Malaysians, 53 Ukrainians, 33 Britons, 35 Australians, and 25 Canadians1. India's Ministry of External Affairs said that 290 Indians in Nepal remain unaccounted for3. The ministry added that about 200 Indians stranded on the Chinese side after the Kailash Mansarovar Yatra sought assistance3.

Across the border in Tibet, Chinese state media reported that at least 558 people were missing4. That number includes at least 260 foreign nationals4. Chinese state broadcaster CCTV reported that 555 stranded tourists and 499 local residents were transported out of the disaster zone in Tibet by Friday morning4.

Individual families are searching for news of loved ones. Rakesh Nayak, a 45-year-old software engineer from Karnataka living in Australia, went missing near the border during an Isha Foundation tour5. He was last known to be inside an immigration office at about 9:10 pm on August 265. Flash floods struck twenty minutes later5. At least 13 people from Karnataka are stranded in Nepal and Tibet5.

Nepal declined search and rescue team deployment offers from foreign countries1. Offers came from India, China, the US, UK, Japan, South Korea, Sri Lanka, and Bangladesh1. The United States pledged $500,000, and the World Health Organization sent emergency medical supplies1. UN humanitarian chief Tom Fletcher released $2 million in emergency funds4. The Red Cross launched an emergency appeal for $31 million to help an estimated 93,000 affected people4.

Indian tribunal approves 99.97% debt haircut for Subhash Chandra

The National Company Law Tribunal approved a personal insolvency plan for Essel Group chairman Subhash Chandra1. The ruling allows Chandra to pay Rs 6.5 crore against creditor claims of over Rs 22,000 crore1. Under the plan, creditors will recover about 0.028% of their claims, or three paise for every Rs 100 owed2. For example, LIC Housing Finance had an admitted claim of Rs 1,322.39 crore, but its approved repayment is Rs 38,09,2942.

NCLT Member Nilesh Sharma approved the plan as a third member after a split verdict by a two-member bench2. Creditors holding 80.8% of voting rights approved the plan3. Dissenting lenders representing about 15% argued that related entities like Veena Investments controlled over 61% of votes3. HDFC Bank said it is exploring an appeal against the 99.97% debt haircut3.

Political opposition leaders criticized the ruling. Congress leader Rahul Gandhi called the tribunal the Neta-Company Loot Tribunal1. Congress leader Jairam Ramesh called the 99.97% debt reduction a mockery of insolvency laws1. Chandra's office said he was only a personal guarantor and had not borrowed money personally1. Chandra served as an Independent MP backed by the BJP from 2016 to 20224.

Narendra Modi plans visit to Uzbekistan and Kyrgyzstan for summit

Indian Prime Minister Narendra Modi will begin a four-day visit to Uzbekistan and the Kyrgyz Republic on August 291. Modi will visit Uzbekistan from August 29 to 30 before traveling to the Kyrgyz Republic from August 31 to September 11.

In Tashkent, Modi will hold talks with Uzbek President Shavkat Mirziyoyev to strengthen their strategic partnership1. Bilateral trade and investment between India and Uzbekistan stand close to $1 billion1. More than 16,000 Indian students study in Uzbekistan, which hosts four Indian university campuses1. Modi will also visit the Lal Bahadur Shastri Memorial and the Mahatma Gandhi Centre1.

Modi will then attend the Shanghai Cooperation Organisation summit in Bishkek2. The summit marks the 25th anniversary of the organization, which India joined in 20173. On the sidelines, Modi will hold a bilateral meeting with Russian President Vladimir Putin2. Ahead of the summit, External Affairs Minister S. Jaishankar met Putin in Moscow2. Putin assured Jaishankar that Russia will continue supplying fertilizers to Indian farmers3.

Modi is also scheduled to host Putin and Chinese President Xi Jinping in India for a BRICS summit on September 12 and 133.
