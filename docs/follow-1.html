US and Iran Exchange Military Strikes as Houthis Target Saudi Arabia

Closed Sat 25 Jul · 0 updates

The full picture

The current military exchanges between the United States and Iran, and Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia, are part of a long-running and complex set of conflicts in the Middle East. These events did not begin suddenly. They stem from years of escalating tensions between the United States and Iran, and a civil war in Yemen that has drawn in regional powers.

The Yemeni civil war began in late 2014. Houthi forces, a Shiite rebel group, took control of Yemen's capital city, Sanaa, in September 2014.1 This led to the resignation of the internationally recognized government. In March 2015, a coalition of Gulf states, led by Saudi Arabia, launched a military intervention. Their goal was to restore the Yemeni government and counter the Houthi advance.1 The United States has provided logistical and intelligence support to this Saudi-led coalition since 2015.1

Iran has been a key supporter of the Houthi movement. This support includes military, financial, and logistical aid.6 While both Iran and the Houthis have officially denied the extent of this assistance, experts say Iran has supplied advanced weapons, military training, and intelligence.6 This support has helped the Houthis develop their military capabilities, including the use of sophisticated missiles and drones.6 The Houthis are considered an informal partner of Iran and part of a broader "Axis of Resistance" against Western and Israeli influence in the region.8

Tensions between the United States and Iran have a long history, marked by periods of crisis and confrontation.11 A significant escalation occurred in February 2026, when a "war" between the United States and Iran began with US-Israeli airstrikes on Iranian targets.13 There was a brief ceasefire in June 2026. However, a battle for control over the Strait of Hormuz, a vital shipping lane for global energy supplies, quickly shattered this truce.14 Iran has been attempting to block the strait, while the United States has been conducting military operations to keep it open.15

In March 2026, the Houthis resumed their attacks against Israel and formally joined the ongoing 2026 Iran war.13 This further linked the Yemeni conflict to the broader regional tensions.

In July 2026, events rapidly escalated. On July 13, Iran reportedly flew Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps commanders and missile equipment to Yemen, according to sources familiar with the matter.7 On July 14, Saudi Arabia reportedly conducted an airstrike on Sanaa International Airport, aiming to prevent an Iranian plane from landing.13 On July 19, the Houthis announced a naval blockade on Saudi Arabia.15 Days later, on July 22, the Houthis attacked two Saudi oil tankers in the Red Sea using missiles and drones. They stated these attacks were to enforce their blockade.16

Saudi Arabia retaliated on July 24, conducting airstrikes on Houthi military targets in the port city of Hodeidah and on Kamaran Island in Yemen.17

As of July 25, 2026, the situation remains highly volatile. The Houthis claimed they launched missile and drone attacks on Saudi Aramco oil facilities in the Red Sea cities of Yanbu and Jizan. They stated these attacks were in response to Saudi airstrikes on Hodeidah and Kamaran Island.16 Saudi air defense systems reportedly intercepted ballistic missiles coming from Yemen.16 Meanwhile, Iran reported its first night without new United States strikes, after nearly two weeks of consecutive nightly attacks by the United States on Iranian military targets.16 These US strikes had targeted Iranian command centers, drone storage facilities, and maritime capabilities.21 United States President Donald Trump stated that he had not yet decided whether to launch major new strikes on Iran. He also mentioned that discussions with Iran were ongoing.15 The United Kingdom Maritime Trade Operations also issued a warning about a reported incident between a tanker and military forces in the Gulf of Oman.16
