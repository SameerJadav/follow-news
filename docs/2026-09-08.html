Alternative for Germany wins state election in Saxony-Anhalt

The Alternative for Germany party won the state election in Saxony-Anhalt1. The party is also known as the AfD1. Outlets reported slightly different final vote totals for the winning party. Al Jazeera reported that the AfD finished on top with about 44% of the vote1. The Times of India reported that the party won 44.5% of the vote2. Preliminary election results showed that the party won 43.8% of the vote3. Those preliminary results leave the party three seats short of an outright majority3.

Ulrich Siegmund is an AfD candidate in Saxony-Anhalt4. Siegmund is 35 years old4. He is a former fragrance salesman4. Germany's domestic intelligence agency has closely watched the political group1. The agency classified the AfD branch in Saxony-Anhalt as a proven right-wing extremist group1.

German Chancellor Friedrich Merz spoke about the election results1. Merz insists there can be no cooperation between mainstream parties and the AfD1. Merz said the AfD victory was the worst electoral defeat his party had suffered in decades4. His party is the Christian Democratic Union, known as the CDU4. Merz said the defeat had shaken the CDU to its very foundations4.

Voter support for the CDU fell sharply during the state vote3. Outlets reported different numbers for the party's final share. The Times of India reported that the Christian Democrats fell to 18.5% of the vote2. The BBC reported that the CDU vote share almost halved to 17.2%3. Voters expressed strong dissatisfaction with Merz during the vote4. An exit poll in Saxony-Anhalt showed that only 9% of voters approved of Merz's performance4.

The state election outcome attracted international attention3. US President Donald Trump posted a screenshot of the projected election results on Truth Social3. In other European news, Italian Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni reached a political milestone2. Meloni's administration has surpassed 1,413 consecutive days in office2.

Thousands attend Belgrade funeral for convicted war criminal Ratko Mladic

Mourners buried convicted Bosnian Serb war criminal Ratko Mladic in Belgrade1. Thousands of people attended the service1. Mladic died in The Hague at age 841. He died while serving a life sentence for genocide, war crimes, and crimes against humanity1. The United Nations war crimes court convicted Mladic of genocide over the 1995 Srebrenica massacre1. Around 8,000 Bosnian Muslim men and boys were killed in that massacre1.

Serbian Patriarch Porfirije personally officiated at the funeral service2. The service took place at the St Luka church in Belgrade2. Organizers provided free bus services from Bosnia and towns across Serbia for people attending3. Fans of football club Red Star Belgrade displayed an image of Mladic before a match against Partizan Belgrade3. Serbian President Aleksandar Vucic did not attend the funeral2. Vucic cited a scheduled meeting with the president of Uzbekistan2.

The military honors at the funeral drew international criticism. An EU spokesman said glorifying war criminals, denying genocide, and revisionism contradict fundamental European values1. EU Enlargement Commissioner Marta Kos canceled a planned visit to Belgrade after the funeral2. Oil prices jump as shipping disruptions hit Strait of Hormuz

Global oil prices rose as military actions disrupted shipping routes in the Middle East1. Brent oil futures rose to around $97 a barrel1. Brent crude is up 9% over five days1. The price is also up 19% over the past month1. US West Texas Intermediate crude rose to $92.27 a barrel1.

Military forces reported attacks on oil infrastructure and vessels1. US forces struck three Iranian oil tankers1. Iran's IRGC said it struck three tankers and three US-linked vessels1. Saudi Aramco's Jizan facilities were struck for the second time in a month1.

The disruptions reduced shipping traffic through a key waterway2. Macquarie analysts estimated that around 7 million barrels per day of crude and refined fuels cross the Strait of Hormuz2. That volume is down from roughly 20 million barrels per day before the conflict2. Iran and Oman are in the final stages of negotiating an agreement2. The two countries want to establish a temporary safe shipping route through the Strait of Hormuz2.

India's average crude oil import basket crossed $100 per barrel on Friday for the first time in six weeks3. The price reached $101.073.

US envoys hold peace talks with leaders in Russia and Ukraine

American envoys held separate peace talks over the weekend with Russian President Vladimir Putin and Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy1. US Special Envoy Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner held discussions in Moscow and Kyiv1. Witkoff and Kushner met Putin in Moscow first1. The envoys then traveled to Kyiv by train from Poland2. Putin declared a three-day ceasefire to ensure the safety of the visiting US envoys2.

Zelenskyy held three rounds of talks with the US envoys in Kyiv2. National security advisers from the UK, France, and Germany joined the final round2. Witkoff said on social media that substantive progress was made1. He said the progress included movement on scheduling additional trilateral talks1. Zelenskyy said Ukraine will need a winter package of military assistance if the war drags into winter2. He said that package must include Patriot missile systems2.

The talks drew criticism within Ukraine. Ukrainian MP Lisa Yasko said Ukraine does not trust the talks3. Yasko called the envoys' flattery towards Putin disgraceful3. In separate human rights news, UN Human Rights Chief Volker Turk called for an urgent ban on fully autonomous weapons1. Turk made the call after reports that autonomous drones killed people in Ukraine1.

Israeli military strikes in southern Lebanon killed dozens of people over several days1. Lebanese health authorities reported that Israeli attacks across southern Lebanon killed at least 27 people over a three-day period2. An Israeli overnight strike hit a building in Kfar Rumman1. That strike killed 11 people, including two children and four women1. A second strike in Kfar Rumman killed an 18-year-old rescuer affiliated with the Amal movement1. That second strike also wounded another person1.

The military strikes followed clashes along the border1. Two explosive drones were launched towards Israeli troops near the Ali al-Taher ridge2. Amazon cargo plane crashes past runway at Miami airport

An Amazon cargo plane crashed after overshooting the runway at Miami International Airport1. The crash killed five people and injured five others1. The aircraft was a Boeing 767-300 operated by 21 Air1.

The plane was operating as Amazon Air flight 75982. It had flown from Cincinnati to Miami, then from Miami to San Juan2. The crash occurred while the plane was returning from San Juan to Miami2.

The plane overran the runway and traveled approximately 1,300 feet past the end of the paved area1. It entered a perimeter area near where Tesla robotaxis were stationed2. During the overshoot, the plane struck two vehicles1. It hit a Ford van carrying seven contract cleaning workers1. It also hit a Toyota Corolla carrying three passengers1.

Investigators have started looking into the cause of the crash. The NTSB recovered the flight data recorder and cockpit voice recorder from the aircraft1. Safety features at the airport have also come under scrutiny2. Miami International Airport is not equipped with engineered materials arresting systems at the end of its runways2.

Seven dead after illegal hostel building collapses in Delhi

A building collapse in the Satya Niketan area of Delhi killed seven people1. Reports gave different details about the age of the structure1. Livemint reported that the building was 55 years old1. Scroll.in reported that the building was 40 to 50 years old2.

The five-storey building housed a boys' paying guest facility1. The facility had two rooms per floor and two beds per room1. A police source cited by PTI said the 55-square-metre structure had no columns or beams for support1. Basement renovation work and water accumulation likely contributed to the collapse1.

Reports also differed on the number of people rescued from the rubble1. Livemint reported that rescue agencies rescued 12 people, of whom seven died1. Scroll.in reported that 11 people were rescued2. Scroll.in also reported that an estimated 30 to 35 students remained trapped under rubble2. One victim was 19-year-old Arpit3. Following his death, his family donated his corneas3.

Police launched a criminal investigation into the managers1. The property is registered in the name of 75-year-old Urmila Gupta1. Her husband, 81-year-old retired Indian Air Force Sergeant Hariram Gupta, managed the facility with their 52-year-old son Mahesh Gupta1. Delhi Police arrested all three family members1. Scroll.in reported that the owner was arrested in Bhiwadi, Rajasthan2.

Police filed a case at the South Campus Police Station1. Charges include culpable homicide, negligence in upkeep of a structure, and endangering safety1. An alleged agreement signed by occupants said facility authorities were not responsible for casualties or compensation4. The agreement claimed exemption from liability for sealing, fire, heavy rains, earthquakes, or natural calamities4.

The Municipal Corporation of Delhi, known as the MCD, had previously flagged the structure4. The MCD identified it as an unauthorized four-storey building above ground level in a resettlement colony4. Permission in that area was only granted for a single storey above ground level4. Authorities suspended five municipal officials after the collapse2. The Delhi government also ordered a citywide sealing of illegal five-storey buildings1.

Legal and political reactions followed the collapse5. The Delhi High Court directed the MCD to conduct an inquiry at the highest executive level5. A bench of Chief Justice DK Upadhyaya and Justice Tejas Karia reviewed the case5. The judges said responsibility extends to university authorities and the MCD5. The judges said blame does not rest solely on the property owner5. The court directed the MCD to audit paying guest facilities and hostels4. It also asked Delhi University for details on outstation students and hostel capacity4.

Political groups demanded official action5. The Cockroach Janta Party released a list of seven demands5. Spokesperson Saurav Das called for a probe into the larger paying guest mafia and the construction of government hostels5. Das requested compensation of up to 1 crore rupees for families of the deceased5. He requested 20 lakh rupees for the injured5. Das questioned Delhi Urban Development & Planning and Education Minister Ashish Sood5. Das asked why 13 district committees created after a June 2026 hotel fire were wound down5.
