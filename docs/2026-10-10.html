Navi Pillay wins Nobel Peace Prize as US imposes sanctions on International Criminal Court

Thinly sourced Only 2 outlets reported the facts in this story.

South African judge Navi Pillay won the 2026 Nobel Peace Prize1.

The Trump administration announced sanctions targeting the International Criminal Court as an institution2. US Secretary of State Marco Rubio said the sanctions ban US entities from financial transactions with the court2. Rubio warned that either the court ends its threats or the US will end the court2. The court was founded in 2002 to prosecute war crimes, crimes against humanity, and genocide2. It currently has 125 member states2. But the United States, Russia, China, India, and Israel are not member states2.

The United States gave a six-month grace period before certain sanctions take effect2. UN Secretary-General Antonio Guterres called the US decision a serious blow to collective criminal justice2. He said the court is a key pillar of that justice2. Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu welcomed the sanctions2. Netanyahu called the court a political body that threatens democratic sovereignty2.

US agrees deal with Russia to import diesel despite opposition from Ukraine and lawmakers

US President Donald Trump announced an agreement with Russian President Vladimir Putin to allow Moscow to supply diesel to global markets1. The deal permits Russia to sell diesel to the United States and other countries1.

The US Treasury Department issued a temporary licence to support the agreement1. The licence suspends sanctions on Russian diesel exports through April 7, 20271. Russia will supply 300,000 tonnes of diesel immediately1. It will supply 500,000 tonnes in November1. Moscow will then export 1 million tonnes shortly after1. It is scheduled to export another 3 million tonnes depending on refinery conditions1.

President Donald Trump reached the diesel agreement during a phone call with Vladimir Putin2. The two leaders originally scheduled the call to discuss a suspected case of plague in Russia2.

Fuel prices in the United States were high before the deal1. The average price of diesel in the US stood at $6.28 per gallon at the time of the announcement1. That price was down from a record $6.53 per gallon in late September1. Average regular gasoline prices in the US rose more than 46 percent since late February to reach $4.37 per gallon3. US diesel prices increased 67 percent over the same time frame3.

Russia faced production problems before the agreement1. Ukrainian drone strikes on Russian oil refineries caused Russia's diesel production to fall by an estimated 30 percent1.

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy condemned the new agreement1. Zelenskyy said that easing sanctions on Russian energy plays into Moscow's hands1. He said the move prolongs the war1.

American politicians also criticized the move4. Democratic Senator Chris Van Hollen condemned President Donald Trump for lifting sanctions on Russian fossil fuels4. Several Republican lawmakers also condemned Trump for lifting the sanctions4.

Strong earthquake hits Panama causing infrastructure damage and economic losses

A 7.6-magnitude earthquake struck Panama near the community of Pitaloza Arriba1. The US Geological Survey also registered the main earthquake at magnitude 7.7 at a depth of 12.6 kilometers2. It was the strongest quake in that part of Panama since at least 19512.

Panamanian President José Raúl Mulino appealed for calm after the earthquake1. Mulino wrote on X that what happened should not generate alarm and disorder1. Preliminary estimates from the US Geological Survey show economic losses in Panama will likely exceed $1 billion2.

The quake caused structural collapses and damage in Penonomé1. Damaged sites include the St John Baptist Cathedral, the governor's office building, and the Dos Continentes hotel1. Panama's Ministry of Education ordered schools across the country to close3.

Transportation was disrupted in several areas1. Tocumen International Airport in Panama City temporarily suspended flights and evacuated facilities for safety checks1. Panama City's Metro system halted service on lines 1 and 2 as a precaution2. But the Panama Canal Authority confirmed ship traffic continued normally1.

Earlier in 2026, twin earthquakes in Venezuela killed at least 6,500 people in June3. A magnitude 7.4 quake in Colombia killed at least 289 people in August3.

Delhi authorities deploy heavy security and restrict transit ahead of opposition protest

The Cockroach Janta Party called on supporters to gather at Jantar Mantar in Delhi on October 10, 20261. The group released a poster demanding the resignation of Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar1. They also demanded the rollback of the Special Intensive Revision1.

Delhi Police denied permission for the planned protest at Jantar Mantar1. Officers cited alleged rule violations during an earlier demonstration by the group1. Delhi Police sent a letter on October 9 to CJP co-convener Saurav Das and joint secretary Balkrishna2. Police said their communication on October 7 was not a proper application submitted 10 days in advance2. Police also said Jantar Mantar has an official capacity to hold about 1,000 people at a time2.

Authorities took major security measures across the city1. Officials deployed 23,000 security personnel and closed 45 metro stations across Delhi1. The Union Ministry of Home Affairs directed all States and Union Territories to coordinate with Delhi Police1. They were instructed to stop protesters from moving toward Delhi1.

Security forces also made local detentions1. NSUI Jamia Millia Islamia Secretary Ebin Basil reported that police detained four female students near Jamia around 11:30 p.m.1. He said police released them around 3 a.m.1.

The Centre alleged that over 200 police personnel were injured during a CJP protest in July3. Delhi Police said they identified 2,873 people using CCTV footage from a July 20 march2. Police claimed 989 of those identified had criminal antecedents2.

Despite the actions, CJP founder Abhijeet Dipke urged supporters to continue1. Dipke said on X that he would reach Delhi by 9:30 a.m.1. He urged protesters to keep going even if he is detained1.

Union Minister Chirag Paswan said the protesters are the youth of India and nobody can stop them4. Paswan had previously called for clarification about Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar's decision-making4. But Paswan backed out after the Election Commission said decisions were unanimous4.

In an open letter, 42 retired judges defended Gyanesh Kumar5. They criticized the CJP-led protests against the Election Commission of India5. Political protests against the commission have occurred before5. In May 2014, the BJP staged protests against the Election Commission after candidate Narendra Modi was denied permission to hold a rally in Varanasi5.

US suspends PERM green card processing for major technology companies

The Trump administration suspended eight major tech companies from the US PERM program on October 81. The affected firms include TCS, Infosys, Wipro, HCLTech, Microsoft, Adobe, Cognizant, and Capgemini1. US Labor Secretary Keith Sonderling announced that authorities will stop accepting new applications and processing pending ones for these companies1.

US Vice President JD Vance urged US companies to hire great American workers instead of foreign indentured servants2. India's Ministry of External Affairs called Vance's comments deeply offensive and unwarranted2. The ministry issued an official statement on October 9 regarding the immigration restrictions3.

Ministry spokesperson Randhir Jaiswal said talent mobility adds value to both the Indian and US economies4. He said Indian talent helps US companies with cutting-edge work and job creation4. The ministry noted that existing H-1B visas remain valid, though Indian nationals received 71 percent of all H-1B visas in the 2023-24 financial year1.

Tata Consultancy Services said in a stock filing that it submitted fewer than 10 PERM applications over two years1. It still plans to hire 15,000 US workers over five years1. Pan IIT Alumni India Chairman Prabhat Kumar urged graduates to return to India, saying a visa was never a stable foundation5.

Assembly and parliamentary bypoll election results declared across Indian states

Prime Minister Narendra Modi said recent bypoll election victories reflect public connection with the ruling coalition's agenda1.

In Tamil Nadu, political party TVK won two assembly seats in Dharapuram and Madurantakam2. Candidate P Sathyabama won Dharapuram by 9,471 votes, while Maragatham Kumaravel won Madurantakam by 20,040 votes2. The wins increased TVK's total in the 234-member assembly from 107 to 109 seats2.

In West Bengal, the BJP retained the Nandigram seat and won Rejinagar from AJUP2. BJP candidate Shankharavh Sarkar won Rejinagar with 1,29,914 votes, beating Golam Nabi Azad3. In Nandigram, BJP's Hasirani Rath defeated Congress candidate Milan Pradhan by 1,22,753 votes3.

In Assam, BJP candidate Devasish Sharma won the Nagaon Lok Sabha seat by over 1.5 lakh votes against AIUDF's Badruddin Ajmal2. Following the loss, Raijor Dal chief Akhil Gogoi demanded the resignation of Assam Congress president Gaurav Gogoi4. Gogoi said that Congress's minority vote share in Nagaon dropped from 25 percent to 5 percent4. In Puducherry, AINRC candidate Ashok Anand won the Thattanchavady seat by over 8,400 votes2.
