Petitions were filed before the court after The Indian Express reported on September 23 that Sandhu and Joshi had formally objected 14 times in 10 months to decisions by the poll panel related to the special intensive revision, saying some had been taken without their knowledge.

Their objections were reportedly related to the changes to Form 6, appeals against the inclusion of voters in West Bengal and the handling of voter data in Goa, among other matters. Form 6 is used to register new voters.

In one written objection, Sandhu was quoted as having stated that the poll body had acted against the advice of both commissioners and described the action as “unauthorised and illegal”.

The two election commissioners had also raised concerns over access to the digital electoral roll database.

The Election Commission has rejected reports of a rift, saying differences in views and observations are a normal part of its deliberations.

One of the petitions in the Supreme Court sought that all decisions taken by the poll panel not in concurrence with the election commissioners should be declared illegal, reported Bar and Bench.

It also sought that there should be an investigation into the deletion of 13.8 crore voters from the electoral rolls of 31 states and Union Territories during the special intensive revision.

“We are dealing with a grave situation which can undermine the very basis of our democracy,” the petitioner told the court.
