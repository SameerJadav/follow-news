Less than a week after the Flydubai terror incident, questions around motive, operational failures and the possibility of a wider conspiracy have begun to dominate the discussion. In a conversation with Hindustan Times senior anchor Aayesha Varma, HT executive editor Shishir Gupta argued that the episode should not be reduced to online speculation or dismissed as a “lone wolf” act.

Gupta described the incident as a potentially catastrophic attempted terror attack that, if successful, could have reshaped the security and diplomatic environment in the Middle East. At the centre of the episode, he said, was the courage of Indian captain Smit Machchhar, who was attacked but still managed to raise an alert and help prevent the aircraft from being used as a weapon.

The wider implications, Gupta warned, go far beyond one flight, one airline or one airport. The case raises serious questions about pilot vetting, cockpit safety, intelligence coordination and the vulnerability of aviation routes linked to Israel.

Gupta said the most important way to understand the plot is to consider what could have happened had the crew and passengers failed to stop the co-pilot.

According to his assessment, the aircraft could have been deliberately crashed into a high-value target in Israel—possibly Ben Gurion Airport, Tel Aviv, Jerusalem or another densely populated location. Such an event, he said, would have caused major loss of life and triggered an immediate regional crisis.

“It could have actually changed the path of history,” Gupta said, comparing the potential consequences to events that have historically triggered wider conflict. He argued that an aircraft crashing into Israel, particularly amid existing regional tensions, would have placed enormous pressure on the Israeli government to retaliate and could have jeopardised its ties with the United Arab Emirates.

The incident, in his view, was not merely about the safety of the approximately 180 people on board. It carried the possibility of becoming a diplomatic and security flashpoint at a particularly sensitive moment for the region.

Gupta rejected claims that the episode was orchestrated by Israel or was a “false flag” operation, calling such suggestions part of a wider ecosystem of conspiracy theories. Instead, he said, the incident must be assessed through the available operational details: an attempt to incapacitate the captain, take control of the aircraft and direct it towards Israel.

A key concern raised by Gupta was the background of co-pilot Haman al-Hamami. He claimed that the pilot had previously flown the Tel Aviv sector, had familiarity with the route and was allegedly radicalised.

Gupta said the attacker had reportedly been born to a Syrian mother and Omani father, had worked or sought work with other airlines before joining Flydubai, and had been exposed to extremist religious content and jihadist material online. If these details are established by investigators, they would point to major failures in background verification, he argued.

The critical question, according to Gupta, is not simply whether one individual became radicalised. It is how that individual was cleared to operate on a strategically sensitive route involving Israel.

“This does not slip through the cracks,” he said, arguing that the incident demands a deeper probe into the individual’s contacts, movements, digital activity and possible associates.

He also questioned whether previous employers, aviation authorities or intelligence agencies had flagged potential concerns. The failure, Gupta argued, would involve multiple layers: the airline’s hiring process, national aviation oversight, intelligence-sharing mechanisms and security procedures for flights entering Israeli airspace.

Tales of the incident repeatedly highlight the heroic role of Captain Smit Machchhar, described as an Indian national who suffered serious injuries during the confrontation. Gupta said the captain was struck with an axe on his head, chest and hands, yet managed to open the cockpit door and communicate an emergency alert.

That action, he said, changed the entire outcome of the incident.

Along with the captain’s response, Gupta credited passengers who intervened to restrain the co-pilot. He also pointed to the role of two spare pilots who were reportedly on board and helped land the aircraft safely at Tabuk airport.
