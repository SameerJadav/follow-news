The Supreme Court on Monday (September 28, 2026) took suo motu cognisance of a spate of sexual assault incidents against minors and women in the Delhi-National Capital Region (NCR), and directed the Delhi Police to immediately identify vulnerable areas, step up patrolling, and undertake an audit of lighting and CCTV coverage in public spaces within a period of four weeks.

A Bench of Justices J.B. Pardiwala and K. Vinod Chandran observed that public spaces, including parks, buses and Metro stations, could not be allowed to become “zones of high risk” because of deficiencies, including “grossly inadequate illumination” and “lax patrolling”.

It took note of several recent cases of sexual violence reported in the national capital, including the rape of a 17-year-old at Astha Kunj park near Lady Shri Ram College, the gang-rape of a minor at Swaroop Nagar, and the sexual assault of another minor inside a bus travelling from Greater Noida to Delhi.

“These incidents, considered alongside recent reports of sexual offences across Delhi-NCR, starkly underline the failure of law enforcement and public administration authorities to guarantee basic public safety. The right to live with dignity, free from the fear of violence, is an intrinsic facet of the fundamental guarantee of life and personal liberty enshrined under Article 21 of the Constitution,” the Bench said.

The court observed that public spaces, including roads, buses, Metro stations, footbridges, and subways, cannot be permitted to become “zones of high risk”. Where basic measures of public safety remain compromised, no individual, irrespective of age, gender or sexual identity, could navigate public spaces with a sense of security, it said.

“The primary obligation of the state and its instrumentalities is to maintain public spaces that are safe, accessible, and secure for all, while remaining particularly attentive to the heightened risks faced by children, women, and other sexual minorities,” the Bench said.

Expressing serious concern over the gang-rape of a 17-year-old girl in a moving bus that travelled nearly 47 km from Greater Noida to Kashmere Gate in Delhi without being stopped at a single police picket earlier this month, the apex court said it could not help but “draw painful parallels with the 2012 Nirbhaya case”.

The incident, it said, raised questions over whether systemic law enforcement mechanisms had made any “meaningful progress” in the years since then.

The Bench also took note of statements by public authorities and elected representatives expressing concern over such incidents, but said expressions of solidarity without accountability failed to address larger institutional failures that transcended individual cases.

“Expressions of concern, devoid of accountability, fall short of answering the larger institutional question. Expressing solidarity is not the solution to this social evil. What is required is a measurable response, with responsibility and accountability fixed upon the authorities entrusted with prevention of crime, maintenance of public order, and protection of vulnerable persons,” the Bench said.

As an interim measure, the court directed the Delhi Police Commissioner to constitute dedicated teams for each police district, headed by an officer not below the rank of Deputy Superintendent of Police, to immediately survey and inspect vulnerable pockets within their respective jurisdictions.

The exercise will cover isolated stretches, parks, transport hubs, markets, areas around educational institutions, and other public places. The assessment will be based on National Crime Records Bureau (NCRB) data, local intelligence, past and present complaints, previous incidents, and other objective indicators.

The teams have been tasked with identifying locations where poor lighting, inadequate surveillance, insufficient patrolling, abandoned structures, poor access control or other deficiencies could increase the risk of crime.

The court also directed the preparation of district-wise vulnerability maps, which will be shared among adjoining police stations to ensure that information on recurring patterns of crime and habitual offenders is not confined to individual police station jurisdictions.

The Bench further ordered patrolling and security arrangements at identified public parks and other vulnerable areas, particularly during the evening and early morning hours, or during other periods identified through the vulnerability assessment.
