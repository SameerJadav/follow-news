It’s worth noting that Abdul El-Sayed’s victory in Michigan was the latest win for progressives across the country, many of whom challenged establishment-backed Democrats in key races across the country.

Last month, Democratic socialist Melat Kiros unseated the long-serving US representative Diana DeGette in Colorado’s primary elections.

And in New York, Brad Lander, Claire Valdez and Darializa Avila Chevalier, who were all backed by mayor Zohran Mamdani, also won races in the June congressional primaries. Lander won his race comfortably, defeating the two-term Democratic representative Dan Goldman. Valdez won against Antonio Reynoso – the handpicked successor of Nydia Velázquez. And Chevalier toppled Representative Adriano Espaillat, the powerful five-term incumbent who chairs the congressional Hispanic caucus.

Despite progressive nominee Cori Bush’s loss in Missouri against pro-Israel incumbent Wesley Bell on Tuesday, Michigan insurgents in two key House races also claimed wins.

Trump praises election conspiracy theorist running to oversee Nevada's elections

Moments after Nevada’s Republican governor, Joe Lombardo, stepped off stage at the midterm campaign rally hosted by Donald Trump on Wednesday, the president praised an election conspiracy theorist who is running to oversee Nevada’s elections as its secretary of state, Jim Marchant.

The transition was awkward because Marchant won the Republican nomination for the office in June, narrowly defeating a candidate endorsed by Lombardo who is not an election-denier.

“A very special man, I wish you’d get him in office, because he’s incredible, I’ve known him so long and he’s got to get in, he just has to,” Trump said. “He had a big victory with spending very little money because everyone respects him, Jim Marchant, he’s a candidate for secretary of state.”

Marchant, who has spread baseless conspiracy theories about election fraud in Nevada for years, and previously said he would not have certified Joe Biden’s 2020 victory in the state, is attempting to unseat the Democratic incumbent, Cisco Aguilar, in a rematch of the 2022 election the Democrat won by just 23,000 votes.

Jon Ralston, the chief executive of the non-profit Nevada Independent, noted with amazement Trump’s endorsement of “an unhinged conspiracy theorist who said he didn’t even trust the results of his own primary he won”.

In racist rant, Trump claims Abdul El-Sayed 'hates' Jews 'with a passion that burns in his heart'

In a speech laced with anti-Muslim animus, Donald Trump just accused Abdul El-Sayed, the Democratic nominee for the US Senate from Michigan, who is Muslim, of being motivated by deep-seated antisemitism.

El-Sayed, who narrowly won the Democratic primary in Michigan despite $30m in negative ads paid for by groups linked to the American Israel Public Affairs Committee (Aipac), has campaigned with Jewish supporters, but strongly condemned Israel’s killing and mistreatment of Palestinians.

According to Trump, El-Sayed is “a hater of Jews and a hater of Israel”.

“Just say this guy hates Jews. Somebody said, ‘Oh, that’s a little strong.’ No, he hates Jews and he hates Israel,” Trump said. “He’s a man of hate.”

The president then mockingly pronounced El-Sayed’s name, clearly stressing its Arabic origins, eliciting boos from his supporters in Nevada, just as his earlier reference to “Barack Hussein Obama” had. At another point in the speech, Trump also made a sarcastic reference to “the lovely Islamic Republic of Iran”.

Earlier on Wednesday, as other Republicans released frankly racist statements attempting to cast El-Sayed as a Muslim extremist, El-Sayed addressed Jewish Americans in comments carried live by Fox News.

“To my Jewish American sisters and brothers, one of the most indelible memories of this campaign will have been the opportunity to break bread with you at seder,” El-Sayed said, “and listening to sisters and brothers who said, ‘I want to join this movement because Aipac doesn’t speak for me; because I understand that self-determination for one people does not require the quenching of self-determination for another people; that unless all of us are free, none of us are free,’ I want to tell you the great gift that you’ve given me.”

“I want you to know,” he continued, “that my commitment to your safety, my commitment to Jewish safety, is the same commitment that I have to the safety of my own daughters.”

Apparently referring to these comments, which were carried live on the president’s favorite network, Trump said: “Now he’s going around saying, ‘No, I love everybody … ’ He doesn’t love everybody. Put him in office, you’re going to find out what he loves. He doesn’t love Israel. He doesn’t love Jewish people. He hates them. He hates them with a passion that burns in his heart and is not a fricking thing he can do about it. There’s not a thing he can do about it ’cause he hates them.”

The idea that many non-Jews hold a deep-seated, unreasoning, anti-Jewish animus is a frequent claim made by Israeli politicians, including Israel’s prime minister, Benjamin Netanyahu.

“When I watch Abdul,” Trump said later in the speech, “he’s full of shit.”

In Nevada, Donald Trump praises Donald Trump

Donald Trump is now speaking to supporters in Nevada, and so far he is giving a standard campaign rally speech, running through his standard boasts about how well he is doing and repeating false claims he has made again and again.

The president began by claiming that he had won Nevada in the 2024 election “in a landslide”. In fact, he won the state by 46,008 votes over Kamala Harris. Four years earlier, Trump had lost the state to Joe Biden by a similar if slightly smaller margin of 33,596 votes.

In a speech that was billed as a presidential address on the economy, but is in effect a midterm campaign rally for Republican candidates that support him, Trump went on to repeat a series of false claims, including: his wildly inflated estimate that $19tn has been invested by foreign businesses since he returned to office; “the economy is the greatest economy we’ve ever had by far”; the fiction that “25 million poured through our southern border” during the Biden administration.

Trump also told his entirely invented tale that “the king of Saudi Arabia” told him last year: “a year ago your country was dead; it was a dead country. We never thought it was coming back. And now you have the hottest country anywhere in the world.”

In fact, during his visit to Riyadh in 2025, Trump told the Saudi crown prince, Mohammed bin Salman, the US was “the hottest country in the world”, a claim that he previously made at least a dozen times in public comments during his first term, starting with rally speeches ahead of the 2018 midterms, and has repeated dozens of times since.

Trump's lawyers fight disclosure of his financial records to BBC in defamation suit

Lawyers for Donald Trump asked a federal judge in Miami on Wednesday to pause an earlier order that requires financial information for 400 of his business entities to be disclosed to the BBC, as the British broadcaster works to defend itself from Trump’s $10bn defamation suit over the editing of a documentary in 2024.

The earlier discovery order granted the broadcaster’s lawyers access to the financial information because, as the British legal writer David Allen Green explains, Trump had claimed that the editing of his speech to supporters on 6 January 2021, before they stormed the Capitol, had caused him massive reputational harm that cost his businesses vast sums of money. Framing the case that way let the BBC argue that it had a right to discovery on the actual state of Trump’s businesses.

As Courthouse News reports, Trump’s lawyers now want to narrow the scope of his defamation claim against the broadcaster to prevent the full disclosure of his financial affairs.

Life for Trump’s lawyers has not been made any easier by the fact that the president has, on at least five occasions since December, falsely claimed that the BBC did not just misleadingly edit his remarks from that day, but “used AI” to put words in his mouth that he did not say.

The actual BBC edit at issue spliced together two parts of Trump’s remarks that were actually spoken 54 minutes apart, but made no use of AI and the words were spoken by him, and were not, as he has claimed again and again, “AI generated”.

Here's a recap of the day so far

  • Abdul El-Sayed won the Democratic nomination for the US Senate seat in Michigan, beating Haley Stevens in a bitter competition that became a referendum on the changing face of the Democratic party. El-Sayed won the race by a single point, fewer than 15,000 votes, according to the Associated Press. He will now face Republican nominee Mike Rogers – who ran unopposed – for a key battleground seat that is vital for Democrats to retain in order to achieve their lofty goal of reclaiming the majority in the upper chamber of Congress.   • Democrats quickly coalesced around El-Sayed following his narrow victory, urging their fractured coalition to back him with the goal of keeping the seat blue. Stevens said she has offered her “full support” to El-Sayed, while the Michigan senator Elissa Slotkin, a Democrat, said there is “nothing more important” than “uniting” and moving forward.   • Further down ballot, progressive Donavan McKinney beat incumbent Shri Thanedar in a closely watched Democratic primary for Michigan’s 13th congressional district. It was a contest that mirrored many of identity battles facing the party this midterm cycle.

Senate confirms Erica Schwartz to lead CDC after almost a year without a permanent director

By a vote of 51-44, mainly along party lines, the Senate confirmed Erica Schwartz to lead the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC), after the agency has been without a permanent director for almost a year.

Tim Kaine of Virginia was the only Democrat to join all Republicans and vote yes on Schwartz’s nomination.

Bernie Sanders congratulates El-Sayed on primary win and taking on 'the entire billionaire class'

Bernie Sanders, the independent senator from Vermont, congratulated the progressive Democrats he endorsed, who won in Michigan’s closely watched primary elections –including the Democratic Senate nominee Abdul El-Sayed.

Todd Blanche rides the fence in Pirro-Trump reflecting pool battle

During a press conference at the Department of Justice earlier today, the acting US attorney general, Todd Blanche, avoided siding with Donald Trump or the US attorney for the District of Columbia, Jeanine Pirro, over her decision to dismiss vandalism charges at the reflecting pool against Olympic canoeist David Hearn.

Blanche said that he did not want the “message” to be that “because Trump expressed appropriate frustration at the damage done to the reflecting pool” that Pirro is not “working every day to combat violent crime and to make the city safe”.

These comments come as Blanche awaits the final vote in the Senate for his confirmation as attorney general. Two key Republican holdouts, senators John Cornyn and Thom Tillis, said they would now vote for Blanche’s nomination after he said in writing that a planned $1.8bn slush fund for Trump’s allies was dead. However, Susan Collins, a moderate Republican senator from Maine, said she would note vote to confirm Blanche – citing the creation of the so-called “anti-weaponization fund” as part of her reasoning.

Blanche is hoping to gain the votes of two other GOP lawmakers in order to shore up his position – Lisa Murkowski and Bill Cassidy.

Cassidy, the outgoing senator from Louisiana who lost his primary earlier this year to a Trump-backed challenger, said in a radio interview on Wednesday that he had still not made his decision. “I’ve got to be convinced that he is an attorney general who just happens to have once been President Trump’s personal attorney, as opposed to President Trump’s personal attorney who is now the attorney general,” Cassidy said. “I think there’s a big difference between the two.”

A quick note that we can expect a vote today on Erica Schwartz’s nomination to lead the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC). This would mean that the beleaguered public health agency finally has a permanent director in almost a year, since the last Senate-confirmed leader – Susan Monarez – was ousted by Robert F Kennedy Jr last August, after they clashed over the health secretary’s vaccine policy.

Schwartz received a majority of the votes to advance her nomination out of the Senate health committee, and is expected to be confirmed along party lines. During her confirmation hearing Schwartz, who previously served as deputy surgeon general in Donald Trump’s first administration, was questioned about how she would handle Kennedy’s vaccine skepticism as a longstanding supporter of childhood inoculations.

A former megachurch pastor who endured 15 years of conversion practices is set to become the first transgender member of Michigan’s state legislature after winning a Democratic primary election on Tuesday.

Joanna Whaley comfortably defeated Frank Liberati, a former state representative, and Gary Schlack in the primary for the second district of the Michigan statehouse, a Democratic stronghold.

Earlier this year Liberati sought to have Whaley disqualified from the ballot over her name change, but the Wayne county clerk’s office dismissed the challenge, finding that Whaley’s candidate affidavit was legal and proper.

Whaley, 38, who grew up in a Catholic family, previously spent two decades working as a pastor at an evangelical megachurch.

She faced indignities from her opponent, channeled the energy into knocking on doors, doubled down on her economic agenda, and she defeated that former Dem legislator (who took the low road against her) by 30+%! The American Israel Public Affairs Committee (Aipac), which spent roughly $30m in a failed effort to defeat El-Sayed in the Democratic primary, says it’s committed to opposing him in the general election, signaling plans to spend for Republican Mike Rogers.

“Our members remain determined to ensure that voters reject Dr El-Sayed and his radical anti-Israel agenda in November,” the group’s statement said.

“While disappointed that some of our Aipac-backed candidates did not prevail, our community is proud to support pro-Israel Democrats and Republicans who stand for our values, and we are encouraged that voters in races across this primary season continue to broadly choose leaders who support a strong US-Israel partnership,” it added.

Aipac’s brand has struggled as of late to capture new voters, with El-Sayed’s victory underscoring the continued momentum of progressive candidates. But the funding power and influence of the lobbying group still remains, with some Aipac-supported candidates triumphing on Tuesday.
