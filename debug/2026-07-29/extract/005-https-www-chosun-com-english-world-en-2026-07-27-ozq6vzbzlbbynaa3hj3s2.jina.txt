India’s Education Minister Dharmendra Pradhan, a key figure in Narendra Modi’s government, resigned on the 25th amid a nationwide scandal over leaked medical school entrance exam questions. This came a month after the youth political group “Cockroach Janta Party (CJP),” formed in response to the leak, began demanding his ouster.

This marks the first time a minister in Modi’s administration has stepped down under public pressure. Pradhan, widely seen as a potential successor to Modi, was a powerbroker in the government, raising speculation that this incident could ignite broader resistance. Analysts warn that if the government fails to address youth frustrations—exacerbated by severe unemployment—the protests could escalate into large-scale anti-government demonstrations, mirroring global trends since last year.

**◇“Modi Makes Unprecedented Concession to Public Pressure”**

In a social media statement, Pradhan announced his resignation, stating, “I am stepping down to prevent anti-national forces from exploiting the nationwide situation, including at Jantar Mantar, and to ensure young people are not drawn into chaos.” Jantar Mantar, a central Delhi site, became symbolic of the government’s accountability in the exam leak after the CJP held its first protest there on June 6.

The scandal erupted on May 3 when widespread cheating was detected during the National Eligibility-cum-Entrance Test (NEET), a critical medical school exam. Authorities confirmed that question papers were leaked in Rajasthan days before the test. Over 2.2 million students were forced to retake the exam, but at least 20 youths died by suicide amid the pressure.

Abhijeet Dipke, 30, a Dalit and former party spokesperson who studied in the U.S., channeled this anger. After returning from abroad post-leak, he mobilized youth to form the CJP. The party’s name—a jab at Chief Justice Surya Kant’s derogatory comparison of unemployed youth to “cockroaches”—embodied defiance against the establishment.

Pradhan initially dismissed the CJP as “terrorists” during media interviews. Police violence against protesters intensified backlash, prompting opposition leaders to hold rallies outside the prime minister’s residence. Modi eventually promised investigations and punishments, signaling a retreat.

Indian media called Modi’s concession unusual. The Times of India noted, “Modi has never yielded to opposition pressure over scandals, controversial laws, or ministerial corruption since taking office. Pradhan’s resignation deals a significant political blow to Modi and the BJP.”

A longtime BJP loyalist, Pradhan rose through the party’s youth wing, served as a member of parliament, and held key portfolios—petroleum, steel, and education—since Modi’s 2014 premiership. He was widely viewed as a future leadership contender.

The CJP, having secured Pradhan’s resignation, ended its month-long protests. It stated, “The government accepted all demands: education system reforms, compensation for bereaved families, and no legal action against protesters. We will withdraw peacefully.” Protesters cheered, waving flags upon hearing the news.

**◇“Anti-Establishment Movements May Persist”**

While Pradhan’s exit halted protests, analysts argue youth anger remains unresolved. A report by Azim Premji University revealed that 40% of 15–25-year-old graduates and 20% of 25–29-year-old graduates are unemployed. With 367 million people aged 15–29, India’s demographic advantage could turn into a crisis as the working-age population declines post-2030.

Last year, Gen Z-led protests swept Nepal, Asia, Latin America, and Europe, toppling governments over corruption, incompetence, and inequality. In India, fears of a similar trajectory persist due to economic anxieties and Modi’s authoritarian governance. Political analyst Amitabh Tiwari told Reuters, “The exam leak was a trigger, but the protests reflect deeper youth disillusionment. If the CJP becomes a formal party, it could threaten Modi in the 2029 elections.”
