The Delhi High Court has notified a dedicated special court to handle criminal cases related to examination paper leaks and unfair practices, marking a significant judicial step amid growing concerns over the credibility of India's competitive examination system. The decision comes at a time when students and aspirants across the country have been protesting against alleged irregularities in major exams, including the NEET paper leak controversy.

The newly designated court is expected to ensure faster trials and focused hearings in cases involving exam fraud, question paper leaks and other malpractices affecting millions of students.

The special court has been established at the Rouse Avenue Courts complex in New Delhi and will exclusively hear criminal matters arising from examination-related offences. The move aims to prevent delays in investigations and trials by allowing these sensitive cases to be handled separately from regular criminal proceedings.

The court will deal with cases involving alleged question paper leaks, cheating, use of unfair means and other activities that compromise the fairness of public examinations. The initiative is expected to strengthen accountability and provide quicker legal action against those accused of damaging students' academic futures.

The Delhi High Court has appointed Judge Anu Grover Baliga as the Special Judge to preside over cases related to examination paper leaks and malpractice. Judge Baliga currently serves as District Judge (Commercial)-04 in the South-East District and will oversee criminal matters connected with examination irregularities.

Her appointment is part of the court's effort to create a specialised mechanism for dealing with cases that require urgent attention. The move is aimed at ensuring that investigations and legal proceedings progress efficiently without unnecessary delays.

The creation of a designated court reflects increasing pressure on authorities to take stronger action against individuals and groups accused of manipulating competitive examinations. Paper leaks can impact thousands of candidates who spend years preparing for entrance tests and recruitment examinations.

By introducing a dedicated judicial process, the court hopes to deliver faster outcomes while sending a strong message against examination fraud. The development is expected to be closely monitored by students, parents and education stakeholders seeking greater transparency in India's examination system.

The Delhi High Court's decision comes amid continued protests by students and activists at Jantar Mantar in New Delhi, where demonstrators have been demanding justice, transparency and strict punishment for those responsible for alleged examination irregularities.

Students protesting at the iconic protest site have raised concerns over the impact of paper leaks on their careers and future opportunities. Many aspirants have argued that years of preparation and hard work are unfairly affected when examination processes are compromised.

The protests have intensified following allegations surrounding the NEET examination, one of India's largest medical entrance tests. Candidates have demanded stronger safeguards, accountability from examination authorities and reforms to prevent similar incidents in the future.

The demonstrations at Jantar Mantar have become a central point for students seeking government intervention. Protesters have called for swift investigations, cancellation or review of affected examination processes where necessary, and strict action against those involved in leaking question papers.

Prime Minister Narendra Modi recently addressed students and parents affected by examination paper leaks through a late-night video message, acknowledging the issue as deeply painful. He said the government understands the concerns of young aspirants and assured them that protecting the future of India's youth remains a top priority.

In his message, PM Modi highlighted the steps taken by the government over the past two and a half months. He said those allegedly involved in paper leak cases had been arrested and sent to jail, while investigations against others involved were continuing.

PM Modi also announced that the government was working towards establishing fast-track courts and introducing stricter punishment for those responsible for examination fraud. He said instructions had been given to prepare a legal framework to deal firmly with paper leak cases.
