On July 21, Indian Prime Minister Narendra Modi stated that those responsible for exam paper leaks had been punished, and 13 people were arrested and jailed. Protests continued in Jantar Mantar, with demands for the education minister's resignation, and opposition leader Rahul Gandhi also protested outside the Prime Minister's residence. On July 23, the Supreme Court of India acknowledged that at least 155 students had directly benefited from the NEET-UG paper leak. However, the Court ruled there was no evidence of systemic failure beyond isolated incidents and declined to order a re-examination for all, citing a lack of credible proof of widespread irregularities.
