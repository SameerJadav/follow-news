Since July 27, 2026, several significant developments have occurred in India's exam paper leak scandal and student protests.

On July 27, 2026, the Indian government introduced the Public Examinations (Prevention of Unfair Means) Amendment Bill, 2026, in the Lok Sabha. This bill aims to strengthen measures against paper leaks, propose faster trials in special courts, and enhance punishments, with investigations to be completed within two months. The introduction of the bill was met with protests from opposition parties demanding accountability for police actions against students during a July 20 march to Parliament. Also on July 27, the "Cockroach Janta Party" (CJP) formally announced the suspension of its protests after the government accepted its key demands, including the resignation of Education Minister Dharmendra Pradhan and reforms to the education and public examination systems. Pralhad Joshi replaced Pradhan as Education Minister. The agreement also included compensation for families of students who died by suicide and a pledge not to prosecute protesters.

On July 28, 2026, India's government proposed longer jail terms, up to 10 years, and fines exceeding US$1 million for those involved in exam paper leaks. The Supreme Court also ordered the release of all detained protesters who are under 18 and have no prior criminal record. In Patna, applications were filed to withdraw prosecution in 27 cases related to NEET-UG protests, including one against Tej Pratap Yadav, founder of the Jan Shakti Janata Dal.

The resignation of Education Minister Dharmendra Pradhan, which occurred on July 25, 2026, was a major outcome of the protests. This resignation was celebrated by CJP supporters on July 26. The protests, spearheaded by the CJP, drew massive support from young students and focused on education reforms, unemployment, and accountability. The scandal originated from the leak of the National Eligibility-cum-Entrance Test (NEET) in May 2026, which led to the cancellation of the exam and a retest, causing immense stress and reportedly leading to at least 20 student suicides. Prime Minister Narendra Modi had previously vowed "stringent punishment" for those involved in exam fraud.
