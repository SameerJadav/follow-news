QUESTION: Have any courts, such as the Supreme Court or High Courts, issued rulings, directives, or taken suo motu cognizance regarding the exam paper leaks or the student protests?
FINDINGS: The Supreme Court of India has issued rulings, directives, and taken suo motu cognizance regarding exam paper leaks and student protests. In 2024, the Supreme Court took suo motu cognizance of the National Eligibility cum Entrance Test (NEET-UG) paper leak under Article 32 and Article 142 of the Constitution. This intervention followed nationwide protests and numerous petitions in various High Courts and the Supreme Court concerning alleged irregularities in the NEET-UG exam held on May 5, 2024. The Supreme Court tagged multiple petitions together, including those seeking a re-examination, an investigation by a central agency, and reforms in the National Testing Agency (NTA). It issued notices to the Centre, NTA, and CBSE, requesting detailed affidavits on security measures, the paper leak timeline, and affected centers and candidates. The Court also directed the Central Bureau of Investigation (CBI) to independently probe the matter.

On June 11, 2024, the Supreme Court stated that the "sanctity [of the exam] has been affected" and demanded answers, though it refused to stay the counseling process. On June 13, 2024, the Supreme Court allowed the cancellation of scorecards for 1,563 candidates who received grace marks and ordered a re-test for them. By July 8, 2024, a Supreme Court bench, including Chief Justice of India D.Y. Chandrachud and Justices J.B. Pardiwala and Manoj Misra, observed that the exam had been compromised by a paper leak. On July 23, 2024, the Supreme Court acknowledged that at least 155 students directly benefited from the paper leak but ruled there was no evidence of systemic failure beyond isolated incidents, declining to order a widespread re-examination due to a lack of credible proof of widespread irregularities.

More recently, concerning the 2026 NEET controversy, the Supreme Court has been actively involved. On May 29, 2026, the Supreme Court stated that the NEET-UG paper leak traumatized entire families and questioned the NTA's credibility, pointing to "ad-hocism" in its functioning. A petition was also submitted to the Supreme Court on May 13, 2026, seeking suo motu intervention into repeated NEET leaks, impersonation scams, and alleged systemic failures within the NTA, demanding a court-monitored probe by the CBI or a Special Investigation Team (SIT) and the replacement of the NTA. On May 25, 2026, the Supreme Court sought a status report on the implementation of recommendations from a committee formed after its 2024 ruling on the NEET-UG paper leak.

Regarding student protests, on July 24, 2026, the Supreme Court agreed to hear two petitions alleging excessive use of police force against students protesting exam paper leaks. On July 28, 2026, the Supreme Court restrained authorities from taking coercive action against students involved in recent protests over the NEET paper leak and ordered the release of detained minors without criminal records. The Court also stated that allegations of police brutality across several states disclosed a prima facie case for an independent inquiry and directed the preservation of CCTV footage and protesters' data.

In addition to the Supreme Court, the Delhi High Court has also taken action. On July 23, 2026, the Delhi High Court notified a designated fast-track court to deal with criminal cases arising from paper leaks and the use of unfair means in public examinations. This move followed Prime Minister Narendra Modi's announcement that fast-track courts would be established to swiftly handle paper leak cases. Judge Anu Grover Baliga was designated as the Special Judge for this fast-track court, which will try offenses under the Public Examinations (Prevention of Unfair Means) Act, 2024, and related offenses.

QUESTION: Have any families of injured students or victims of the leaks filed official complaints, sought compensation, or initiated legal action against the state or testing authorities?
FINDINGS: Families of students affected by India's widespread exam paper leak scandal have experienced significant trauma, as acknowledged by the Supreme Court on May 29, 2026, which noted that "students and entire families have been traumatised" by the NEET-UG paper leak.

While there isn't extensive reporting on individual families filing official complaints or initiating widespread legal action for compensation, there have been notable developments:
*   **Demands for Compensation**: Following reports of multiple student suicides linked to the NEET paper leak, the Cockroach Janta Party founder Abhijeet Dipke, in an open letter to Prime Minister Narendra Modi on June 19, 2026, demanded ₹1 crore (10 million rupees) in compensation for the families of students whose deaths were allegedly connected to the crisis.
*   **Government Acceptance of Compensation**: On July 28, 2026, it was reported that the government accepted demands from the CJP, including monetary compensation for the families of students who died by suicide after the paper leak.
*   **Supreme Court Petitions**: The Supreme Court has been involved in the broader context of the paper leak. On July 26, 2026, the Supreme Court was scheduled to hear petitions alleging excessive police action during the NEET paper leak protests. On July 28, 2026, the Supreme Court ordered the release of all detained minors without criminal records who were held in connection with the protests and restrained state authorities from taking coercive action against protesters. The court also sought replies regarding allegations of police brutality during these protests.

The Public Examinations (Prevention of Unfair Means) Act, 2024, which came into force on June 22, 2024, aims to prevent unfair means in public examinations and includes provisions for institutions found complicit in paper leaks, such as property seizure and recovery of examination costs. This law also stipulates penalties for individuals involved in unfair means, including imprisonment and fines. However, these provisions primarily target those perpetrating the leaks and malpractices, rather than outlining a direct legal recourse for affected students or their families to seek compensation from the state or testing authorities for damages incurred due to the leaks.

QUESTION: What is the current status of the NTA's 'complete overhaul' and the implementation of the Radhakrishnan Committee's recommendations?
FINDINGS: The Radhakrishnan Committee, formed in June 2024 after the NEET-UG 2024 paper leak, submitted its report with 101 recommendations in October 2024, focusing on examination reforms, data security, and NTA restructuring. These recommendations were categorized into 60 short-term and 41 long-term measures.

As of July 27, 2026, an analysis indicated that 57 of the 101 recommendations (56.4%) have been fully implemented, and another 10 (9.9%) have been partially implemented. In May 2026, Dr. Radhakrishnan informed the Supreme Court via an affidavit that a "majority of recommendations" had either been implemented or were under active execution.

Key implemented recommendations include strengthening the NTA with domain-specific leadership and manpower, building psychometric expertise, and enhanced examination security measures for JEE (Main) in January 2025, such as Aadhaar-based authentication, strengthened frisking, and repeat biometric verification. The government also announced plans to implement multi-session testing for large examinations, a multi-stage format for NEET-UG, and reducing the high-stakes nature of entrance exams, though these are yet to be rolled out.

However, 27 recommendations (26.7%) remain unimplemented. These include the proposed DIGI-EXAM identity verification system, hybrid and computer-assisted secure question paper transmission, mobile testing centers, adaptive testing, oversight mechanisms for coaching centers, and international collaborations. The committee also recommended that the NTA should primarily focus on higher education entrance examinations, a recommendation intended to allow the agency to focus exclusively on entrance tests and strengthen its institutional capacity.

The institutional overhaul of the NTA is underway, with the Ministry of Education launching an overhaul that includes relieving over 47 contractual staff and announcing a fresh recruitment drive for key roles. The Radhakrishnan Committee had recommended restructuring the NTA into an autonomous body with dedicated verticals, reducing dependence on outsourced agencies, and placing it under a Director General of Additional Secretary rank. A High-Powered Steering Committee, chaired by Dr. Radhakrishnan, was constituted on November 14, 2024, to monitor the implementation of these recommendations.

Despite these efforts, the 2026 NEET paper leak indicates that much of the blueprint remains unimplemented, highlighting a challenge in sustained and accountable execution. Prime Minister Narendra Modi announced a new high-powered task force, led by Infosys co-founder Nandan Nilekani, to further overhaul India's examination system and curb paper leaks through greater use of technology. The Supreme Court is also closely monitoring the Centre's overhaul of the NEET process and has asked for details on actions taken regarding the Radhakrishnan Committee's recommendations.

QUESTION: What were the key findings or recommendations of the 371st Report of the Department-related Parliamentary Standing Committee on Education, chaired by Digvijaya Singh?
FINDINGS: The 371st Report of the Department-related Parliamentary Standing Committee on Education, Women, Children, Youth and Sports, chaired by Digvijaya Singh, was tabled in the Rajya Sabha on December 8, 2025. The report focused on autonomous bodies under the Department of Higher Education, including the National Testing Agency (NTA) and the National Assessment and Accreditation Council (NAAC).

Key findings and recommendations included:
  * Performance of NTA: The Committee expressed concern over the NTA's performance, noting that recent irregularities in examinations "have not inspired much confidence." It highlighted repeated delays and errors in major examinations like NEET-UG, UGC-NET, CUET, and JEE (Main), urging that such errors "must not recur."
  * Strengthening NTA: The report recommended that the NTA build greater in-house capacity and place renewed emphasis on pen-and-paper testing to reduce vulnerabilities associated with digital and outsourced exams. It also advised using NTA's surplus funds to strengthen internal capacities. The Committee found that the NTA had a surplus of approximately ₹448 crore over six years, which it recommended be used for internal capacity building or strengthening checks on private vendors.
  * Blacklisting Firms: The Committee recommended creating a nationwide list of blacklisted firms involved in paper-setting and administration to prevent the recurrence of lapses. It noted that private companies banned by one state or organization for issues like paper leaks were still able to secure contracts elsewhere.
  * Computer-Based Tests: It recommended hosting computer-based tests only in government or government-controlled centers.
  * Regulation of Coaching Centers: The report recommended measures to regulate the proliferation of private coaching centers and ensure that examination papers reinforce school curricula rather than parallel coaching syllabi. It also recommended setting up a high-level committee to address this issue.
  * Accreditation (NAAC): The Committee raised concerns about the National Assessment and Accreditation Council (NAAC), calling for a white paper detailing irregularities and corrective steps. It emphasized the need to implement reforms like the Basic Accreditation Framework (BAF) and Maturity Based Graded Levels (MBGL) for a more objective and transparent process.
  * Vacancies and Infrastructure: The report noted persistent gaps in faculty recruitment and infrastructure, including a lack of seed grants for new faculty in central universities and UGC-funded institutions. It recommended that high-level vacancies in several institutions be filled immediately and that the practice of assigning additional charge offices for long periods be avoided.
  * Draft UGC Regulations: The Committee recommended that the draft UGC regulations of January 2025 be referred to the Central Advisory Board of Education (CABE) for wider stakeholder consultation. It also insisted that the draft regulations explicitly include harassment of students from Other Backward Classes (OBCs) as caste-based discrimination and disability as an axis of discrimination.

The Committee adopted the draft report in its meeting held on October 13, 2025.

QUESTION: What was the immediate student and public reaction, including any protests or petitions, between May 3, 2026 (exam day/discovery) and May 12, 2026 (exam cancellation)?
FINDINGS: The NEET-UG 2026 examination was held on May 3, 2026, for over 2.27 million candidates. Soon after the exam, allegations of a paper leak emerged, with reports of a "guess paper" or question bank being circulated through WhatsApp and other channels that closely matched many questions in the actual exam.

Between May 3 and May 12, 2026, student and public reaction was characterized by growing outrage and calls for accountability. The Rajasthan Police Special Operations Group initiated an investigation, leading to several arrests.

On May 12, 2026, the National Testing Agency (NTA) officially cancelled the NEET (UG) 2026 examination, stating the decision was made "in the interest of students" and to protect the credibility of the national examination system. The NTA announced a re-examination would be held on dates to be announced separately, and the case was transferred to the Central Bureau of Investigation (CBI).

Immediately following the cancellation, massive student protests erupted in Delhi and other cities. Members of the National Students' Union of India (NSUI) staged protests outside Shastri Bhawan in Delhi, holding placards with slogans like "Doctor degree on sale" and raising demands for accountability from the NTA. Students expressed anxiety and frustration, describing the situation as "extremely demotivating" and emotionally exhausting after months of preparation. Many student groups highlighted the stress, anxiety, and loss of motivation caused by the repeated disruptions.

Medical organizations, such as the Federation of All India Medical Associations, filed petitions in the Supreme Court, seeking reforms within the NTA and a court-monitored re-examination. Education experts also criticized the re-exam, arguing it was unfair to honest students and exposed major problems in the exam system. The controversy also led to reports of multiple student suicides due to the stress and uncertainty. Former Chief Minister M.K. Stalin urged the central government to exempt NEET-UG for the 2026 school year and allow states to determine their own medical admission processes.

The Cockroach Janta Party (CJP), founded by digital strategist Abhijeet Dipke, launched physical demonstrations at Jantar Mantar, New Delhi, on June 6, 2026, demanding the resignation of Union Education Minister Dharmendra Pradhan.

QUESTION: What is the full background of Abhijeet Dipke, including his previous political work and his decision to return to India to lead the protests?
FINDINGS: Abhijeet Dipke, born on September 29, 1995, is an Indian political communications strategist and activist. He belongs to the Mahar caste of Dalits from Aurangabad, Maharashtra. Dipke completed his master's degree at Boston University in the United States.

His previous political work includes volunteering with the social media team of the Aam Aadmi Party (AAP) between 2020 and 2023. During this time, he created meme-based digital content aimed at young voters for the 2020 Delhi Legislative Assembly election and served as a communications advisor for the Government of Delhi's education department. He left the AAP's media team in 2023 before moving to the United States for postgraduate study.

Dipke gained nationwide attention in May 2026 after launching the satirical political movement known as the "Cockroach Janta Party" (CJP). This was in response to remarks made on May 15, 2026, by Chief Justice of India Surya Kant, who reportedly compared certain unemployed youth and social media activists to "cockroaches" and "parasites of society" during a Supreme Court hearing. Dipke responded on X (formerly Twitter) on May 16, asking, "What if all cockroaches come together?" This post went viral, leading to the establishment of the CJP as a satirical online platform. The CJP's name is a parody of the ruling Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP) and its tagline is "Voice of the Lazy & Unemployed."

Despite receiving threats to himself and his family, Dipke decided to return to India from Boston in early June 2026 to lead the protests. He arrived in Delhi on June 6, holding aloft a copy of B.R. Ambedkar's "My Autobiography," and has since been a constant presence at the protest site at Jantar Mantar. He has stated that he will stay until the students get justice. Dipke has publicly taken inspiration from the writings of Dalit social reformer B.R. Ambedkar, and his Dalit identity has long shaped his politics. He coordinates the core ground campaign in Delhi, aiming to force national media and policy discussions to address the NEET exam scams and public testing integrity.

QUESTION: When did activist Sonam Wangchuk begin his hunger strike at Jantar Mantar, and what were his specific demands when he joined the CJP-led agitation?
FINDINGS: Activist and environmentalist Sonam Wangchuk began his indefinite hunger strike at Jantar Mantar on June 28, 2026, joining the Cockroach Janta Party (CJP)-led agitation. He had announced his intention to start the hunger strike from June 28 if the government did not respond to demands related to the NEET paper leak allegations by June 27.

His specific demands when he joined the CJP-led agitation included the immediate resignation of Union Education Minister Dharmendra Pradhan over irregularities in national examinations, particularly the 2026 NEET paper leak and CBSE On-Screen Marking irregularities. He also called for broader reforms in the conduct of public examinations and compensation for the families of students who died by suicide due to the examination turmoil.

Wangchuk's hunger strike drew national attention and gained significant public support. He ended his 26-day hunger strike on July 23, 2026, after the government assured him that no legal action would be taken against students protesting at Jantar Mantar, and that discussions would be held on preventing future paper leaks and providing compensation to students who committed suicide. While his initial primary demand was the resignation of Dharmendra Pradhan, he reportedly toned down this demand towards the end of his fast. Dharmendra Pradhan subsequently resigned on July 25, 2026.

QUESTION: What were Dharmendra Pradhan's initial public statements or stance regarding the NEET UG 2026 paper leak and the Cockroach Janta Party protests before his resignation?
FINDINGS: Before his resignation on July 25, 2026, Union Education Minister Dharmendra Pradhan's initial public statements and stance regarding the NEET UG 2026 paper leak and the Cockroach Janta Party (CJP) protests were generally dismissive of the allegations and supportive of the National Testing Agency (NTA). On June 13, 2024, he stated there was "no evidence" of a paper leak in NEET-UG and defended the NTA as a "credible body," asserting that the government was ensuring no student was at a disadvantage. In 2024, following an earlier NEET-UG paper leak in Hazaribagh and Patna, Pradhan assured Parliament that there was no evidence of leaks in seven years. On August 2, 2024, after the Supreme Court observed no systemic violations in the NEET-UG 2024 exam, Pradhan declared that "the truth always triumphs" and that the court's decision rejected "the propaganda being spread on this subject," vindicating the government's position. He also took moral responsibility for what he called an institutional failure of the NTA in June 2024 but did not resign at that time. For nearly two months leading up to his resignation in July 2026, the government maintained that Pradhan would remain in his position, with sources as late as July 24 insisting his resignation would be "running away" from the problem. While the government expressed readiness for discussions on the NEET-UG paper leak, it did not concede to the opposition's demand for Pradhan's resignation as a precondition for a fair debate. The Cockroach Janta Party, formed around May 15-16, 2026, began protesting on June 6, 2026, demanding Pradhan's resignation, but his initial public statements did not directly address these specific protests, maintaining a broader stance of denial and defense.

In India, a paper leak refers to the criminal act of leaking a government recruitment or academic examination paper before the scheduled date and time of the examination.[1] It is a form of organised crime that involves the unauthorised disclosure, access, and distribution of question papers, often for monetary gain. This phenomenon has become a recurring crisis, undermining the integrity of the country's education and public employment systems, affecting millions of aspirants annually.[2][3]

The execution of a paper leak is often a sophisticated operation involving a nexus of corrupt officials, coaching centre owners, printing press staff, and middlemen. The chain of events typically follows a pattern.[4] The leak can originate at multiple points in the examination supply chain, such as from staff at the printing press with access to master copies, during the transportation and storage of papers, or from bribed officials at the examination centres who open sealed packets prematurely. Once the paper is obtained, it is rapidly distributed through a clandestine network. Unscrupulous coaching institutes often play a pivotal role in selling the paper to their students for exorbitant sums, ranging from a few lakh to tens of lakh rupees.[5] Digital channels like WhatsApp and Telegram are used for mass distribution.[6][7] Finally, the aspirants who buy the paper are often gathered at a secret location a day before the exam, where they are made to memorise the answers before being released just hours before the examination begins.[8][9]

The persistence of paper leaks in India is attributed to a combination of interconnected socio-economic, systemic, and legal factors. A primary driver is the high-stakes competition for a limited number of seats in prestigious courses and coveted government jobs.[10] For instance, in 2024, over 2.4 million students appeared for the NEET-UG exam for approximately 100,000 MBBS seats.[11][12] This desperation makes aspirants and parents vulnerable and is exploited by a well-entrenched "education mafia". Furthermore, the system itself has logistical vulnerabilities in the large-scale process of conducting pen-and-paper examinations.[13][14]Historically, weak legal deterrents and low conviction rates failed to create fear of the law. Lastly, modern technology has become an enabler, as smartphones and encrypted messaging have made the rapid distribution of leaked papers easier than ever.[citation needed]

Paper leaks weaken the very principle of meritocracy. They create inequality, allowing privilege and dishonesty to triumph over hard work. When undeserving candidates secure jobs or admissions, the quality of professionals declines, affecting industries and governance alike. Students and families suffer psychological stress, leading to frustration and cynicism among the youth. Economically, re-examinations drain public funds and delay academic timelines. Trust in institutions erodes, and corruption becomes normalized, embedding itself into the social fabric. On a global scale, nations plagued by exam leaks face reputational damage, with their degrees and qualifications viewed with suspicion abroad. Thus, the ripple effects of paper leaks extend far beyond classrooms, weakening society’s ethical, economic, and professional foundations.[citation needed]

Human Rights Watch interviewed 9 witnesses, including protest organizers, journalists, and student protesters; and analyzed and verified 10 videos posted on social media.

On July 21, political opposition leaders criticized the police crackdown, calling the government “insensitive” and “authoritarian.” Delhi police temporarily detained several top opposition leaders after a nearly three-hour protest in front of Modi’s residence.
