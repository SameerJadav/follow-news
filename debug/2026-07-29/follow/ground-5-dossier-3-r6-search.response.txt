Before his resignation on July 25, 2026, Union Education Minister Dharmendra Pradhan's initial public statements and stance regarding the NEET UG 2026 paper leak and the Cockroach Janta Party (CJP) protests were generally dismissive of the allegations and supportive of the National Testing Agency (NTA). On June 13, 2024, he stated there was "no evidence" of a paper leak in NEET-UG and defended the NTA as a "credible body," asserting that the government was ensuring no student was at a disadvantage. In 2024, following an earlier NEET-UG paper leak in Hazaribagh and Patna, Pradhan assured Parliament that there was no evidence of leaks in seven years. On August 2, 2024, after the Supreme Court observed no systemic violations in the NEET-UG 2024 exam, Pradhan declared that "the truth always triumphs" and that the court's decision rejected "the propaganda being spread on this subject," vindicating the government's position. He also took moral responsibility for what he called an institutional failure of the NTA in June 2024 but did not resign at that time. For nearly two months leading up to his resignation in July 2026, the government maintained that Pradhan would remain in his position, with sources as late as July 24 insisting his resignation would be "running away" from the problem. While the government expressed readiness for discussions on the NEET-UG paper leak, it did not concede to the opposition's demand for Pradhan's resignation as a precondition for a fair debate. The Cockroach Janta Party, formed around May 15-16, 2026, began protesting on June 6, 2026, demanding Pradhan's resignation, but his initial public statements did not directly address these specific protests, maintaining a broader stance of denial and defense.

=== BLOCK q1 ===
The handwritten 'guess paper' for NEET UG 2026 reportedly circulated among students "days before" the examination, which was held on May 3, 2026. This circulation occurred via WhatsApp, Telegram groups, and coaching networks, particularly in areas like Sikar, Rajasthan, and Dehradun, Uttarakhand. The number of questions that allegedly matched the actual paper varies slightly across reports: one investigation revealed that a 60-page handwritten PDF contained 135 questions that matched the NEET exam, while another report stated that 120 questions from the 'guess paper' matched the actual NEET-UG 2026 question paper. The handwritten material reportedly included over 400 questions, with key NEET questions allegedly hidden within the larger set to avoid suspicion.
