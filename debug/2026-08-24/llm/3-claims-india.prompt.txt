=== STORY 3 [india] — Panama-flagged cargo ship sinks off Odisha coast, triggering major search operation for missing crew ===

The fates of 22 crew members aboard a Panama-flagged bulk carrier that sank in the Bay of Bengal off Odisha remained unknown even after 24 hours of intensive search and rescue operations by the Indian Navy and the Indian Coast Guard (ICG) on Sunday (August 23, 2026).

According to the latest reports from the Navy and the ICG, two Chinese crew members of the ill-fated M.V. Ocean Winner, which was carrying iron ore from Odisha, were rescued, even as Navy ships and aircraft continued to search for the remaining crew.

Ocean Winner (IMO 9145530), with 24 (20 Chinese, three Myanmarese, and one Bangladeshi) crew onboard, had left Paradip Port late on August 20, 2026.

The ICG said that 36 hours after the ship’s departure, the Maritime Rescue Coordination Centre (MRCC) received information that communication with the vessel was lost. It also received a request for a search and rescue operation.

The Eastern Naval Command said on X that it responded to the distress alert by deploying the P8I surveillance aircraft in coordination with MRCC, Sri Vijaya Puram. “The Indian Navy aircraft located a geographically dispersed liferaft in the area and soon coordinated with MT Aisopos, which was transiting nearby, with a request for the merchant vessel to render assistance. By late evening on August 22, MT Aisopos had rescued two of the 24 crew members from the liferafts,” the Navy said.

On 22 Aug 26, responding to the distress alert received in respect of MV OCEAN WINNER, approximately 700 Km East of #Visakhapatnam, #IndianNavy aircraft P8I on surveillance sortie was rapidly re-deployed for Search & Rescue mission in coordination with #MRCC at Sri Vijay Puram.… pic.twitter.com/rAz3Gg10uf

The Navy said it continues to coordinate with the ICG in the operation to find the remaining crew.

“Sustained monitoring and focused aerial search by Indian Navy P8I MR aircraft led to the successful detection of two floating liferafts and oil slick near the reported position,” the Navy posted on X.

According to Equasis, a global merchant shipping database, Ocean Winner was a 28-year-old vessel that could carry around 73,000 tonnes of cargo. Built in 1998, it was 225 metres long and 32.26 metres wide, and was in its end-of-life period. Periodic inspections by national authorities uncovered several deficiencies. The last inspection by Bangladesh authorities in Chittagong this June reported three deficiencies, including in the ship’s magnetic compass.

Ocean Winner was covered by Protection and Indemnity insurance that would compensate for the loss of life. The insurance is up-to-date, starting from August 1, 2026. The ship has changed hands many times, starting with Greek ownership. The current owner is located in Fujian, China.

[a3] Livemint <https://www.livemint.com/news/india/panamaflagged-cargo-incident-2-liferafts-oil-slick-detected-2-rescued-search-operation-sank-off-odisha-coast-update-11787485691061.html> (FULL TEXT)
The Andaman & Nicobar Command on Sunday said Indian Navy P-8I aircraft led to the detection of two floating liferafts and an oil slick near the reported location and two survivors have since been safely rescued by MV AOSPOS after the Panama-flagged cargo vessel MV Ocean Winner carrying iron ore from Odisha's Paradip Port to China sent a distress alert on Saturday from a position around 300 nautical miles north-northwest of Diglipur.

The search operations remain underway. The identities of the two rescued survivors were not immediately available

It said the headquarters coordinated the rescue efforts with the Maritime Rescue Coordination Centre (MRCC), Coast Guard Regional Headquarters in the Andaman and Nicobar Islands, Headquarters Eastern Naval Command and Coast Guard Regional Headquarters (Northeast), deploying naval ships and maritime reconnaissance aircraft, according to ANI.

Union Shipping and Waterways Minister Sarbananda Sonowal expressed concern over the sinking of a Panama-flagged cargo vessel in the Bay of Bengal, saying all concerned agencies were involved in the search and rescue operation.

"Deeply concerned by the unfortunate incident involving MV OCEAN WINNER off the coast of Odisha. The Panama-flagged vessel was carrying iron ore from Paradip Port to China. All agencies are coordinating closely and actively doing search and rescue efforts," he said, adding, “Two survivors have been successfully rescued and are being given medical care; their rank and identity are currently being verified. My prayers for the safety of the remaining crew."

A Panama-registered cargo vessel carrying 24 crew members sank in the Bay of Bengal off India’s eastern coast on Saturday, officials said. The crew comprised 20 Chinese nationals, three Myanmar nationals and one Bangladeshi national.

The Maritime Rescue Coordination Centre (MRCC) was alerted late on August 22 after communication with MV Ocean Winner was lost. Victory Star Group Co Ltd subsequently sought assistance for a search and rescue operation.

Officials said the vessel was last reported at a location around 310 nautical miles from East Island. Following the distress alert, MRCC (SVP) activated the International Safety Net and diverted MT AISOPOS to locate the missing vessel and its liferafts and provide assistance.

MT AISOPOS later found two liferafts and rescued two survivors at around 8 pm.

The Indian Coast Guard also joined the operation, deploying ICGS Varad and ICGS Anmol, while ICGS Vijit was sent from Sri Vijaya Puram to reinforce the search. A senior police official said efforts were continuing to locate the remaining crew members.

The iron ore carrier reportedly vanished from the tracking system after leaving Odisha’s Paradip Port before sinking in the Bay of Bengal, over 200 nautical miles from the Paradip coast.

Maritime vessel tracking platform MarineTraffic identified MV Ocean Winner as a Panama-flagged bulk carrier. The vessel measured 225 metres in length and had a beam of 32.26 metres.

[a4] Hindustan Times <https://www.hindustantimes.com/india-news/2-rescued-22-missing-after-china-bound-iron-ore-vessel-sinks-off-odisha-coast-101787484142328.html> (FULL TEXT)
Twenty-two seafarers are missing in deep sea, while two have been rescued after a Panama-flagged cargo ship MV Ocean Winner carrying more than 72,000 tonnes of iron ore fines from Odisha to China sank in the Bay of Bengal, about 230 nautical miles off the eastern coast, officials said on Sunday.

Two of the 24 crew members aboard the ship were rescued from life rafts, while an intensive search is underway for the remaining seafarers, officials said.

The vessel left Paradip Port on Friday after loading iron ore fines and was bound for China. It reportedly sank in deep waters on Saturday after contact was lost. The crew comprised 20 Chinese nationals, three from Myanmar and one Bangladeshi national, officials said.

The Maritime Rescue Coordination Centre (MRCC) in Sri Vijaya Puram (formerly Port Blair) activated a search-and-rescue operation after receiving a distress alert and losing contact with the vessel.

Indian Coast Guard ships and a Navy maritime surveillance aircraft have been deployed, while merchant vessels in the area have been alerted to assist in the search.

“MRCC Sri Vijaya Puram initiated a swift SAR effort following a distress alert and loss of contact with MV Ocean Winner, nearly 230 NM South East off Paradip,” Coast Guard spokesperson Commandant Amit Uniyal said. “Diverted via international safety net, MT AISOPOS successfully rescued two survivors from life rafts.”

The rescued crew members were being brought to the mainland for medical treatment. “We are yet to verify their nationalities,” said the spokesperson. He said rough sea conditions in the Bay of Bengal were hampering the search.

The cause of the sinking has not been established. Authorities are expected to examine whether adverse weather, sea conditions, problems involving the cargo, mechanical failure or other factors contributed to the incident. The loss of the vessel from tracking systems before it sank is also likely to form part of the investigation.

Ports, shipping and waterways minister Sarbananda Sonowal expressed concern over the incident, saying all agencies were coordinating the search and rescue efforts. “Deeply concerned by the unfortunate incident involving MV OCEAN WINNER off the coast of Odisha,” Sonowal said in a social media post.

The MV Ocean Winner, built in 1998, had a deadweight capacity of about 72,928 tonnes, according to available vessel details.

The incident occurred during the southwest monsoon, when rough seas and heavy weather can pose risks to vessels carrying bulk cargoes.

Paradip is one of India’s major ports for bulk commodities and handles large volumes of iron ore, coal and other mineral cargoes. Bulk carriers regularly sail from the port to international destinations across the Bay of Bengal.

The sinking recalls earlier accidents involving bulk carriers carrying iron ore from Indian ports.

In September 2009, the Mongolian-flagged MV Black Rose sank a few kilometres from Paradip after developing a hole in its hull. The vessel was carrying about 23,847 tonnes of iron ore and 920 tonnes of furnace oil.

In another incident that year, the Vietnam-flagged MV Vinalines Mighty developed a serious stability problem while carrying about 24,500 tonnes of iron ore from Paradip and sent an SOS after beginning to tilt. A major accident was eventually averted.

In 2009, during the monsoon, the MV Asian Forest capsized after loading iron ore fines at Mangalore Port on India’s west coast. The vessel sank after cargo liquefaction caused it to lose stability.
