Estonia's government loses majority

There also some political news from the Baltics, where Estonia’s governing coalition lost its majority today, after two MPs left their parties.

The move leaves the government with 50 seats in the 101-seat parliament.

Prime minister Kristen Michal acknowledged the impact of the resignations, saying his administration will need to “seek compromises” to function.

He said his government will “hold the line so that Estonia is securely defended, our prosperity turns to growth, nature remains clean, and our vision is future-oriented,” public broadcaster EER reported.

The next parliamentary election needs to be held by March 2027.

Germany will devote more resource to its anti-drone police unit, the government has said, just days after an explosives-laden unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV) was found in a strategically important airport.

Last week a UAV was defused after it was found near a Ukrainian cargo plane at Leipzig-Halle airport, a key hub for military equipment transports.

AFP reports interior minister Alexander Dobrindt labelled the incident a “hybrid attack scenario” potentially involving a “foreign power”. Berlin now plans to double personnel in the federal police anti-drone unit to 300.

Seven Uzbek citizens among 13 killed in Ukrainian drone attack on Nizhnekamsk in Tatarstan

AP is quoting Russian authorities who have issued an updated casualty count after the Ukrainian drone strike on the petroleum hub of Nizhnekamsk in Tatarstan. It is now said that it killed 13 people and wounded 75 others.

At the same time Reuters has issued a newsflash the Uzbek consulate has said that seven of those killed were Uzbek citizens.

The Ukrainian military said it hit an oil refinery in Nizhnekamsk, which is about 1,200 kilometers (750 miles) east of the Ukrainian border. The city has a population of about 240,000.

Tatarstan’s head, Rustam Minnikhanov, has declared a day of mourning, and claimed a child was among those killed.
