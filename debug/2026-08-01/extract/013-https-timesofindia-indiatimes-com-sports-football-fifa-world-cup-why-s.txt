The escalating dispute between FIFA and UEFA over Gianni Infantino's controversial World Cup investment proposal is no longer just a governance battle. If FIFA presses ahead with the plan and UEFA follows through on its boycott threat, an unprecedented scenario could unfold at the 2030 World Cup, with co-hosts Spain and Portugal potentially absent from the tournament they are due to stage.The prospect has emerged after UEFA unanimously declared that none of its national teams would participate in FIFA competitions "for so long as these proposals remain alive", while FIFA has insisted it will continue consulting its 211 member associations on plans to establish FIFA Forward Enterprise (FFE), a new commercial subsidiary that would sell a minority stake in the governing body's commercial rights to private investors.Although no decision has yet been taken and FIFA maintains that the proposal can still be approved, amended or rejected, the standoff has raised fresh questions about what the 2030 World Cup could look like if neither side backs down.UEFA's boycott threat carries major implicationsUEFA's warning followed an emergency meeting of its 55 member associations, which unanimously rejected FIFA's proposal."The World Cup cannot be treated as an investment product," UEFA said in its statement."It is one of football's greatest sporting legacies. It has been built over generations by players, national teams and supporters across every continent. No part of it should ever be surrendered to private investors."The World Cup is not for sale."The confederation later confirmed that none of its national teams would take part in FIFA competitions while the proposal remained under consideration, extending the position to both the men's and women's World Cups.UEFA vice-president Laura McAllister described the proposal as "catastrophic" and accused FIFA of using "a form of blackmail financially" through the funding package being offered to member associations that support the initiative.Why Spain and Portugal are at the centre of the debateThe implications are particularly significant because the 2030 World Cup is scheduled to be hosted primarily by Spain, Portugal and Morocco.The tournament will also feature one centenary match each in Uruguay, Argentina and Paraguay to commemorate the 100th anniversary of the inaugural FIFA World Cup, making it the first edition to span multiple continents in this way.If UEFA ultimately honours its boycott and Spain and Portugal remain bound by the confederation's position, the two European co-hosts would not participate in the tournament despite hosting the majority of the competition.Such a situation would be unprecedented in World Cup history, with principal host nations absent not because they failed to qualify, but because of a dispute between football's governing bodies.Morocco's position remains unclearUnlike UEFA, Concacaf and the Asian Football Confederation (AFC), the Confederation of African Football (CAF) has not formally declared its position on FIFA's investment proposal.CAF initially indicated it was open to the consultation process, while Morocco, one of the three principal hosts for 2030, has not publicly stated whether it supports or opposes the creation of FIFA Forward Enterprise.South America's governing body, CONMEBOL, has likewise not taken an official position on the investment proposal.
