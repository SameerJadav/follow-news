The government on Friday rejected claims that diversion of sugar for ethanol production was responsible for the recent sharp rise in sugar prices, attributing the increase instead to lower domestic output, festive-season demand, weather-related crop damage, tighter global supplies and hoarding, PTI reported.

"It is incorrect to attribute the recent increase in sugar prices to diversion of sugar for ethanol production," the Ministry of Consumer Affairs, Food & Public Distribution said in a statement.

The ministry said the share of sugar diverted for ethanol production has actually declined to around 9% in the 2025-26 season from about 12% in 2022-23. Nearly three-fourths of India's ethanol production now comes from grains, particularly maize, it added.

The rise in sugar prices is attributed to lower domestic production, increased festive-season demand, weather-related crop damage, tighter global supplies, and speculation and hoarding by some industry players.

The government has imposed stock limits on sugar dealers, allowed duty-free imports of 1 million tonnes of raw sugar, and ordered physical stock verifications at mills to curb hoarding and ensure availability.

The government clarified that the share of sugar diverted for ethanol production has decreased, and the main factors for rising prices relate to domestic supply issues and demand rather than ethanol production.

The festive season typically increases demand for sugar, which exacerbates pressure on prices, particularly during periods of low supply due to crop damage or environmental factors.

Measures include limiting stockholding for bulk consumers, allowing duty-free sugar imports, and encouraging mills to start crushing cane earlier to boost supply during the festive period.

Retail sugar prices rose to ₹55.70 per kg on August 20 from ₹48.18 per kg on July 20. The government attributed the increase to five major factors.

Lower domestic production: Sugar production in the current season is expected to be around 30.6 million tonnes, below the initial estimate of about 34.3 million tonnes made by sugarcane-growing states.

The government attributed the shortfall to Red Rot and Top Borer diseases affecting sugarcane, as well as waterlogging caused by excess rainfall.

Festive-season demand: Demand for sugar is expected to strengthen ahead of the festive season, putting additional pressure on prices.

Weather-related crop damage: Excess rainfall and waterlogging have affected sugarcane output, contributing to the lower production estimate.

Tighter global supplies: International sugar prices have also risen amid tighter global supplies. The government estimates a global sugar deficit of about 3.3 million tonnes in 2026-27. International sugar prices increased more than 16% to $552 a tonne on August 20 from $474 a tonne on June 30.

Speculation and hoarding: The government also cited speculation and hoarding by some sections of the industry as factors contributing to the price increase.

To ensure adequate domestic availability and curb the price rise, the government has imposed a stock limit of 400 tonnes on sugar dealers nationwide until November 30.

From September 1, bulk consumers will also be barred from holding stocks exceeding 15 days of consumption.

The government has approved duty-free imports of 1 million tonnes of raw sugar and ordered physical verification of stocks at sugar mills by joint central and state government teams to check hoarding and artificial scarcity.

The ministry said existing domestic stocks are adequate to meet demand until the new crushing season begins in October.

The government has also advised states and mills to begin crushing from October 15. October sugar production is expected to exceed 1 million tonnes, compared with the usual 300,000-400,000 tonnes, which could strengthen supplies during the festive season.

The government said India's ethanol programme has helped address structural surpluses in the sugar industry by diverting part of the excess sugar production towards ethanol.

India typically produces 32-34 million tonnes of sugar annually against domestic consumption of around 28-29 million tonnes. In surplus years, excess inventories can lock up working capital at mills and contribute to delays in payments to sugarcane farmers.

The government said the ethanol programme has strengthened mill finances and improved payments to farmers. As of August 20, 97% of sugarcane dues for the 2025-26 season had been paid.

It also said improved industry finances have reduced the sector's dependence on government support. Around ₹14,600 crore was provided in subsidies between 2014 and 2021, but no similar subsidy has been announced since 2021-22.
