The Supreme Court on Wednesday directed the Andhra Pradesh State Election Commission to defer publishing the final list of candidates contesting the local body polls in the state until October 25, reported Bar and Bench.

The direction followed allegations by the Yuvajana Sramika Rythu Congress Party that the ruling Telugu Desam Party’s cadre and supporters had obstructed its candidates from filing nominations, reported The Indian Express.

The list was scheduled to be published on Wednesday, Bar and Bench reported.

A bench of Justices Joymalya Bagchi and V Mohana told the State Election Commission to look into the allegations by the YSRCP candidates, reported Live Law. The bench told the poll body to extend the deadline for filing nominations wherever it finds that a candidate was unable to do so due to a law and order situation.

“Such decision shall be taken by the Election Commission positively by tomorrow,” the bench was quoted as saying. “And wherever the Election Commission chooses to extend the time, such time shall be extended till not less than 48 hours from the date of taking a decision.”

The bench also told the director general of police to ensure that candidates are provided with necessary protection and that there are closed-circuit television cameras at the nomination centres.

The election to 100 urban local bodies in Andhra Pradesh is scheduled to be held on October 28. Votes will be counted on October 30. Subsequently, the Mandal Parishad Territorial Constituency and Zilla Parishad Territorial Constituency elections are scheduled for November 3, with counting on November 5. Gram Panchayat elections will be held on November 26, November 29 and December 2.

On Monday, the YS Jagan Mohan Reddy-led YSRCP moved the Supreme Court, alleging that violence and intimidation had prevented several of its candidates from filing nominations.

During the hearing on Wednesday, the party’s counsel told the court that 63.5% of its candidates were not allowed to submit nominations in 19 of the urban local bodies, reported Bar and Bench.

In its petition, the YSRCP had alleged that its candidate’s nomination papers were snatched and torn at Kadapa. In Tirupati, the party alleged, the candidate was prevented from reaching nomination centres.

Nine candidates from Tirupati also filed affidavits saying they had been obstructed from filing their nominations.

On October 10, the party moved the Andhra Pradesh High Court, seeking to secure additional nomination centres. A single-judge bench had dismissed the plea on October 15.

Subsequently, it moved the High Court seeking that the deadline to file nominations be extended. On Sunday, the court rejected the plea, stating that any direction would amount to interference with the election process.
