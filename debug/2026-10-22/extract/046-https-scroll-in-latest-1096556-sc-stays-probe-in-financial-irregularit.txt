The Supreme Court on Wednesday stayed for seven days further investigation into a case involving former Kerala chief minister Pinarayi Vijayan, his daughter and son-in-law linked to alleged financial irregularities, the Hindustan Times reported.

The court was hearing their petition challenging a Kerala High Court order directing the registration of a criminal case against them over alleged irregularities in financial transactions between state-owned Cochin Minerals and Rutile Limited and Exalogic Solutions Private Limited, a company owned by Vijayan’s daughter Veena Thaikkandiyil.

The bench clarified that the stay applied only to further investigation and did not prevent the registration of the first information report. The Kerala Police registered the FIR on October 12 and had begun investigating the case.

On October 9, a single-judge bench of the Kerala High Court directed the State Vigilance and Anti-Corruption Bureau to register an FIR against Vijayan, his daughter Veena, his son-in-law PA Mohammed Riyas and others, based on information provided by the Enforcement Directorate.

Vijayan, Veena and Riyas challenged the order before a division bench of the High Court. However, on October 15, the bench refused to permit them to proceed with the appeal because they were not parties to the original case in which the FIR was ordered. The bench said they could challenge the FIR itself after it was registered.

The three then approached the Supreme Court against the High Court’s direction to register the FIR.

The Supreme Court noted that the state had already constituted a special investigation team. It said that the High Court should not have acted in haste in ordering the registration of an FIR without first hearing Vijayan and the others, Bar and Bench reported.

The court also said it needed to examine whether a state agency was required to register an FIR in a corruption case based on a recommendation by the Enforcement Directorate.

“Don’t extend your hand beyond your statutory limits,” Bar and Bench quoted the court as telling the Enforcement Directorate. “We are making it very clear. Don’t use the process of statutory powers to meddle into the statutory domain of other entities.”

The Enforcement Directorate is investigating whether Cochin Minerals and Rutile Limited, a private company, paid around Rs 1.7 crore to the firm run by Vijayan’s daughter between 2017 and 2021.

The mining company, in which the Kerala State Industrial Development Corporation has 13.4% stake, had allegedly made the payments to Thaikkandiyil’s Exalogic Solutions with no evidence of any services rendered in return.

In May, the ED carried out searches at about 12 locations in Kerala, including the homes of Vijayan’s daughter and son-in-law in Kozhikode. Offices linked to Cochin Minerals and Rutile Limited officials were also raided.

Shortly after, the ED officials who raided the houses were attacked and their vehicles vandalised allegedly by Communist Party of India (Marxist) workers.
