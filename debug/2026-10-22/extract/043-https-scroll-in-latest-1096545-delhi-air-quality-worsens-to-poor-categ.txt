The air quality in Delhi worsened to the “poor” level on Wednesday.

The Commission for Air Quality Management enforced Stage 1 restrictions under the Graded Response Action Plan in Delhi and the National Capital Region after the average Air Quality Index crossed 200.

As of 4 pm, the national capital recorded an average Air Quality Index of 244, showed the Sameer application that provides hourly updates published by the Central Pollution Control Board.

Forty-three of the 55 active monitoring stations recorded “poor” or worse air quality levels. At 328, the AQI in Pusa was the worst in Delhi.

This was the highest AQI in the national capital since mid-July.

In Uttar Pradesh, Ghaziabad recorded a “poor” AQI of 222, Greater Noida 248 and Noida 203, the application showed.

In Haryana, Gurugram recorded an average AQI of 204. Faridabad recorded an air quality level of 124, which is in the “moderate” category.

GRAP is a set of incremental anti-pollution measures that are triggered to prevent further worsening of air quality once it reaches a certain threshold in the Delhi-NCR region.

The first stage involves measures such as mechanical sweeping of roads and sprinkling water on them to keep dust from rising. It also bans some kinds of construction and demolition activities.

Under this stage, vehicles without a valid Pollution Under Control certificate will not be allowed to refuel, while parking charges will be doubled across Delhi, except at Metro parking facilities. Staggered working hours and work-from-home arrangements will also be introduced for Municipal Corporation of Delhi and government offices to reduce vehicular movement, NDTV quoted Delhi Environment Minister Manjinder Singh Sirsa as saying.

An index value between 0 and 50 indicates “good” air quality, between 51 and 100 indicates “satisfactory” air quality and between 101 and 200 indicates “moderate” air quality. As the index value increases further, air quality deteriorates. A value of 201 and 300 means “poor” air quality, while between 301 and 400 indicates “very poor” air.

Between 401 and 450 indicates “severe” air pollution, while anything above the 450 threshold is termed “severe plus”.

Stage 1 of GRAP is activated when the AQI is in the “poor” category. The second, third and fourth stages are activated when the AQI crosses the “very poor”, “severe” category and “severe plus” category.

Air quality deteriorates sharply in the winter months in Delhi, which is often ranked the world’s most polluted capital. Stubble burning in Punjab and Haryana, vehicular pollution, along with the lighting of firecrackers during Diwali, falling temperatures, decreased wind speeds and emissions from industries and coal-fired plants contribute to the problem.
