On Monday, he had said in an Instagram post that he had no intention of leaving the country and that he would "hand over my passports and defend myself against this travesty, which is a clear attempt to intimidate me."

According to the AFP news agency, Bolsonaro also holds Italian citizenship and so was able to travel despite his Brazilian and diplomatic passports being canceled.

Bolsonaro's departure and his arrival in Florida — like his father Jair Bolsonaro before him after Brazil's previous presidential elections — could deepen a rift between the two most populous countries in the Americas.

During the election campaign, incumbent leftist President Lula had accused the Trump administration of colluding with the Bolsonaro family in a bid to help them return to power.

After the election results, Trump appeared to question the integrity of the vote, saying he thought the extension to voting times was a "very bad look." He had also alleged in the past that Jair Bolsonaro was the victim of judicial persecution in Brazil.

Bolsonaro senior is now in jail in Brazil on charges of leading a conspiracy designed to keep him in power despite defeat in 2022's presidential election.

Far-right senator and family land in US after crossing from Brazil into Argentina and then flying via Ecuador

Flávio Bolsonaro declared himself the “true president” of Brazil after losing last Sunday’s presidential election and fleeing to the United States.

After landing on a private jet with his family in Miami, Florida, on Wednesday morning, the far-right senator published the claim in a letter – even though his own party has recognised the legitimate victory of Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva, as have all of Brazil’s institutions, and international observers who monitored and endorsed the vote.

Brazil’s three main newspapers – Folha de S.Paulo, O Globo and O Estado de S.Paulo – all described Bolsonaro’s declaration as “coup-mongering”.

The senator later deleted the letter and published a new one in which, instead of calling himself the “true president”, he described himself as “the leader of Brazil”.

After losing Sunday’s runoff vote to Lula, Bolsonaro disappeared for more than 24 hours, until Argentina’s migration authorities said he had entered the country by land on Tuesday night.

From there he boarded a Gulfstream IV, registered LV-JQW, which had been parked at Puerto Iguazú airport since the early hours of the previous morning, when the first reports emerged in Argentinian media that he intended to flee on it.

Bolsonaro, the son of the jailed former Brazilian president Jair Bolsonaro, took off on the jet with his wife and two teenage daughters at about 11.30pm, bound for Guayaquil in Ecuador, reportedly to avoid Brazilian airspace. They made a brief stop in Ecuador and continued on to the Miami-Opa Locka airport, a private aviation hub about 11 miles north of downtown Miami catering to corporate executives and wealthy travelers.

Bolsonaro, wearing a black bulletproof vest and looking somber, and his family emerged from the plane into a postcard-perfect sunny south Florida morning at 9.24am. They briefly remained inside the airport before leaving, shielded by black umbrellas, climbing into a dark gray Ford Expedition Max and speeding away under police escort.

Bolsonaro said nothing to reporters before boarding the vehicle and the mood was low-key, without any supporters present to welcome him and only two dozen journalists who were kept at a distance by two Customs and Border Protection (CBP) agents.

In the letter, published about seven hours after his arrival, Bolsonaro also thanked Argentina’s president, Javier Milei, “for his courage, sense of justice and legality”, and Donald Trump “and the wonderful American people for welcoming me”.

He again repeated, without evidence, his claim that there had been “electoral fraud” – a claim rejected by Brazil’s electoral court and supreme court, as well as the presidents of the lower house and senate. Bolsonaro’s party said on Monday: “We recognise the presidential result.”

At least three different versions of the letter were published by Bolsonaro and his allies, including one that used the country’s pre-1968 name and the English spelling “Brazil” rather than the Portuguese “Brasil” – which some saw as another sign of the Bolsonaro clan’s subservience to the US.

Brazilian newspapers reported that Bolsonaro and his family had been granted humanitarian visas in the US. On Tuesday night the US president said he would approve their move to the country.

“They’re fine people. … I didn’t take sides, you know. I know them both. I know Lula. I know Flávio a little bit. I know the father very well,” the US president said.

On Wednesday, however, when asked about Bolsonaro’s claim, Trump said he would stay out of Brazil’s post-election turmoil.

“Well, I’m not going to make a comment about that, but I know that we gave him a pass to come into the country, so we’ll see,” he said. “But I won’t get involved. It was a very strange election, that’s for sure.”

Lula called Bolsonaro’s flight “shameful”. “This is what the Bolsonaro family are like. Brazil is their second homeland. Their first homeland is licking the boots of the US, as they’ve become accustomed to doing,” he said at an event celebrating his 81st birthday in Brasília.

Some in the Brazilian government suspected Bolsonaro allies had spent Tuesday privately telling the press he was already on his way to the US to distract from his real plan to go through Argentina. The federal police are facing criticism for failing to prevent him from leaving the country.

There is a gap between the last time he was seen by allies, at about 6pm on Monday, and the moment he entered Argentina late on Tuesday. It remains unclear how or when he travelled to the border, or where he was during that time.

Footage from a television channel there shows two vans crossing the border checkpoint and then being escorted by airport security officers to the airport.
