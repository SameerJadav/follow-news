Twenty-two Indian nationals, including 14 seafarers, have lost their lives in the ongoing conflict in West Asia, the Ministry of External Affairs confirmed on Wednesday.

Addressing the bi-weekly media briefing, MEA Official Spokesperson Randhir Jaiswal reiterated India's serious concern over the deteriorating security situation in the region and detailed the human death count.

"We remain deeply concerned about the situation," Jaiswal stated during the briefing. "Regrettably, 22 Indian nationals have lost their lives in the conflict zone, including 14 seafarers."

Highlighting the safety of the Indian community and maritime personnel in the Gulf region, Jaiswal emphasised that diplomatic missions are actively maintaining contact with Indian citizens.

"We remain in close contact with our missions and community members there," Jaiswal added.

Providing additional details on casualties across the broader region, the spokesperson confirmed that three deaths occurred within Saudi Arabia as a result of regional instability.

"In Saudi Arabia, three Indian nationals have lost their lives in incidents related to the broader regional tensions," Jaiswal noted.

The Ministry reaffirmed that Indian diplomatic posts across West Asia continue to monitor developments closely to ensure the safety, security, and welfare of Indian workers and seafarers operating in vulnerable maritime corridors.

Meanwhile, the Consulate General of India in Jeddah has issued a new travel advisory outlining enhanced flight options to facilitate air travel for Indian nationals, particularly Umrah pilgrims affected by recent flight disruptions in Saudi Arabia.

Building on prior advisories, diplomatic officials confirmed ongoing coordination with Saudi aviation authorities, commercial air carriers, and community groups to restore and expand connectivity to multiple Indian destinations.

"In continuation of earlier advisories and as part of our ongoing engagement with airlines to facilitate additional air connectivity between Saudi Arabia and India, the Consulate General of India, Jeddah, wishes to share the following updates," the Consulate stated in an official announcement shared on X.

Addressing operational routes across major Saudi transit hubs, the mission confirmed that services out of Western Province airports remain active through local and international carriers.

"Jeddah & Madinah: Saudi air carriers continue to operate services to India. Other international airlines also offer one-stop connections through their respective hubs," the advisory detailed.

To assist stranded travelers in the Eastern and Central regions, private Saudi carrier Flynas has added services linking Dammam and Riyadh directly to major Indian metropolitan and regional airports.

"Flynas – Dammam: Additional flights announced to Lucknow, Mumbai, Delhi, Hyderabad, Kozhikode and Kochi," the Consulate noted. "Flynas – Riyadh: Flights have also been announced to Delhi, Hyderabad and Kozhikode on select dates."

Diplomatic missions in Riyadh and Jeddah urged tour operators managing pilgrimage groups to utilise the expanded flight schedules to ensure orderly returns.

"The Embassy and Consulate continue to remain in touch with various airlines and community organisations to help and guide Indian nationals affected by flight disruptions, particularly Umrah pilgrims," the post read. "We encourage tour operators to explore the available enhanced flight options for Indian nationals."

The Consulate cautioned passengers against travelling to airports without confirmed rebookings and urged direct verification with carriers.

"Passengers are advised to verify flight schedules, bookings and travel arrangements directly with the concerned airlines before proceeding to the departure airports," the notice warned. "The Consulate's 24×7 Control Rooms in Jeddah and Makkah remain operational for Indian nationals requiring assistance."

This comes as Saudi Arabia has reportedly offered USD 1 billion to Yemen's Houthi group as part of an Iran-brokered deal to secure a ceasefire and halt attacks on the kingdom, Israeli media reported, citing diplomatic sources in the Gulf.

According to the reports, Saudi Defence Minister Prince Khalid bin Salman travelled to Tehran for talks aimed at securing a ceasefire with the Houthis, with the proposed arrangement involving the transfer of USD 1 billion to the group through Iran.

The Special Intensive Revision of India’s voter rolls has sparked concerns about the widespread disenfranchisement of eligible voters and that it is becoming a citizenship test. Its method is not new, but what is different this time is that it threatens the right to vote.

Digital tools have served the rolls since the 1990s: voter identity cards from 1993, computerised rolls, voting machines in every constituency by the 2004 general election and photo rolls nationwide by 2009. Through those years, rolls were corrected one application at a time, with officers deciding each case – former Chief Election Commissioner SY Quraishi says they were already about 99% accurate. From 2015 the Commission set out to “purify” the rolls by matching them against Aadhaar records.

The SIR, which began in Bihar in June 2025, completes the turn. It denies the roll on which the 2024 general election was held any presumption of eligibility, and presumes eligible only those found on a roll two decades old, so that everyone under about 40 can be linked only through a parent or grandparent or by producing supporting documents.

On September 23, The Indian Express reported that Election Commissioners Sukhbir Singh Sandhu and Vivek Joshi had objected on record 14 times in 10 months to steps taken under the Chief Election Commissioner in the SIR, from keeping them in the dark to adding and deleting voters.

By September 1, 13.37 crore names were missing from draft rolls – some of these were voters who had died, were absent during verification or had moved homes. But those who cannot be linked to the old roll, increasingly after software verification, must produce documents such as proof of citizenship or risk being struck off.

In 2025, the Association for Democratic Reforms and others challenged the SIR in the Supreme Court, arguing that the commission’s task is to enrol citizens, not to adjudicate citizenship.

On May 27, the court disagreed, allowing a “limited enquiry into citizenship”. It held that the deletion of a voter is not a finding that someone is not a citizen but only a sign of the commission’s “inability to be satisfied”. It said this consequence was “correspondingly limited”, while acknowledging that it affects a person’s “right to participate in the electoral process”.

Citizenship is for an authority under the Central Government to decide, “in any event” before the next election, says the judgement. But in the directions, which are the actual orders, the court only says the decision must be taken “preferably” before the election.
