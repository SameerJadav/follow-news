At least 68 people have been killed and dozens more injured after two passenger trains collided in Toledro province in central Spain. The Spanish prime minister, Pedro Sánchez, has declared three days of mourning in the wake of the disaster – the second high-profile train crash in Spain this year and the third deadliest incident on its railway in the last 50 years. Sánchez said his government would act with urgency to “clarify what has happened, why this tragedy occurred.”

The two trains with 544 people on board in total were travelling in the same direction on the high-speed line from the Spanish capital, Madrid, to Seville in the south-west of the country. One of the trains ran into the tail of the other that had stopped, knocking several carriages off the track. Video footage from the scene showed extensive damage to both trains.

Sánchez insisted his government would get to the bottom of incident and address potential safety failures with “utmost firmness”. The government, he said, “must further strengthen safety to rebuild confidence in our high-speed rail network”. He added: “This is essential, as it is unacceptable for a country like Spain to experience two high-speed rail accidents in a single year.”

Transport minister Óscar Puente said initial review of the evidence pointed to an “inexplicable” decision to order trains to go ahead after a signalling failure, implying a human error. The investigation is likely to focus on 17 minutes between the trains were halted and the crash. Spanish media reported that the controller responsible was questioned by investigators this afternoon. But, as my colleague Gwyn Topham wrote earlier, the Spanish public will want to know what led to the initial outage of a signalling system that was due to be phased out – and what circumstances or daily experience could have pressed someone to urge the trains to continue regardless, however tragically wrong that decision proved to be.

Both main parties suspended their campaigning activities ahead of next month’s parliamentary election, but the far-right Vox party continued with its rallies. Spain’s football federation also postponed all 24 Copa del Rey games scheduled for Wednesday.

The Health Department of Castilla-La Mancha has reduced the number of people in hospitals in the region to 53, after 56 were discharged, El Pais reported.

The British monarch, who is currently out of the country visiting the Bahamas, “sent a private message of condolence” to his Spanish counterpart earlier today, a Buckingham Palace spokesperson said.

The CSIF civil servants’ union has called for the resignation of Transport Minister Óscar Puente following Wednesday’s train accident.

“The minister is shamefully using a worker as a scapegoat instead of taking responsibility,” the union said, adding: “The minister has evaded all responsibility despite the initial computer system failure that triggered the events.

“Throughout this year, CSIF has demanded increased investment in the maintenance of the railway sector: safety, review and updating of safety protocols, and greater investment in infrastructure maintenance, as well as in railway workshops.”
