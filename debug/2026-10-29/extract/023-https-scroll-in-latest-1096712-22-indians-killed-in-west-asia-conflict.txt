Twenty-two Indians have been killed in the ongoing war in West Asia since February, the Ministry of External Affairs said on Wednesday. Fourteen of them were seafarers.

“We are deeply concerned about the ongoing conflict in West Asia,” said Ministry of External Affairs Spokesperson Randhir Jaiswal.

He added that the government was in close contact with the Indian community in the region.

The United States and Israel launched attacks on Iran on February 28, killing its supreme leader and other senior officials. Tehran retaliated by targeting US bases across the Gulf and restricting traffic through the Strait of Hormuz.

Vessels carrying several Indian seafarers have come under attack in the region since the conflict began. Many of the sailors who died were working aboard foreign-flagged ships.

On June 15, the US and Iran arrived at an interim agreement to stop the fighting and reopen the Strait of Hormuz for commercial vessels. They also held talks in Switzerland aimed at reaching a final peace deal within two months.

However, on July 9, the US military launched fresh attacks on Iran hours after President Donald Trump said that the ceasefire was “over”. This came after Tehran’s alleged attacks on commercial ships transiting the Strait of Hormuz. Iran has been retaliating by striking sites in Bahrain, Kuwait and Qatar.

Separately, three Indians were also killed in Saudi Arabia, the foreign ministry spokesperson said on Wednesday.

Saudi Arabia has faced several attacks by Yemen’s Houthi militant group this month, including a strike on Riyadh’s international airport that marked the kingdom’s deadliest attack in years.

The attacks mark an escalation in the conflict between the Iran-backed Houthis, who are seeking to topple Yemen’s internationally recognised government, and Saudi Arabia, which backs the government.
