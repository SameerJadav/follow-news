Around 64.5 lakh electors are potentially missing from the final electoral rolls of Karnataka released after the Special Intensive Revision (SIR) exercise on Tuesday (October 27, 2026), based on the Elector-to-Population (E-to-P) ratio of 66.11 released by the office of the Chief Electoral Officer (CEO). The State’s E-to-P ratio quoted in the CEO’s statements was calculated as the ratio between the total number of electors on the rolls and the estimated population in 2026 in all age groups, including those below 18 years of age and not eligible to vote. As per the report, the estimated population in Karnataka as of March 1, 2026 should be around 6.89 crore, of which the population eligible to vote should be 5.19 crore. If all eligible voters had been included, the total in the rolls would have been roughly 5.19 crore. However, the final rolls released on Tuesday (October 27, 2026) had only 4,54,55,689 electors (excluding Service Electors), which is a shortfall of about 64.5 lakh voters. Karnataka had 5.54 crore electors on its rolls before the SIR commenced, which was above the estimated population in the 18+ age group, indicating that the rolls required removal of redundant entries, mainly duplicated and deceased voters. However, of the 1.08 crore names deleted during the enumeration phase of the SIR, only 23.5 lakh (21.8%) were deleted as duplicates and deceased, while the remaining 78.2% were deleted with shifted or absent marked as reasons. Only a modest 8.2 lakh net additions were made during the Claims and Objections phase.
