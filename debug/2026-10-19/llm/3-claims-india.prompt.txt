The government has asked Meta, which owns Facebook and Instagram, to review its content recommendation systems to prevent users from being disproportionately exposed to a single or limited set of narratives and strengthen checks on material that could cause public disorder or incite violence, PTI reported.

The Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology (MeitY), in an advisory dated October 10, asked Meta to provide adequate context for photographs, videos, graphics and other content depicting ongoing public events, particularly when their authenticity, date, location or circumstances may not be readily apparent to users, the news agency stated.

The advisory does not identify a specific incident that prompted its issuance. However, it comes amid protests in Delhi led by the Cockroach Janta Party (CJP) and opposition parties demanding the resignation of Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar over the Special Intensive Revision (SIR) of electoral rolls.

According to the news outlet, the ministry has also asked Meta to review its recommendation and content-ranking mechanisms to prevent potentially misleading, manipulated or out-of-context material from being disproportionately amplified or repeatedly recommended to users.

The advisory calls on Meta to ensure that its recommendation systems do not expose users disproportionately to a single or limited set of narratives and to endeavour to provide a diversity of relevant and credible information.

The ministry has also sought stronger mechanisms for the rapid identification, review and escalation of content that gains significant reach through recommendation systems and could trigger public confusion, apprehension, violence or disturbances of public order.

IT Ministry sources said, as per PTI, the ministry had received reports from agencies suggesting that Meta's platforms were not promoting all narratives equally, with their algorithms appearing to favour one kind of content over others.

The ministry reportedly said it had observed that certain content relating to ongoing public events was being prominently displayed and recommended to users on Meta-operated platforms. Such material included videos, images, statements and static graphics concerning public-order and law-and-order situations.

According to the advisory, content displayed without adequate context or amplified disproportionately could contribute to public confusion, apprehension and disturbances of public order and social tranquillity.

MeitY has asked Meta to pay particular attention to content that presents old, unrelated or selectively edited visuals as current events. It has also flagged unverified or misleading claims concerning emergencies, restrictions, police action and other official measures.

The advisory further identifies audio-visual material relating to law-and-order situations that lacks adequate context, content likely to incite violence or other unlawful activity, and potentially misleading material that is amplified disproportionately.

The ministry said Meta, as an intermediary, must exercise due diligence under the Information Technology Rules, 2021.

These rules require intermediaries to inform users about prohibited information, including content that could cause public disorder or incite a cognisable offence relating to public order.

The advisory outlined six specific requests to Meta, including measures to identify and address content under the IT Rules and a review of recommendation and ranking systems to prevent misleading, manipulated or out-of-context material from being disproportionately amplified.

The ministry has also advised Meta to cooperate with law enforcement agencies and other authorised government bodies in a timely manner.

It advised Meta to implement the necessary measures with immediate effect and inform the ministry about the action taken.

The ministry also said any action taken in response to the advisory must comply with the Information Technology Act, the IT Rules and other applicable laws.

Apar Gupta, founder-director of the Internet Freedom Foundation, a digital rights advocacy group, criticised the advisory, arguing that although it was not legally binding, it amounted to a continuing censorship directive.

According to PTI, Gupta called on MeitY to withdraw the advisory and urged Meta to publicly disclose every takedown and instance of content downranking carried out in response to it.

He argued that the advisory's references to content being shown "without adequate context" and periods of "heightened public-order sensitivity" were not tied to any provision of the Information Technology Act or the IT Rules.

“There were not enough police and organisers couldn’t be seen anywhere. It was the local residents who started the rescue operation. I helped an elderly man who had come with his grandson. I made the sit in an empty food stall and arranged water from a house. I told them to wait there till the rush was over,” said Vivek Sonkar, another resident.

Most of the victims were rushed to the Calcutta Medical College and Hospital which was around two kilometres away from the stampede spot.

A junior doctor, who did not wish to be named, said injured victims and bodies were brought to the hospital in cabs and ambulances. Relatives and family members of the victims also arrived one after another.

“Some of the victims were declared dead while some had fractures. Many had fallen ill due to the suffocation and pressure of crowd and stampede in the pandal,” he said.

Allegations also surfaced that the state-run hospital lacked adequate facilities to treat the victims, forcing some of them to be referred to a private hospital. A section of junior doctors at Calcutta Medical College and Hospital told media persons that the hospital lacked proper trauma-care facilities, prompting the referrals.

The junior doctors also said that critically injured road accident victims brought to the hospital are referred to SSKM Hospital or RG Kar Medical College and Hospital.

“This is true. There is no proper facility at the CMCH for treating such trauma victims. The state health minister was seen on saying before television channels that victims would be treated at a private hospital. Several victims were referred to a private hospital on EM Bypass. This only implies that the state-run hospital can’t cater to such emergencies and disasters,” said Aniket Mahata, a prominent face of the RG Kar movement.

“These are baseless allegations. Those who are making these allegations seem to be knowing everything. Why study in a medical college then? There are so many avenues. If anyone has allegations he should come and meet the authorities and raise the demands,” Sharadwat Mukhopadhyay, state health minister told the media.
