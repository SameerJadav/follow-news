[Saudi Arabia](https://www.france24.com/en/tag/saudi-arabia/) accused Yemen’s Iran-backed [Houthi rebels](https://www.france24.com/en/tag/houthi-rebels/) of [trying to attack Islam’s holiest city](https://www.france24.com/en/middle-east/20260916-middle-east-live-saudi-arabia-accuses-houthi-rebels-of-launching-a-drone-strike-on-mecca), as the region responded with condemnation Wednesday but no sign of military support. The kingdom, a key US ally, declared [Mecca](https://www.france24.com/en/tag/mecca/) a “red line” as[slid deeper into the new front](https://www.france24.com/en/middle-east/20260915-middle-east-live-houthi-attacks-wound-13-civilians-in-southern-saudi-arabia) of the [Iran war](https://www.france24.com/en/tag/iran-conflict/).

With the world’s biggest oil exporter under fire from the rebels backed by the Saudis' regional adversary, the kingdom needs a safe outlet for its crude as well as military assistance. There's no clear path to either.

[A crucial Saudi pipeline has been shut down](https://www.france24.com/en/middle-east/20260912-saudi-arabia-shut-down-east-west-pipeline-as-houthis-tighten-grip-on-red-sea), reportedly for weeks, after an attack by Iranian-backed militias in [Iraq](https://www.france24.com/en/tag/iraq/). The Houthis have been targeting infrastructure in the kingdom and its shipping on the [Red Sea](https://www.france24.com/en/tag/red-sea/), where they have [seized several islands](https://www.france24.com/en/video/20260915-houthis-seize-red-sea-islands-attack-saudi-targets) in the [Bab el-Mandeb Strait](https://www.france24.com/en/middle-east/20260330-will-iranian-backed-yemen-houthis-rebels-block-the-bab-al-mandeb-strait). And [Iran](https://www.france24.com/en/tag/iran/) is still targeting ships in the [Strait of Hormuz](https://www.france24.com/en/tag/strait-of-hormuz/). Oil prices remain above $100 a barrel.

The Houthis denied firing drones toward Mecca, about 1,100 kilometres (680 miles) from Yemen’s border. The Saudis said they intercepted and destroyed the drone before it entered the airspace over the birthplace of the [Prophet Mohammed](https://www.france24.com/en/tag/prophet-mohammed/).

The attacks are the first test of a new defence pact among Saudi Arabia, [NATO](https://www.france24.com/en/tag/nato/) member [Turkey](https://www.france24.com/en/tag/turkey/) and nuclear power [Pakistan](https://www.france24.com/en/tag/pakistan/). But officials from Turkey and Pakistan told The Associated Press they have not received a Saudi request for support in response to the Houthis' escalation.

The Turkish official said efforts to “institutionalize” the alliance need to be completed before the pact becomes operational. The officials spoke on condition of anonymity because they were not authorized to speak publicly on the issue.

Amid the condemnation of the attempted attack on Mecca, Pakistani Prime Minister [Shehbaz Sharif](https://www.france24.com/en/tag/shehbaz-sharif/) said his country and the Saudis stand “unwaveringly, shoulder to shoulder”. Turkey's foreign minister expressed solidarity and said no country can “remain indifferent”, state-run Anadolu Agency reported.

Criticism also came from the Organization of Islamic Cooperation, representing over 57 Muslim states, which said the attempt violates the “sanctity of holy sites”.

This was the second attempted attack by the Houthis against Mecca after the rebels fired a ballistic missile toward the city in 2017, according to Turki al-Maliki, spokesperson for the Saudi-led military coalition fighting the rebels.

Saudi Arabia has been in talks with allies in hopes of replenishing its stock of interceptors, which has been depleted because of the US-Israeli war on Iran, according to two regional officials.

The officials said the Saudis have asked allies, including [France](https://www.france24.com/en/tag/france/), [Britain](https://www.france24.com/en/tag/united-kingdom/), Pakistan and [Egypt](https://www.france24.com/en/tag/egypt/), to deploy air-defence teams to help defend against missiles and drones.

The officials spoke on condition of anonymity because they weren’t authorized to brief the media.

One of the officials said the Saudis turned to European and regional allies because the United States has seen its own stock of interceptors depleted.

A second official said Saudi Arabia is also seeking to rally its regional and international allies to launch a collective response against the Houthis. “But the decision now is to give diplomacy a chance,” he said, noting the roles of [Oman](https://www.france24.com/en/tag/oman/) and Egypt in the process.

The United States has sold billions of dollars in weapons to Saudi Arabia and has committed to other support, but top US officials have indicated a lack of desire to get involved in the conflict.

US Vice President [JD Vance](https://www.france24.com/en/tag/jd-vance/) on Monday said Washington had been in “constant contact” with the Saudis and had “engaged in direct conversations with the Houthis.”

When asked whether Saudi Arabia had requested intervention, a US official said the US military’s Central Command has a task force in place for “enhancing intelligence sharing and planning support with Saudi forces”.

CENTCOM last week hosted senior military leaders from eight countries at a conference in [Germany](https://www.france24.com/en/tag/germany/) to discuss “opportunities for enhancing security cooperation in the [Middle East](https://www.france24.com/en/tag/middle-east/)”, the official also said, speaking on condition of anonymity because they were not authorized to comment publicly. The official did not confirm which countries attended.

On the ground, forces with Yemen's internationally recognized government have said they are regrouping to combat the Houthis after fleeing the rebels' advance. But those forces are a collection of armed groups with varying aims.

Later Wednesday, the rebels claimed, without offering evidence, that they shot down an F-15 fighter jet operated by the Saudi-led coalition over Yemen’s central province of Marib.

Houthi military spokesperson Yahya Saree didn’t say when it occurred, and the AP could not independently verify the claim. The rebels at times take days to claim attacks.

Spokespeople for the Saudi-led coalition and the Saudi government didn’t immediately respond to a request for comment.

While Saudi Arabia appeared to face the choice of pursuing diplomacy or taking the fight to the Houthis, the top diplomat for rival Iran was in China, the biggest buyer of Iranian oil.

Chinese Foreign Minister Wang Yi called on the US and Iran to exercise restraint and reopen the Strait of Hormuz as soon as possible, and expressed China’s concern about the spread of the fighting to Yemen and the Red Sea, according to China’s foreign ministry.

Iran's Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said in a statement after the meeting that Tehran seeks “the restoration of calm to the region and friendly and peaceful relations with our neighbors”.

“In this regard we have begun dialogue with the countries of the region,” he added.

[China](https://www.france24.com/en/tag/china/) has avoided direct involvement in the war, but President [Xi Jinping](https://www.france24.com/en/tag/xi-jinping/) at the [BRICS](https://www.france24.com/en/tag/brics/) summit in [New Delhi](https://www.france24.com/en/tag/new-delhi/) over the weekend again offered [Beijing](https://www.france24.com/en/tag/beijing/)'s help in ending it.

Oil flows through the Strait of Hormuz have recovered somewhat as tankers risk using a route near Oman on the other side of the waterway, with guidance from US forces. Estimates vary because ships can turn off location systems but range from 5 million to 7 million barrels per day on average.

That's less than half the amount before the war began with [US and Israeli strikes on Iran](https://www.france24.com/en/tag/iran-conflict/) on February 28.
