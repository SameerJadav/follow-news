[Saudi Arabia](https://www.france24.com/en/tag/saudi-arabia/) accused Yemen’s Iran-backed [Houthi rebels](https://www.france24.com/en/tag/houthi-rebels/) of [trying to attack Islam’s holiest city](https://www.france24.com/en/middle-east/20260916-middle-east-live-saudi-arabia-accuses-houthi-rebels-of-launching-a-drone-strike-on-mecca), as the region responded with condemnation Wednesday but no sign of military support. The kingdom, a key US ally, declared [Mecca](https://www.france24.com/en/tag/mecca/) a “red line” as[slid deeper into the new front](https://www.france24.com/en/middle-east/20260915-middle-east-live-houthi-attacks-wound-13-civilians-in-southern-saudi-arabia) of the [Iran war](https://www.france24.com/en/tag/iran-conflict/).

With the world’s biggest oil exporter under fire from the rebels backed by the Saudis' regional adversary, the kingdom needs a safe outlet for its crude as well as military assistance. There's no clear path to either.

[A crucial Saudi pipeline has been shut down](https://www.france24.com/en/middle-east/20260912-saudi-arabia-shut-down-east-west-pipeline-as-houthis-tighten-grip-on-red-sea), reportedly for weeks, after an attack by Iranian-backed militias in [Iraq](https://www.france24.com/en/tag/iraq/). The Houthis have been targeting infrastructure in the kingdom and its shipping on the [Red Sea](https://www.france24.com/en/tag/red-sea/), where they have [seized several islands](https://www.france24.com/en/video/20260915-houthis-seize-red-sea-islands-attack-saudi-targets) in the [Bab el-Mandeb Strait](https://www.france24.com/en/middle-east/20260330-will-iranian-backed-yemen-houthis-rebels-block-the-bab-al-mandeb-strait). And [Iran](https://www.france24.com/en/tag/iran/) is still targeting ships in the [Strait of Hormuz](https://www.france24.com/en/tag/strait-of-hormuz/). Oil prices remain above $100 a barrel.

The Houthis denied firing drones toward Mecca, about 1,100 kilometres (680 miles) from Yemen’s border. The Saudis said they intercepted and destroyed the drone before it entered the airspace over the birthplace of the [Prophet Mohammed](https://www.france24.com/en/tag/prophet-mohammed/).

The attacks are the first test of a new defence pact among Saudi Arabia, [NATO](https://www.france24.com/en/tag/nato/) member [Turkey](https://www.france24.com/en/tag/turkey/) and nuclear power [Pakistan](https://www.france24.com/en/tag/pakistan/). But officials from Turkey and Pakistan told The Associated Press they have not received a Saudi request for support in response to the Houthis' escalation.

The Turkish official said efforts to “institutionalize” the alliance need to be completed before the pact becomes operational. The officials spoke on condition of anonymity because they were not authorized to speak publicly on the issue.

Amid the condemnation of the attempted attack on Mecca, Pakistani Prime Minister [Shehbaz Sharif](https://www.france24.com/en/tag/shehbaz-sharif/) said his country and the Saudis stand “unwaveringly, shoulder to shoulder”. Turkey's foreign minister expressed solidarity and said no country can “remain indifferent”, state-run Anadolu Agency reported.

Criticism also came from the Organization of Islamic Cooperation, representing over 57 Muslim states, which said the attempt violates the “sanctity of holy sites”.

This was the second attempted attack by the Houthis against Mecca after the rebels fired a ballistic missile toward the city in 2017, according to Turki al-Maliki, spokesperson for the Saudi-led military coalition fighting the rebels.

Saudi Arabia has been in talks with allies in hopes of replenishing its stock of interceptors, which has been depleted because of the US-Israeli war on Iran, according to two regional officials.
