An estimated 237 people have been rescued but 18 are missing after accident off the coast of northern Cyprus

Fatalities after ferry carrying nearly 270 capsizes off northern Cyprus

The death toll has risen to “at least 8”, with 18 people still missing.

Ünal Üstel, the north’s Turkish Cypriot prime minister, told Turkish state broadcaster TRT that 237 people have so far been rescued.

Survivors have described a blast which went off at the vessel’s bow before the catamaran-style ship rolled over.

The captain and seven other crew members have been arrested and are in custody

The captain was allegedly among the first to abandon ship, according to witnesses who spoke to local media.

The boat was sailing from the port of Kyrenia, in the self-proclaimed Turkish Republic of Northern Cyprus, to the port of Taşucu in Turkey, when the disaster occurred about 4 nautical miles (7.5km) off the coast.

Three children rescued from the sea were in intensive care on Sunday.

The ferry has “completely sunk to a depth of 514 metres (1,686 feet)”, according to the Turkish Republic of Northern Cyprus’ transport minister

Ünal Üstel, the north’s Turkish Cypriot prime minister, has announced an inquiry has been launched into the marine disaster which he described as “the worst in Cypriot history”.

“Every angle of this incident will be investigated,” he told local media.

“Even the smallest negligence will be uncovered. There will be no leniency for anyone thought to bear responsibility.”

More survivors have described how the bow of the two-hulled vessel was damaged while at sea. According to Reuters those who were onboard have reported that the ferry had been moving erratically, and that as it moved through the water, a hard impact with the water’s surface broke the bow of the boat, ⁠allowing water to seep in.

One survivor, Efe Multici, told CNN Turk: “The vessel had been moving very badly. It was constantly lifting out of the water and slamming back down. It was shaking violently,” he said.

“Three or four minutes later, the ferry leapt high into the air, then came ‌crashing down. The front left side burst open and water started pouring in.”

Several survivors from the ferry which capsized off northern Cyprus have described a blast which went off at the vessel’s bow before it rolled over, and also claimed the captain was among the first to abandon ship.

One rescued passenger who spoke of a blast on board told local TRNC television after he was taken back ashore: “The front part exploded, they tried to turn around. And the side windows blew out. The boat started taking on water.”

The man, aged in his 50s and not named, said he spent an hour and a half in the water before being rescued, according to AFP. He said many people had been inside the ferry at the moment started taking on water.

“Those who could, got out,” he said, adding that he saw the captain among the first to abandon ship “without organising anything”.

Another witness, in his thirties, corroborated reports of an explosion. He said he had reported water leaks to the captain.

“The seats started to float, the captain kept going, and the bow exploded,” he said.

EU diplomats based in the Mediterranean island’s internationally recognised south have suggested the ferry may have been “in poor condition”.

“It was not in the best condition. It may well have been unseaworthy,” one diplomat said. “We are not hearing it was a new ship.”

Turkish Cypriot MP Sami Ozuslu told the Guardian it was still unclear who was on the passenger list. “We still do not know if any foreigners were on board,” he said. “This is a service used primarily by [mainland] Turks and Turkish Cypriots.”

“Rescue efforts are still ongoing but the ship now lies very deep beneath the sea surface and there is talk of bringing a special ship from Turkey to bring it up,” he added.

Cyprus has been lashed by storms over the past 48 hours with officials saying the adverse weather could have played a role in the disaster.
