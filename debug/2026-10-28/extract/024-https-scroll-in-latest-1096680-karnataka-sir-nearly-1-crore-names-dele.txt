The names of more than 99.3 lakh voters have been deleted from Karnataka’s final voter list published on Tuesday following the special intensive revision of electoral rolls, the Hindustan Times reported.

The number of voters fell to 4.5 crore from 5.5 crore that were on the state’s electoral rolls before the exercise began. This amounts to a decrease of about 17.9%.

In the final stage of the exercise, more than 8.6 lakh names were reinstated to the draft list, which had excluded over one crore voters in August.

Persons who applied for inclusion through Form 6 after Friday, when the period for claims and objections ended, would be added through supplementary lists to be published on a quarterly basis, The Hindu reported. Form 6 is used to register new voters.

Karnataka is among the 19 states and Union Territories where the voter roll revision is being carried out in the third phase of SIR.

The enumeration phase of the exercise in Karnataka began on June 30.

At the end of the draft stage, 65.4 lakh voters had been marked as permanently shifted, 12.3 lakh as dead, 15.1 lakh as absent and 7 lakh as duplicate entries. The highest deletions were recorded in Bengaluru’s urban districts.

The state Election Commission had said at the time that notices would be sent to 43 lakh persons whose enumeration forms either had anomalies or those who had not been mapped.

In 2025, the first phase of the exercise was conducted in Bihar. It was extended to 12 states and Union Territories in the second phase in late 2025 and the first half of 2026.

Since the exercise began, concerns have been raised that the revision could eliminate genuine voters from the rolls and even shape electoral outcomes, as Scroll has reported.

In May, the Supreme Court upheld the legality of the revision. But it said that the exercise does not mean that the Election Commission can decide on whether the person is an Indian citizen.

Despite this, Bharatiya Janata Party-ruled states have cited the SIR to deny ration cards to those who have been eliminated from the rolls. In some cases, the police have denied permission for their passports to be renewed.

Since October 2, nationwide protests have been held calling for Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar’s resignation over concerns about the functioning of the Election Commission and the alleged irregularities in how the voter roll is being revised.
