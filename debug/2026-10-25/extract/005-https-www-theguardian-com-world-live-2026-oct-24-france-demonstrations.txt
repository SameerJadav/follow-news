French police have made 1,000 arrests and Italian officers have raided homes in Milan, with protests also taking place in Spain and Albania

French police arrested almost 1,000 people on Saturday during the protests held across the country, interior minister Laurent Nunez said.

He told reporters that the number “will rise” as more data from police becomes available.

Although marches over the cost of living “passed off well” in cities, officers found themselves later confronted with “an extremely elevated level of violence” from “ultra-left” groups, Nunez said.

French police arrested 1,000 people during protests about the cost of living and education, with that number expected to rise, according to the country’s interior minister Laurent Nunez

The government estimated that at least 400,000 people had marched across France. Trade unions estimated the number at double that.

Two serious injuries were sustained during the protests, Nunez said, including a 20-year-old man who had his fingers blown off.

A demonstration on the outskirts of Paris was largely peaceful but was marred by violence at the end, with police using a water cannon and teargas to quell the crowds.

More than 70,000 police were on the streets of French cities, with shops boarded up and many museums closed.

In Italy, nine were arrested and more than 370 people “briefly detained” by police.

Protests also took place in Belgium, Spain, Portugal and Albania over different issues.

A peaceful protest during the day in Paris descended into violent clashes with police by the time night fell. The sound of pop music blaring and chanted slogans was swapped for the sound of teargas being released and fireworks being released, according to reports. Eyewitnesses say the mood soured at the end of the march, where rows of police officers and vans awaited the crowd.

Protesters reportedly fired fireworks towards law enforcement officers, who responded with charges and baton strikes.

The AFP news agency quoted David, a protester who gave only his first name. He is said to have appeared shaken and had a gash on his face, which he said was caused by a police officer throwing a baton at him.

“The police started moving towards everyone, telling them to get down and back off. They were just taking people down, you know, beating protesters,” he said.

Mike, a 23-year-old student who also declined to give his surname, said: “Look around, they’re just teenagers. Is it normal for teenagers to be teargassed and beaten with batons?”

For Jessica, a 24-year-old student, said: “I just don’t understand why there’s so much violence. I feel like our right to protest is being trampled on.”

As night fell, police began clearing the streets and protesters started to disperse.

Italian police said nine people were arrested, and 371 briefly detained during protests in eight cities.

In a statement, the police said 110 of those either arrested or detained were minors.

A man reportedly had his fingers blown off during trouble in France, a minister has said.

France’s interior minister – equivalent to the home secretary in the UK – Laurent Nunez said two people had been “seriously injured” during what he called “an extremely elevated level of violence” at today’s protests.

A 20-year-old man in Toulouse, in southern France, is said to have had his fingers blown off, and the other serious injury occurred in Clermont-Ferrand in the centre of the country. He did not give details of either case.

In an update, Nunez said “ultra-left” groups had thrown projectiles and aimed fireworks at police to provoke clashes.

He struck a more positive tone than last week when violence erupted across French cities during protests about the level of investment into public services and the cost of living.

Nunez said that, while some shops and businesses had been vandalised and looted today, “the scale was far smaller” than a week earlier. He stressed that shopping centres had been well protected this time.
