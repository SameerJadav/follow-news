"We all know that nowadays wars are not fought only with weapons. If a country is to be weakened, it can be done by attacking its economy. As India is becoming economically stronger, it is equally imperative that we protect our banking system from cyber robbers and cyber terrorists," the prime minister said.

The nature of cyber threats is also changing, and the nation cannot face such new challenges with old systems, training systems and tools, he said.

To face the new challenge, the country needs modern infrastructure and skilled human resources, he said, adding that here comes the role of institutions like RBI's data centre and Cybersecurity Training Institute.

Noting that these new facilities reflect a new priority of India, PM Modi said eastern India is emerging as a powerful growth engine of 'Viksit Bharat' (developed India).

PM Modi also launched a scathing attack on the opposition Congress, alleging that the masses were denied banking services during its rule.

He said banks were nationalised in the 1960s and 1970s in the name of the poor, but in 2014, it was found that more than half of the country's population was outside the banking ecosystem.

He said bank loans were a dream even for the middle-class, while the poor, small farmers, and women could not even think about it.

"However, loans of thousands of crores of rupees were available for the rich and well-connected people over phone calls during the Congress rule. As a result of this mismanagement, India's banking system became vulnerable to NPAs (non-performing assets). The banking system was on the brink of collapse," the prime minister said.

He said things changed after the BJP came to power in 2014, and now India's banking sector was among the most efficient in the world, with people having access to banking services round the clock.

Small entrepreneurs, traditional artisans, street vendors and workers in the unorganised sector have benefited from formal banking services for the first time, he said.

"A former PM had said from the soil of Odisha that for every rupee sent from Delhi, only 15 paise reaches the people. Today, the entire amount reaches the bank accounts of the beneficiaries. Around 60 crore Jan Dhan accounts have been opened," he added.

Debit and credit cards were earlier luxury items for the masses, but those are now available for all, he said.

"Development for us means balanced growth of India and opportunities for villages and small towns," PM Modi said.

The past decade was about taking banking to those deprived of it. The coming decade is about making banking more efficient and reliable, he added.

The PM said Odisha will write its future in sectors from mines to microchips and many doors of opportunity will be opened for the youth.

Union Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman, RBI Governor Sanjay Malhotra, Odisha Governor Brajmohan Ram, Chief Minister Mohan Charan Majhi, Union Tribal Affairs Minister Jual Oram, and Deputy CMs Kanak Vardhan Singh Deo and Pravati Parida were also present at the event.

PM Modi landed at Biju Patnaik International Airport here at 4.15 pm aboard a special Indian Air Force aircraft. From the airport, he took a helicopter to Khurda, and then proceeded by road to the RBI facility.

The PM returned to the airport by road as people lined up the roads and he waved at them. He departed around 6.20 pm, officials said.

Police had made elaborate security arrangements, deploying around 2,100 personnel for the PM's visit, they said.

Traffic restrictions were also imposed from 5.40 pm to 6.40 pm along the route connecting the RBI data centre to the airport.

The RBI's facility is spread across 18.55 acres at Info Valley-II in Jatni, and the foundation stone was laid by then RBI governor Shaktikanta Das on March 22, 2023.

The data centre will host mission-critical payment and settlement systems, along with other flagship initiatives, the government said in a statement.

It will contribute to widening the reach, efficiency and reliability of digital financial services, and will form the secure digital foundation on which India's increasingly technology-driven financial economy can grow, it said.

Similarly, the Cybersecurity Training Institute will boost capability building, providing a dedicated institutional platform for cybersecurity and enterprise-technology training, research, and sector-wide capability development, it said.

Majhi, in a social media post, said the data centre will add to Odisha's journey of development.

He said this was the PM's ninth visit to Odisha after the formation of the BJP government in the state in 2024.
