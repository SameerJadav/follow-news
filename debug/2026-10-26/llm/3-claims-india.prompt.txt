The Odisha government has intensified its preparations for a potential cyclone threat, placing emergency teams on alert and ordering officials to remain at their headquarters as a very severe cyclonic storm is expected to make landfall along the Andhra Pradesh coast on October 28.

In view of the situation, the leave of district collectors, district magistrates and other officers posted in critical offices has been cancelled, while schools and colleges have also been closed.

The India Meteorological Department (IMD) has forecast that the system, currently a depression over the Bay of Bengal, could develop into a very severe cyclonic storm before crossing the coast between Bapatla and Visakhapatnam. Wind speeds are expected to reach 115-125 kmph, with gusts of up to 140 kmph.

Heavy to very heavy rainfall is likely to continue in Puducherry, Karaikal and adjoining districts of Tamil Nadu, according to the weather forecast.

Although the predicted landfall area is around 300 km from Odisha’s Gopalpur, the Odisha government is preparing for possible impacts, including heavy rainfall and strong winds across southern and coastal districts, news agency PTI reported.

Chief Minister Mohan Charan Majhi has directed officials to prioritise public safety, evacuations and emergency response, reiterating the government’s objective of ensuring zero casualties.

In view of the approaching cyclone, the Odisha government has cancelled the leave of all district collectors, district magistrates and other officers posted in critical offices.

Also read | Andhra Pradesh, Odisha on alert as IMD warns of very severe cyclonic storm over Bay of Bengal by October 27

According to a notification issued by the General Administration Department, the officials have been instructed to return to their respective headquarters immediately to ensure administrative preparedness.

Majhi said Odisha could experience heavy rainfall in southern and coastal districts, accompanied by gusty winds reaching 70-90 kmph.

Ganjam, Gajapati, Rayagada, Koraput and Malkangiri are expected to face the greatest impact, while other coastal areas could also receive significant rainfall.

The chief minister has ordered the closure of all schools, colleges and Anganwadi centres in Ganjam, Gajapati, Rayagada, Koraput and Malkangiri on October 28 and 29.

Collectors in other districts will decide whether educational institutions need to be shut, depending on how the situation develops.

Majhi has also instructed officials to relocate people living in vulnerable areas to safer places. Special attention has been directed towards elderly people, patients and pregnant women, who should be shifted to healthcare facilities if required.

Emergency response teams have been positioned in vulnerable areas as the state prepares for possible evacuations and cyclone-related damage.

Majhi said around 60 Odisha Disaster Rapid Action Force (ODRAF) teams and 17 National Disaster Response Force (NDRF) teams had been deployed, while 225 Fire Services teams were on high alert.

Collectors in southern and coastal districts have been asked to ensure that cyclone shelters are ready to accommodate residents who may need to be evacuated.

Ganjam District Collector Keerthi Vasan V said 100 cyclone shelters had been prepared, while 1,000 temporary locations had been identified to accommodate people if necessary.

The chief minister has directed the Health and Family Welfare, Water Resources, Energy, Agriculture, Panchayati Raj and Rural Development departments, along with engineering services, to remain prepared and ensure prompt repairs to any damage caused by the cyclone and associated rainfall.

Cyclone warning signals have been hoisted at nine ports, including Chennai, Nagapattinam, Cuddalore, Karaikal, Pamban and Thoothukudi, following the intensification of a low-pressure area over the Bay of Bengal into a depression.

Puducherry and neighbouring areas have been experiencing continuous rainfall since Saturday night as the northeast monsoon remains active in the region.

The IMD has forecast heavy to very heavy rainfall in Puducherry, Karaikal and adjoining districts of Tamil Nadu, with wet conditions likely to persist as the system moves towards the Andhra Pradesh coast.

The India Meteorological Department (IMD) on Sunday said that the Depression over the East Central and adjoining Southeast Bay of Bengal is likely to move southwards and intensify into a Deep Depression. According to the IMD’s latest update, the weather phenomenon will further in intensify further into a cyclonic storm and into a severe cyclonic storm by the morning of Tuesday, October 27.

The weather agency noted that the system would intensify further into a very severe cyclonic storm and reach Westcentral Bay of Bengal near the Andhra Pradesh coast around the morning of October 28.

Cyclone Arnab is expected to make landfall between Bapatla and Visakhapatnam as a very severe cyclonic storm with the maximum sustained wind speed of 115-125 kmph gusting to 140 kmph.

IMD Bhubaneswar Director Manorama Mohanty said Odisha was likely to be affected by rainfall and wind activity as the system intensified and moved towards the coast.

The weather department has warned of heavy rainfall in parts of Odisha and Andhra Pradesh as the system approaches the coast. The expected impact has prompted the Odisha government to review its response mechanisms and assign responsibilities to different departments to ensure coordinated action.

Odisha Chief Minister Mohan Charan Majhi on Saturday said the state government had reviewed the possible impact of the cyclone and assigned specific responsibilities to departments to ensure preparedness ahead of the system's expected landfall.

"We have just concluded a review meeting regarding the impact of cyclone Arnab and the measures to tackle it. We have assigned specific responsibilities to each department, ensuring they are fully prepared in advance. The state government is completely ready to handle this situation. As always, our primary objective remains the same: Zero casualties," CM Majhi said.

In neighbouring Andhra Pradesh, the Met Department forecast rainfall in parts of the southern state from October 26 to 29.

It said thunderstorms accompanied by lightning are expected at isolated places over Coastal Andhra Pradesh, Yanam and Rayalaseema on October 26, 27 and 28, followed by scattered heavy to very heavy rain with extremely heavy rain likely at isolated places over Coastal Andhra Pradesh and Yanam on October 27.

For October 28, the Met Department forecast scattered heavy to very heavy rain with extremely heavy rain likely at isolated places over Coastal Andhra Pradesh and Yanam.

On October 29, it predicted heavy rain in parts of Coastal Andhra Pradesh and Yanam.

Krishna District Collector D.K. Balaji has directed officials to remain alert for the cyclone and take steps to prevent human and property loss.

Mr. Balaji, who reviewed cyclone preparedness with the district and mandal-level officials, instructed them to be available at the posted places and take measures for supply of drinking water and other essentials.

Meanwhile, Andhra Pradesh Central Power Distribution Corporation Limited (APCPDCL) CMD P. Pulla Reddy said leaves of officials and staff were cancelled in wake of the cyclonic storm. He also asked the field staff to caution the public against touching snapped electric cables. Control Rooms have been set up in all districts under CPDCL, Mr. Pulla Reddy said.

Eluru District Collector K. Vetri Selvi has directed the officials to be alert and asked the Irrigation department engineers to take measures to prevent damage to canal and tank bunds.

Villagers were requested not to venture into rivers and canals and move on the bunds as the impact of the cyclone would be heavy.

Control Rooms have been set up at Eluru Collectorate with helpline numbers 18002331077, 08812-224519 and 9491041419, and public are requested to contact and alert the officers in emergency, the Collector said.

NTR District Collector G. Lakshmisha reviewed the preparedness for the severe cyclone with the officials of Revenue, Irrigation, A.P. Transco, Medical and Health, Fire and other departments. He directed the Tahsildars to shift people staying in vulnerable areas to safer locations.

A control room has been set up the Collectorate with the helpline number 91549 70454 in Vijayawada, Mr. Lakshmisha said.

He appealed to the public not to take shelter in dilapidated buildings, under the trees, electric poles and hoardings during the event of the cyclone.

Prime Minister Narendra Modi on Sunday urged constant vigilance to safeguard India’s banking system against cyber threats as the nation grows economically stronger.

The prime minister said that following a decade focused on expanding banking access to the unbanked, the coming decade will focus on making banking more efficient and reliable.

“The past decade was about taking banking to those who were deprived of it; the coming decade is about making banking more efficient and more reliable,” he said.

PM Modi said the inauguration of the two facilities was a significant occasion for Bhubaneswar and would benefit Odisha and the country. He stressed the importance of replacing outdated training tools with modern infrastructure and skilled human resources to address evolving cyber threats. “Right here, the role of this new cybersecurity institute becomes crucial,” he said.

Modi also highlighted the expansion of banking access through digital services and government initiatives. He said digital banking had connected Indians to round-the-clock banking services and enabled people without collateral to access affordable loans to start their own work. “Today, digital banking has connected every Indian to 24-hour banking services,” he said.

He said nearly 600 million Jan Dhan accounts had been opened, adding that government payments now reached beneficiaries’ accounts without leakages.

He also highlighted the role of the Pradhan Mantri MUDRA Yojana, PM SVANidhi, and PM Vishwakarma in extending formal credit to small entrepreneurs, street vendors, traditional artisans and workers in the unorganised sector. He said 130 million people had become first-time entrepreneurs under the MUDRA scheme. He also said self-help groups representing 10 crore rural women had received thousands of crores of rupees in bank assistance.

“For the first time, they have received the benefit of formal banking,” Modi said, referring to small entrepreneurs, traditional artisans, street vendors and workers in the unorganised sector.

The prime minister highlighted the expansion of pension and insurance coverage through government-backed schemes. He said the Atal Pension Yojana covered 90 million citizens, including 380,000 in Odisha.

Under the Pradhan Mantri Suraksha Bima Yojana, accident insurance cover of ₹2 lakh is available for an annual premium of ₹20, while the Pradhan Mantri Jeevan Jyoti Bima Yojana provides life insurance cover at an annual premium of less than ₹450, he said. He said more than ₹25,000 crore in claims had been disbursed under the schemes to families during difficult times.
