Donald Trump blocked numerous sanctions on Russia two days before they were due to take effect, as a row with his Ukrainian counterpart Volodymyr Zelensky deepens.

The sanctions were passed by the Congress and signed into law the president in September.

But Trump issued an executive order Friday night saying that suspending some was in the US national interest, prompting a backlash from US lawmakers. Zelensky said it was unfortunate the US has decided to "completely disregard" the sanctions.

Relations between Washington and Kyiv frayed after the US struck a controversial deal to import Russian diesel last week. Ukraine has accused the Trump administration of withdrawing key military support.

Trump and Zelensky have exchanged sharp words about one another. Ukraine's president declared it was nice to be among "real leaders" while addressing an EU summit, after Trump said Zelensky was "more difficult to deal with" than Russian President Vladimir Putin.

It also included sanctions against senior Russian figures, including Putin, as well as Russian financial institutions and Moscow's so-called "shadow fleet" of vessels used to circumvent existing embargoes.

Russian envoy Kirill Dmitriev called the waiver "an important and truly historic moment".

Posting on X, he said the sanctions would have inflicted "serious damage", primarily on the US and global market, and that economic cooperation benefits both nations.

Democrats including the Virginia Congressman Don Beyer criticised the move, accusing Trump of capitulating to Moscow and turning his back on Kyiv and Zelensky.

"Trump's latest surrender to Putin again betrays our Ukrainian partners and defies clear congressional intent of legislation designed to punish Russia for its illegal war of aggression," Beyer wrote on X.

A handful of Republicans, too, have come out against the move.

Senator Ted Cruz of Texas, a Trump ally, called waving sanctions against Russia a mistake, saying on Fox News: "I wish the president hadn't done it."

He added there were other ways to could address diesel prices and "the current path, unfortunately, just... benefits Putin and that's not a good idea."

Senator John Cornyn, also of Texas, said on X: "Helping Putin in Ukraine is reckless. He would not stop with Ukraine and our NATO allies would be next."

North Carolina Senator Thom Tillis called it "beyond irresponsible", noting the sanctions legislation passed the chamber with bipartisan support and a large majority - only 11 senators voted against it.

Trump could bring down energy prices "by solving the Iran problem, not by doing a solid for Vladimir Putin," he said.

Nebraska Representative Don Bacon said many lawmakers will resist funding the president's agenda "knowing he is propping up a dictator's invasion and throwing an ally under the bus".

The move is likely to draw further anger from Ukraine, where Zelensky has long stressed the need for stringent sanctions to limit funding Russia can use in its war effort.

Zelensky said on Saturday morning that the sanctions "could have helped enormously" and that the law was "supposed to impose truly effective pressure on Russia to bring this war to an end".

He pointed to Russian overnight attacks on 15 Ukrainian energy facilities that had caused power cuts in several regions and said: "Russia is trying to cause a blackout in Ukraine â right before winter."

Zelensky said it was "important that everyone in America also sees what is really happening" on the ground.

He later told reporters that he believed the Trump administration would lift more sanctions against Russia.

Negotiators from Ukraine spoke to their US counterparts on Friday and that both teams could meet next week, he also said.

Trump was initially criticised by Zelensky and Ukraine's European allies for temporarily suspending some US sanctions to import Russian diesel in a bid to bring down US energy prices, which have skyrocketed during the Iran war. His Republican party is at risk of losing control of the US Congress, in next month's midterm elections, largely due to voters' sour mood about affordability.

Zelensky suggested the deal was tantamount to the US investing in Russia's war effort.

A few days later, Trump claimed that both Russia and Ukraine had agreed to an "energy ceasefire" to facilitate the export of Russian diesel.

Both nations have been intensively striking one another's energy infrastructure. Kyiv says this is to limit revenue for Russia's war machine, while Moscow claims it only targets facilities used by the Ukrainian military.
