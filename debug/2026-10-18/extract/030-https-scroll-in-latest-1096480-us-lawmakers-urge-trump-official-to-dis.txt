Six United States lawmakers, including Pramila Jayapal and Ilhan Omar, have urged Secretary of State Marco Rubio to raise with Indian authorities concerns about alleged police excesses against those protesting to demand Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar’s resignation and the suspension of the special intensive revision of electoral rolls.

Rubio will be visiting Mumbai and Jamnagar between October 19 and October 22. External Affairs Minister S Jaishankar will travel to Mumbai to meet the Trump official on October 20.

In a social media post on Saturday, Indian-born Jayapal said that the US and India have an obligation to defend human rights as the world’s largest democracies.

In a letter addressed to Rubio on Friday, Jayapal, Omar, Jim McGovern, Rashida Tlaib, Eleanor Holmes Norton, and Jesus Garcia expressed concern about a “pattern of repression, wrongful detention, and excessive use of force” by authorities in India during nationwide protests to demand Kumar’s resignation.

This came against the backdrop of protests in the national capital and other parts of the country since October 2 over concerns about the functioning of the Election Commission and its handling of the special intensive revision of electoral rolls.

On October 10, around 7,000 protesters in Delhi were detained and allegedly assaulted for taking part in the demonstrations.

The Democratic Party leaders, in their letter, urged Rubio to press the Indian government to investigate the alleged human rights violations during the protests and release any persons detained “for peacefully exercising the rights of freedom of expression and assembly”.

The lawmakers also accused the Indian diplomatic missions in the US of surveilling protesters on American soil.

“Members of US human rights organisations and the Indian diaspora who joined protests in Washington DC, Chicago and San Francisco disclosed that they had been photographed by individuals who identified themselves as having been hired by the Indian embassy and the consulates general,” read their letter.

The trigger for the protests in India was a September 23 investigation by The Indian Express, which revealed that Election Commissioners Sukhbir Singh Sandhu and Vivek Joshi had formally objected 14 times in 10 months to decisions by the poll panel related to the special intensive revision, saying some had been taken without their knowledge.

On Wednesday, Jayapal said that the Indian government’s actions to “purge” 13 crore persons from the electoral rolls through the special intensive revision appeared similar to measures being taken by the Donald Trump administration to “undermine voting rights in a democracy”.

Since the special intensive revision began in June 2025, concerns have been raised that the revision could eliminate genuine voters from the rolls and even shape electoral outcomes, as Scroll has reported. Overall, more than 13 crore names have been removed from voter lists and draft rolls since the first phase of the exercise in June 2025 in Bihar.

Responding to Jayapal’s comments on Friday, Ministry of External Affairs spokesperson Randhir Jaiswal said that the remarks were “unwarranted and uninformed, and tantamount to interfering in the electoral practices of a democracy”.

“This is an approach that undermines democracy itself,” Jaiswal added.
