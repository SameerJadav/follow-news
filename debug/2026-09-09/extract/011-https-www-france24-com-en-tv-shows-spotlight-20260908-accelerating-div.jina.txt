# Accelerating diversification: Canada seeks economic sovereignty amid Trump's 'nonsensical' posturing

François Picard is pleased to welcome Jean Simard, President and CEO of the Aluminum Association of Canada. According to Simard, the US-Canada aluminum dispute is not your conventional disagreement over trade, but rather a 'Trump posture that makes no sense". As American industrial demand substantially exceeds domestic production capacity, tariffs will do nothing to reduce Canadian aluminum from the US market. Protectionism does not eliminate interdependence, explains Simard, it makes that interdependence considerably more expensive.

More significantly, however, he interprets the conflict as an accelerator of structural change within [Canada](https://www.france24.com/en/tag/canada/). External pressure from Washington is, in his account, encouraging Canada to reduce internal trade barriers, diversify export relationships, protect strategically important industries and research capacity, and deepen its relationship with [Europe](https://www.france24.com/en/tag/europe/). And this now rises to the level of economic sovereignty and geopolitical realignment.

Rather than forcing Canada to submit to US demands, sustained economic pressure may now be strengthening domestic support for full-on resistance and diversifying away from US dependence. His striking assertion that Canada is “making a new country out of our country” captures this broader thesis: the [trade war](https://www.france24.com/en/tag/trade-war/) is damaging and disruptive, but he sees it simultaneously as an opportunity to reconstruct Canada’s economic model and embrace new partnerships in other corners of the globe .

Europe occupies a particularly important place in this transformation and has the potential to move “from being a bystander to being a full actor”. Simard highlights the dispute as part of a larger contest over whether US allies will respond individually to American economic coercion or develop greater collective strategic autonomy.
