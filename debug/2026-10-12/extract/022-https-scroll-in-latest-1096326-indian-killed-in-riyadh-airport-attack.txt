The Indian embassy in Saudi Arabia said on Sunday that an Indian citizen injured in the attack on the Riyadh airport had died from his injuries, a day after the strike.

Saturday’s attack on the airport killed 13 persons from several countries and injured more than 300, making it the deadliest strike on Saudi Arabia since May 2015, BBC reported.

At least 11 Indians were among the injured, the Indian embassy said, including the one who later died.

All India Majlis-e-Ittehadul Muslimeen chief Asaduddin Owaisi identified the deceased person as Mohammad Yousuf Ali, a resident of Hyderabad.

He was travelling with his family for Islamic pilgrimage Umrah and was waiting at the airport for a flight when the attack took place, The Indian Express reported.

The Indian embassy expressed its condolences to Ali’s family and said it would provide assistance, including facilitating the early repatriation of the body.

The diplomatic mission said that while three injured Indians were in intensive care but in stable conditions, two others were admitted to regular hospital wards. Other injured Indians had been discharged.

The others who died included four Saudis, two people from Bangladesh and one person each from the United States of America, Jordan, Palestine, Syria, Sudan and Egypt, the Saudi civil aviation authority said.

Yemen’s Houthi militant group has claimed responsibility for several attacks on Saudi Arabia in the past 24 hours, including the strike on the Riyadh airport, BBC reported. The group has warned airlines against using Saudi airports or airspace.

The attacks mark an escalation in the conflict between the Iran-backed Houthis, who are seeking to topple Yemen’s internationally recognised government, and Saudi Arabia, which backs the government.

Separately, India on Sunday condemned attacks on two commercial vessels in the Strait of Hormuz region, including a drone strike on the tanker MT Petro Dolphin that left two Indian seafarers missing.

According to the Ministry of External Affairs, MT Petro Dolphin had 11 Indian crew members aboard when it was reportedly struck by a drone on Sunday.

Union Shipping Minister Sarbananda Sonowal said that nine crew members had been rescued and search operations were underway to find the missing seafarers.

A second vessel, the Marshall Islands-flagged MT Valthera, was struck by an unidentified projectile, resulting in a fire on board, the foreign ministry said.

However, all 11 Indian seafarers on the vessel were reported safe.

“India reiterates the need for immediate de-escalation and a resolution to the ongoing conflict through dialogue and diplomacy,” the ministry said. “Attacks on commercial vessels, seafarers and civilian infrastructure must cease.”

Several ships have been struck in the Strait of Hormuz since the war in West Asia began in February. At least 11 Indian seafarers have died in the attacks.
