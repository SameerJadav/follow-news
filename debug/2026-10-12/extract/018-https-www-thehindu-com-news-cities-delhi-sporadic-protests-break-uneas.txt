Hundreds of protesters, including Rajya Sabha MP John Brittas and All India Students’ Association (AISA) president Neha Bora, were detained as sporadic protests against the special intensive revision (SIR) of electoral rolls and Chief Election Commissioner (CEC) Gyanesh Kumar continued across Delhi for the second consecutive day on Sunday (October 11, 2026) amid heavy security deployment and barricading across central Delhi to prevent protesters from reaching Jantar Mantar.

With shops in Connaught Place reopening after 24 hours and metro services resuming at stations shut on Saturday (October 10, 2026), normalcy returned in the morning. Shoppers trickled into the market, but the relative calm was disrupted as student organisations and other groups attempted to march through the commercial hub, only to be detained by the police. The heavy deployment of police personnel and barricades at key junctions appeared to make visitors hesitant to venture further into the area, as people walked past rows of shops but paused to assess the security arrangements.

The protests, called by the Cockroach Janta Party (CJP), continued despite restrictions on gatherings and movement in New Delhi. With demonstrators unable to assemble at Jantar Mantar, several groups attempted to mobilise at different locations across the city and the Outer Circle of Connaught Place became the epicentre.

Representatives of student and youth organisations, including the All India Students’ Federation (AISF), the Students’ Federation of India (SFI), the Democratic Youth Federation of India (DYFI) and the Krantikari Yuva Sangathan (KYS), attempted to take out a march in the morning.

Around noon, 60 to 70 protesters began marching from Gate 1 of Rajiv Chowk Metro station towards Gate 6 of Palika Bazaar with banners and placards, and raising slogans of “vote chor, gaddi chhod (vote thieves, leave the chair)”. They demanded the rollback of the SIR exercise and the resignation of Mr. Kumar.

The police and the Rapid Action Force (RAF) detained the protesters, and tense scenes unfolded as several demonstrators lay on the road, refusing to leave. Police personnel forcibly lifted some of them and shoved them into a bus. Some protesters sustained injuries and a man was seen bleeding from his forehead.

By 12.30 p.m., barricades had been erected and access to Janpath Road was cut off, further restricting movement through the area.

Around two hours later, a few AISA representatives gathered near Ramakrishna Ashram Marg Metro station and attempted to march towards Connaught Place. The police quickly moved in to stop them, and the ensuing chase halted traffic.

Neha Bora and supporters take part in a protest march from R.K. Ashram Metro Station in New Delhi on October 11, 2026. | Photo Credit: Shashi Shekhar Kashyap

Police personnel, Central Reserve Police Force personnel and RAF members converged on the junction and detained protesters. Journalists covering the demonstrations were also rounded up. Neha Bora and other AISA representatives managed to cross a barricade and run towards Panchkuian Road before being intercepted and detained by a large contingent of police personnel.

Around 4 p.m., nearly 350 people associated with the Revolutionary Workers’ Party of India, Disha Students’ Organisation, Naujawan Bharat Sabha and Stree Mukti League began a rally from Connaught Place’s outer circle. The protesters moved along the inner circle before being picked up by the police near the Pind Balluchi square.

Later in the evening, women activists of the SFI were detained after 6 p.m. while taking out a ‘Tiranga Yatra’.

Several protesters said they returned on Sunday (October 11) because they did not want to abandon the cause, even though they were unable to reach Jantar Mantar.

“I was here yesterday, but the police did not allow me to cross even the first barricade, so I had to stand outside the entire day. Today, they at least let me till here. I will come tomorrow, I do not want to give up,” said a protester who had travelled from Mumbai.
