Prime Minister Narendra Modi on Sunday unveiled infrastructure projects worth ₹13,000 crore across sectors including railways, ports and roads at the Indian Institute of Technology (IIT), Madras, in Chennai, aimed at boosting connectivity, industrial growth and infrastructure in Tamil Nadu.

Earlier in the day, Modi, accompanied by governor Rajendra Arlekar, chief minister C Joseph Vijay, Union minister L Murugan and senior government officials, flagged off metro rail services between Porur Bypass and Poonamallee. He also offered prayers at the Arulmigu Jalakandeswarar Temple on the IIT Madras campus.

After inaugurating the projects virtually alongside Vijay, Modi said, “It is a pleasure to be in Chennai with all of you. This is a historic day for Tamil Nadu. Today’s programme involves projects worth ₹13,000 crore. We are inaugurated many completed projects and laying foundations for new ones.”

Noting that some of the projects would create jobs for young people across the state, Modi said, “The new metro rail connectivity would strengthen the industrial and commercial sectors.”

“Today marks, the auspicious beginning of Navratri. It is a holy occasion. I am in Tamil Nadu, the sacred land of Shakti alongside Shiva. Before, coming to this programme, I had the privilege of receiving the blessings of Lord Shiva at the Arulmigu Jalakandeshwar Temple,” Modi said about his visit to the temple located at the IIT Madras campus.

Modi said, “India draws momentum from its cultural strength. It is advancing through the progress of science and building new opportunities through modern infrastructure. Our heritage and our future aspirations are moving forward hand in hand.”

Recalling his 25 years as head of government, Modi said, “In October 2001, I took oath as the chief minister (of Gujarat) and since then, the countrymen have given me the opportunity to serve as the chief minister and then as the Prime Minister.”

“In this 25 year span, my faith in the two pillars of democracy has grown steadily stronger. First, the people of the country and second, cooperative federalism. I have always taken the mantra of state development to mean national development”, he said.

Modi said that before assuming office as the CM of Gujarat, he had the opportunity to interact with leaders such as the late DMK patriarch M Karunanidhi and the late AIADMK general secretary J Jayalalithaa.

“Even after I became the Prime Minister, I got the opportunity to work alongside with Jayalalithaa, former chief ministers O Panneerselvam, Edappadi K Palaniswami and DMK President M K Stalin,” he said.

“Governments change, but Tamil Nadu’s development and my firm resolve to serve its people have always remained unbroken. And today with this very conviction, we are moving forward on this journey of Tamil Nadu’s development alongside chief minister C Joseph Vijay”, he said.

Underlining that urban development was an opportunity rather than a challenge, Modi said, “Public transport plays a massive role in this. Public transport systems must be excellent as this increases the ease of living.”

“We all know that better transportation requires more than just widening roads. Modern cities require holistic infrastructure and that is why, over the past decade, we have massively expanded the metro network across the country. In 2014, metro services were operational in only 5 cities nationwide, today, metro services operate in 25 cities across the country,” he said.

He noted that the country’s metro network had grown from around 250 km in 2014 to more than 1,100km.

Stressing that the government wanted to empower India, Modi said the Centre was promoting manufacturing in states such as Tamil Nadu. “Tamil Nadu’s manufacturing sector, its small-scale industries, and every person working make every Indian proud. Today, the central government initiatives like Make in India and Vocal for Local are bringing benefits to Tamil Nadu as well,” he said.

Maintaining that every state in the country, from components to technology and finished products, was strengthening the Centre’s comprehensive production chain, Modi said, “The result of these efforts is that today we are the second-largest mobile manufacturer in the world.”

“From 2014 to now, our electronics exports have grown 11 times, our defence exports have grown 50 times, and our engineering exports have also set new records.”, Modi said.

Acknowledging that growth in the manufacturing sector creates lakhs of direct and indirect jobs, Modi said Tamil Nadu made a commendable contribution to this growth.
