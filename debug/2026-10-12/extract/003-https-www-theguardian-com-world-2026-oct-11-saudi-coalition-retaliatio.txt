Unidentified projectile strikes King Khalid airport, killing one and injuring 11, as escalating attacks threaten to draw more countries into Yemen conflict

A projectile hit a passenger terminal at the airport in Riyadh on Sunday, the day after a deadly missile attack on the facility carried out by Yemen’s Iran-backed Houthi rebel group killed 13 people.

An unidentified projectile hit terminal 4 at King Khalid international airport, starting a fire, killing one person and injuring 11, according to Saudi civil aviation authorities. Air defences also intercepted a cruise missile in the vicinity of the airport. It was the latest of several attacks on the airport in the past week.

The escalating attacks threatened to draw more countries into the Yemen conflict and further widen the regional fallout of the US-Iran war, which has already spread to Lebanon and Iraq.

In a post on X, the British embassy in Saudi Arabia advised against all but essential travel to the cities of Jeddah and Dammam and to avoid almost all airports.

After Saturday’s strike on Riyadh airport, the US president, Donald Trump, said he was weighing whether to join the Saudi military campaign in Yemen, telling reporters: “We’re going to look into it.”

The Iran-allied Houthis claimed to have launched several attacks on the airport, including the attack on Saturday, which killed 13 people and injured more than 300 – the deadliest attack on a Gulf country since the war with Iran started in March and an apparent escalation in the Houthis’ war with Saudi Arabia and the Yemeni government that Riyadh sponsors.

In response to the attack, Saudi authorities extended the closure of the airport and said schools would be moved online until Thursday.

The Houthis claimed that Saudi Arabia struck the airport in Yemen’s rebel-held capital, Sana’a, on Sunday, without providing more information on damage or casualties.

Videos circulated on social media showing travellers walking through debris-strewn ground in the Riyadh airport as sirens blared. Four Saudi citizens, two Bangladeshi nationals, a Palestinian, a US citizen, an Egyptian, a Jordanian, a Syrian and a Sudanese were killed in the missile strike.

On Sunday evening, Saudi authorities also confirmed King Fahd international airport in the eastern city of Dammam had been attacked, with the country’s civil aviation authority saying on X: “The assessment of the damage and verification of the safety of airport facilities were still under way, in coordination with the relevant authorities.”

On Saturday night, the spokesperson for the Saudi-led coalition in Yemen, Maj Gen Turki al-Maliki, said the coalition would “respond firmly” to the attack, offering few other details.

The Houthis said a Saudi airstrike on Sana’a on Sunday hit a home and killed a child, as well as injuring six others.

The Houthis doubled down on their threats on Sunday, warning airlines, passengers and staff to stay away from Saudi airports. The group said “the worst is yet to come” if Saudi military action in Yemen continued.

“We renew our warning to all airlines, experts, employees, workers and travellers against using Saudi airports and airspace, as they are vulnerable to attack and have become a theatre of operations for our forces,” the Houthis said on X.

Trump said on Saturday night that he would speak to the Saudi crown prince, Mohammed bin Salman, after the previous attack on the airport.

The US has already been assisting with intelligence cooperation and targeting, with about 200 US military advisers stationed in Saudi Arabia to help with the campaign. Until now, the US has not directly taken part in fighting against the Houthis.

Direct US involvement could lead the group to resume its targeting of ships passing through the Bab al-Mandab strait, through which about 10% of global maritime goods transit.

It would also further entangle the US in Middle East wars as the American public grows increasingly frustrated with the continuing Iran conflict. Trump is facing the midterm elections in November with an unpopular war and soaring prices at home threatening his party’s performance at the polls.

Saudi Arabia is already carrying out airstrikes against the Houthis in Yemen. The Houthi government’s health ministry said coalition airstrikes over the last six weeks had killed 91 Yemenis and wounded 168, 60% of whom it said were women and children.

Saudi-backed government forces in Yemen launched a campaign to push the Houthis back from the areas the group conquered over the past month. Despite claiming initial successes, the government forces have not been able to loosen the Houthis’ grip over areas closest to the Bab al-Mandab strait such as Perim Island.

The Indian embassy in Saudi Arabia said one of its citizens died on Sunday after being wounded in the Riyadh airport attack a day earlier.
