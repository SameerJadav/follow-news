US President Donald Trump announced on Sunday, 11 October, that Russia and Ukraine had agreed to an "energy ceasefire," effective immediately, claiming that both sides would halt attacks on energy infrastructure.

In a Truth Social post, he wrote, "Effective immediately, there is an ENERGY CEASEFIRE in the War between Russia and Ukraine. Both parties have agreed. Do not break it!"

This is the second time in less than a month that Trump has announced an energy ceasefire between Russia and Ukraine. According to The Wall Street Journal, he made a similar announcement on 14 September, but Russian President Vladimir Putin rebuffed the proposal, and both sides continued to exchange strikes.

Shortly after Trump’s announcement, Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy expressed surprise before saying that Kyiv would support the proposal if Moscow did. Speaking to Axios, the Ukrainian president said the announcement "was news for me. But if Russians agree to an energy ceasefire, Ukraine will agree." He added, "If President Trump negotiated it with Putin, please tell us when the ceasefire begins. We are ready, and we will accept it."

In a later social media post, Zelenskyy said, "Today, there was a statement from the President of the United States that an energy ceasefire has been secured. This is good news for everyone – and it is important that it become reality." He added, “Unfortunately, Ukrainian skies have been infested with Russian 'shaheds' all day today. There have been glide bomb strikes and strikes on our energy infrastructure too. A power plant in Kharkiv was attacked this evening.”

The Ukrainian president also accused Russia of carrying out strikes in Kyiv despite Trump’s announcement. Moscow has yet to issue an official statement confirming whether it has accepted the US president’s "energy ceasefire" proposal.

Trump’s latest announcement came after he lambasted the Ukrainian president and pressured Kyiv to halt attacks on Russian refineries. According to Reuters, the US president has blamed Ukrainian strikes for rising diesel prices, which are hurting his Republican Party ahead of next month’s midterm elections.

On 10 October, Trump warned Zelenskyy that he had "better damn well stop" attacking Russian refineries and urged Ukrainians to replace their leader through new elections.

Zelenskyy said in an ABC News interview aired on Sunday that Russia had carried out 196 attacks on Ukraine’s electricity network and 20 on its oil and gas facilities over the previous week. He also said Russian missile and drone attacks on cities had killed 153 people during that period.

With Moscow yet to confirm its acceptance and Kyiv reporting continued strikes, it remains unclear whether Trump’s announcement will lead to a halt in attacks on energy infrastructure.
