Prime Minister Narendra Modi with Tamil Nadu Chief Minister C. Joseph Vijay inaugurating the Poonamallee-Vadapalani stretch of Phase II of the Chennai Metro Rail Project, in Chennai, Tamil Nadu on October 11, 2026. | Photo Credit: YouTube/@NarendraModi

Chennai Metro Rail’s new chapter began on Sunday (October 11, 2026) morning, with Prime Minister Narendra Modi and Chief Minister C. Joseph Vijay flagging off the Phase II network from Porur Bypass Metro Rail station.

The 54-km Phase II network is now 68-km-long, following the opening of the Poonamallee-Vadapalani stretch.

On arrival at the Porur Bypass station around 11.30 a.m., Mr. Modi and Mr. Vijay were briefed on various aspects of the 118.9-km Phase II project, including the balanced cantilever project at Kathipara and the progress of tunnelling work.

Subsequently, both of them flagged off the first service, and boarded the next train for a ride from Porur Bypass to Poonamallee.

The 14.6 km Poonamallee-Vadapalani stretch, which was subsequently inaugurated at Indian Institute of Technology-Madras, comprises eleven elevated stations—Poonamallee Bypass, Poonamallee Bus Stand, Poonamallee Government Hospital, Karayanchavadi, Kumananchavadi, Kattupakkam, Iyyappanthangal, Thelliyaragaram, Porur Bypass, Porur Junction and Vadapalani.

The present fare system will continue (₹10- ₹50). It will cost ₹40 for a one-way trip from Poonamallee to Vadapalani.

The stretch is a part of corridor 4 of the Phase II project, running from Poonamallee to Light House (26.1 km).

The 118.9-km Phase II project has two more corridors that are under construction —corridor 3 (Madhavaram to SIPCOT) and corridor 5 (Madhavaram to Sholinganallur).

At Vadapalani station, both the Phase I and the Phase II networks intersect and are linked by an ‘L’ shaped skywalk. Commuters can use this facility to change trains from Phase II network to the Phase I network, and travel to various locations in corridor I (Wimco Nagar to Chennai airport) and corridor II (Chennai Central to St. Thomas Mount).

Prime Minister Narendra Modi and Tamil Nadu Chief Minister C. Joseph Vijay take a Metro ride from Porur Bypass to Poonamallee after the inauguration in Chennai on Sunday. | Photo Credit: ANI

The new stretch will ease connectivity for the commuters from the suburbs, including Porur, Poonamallee and Karayanchavadi, to some of the important transport hubs such as Chennai airport, Chennai Central, and Chennai Egmore Railway stations. For industrial workers, students and the general public, the newly opened stretch will significantly improve mobility between core areas of Chennai and the western suburbs.

The Phase II network uses Communication Based Train Control (CBTC) signalling system, and unlike the Phase I network, has platform screen doors fixed in the elevated stations for the protection of passengers.

The passenger services began on Sunday from 4 p.m. The regular passenger services from Monday will be from 7 a.m. to 9 p.m.
