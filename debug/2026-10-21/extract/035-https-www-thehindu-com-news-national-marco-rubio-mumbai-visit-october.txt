Jaishankar (@DrSJaishankar) October 20, 2026  Combating terror together There were six American citizens among the 166 victims who lost their lives in the coordinated terror attacks in Mumbai on November 26, 2008. “The Secretary strongly condemned the attacks and reaffirmed the United States’ unwavering solidarity with the people of India. Secretary Rubio underscored the Trump Administration’s commitment to dismantling terrorist networks and ensuring that those responsible for acts of terrorism are brought to justice, including individuals facing charges for their roles in planning the 2008 Mumbai attacks.  His visit reinforced the shared U.S.-India commitment to combating terrorism and securing justice for victims worldwide,” the U.S. Consulate General of Mumbai said in a statement. Mr. Jaishankar said that the entire country appreciated this gesture, welcoming the Trump administration’s decision to extradite key accused Tahawwur Rana to India. “We are clear on the right to defend ourselves against terrorism, and expect that our partners show zero tolerance towards terrorism as well,” he said. ‘The power of super intelligence’ Mr. Rubio termed the Indo-U.S. alliance “extraordinary”, adding that both the countries were equally committed to draw from technological advances in the age of ‘super intelligence’. Both countries had the same expertise in technology, he said. “How do we harness the power of super intelligence? We must mitigate the downsides, but we must accentuate the positive sides. On that, we are both perfectly aligned. We want to see a world where super intelligence is used for the advantage of human kind,” he said.
