[a2] Livemint <https://www.livemint.com/news/india/brics-summit-2026-delhi-airspace-curbs-tighten-as-flight-restrictions-begin-what-it-means-for-igi-passengers-11789072322813.html> (FULL TEXT)
The BRICS Summit is bringing tighter security not only to Delhi’s roads but also to its skies.

Special airspace restrictions are set to apply from 11 September to 13 September as New Delhi hosts the BRICS Summit, with security rules affecting flying activities within a 300-km radius of the capital, according to people familiar with the security advisory.

For airline passengers, however, there is an important distinction: Delhi’s Indira Gandhi International Airport is not shutting down for regular commercial flights.

From September 11 to 13, special airspace restrictions will be in effect within a 300-km radius of New Delhi, primarily affecting non-scheduled flights and requiring prior security clearance for unauthorized aircraft.

Yes, normal scheduled commercial flights at Indira Gandhi International Airport will continue to take off and land despite the heightened security measures during the BRICS Summit.

Passengers should check their flight status, allow extra time for travel due to potential road closures, and monitor advisories from the Delhi Traffic Police to account for delays.

Traffic restrictions are implemented to ensure the smooth movement of foreign leaders, dignitaries, and VVIP convoys, as nearly 4,800 traffic personnel are deployed for security and traffic management.

Emergency and medical vehicles will receive priority passage, while essential services and designated government aircraft will also be allowed to operate despite traffic restrictions in Delhi.

Normal scheduled airline flights will be permitted to take off and land during the restriction period. Regular commercial passengers, therefore, should not interpret the security measures as a blanket suspension of flights at IGI Airport.

Special flights carrying BRICS dignitaries and associated movements will also be permitted, provided their flight plans receive advance approval from air traffic control. These flights will be treated as scheduled movements under the advisory.

The restrictions primarily tighten access for aircraft and flying operations that do not fall under the permitted categories.

The wider measures come as Delhi prepares for the arrival and movement of foreign leaders and delegations attending the summit. The gathering is being held at Bharat Mandapam, with an extensive security operation in place across the capital.

Apart from normal scheduled airline operations, the advisory permits flights operated by the Indian Air Force, Border Security Force and Aviation Research Centre.

Army helicopters undertaking quick-response operations or emergency medical evacuation missions will also be allowed.

State government aircraft and helicopters carrying a Governor or Chief Minister are among the exempted categories as well.

Any flight not included in the permitted list will require prior security clearance from the Ministry of Home Affairs, according to the advisory.

The restrictions also apply to flights operating through the broader 300-km zone around Delhi.

Regular scheduled flights and overflights can continue on their normal routes, but they must comply with specified altitude requirements while approaching or departing the area.

Aircraft climbing near the zone must reach Flight Level 290 — around 29,000 feet — by the time they are 200 km from Delhi.

Similarly, aircraft descending must not begin descending from Flight Level 290 until they are at least 200 km away from the capital.

The rules are designed to allow scheduled airline operations and overflights to continue while tightening the security framework around the summit.

Safdarjung Airport will remain closed during the security period, except for specified IAF helicopters undertaking emergency or VIP duties and certain BSF and IAF helicopter operations connected with the National Security Guard.

The measures add to a broader aviation security plan for the summit. Publicly reported restrictions have also placed limits on other forms of flying activity, including certain light aircraft and unmanned aerial operations around Delhi during the high-security period.

For most airline passengers, the bigger challenge may not be whether their flight can take off or land. It may be getting to the airport.

Delhi is already facing extensive traffic restrictions, diversions and temporary stoppages linked to VVIP movement during the summit. A flight may be operating on schedule, but a passenger could still face delays reaching the terminal because of road closures or diversions.

Travellers should check their airline’s latest flight status before leaving, allow substantially more time than usual for the journey and monitor official Delhi Traffic Police and airport advisories.

The message for passengers is relatively straightforward: commercial flights are permitted to operate, but the summit security operation means both the journey to the airport and movement through Delhi could require considerably more planning than usual.

Russian President Vladimir Putin is expected to arrive here on Friday (September 11, 2026) morning to attend the 18th BRICS summit that will be held here this weekend, Russian sources said.

Mr. Putin is likely to hold a bilateral meeting with Prime Minister Narendra Modi hours after his arrival, where the two sides are expected to discuss defence and other related matters. President Putin is among the top leaders of BRICS members and partner countries who are scheduled to arrive for the two-day summit.

Several heads of multilateral organisations, including the United Nations Secretary-General Antonio Guterres, are also scheduled to attend the BRICS summit.

The External Affairs Ministry has confirmed that, apart from President Putin, Chinese President Xi Jinping, Egyptian President Abdel Fattah El-Sisi, Ethiopia’s Prime Minister Abiy Ahmed Ali, and Indonesian President Prabowo Subianto are among the top leaders who are expected to reach Delhi for the summit that is being held against the backdrop of a global crisis in the supply chains of energy and agriculture because of wars in West Asia and Ukraine.

The conflict in West Asia that started in February, when Israel and the U.S. launched a joint attack on Iran, is likely to be among the top topics of the summit, especially as Iran’s President Masoud Pezeshkian is scheduled to be here. This will be the first visit to India by President Pezeshkian since he took charge after the death of President Ebrahim Raisi in a helicopter crash in 2024.

Mr. Pezeshkian met Prime Minister Modi on the sidelines of the Shanghai Cooperation Organisation summit in Bishkek, Kyrgyz Republic, on August 31. That was the first meeting between the two leaders since the war began. The differences between Iran and the United Arab Emirates (UAE) will be keenly watched as the two sides have been exchanging tough messages because of the ongoing hostilities in the region. UAE will be represented by Sheikh Khaled bin Mohamed bin Zayed Al Nahyan, the Ministry informed. The Gulf Cooperation Council, which is also in focus because of the hostilities in the Persian Gulf region, will be represented by Abdullatif bin Rashid Al Zayani, Foreign Minister of Bahrain.

Among the founding members of BRICS — launched in 2009 with the first summit hosted by Russia — South Africa will be represented by Cyril Ramaphosa and Brazil will be represented by Mauro Vieira. Among the founding members of BRICS, President Putin is the longest-serving leader.

Indonesia’s President Subianto, who was the Guest of Honour at the Republic Day parade here on January 26, 2025, is also scheduled to attend the BRICS summit. Among the ten partner countries of BRICS, the notable leaders will be Malaysia’s Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim and Vietnam’s Prime Minister Le Minh Hung. President of the Philippines Ferdinand R. Marcos Jr, will attend the summit in his capacity as Chair of ASEAN and President of Kazakhstan Kassym-Jomart Tokayev will participate in the event in his capacity as President of the Eurasian Economic Union.

Apart from Mr. Guterres, World Health Organization head Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus, World Trade Organization Director-General Ngozi Okonjo-Iweala, and Dilma Rousseff, former President of Brazil, who will participate as the representative of the New Development Bank, will attend the summit. Bangladesh Prime Minister Tarique Rahman was invited to participate in the summit, but Bangladesh will not be represented at the event, Minister of State for Foreign Affairs Humayun Kobir has confirmed.

Union Commerce and Industry Minister Piyush Goyal on Thursday said India-Russia economic ties must move “from protocols to production, from trade to industry”, with both countries working towards ambitious targets of USD 100 billion in bilateral trade and USD 50 billion in two-way investment by 2030.

Addressing the India-Russia Business Forum 2026 at Bharat Mandapam in the presence of Russian Minister of Industry and Trade Anton Alikhanov, Goyal said bilateral trade has reached around USD 60 billion but needs greater diversification and stronger participation of Indian MSMEs.

He said, “Increasing bilateral trade from the current base of around USD 60 billion to USD 100 billion would require adding about USD 40 billion in the next four years and sustained double-digit growth year-on-year”.

He also highlighted opportunities for Indian MSMEs in pharmaceuticals, auto components, tractors, chemicals, food products and manufacturing, while stressing the need for faster progress on bilateral investment framework, national currency payment systems and improved connectivity through International North South Transport Corridor and Chennai Vladivostok Maritime Corridor.

Russian Minister Anton Alikhanov said joint industrial projects are already expanding. He cited Russian manufacturing projects in India in electrical equipment, synthetic rubber, and polymer composite materials.

He said pharma remains promising with joint production already underway, besides discussions on supply, assembly and maintenance of Russian aircraft in India.

He also identified rare and critical earth metals, microelectronics, electric transport, unmanned systems, digital technologies, smart city solutions and advanced manufacturing as future areas.

Addressing the forum, Vikramjit Singh Sahney, Member of Parliament, Rajya Sabha (Punjab), Member, BRICS Business Council (From India); Chair, BRICS Agri Forum; International President, World Punjabi Organisation and Chairman, Sun Foundation said the longstanding India-Russia partnership is entering a new phase.

Sahney said, “BRICS, which represents nearly half of the world's population and around 40 per cent of global GDP in PPP terms, has an important role in promoting balanced, inclusive and sustainable growth and strengthening South-South cooperation”.

He added that India-Russia cooperation should now expand into food security, agriculture, fertilisers, energy, critical minerals, civil nuclear power, AI and emerging technologies.

He also noted that India’s digital and manufacturing capabilities can complement Russia’s scientific and technological strengths, creating opportunities for MSMEs, farmers, startups and industry.

The speakers shared that India-Russia ties must now convert decades of mutual trust into practical cooperation in food, fuel, fertilisers, technology, manufacturing, finance, energy and innovation.

**New Delhi:**

Taking note of the recent Satya Niketan building collapse in Delhi that claimed seven lives and left 12 people injured, the Supreme Court on Thursday directed authorities across the country to file affidavits on the steps taken to comply with its earlier directions on unauthorised constructions.

A Bench of Justices Ahsanuddin Amanullah and R Mahadevan accepted the suggestion of amicus curiae Ajit Kumar Sinha to require authorities to place before the top court reports on the action taken in terms of its earlier orders.

The matter was listed after the amicus curiae brought to the top court's notice the "tragic incident" at Satya Niketan in the national capital's Motibagh area, where a building collapse resulted in the death of seven persons and injuries to 12 others.

The Justice Amanullah-led Bench was informed that the Delhi High Court, while dealing with a public interest litigation (PIL) concerning the Satya Niketan incident, had on September 7 directed the Municipal Corporation of Delhi (MCD) to submit a report and scheduled the matter for further hearing on September 25.

The amicus curiae submitted that the High Court proceedings could continue, while the authorities should also submit a report before the Supreme Court covering the issues raised in the present proceedings.

The amicus further submitted that inspections had already been conducted in Delhi and Lucknow in the areas covered by the Supreme Court's earlier directions -- Lajpat Nagar, Malviya Nagar, Saket and Sarojini Nagar in Delhi, and Aliganj in Lucknow.

The top court was also told that the issues arising from the Satya Niketan collapse were already covered by the action being undertaken and reports to be submitted by the authorities in the present proceedings.

The amicus curiae suggested that the Supreme Court could either transfer the Delhi High Court proceedings and tag them with the present case or clarify that the High Court proceedings may continue while directing the authorities to additionally submit a report to the top court on all issues raised in the ongoing matter.

Accepting the suggestion, the bench said it would, "for the present, do not disturb the position of the case before the High Court", which will continue.

"The High Court is requested to monitor the (PIL) case at short intervals," the top court said.

Directing the matter to be listed on September 15 at 2 p.m., the Supreme Court ordered the parties concerned to file affidavits, "not limited to Delhi and Lucknow", regarding compliance with the directions issued from time to time.

The proceedings relate to the top court's continuing scrutiny of illegal and unauthorised constructions and the failure of civic authorities to take effective action despite repeated directions.

Earlier, the top court had expressed concern over rampant illegal constructions and directed the constitution of an expert committee comprising senior professors and draughtsmen from IIT Delhi to inspect areas including Saket, Lajpat Nagar and Sarojini Nagar along with MCD officials and the amicus curiae.

It had also stressed that mere issuance of notices was insufficient and that authorities were required to take concrete measures, including sealing or demolition wherever warranted under law.

The proceedings assume significance in the wake of the Satya Niketan tragedy, where the collapse of a five-storeyed PG building highlighted wider concerns over structural safety and enforcement of building norms in Delhi.

The Delhi High Court, while hearing a PIL following the incident, had observed that responsibility for such accidents could not rest solely on the owner of a PG hostel and had also flagged the role of university authorities and the MCD in ensuring compliance with building bye-laws.

"Had the authorities been alive to their responsibilities and duties cast on them statutorily, perhaps, such incidents could have been avoided," it had observed.

The High Court had also directed the civic body to conduct an inquiry at the highest executive level to ascertain whether the collapsed building and other such structures had been constructed with valid permissions and in accordance with applicable building bye-laws, and to fix responsibility on officials if violations were found.

The incident has also prompted the Delhi government to initiate action against MCD officials, with five officials suspended and directions issued for a drive against buildings having unauthorised fifth floors.
