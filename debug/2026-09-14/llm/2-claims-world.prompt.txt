=== STORY 0 [world] — Swedish general election sees tight deadlock between opposition bloc and government parties ===

[a0] BBC World <https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/c0qx5d79kdeo?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss> (FULL TEXT)
Sweden's general election remains too close to call, early results suggest.

With 50% of the votes counted, the two main blocs were separated by only 20,000 votes, Sweden public broadcaster SVT said.

Projected results from Sweden's election authority had given the left-wing political bloc led by the Social Democrats' Magdalena Andersson a one-seat majority over the rival four-party coalition put together by current conservative Prime Minister Ulf Kristersson.

Kristersson has been supported in government by the hard-right Sweden Democrats (SD), which have neo-Nazi roots. He has promised cabinet jobs for the SD if he stays in charge.

But partial results suggest the party was losing support for the first time since 2010 when it entered parliament.

Sweden has two major political blocs featuring four parties on the right and four on the left - meaning coalition and minority governments are relatively common.

On Sunday night, Andersson urged supporters to wait for the final result, but added that if the current results hold, "Sweden will have a new government."

Her party, the Social Democrats, have been a major force in Swedish politics since the end of World War Two.

But SD - founded by Nazi sympathisers in the 1980s - had been shunned for years until 2022 when it became the country's second largest party after campaigning on violent crime and immigration.

After casting her vote on Sunday, Andersson laid out the choice facing the Swedish public: "Should we have a government entirely dominated by the Sweden Democrats, something that has never happened in Sweden before? Or should we have a government led by me, which will steer Sweden in a new spirit of co-operation?"

Kristersson, for his part, said it was important he and his alliance were able to continue their work.

"I think there is a genuine enthusiasm for us being able to continue our important work," he said after casting his ballot in his home municipality of Strangnas.

National security, gang crime and welfare spending dominated the election campaign.

Six people have died and 107 have been rescued from a capsized Indonesian passenger ferry in the Java Sea, authorities have said.

Another 130 people are still missing after contact was lost with the Virgo Transport8 ship, as it encountered bad weather.

The ferry was travelling from Surabaya to the South Kalimantan city of Banjarmasin and was last reported to be about 150km (93 miles) south of Banjarmasin around 02:00 on Sunday (18:00 GMT on Saturday).

Ships and a helicopter have been deployed to the scene, with 280 personnel taking part in a search and rescue operation. All vessels in the area of the Java Sea have been alerted to report any signs of passengers.

There were 213 passengers and 30 crew members on board the 390ft (119m) ferry.

The ship left Surabaya at 10:33 on Saturday and was scheduled to arrive in Banjarmasin on Sunday at 14:00, according to data from two maritime monitoring sites. Authorities received the initial distress report at 04:10.

A joint task force involving Indonesia's search and rescue agency, navy, maritime police and other parties has been set up for the rescue effort.

Rusmilawati, a resident of Banjarmasin, went to the port to seek information about eight relatives who were passengers on the ship. They were travelling back to Banjarmasin after visiting the tombs of several Islamic scholars in Java.

"They flew there initially, but for the return journey they wanted to try travelling by ship," she told BBC Indonesia.

He said he had still not received any information about his sibling's whereabouts.

Rescue workers in Indonesia said at least six people had died and 130 others were missing on Sunday after a ferry sailing between the islands of Java and Borneo capsized in bad weather.

Basarnas, Indonesia’s national search and rescue agency, said 107 people had been rescued from the Virgo ⁠Transport 8 vessel, which set sail on Saturday with 243 ​people onboard, including 30 crew members, ​from ‌the East Java ‌city of Surabaya.

The ferry’s last position before it lost contact at 2am on Sunday was in the Java Sea, about 150km (90 miles) from its destination, the city of Banjarmasin in South Kalimantan province, said Heri Purwanto, the head of the Banjarmasin port authority.

Indonesia’s transport minister, Dudy Purwagandhi, said the vessel had capsized. “From the information we received, based on helicopter surveillance … the ship capsized but has not sunk. As of 3pm [local time] it had not sunk yet, but it was capsized,” he said at a news conference in Banjarmasin.

The local search and rescue chief, I Putu Sudayana, said rescuers were battling waves of up to 2.5 metres (8.2ft) to reach the ferry. “It is extremely risky for vessels. Even our Basarnas vessel, which is 40 metres long, is at great risk,” he said.

A Basarnas helicopter had joined the operation, he said, adding that evacuees had been transported ⁠using a number of different ships and some of them would be taken to ​an East Java ​port.

Paramedics and ambulances were on standby in Banjarmasin, where dozens of relatives and friends of passengers checked a whiteboard on which the names of the rescued were being updated.

Indonesia, an ​archipelago of ​more than 17,000 islands, relies heavily on ferries, with sea routes more affordable and readily available than air travel. However, safety standards are not always rigorously enforced, resulting in a relatively high accident rate.

On Monday, a speedboat carrying eight people, including five journalists working for local and international news agencies, went missing near a volcano, Mount Anak Krakatau, in Lampung province.

UKMTO said a severe fire had broken out and local authorities were evacuating those on board.
