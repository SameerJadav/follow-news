Iran in talks with Oman over shipping route but not US, says Tehran

Donald Trump is speaking at an executive order signing at the White House, where he was asked where direct talks with Iran would take place.

“You’ll find out today or tomorrow,” he said. “They’re going to go quickly one way or the other. It’s not very complex.”

Trump said the strait of Hormuz could be open “literally by tomorrow”.

“The first phase is the opening of the straits. The second phase will be the denuclearisation,” he said. “That’ll take a little while, but … we’re very strong on it.”

New talks between Washington and Tehran are the “last chance” for Iran to forge a deal and avoid an escalation of US strikes on the country, Donald Trump said on Monday. It comes a day after the US president again said he had called off further “massive strikes” because Gulf allies had urged diplomacy. It’s all very consistent with Trump’s repeated pattern over the last five months as he struggles to find an off-ramp; he threatens devastating military action, only to pull back at the last minute and claim the two sides are on the brink of an agreement to end the war. As my colleague Patrick Wintour notes, this is thought to be the 12th time in the war that Trump has called off threatened strikes.

Indeed, Trump said he expected negotiations to begin in the next day or two to reopen the strait and create a pathway for Iran to address the US’s concerns about its nuclear programme. “The first phase is the opening of the straits. The second phase will be the denuclearization,” he told reporters in the Oval Office. “And that will take a little while.” He also reiterated that he was running out of patience with Iran, saying “this the last chance for them to sign a good document.”

However, Tehran had on Monday denied that talks with the US were taking place. Esmaeil Baghaei, Iran’s foreign ministry spokesperson, told reporters at a weekly press briefing on Monday that Iran is not currently holding any talks with the US – contradicting claims made by Trump on Sunday night that talks with Iran would happen the next day.

Baghaei said that there were no plans to receive a delegation or send an Iranian one, and that Iran’s current focus was on negotiations with Oman over the strait of Hormuz. “We are now going to reach an understanding on a route acceptable to both sides – neither the northern route nor the southern route – but one that respects the sovereign rights of both sides and safeguards our national interests and security,” he said in an interview with Iranian state television.

To that, the US president called the Iranian leadership “unbelievably duplicitous” in a post on social media. He wrote on Truth Social: “They ask for a meeting, some would say ‘beg’, talks begin, with more scheduled in the immediate future, and they say, openly and proudly, that they’re not having any discussions, that nothing is being talked about, and they’re only dealing with ‘Oman’.”

The Donald Trump doctrine has always contained a tenuous relationship with the truth. It was his mentor, the infamous New York lawyer Roy Cohn, who taught the young real estate mogul always to claim victory and never to admit defeat. Unfortunately for Trump, in Iran the US president has found a counterpart just as stubborn as himself.

As Trump claims new negotiations are to begin with Tehran this week, Iran has shown it can also reignite the conflict by lobbing ballistic missiles at US bases in the region and maintaining a stranglehold on the strait of Hormuz, effectively taking initiative in the stalled peace process. And time is of the essence for Trump as the clock ticks toward a global energy crisis and a painful midterm elections for him and his Republican party.

But as soon as his latest attempt to hammer out a peace deal was announced, it was denied by Iran and the US president was left fuming once again.

Trump has suggested dozens of times since the beginning of the war that a deal with Iran is imminent, and his latest claim that new negotiations could produce a peace deal within days has shown the administration’s desperation to reopen the strait and find a road map out of the crisis. After promising to hit Iran “very hard”, Trump made a U-turn on Sunday and claimed that talks would continue and that the “perimeters of a deal” for the opening of Hormuz and the “denuclearization of Iran” had been agreed.

Unfortunately, the Iranians don’t agree. In a statement on Monday, an Iranian foreign ministry spokesperson denied any peace negotiations were taking place. “We are not currently negotiating with the United States,” said Esmaeil Baqaei. “Our negotiations are with Oman to secure passage through the strait of Hormuz.”

European Commission president says work needed in five areas of migration control including returning more people

The president of the European Commission has called for action to strengthen Europe’s borders after tens of thousands of people arrived and quickly left the Spanish territory of Ceuta, triggering a furious row between Madrid and other European countries.

In a letter to Spain’s prime minister, Pedro Sánchez, on Monday, Ursula von der Leyen said: “From this incident, it is clear that we must do more to further strengthen our borders at critical points, including vigilant monitoring and the use of physical barriers where needed.”

She praised the Spanish socialist leader’s swift handling of the crisis, after the vast majority of migrants left Ceuta and Spanish officials began installing a 500-metre (1,640ft) floating barrier at the border ‌with Morocco. “Order has been restored and the returns system in place worked,” she wrote in the letter.

Spain ⁠estimates that about 69,500 migrants ​have returned to Morocco over the past four days, exceeding initial estimates of 50,000 having arrived in the enclave in north Africa on Thursday. Authorities in Ceuta reported that 88 people died trying to reach the territory, while at least 11 deaths have been recorded in Morocco.

Ceuta and Melilla, another Spanish enclave, have the EU’s only land borders with Africa. There are periodic surges in attempted crossings to both cities, although neither permits onward free movement to the EU as they are outside the border-free Schengen zone.

EU interior ministers will hold emergency talks on Tuesday, which von der Leyen said would be a chance to draw lessons from the crossings.

She said the EU should double down on five areas: strengthening cooperation with non-EU countries to prevent migration; stronger external borders; implementing early warning systems; dismantling smuggling networks; and boosting returns of people with no right to stay.

Italian media reported that Giorgia Meloni’s government wanted to accelerate the opening of offshore migration centres, which Europe’s top human rights expert recently said risked becoming “human rights black holes”.

The EU opened the door to offshore detention centres in June in an unprecedented departure from legal norms as part of a law aiming to increase the deportations of people with no legal right to stay.

Amid heavy criticism of the EU’s divided response to the Ceuta border crossings, von der Leyen stressed that Spain could access EU funds as well as support from the Frontex border agency. So far Madrid has not requested the assistance of Frontex.

The crossings – seized on by far-right politicians across Europe – have exposed deep divisions over migration that have left Spain’s leader isolated.

Over the weekend, Sánchez criticised other governments for failing to support Spain, after Italy and Denmark led calls for its temporary exclusion from the Schengen zone. Sánchez said such an attitude was “driven by prejudice, fake news, ignorance or political interest” and contrary to European and humanitarian law “and the principles of solidarity that bind us together”.

Italy and Denmark organised a letter signed by 20 other EU member states that appeared to blame the crisis on Sánchez’s decision to regularise the status of 500,000 migrants without documents. “We have a duty to effectively deter and relentlessly combat illegal migration,” the 22 leaders wrote, arguing for the need to address “all policies that can serve as pull factors, such as the regularisation of [a] very large number of irregular migrants”.

EU member states favouring a tough approach to migration are expected to raise the issue of “pull factors” on Tuesday. “There is some concern that the regularisation of 1.2 million people can be used to paint a picture that the door is open,” an EU diplomat said, referring to the approximate number of people who applied for regularisation under the 2026 Spanish government programme.

Academic research has found no evidence of a pull effect from regularisation programmes on arrivals of irregular migrants.

Michele LeVoy, the director of Picum, an umbrella group that campaigns for the rights of undocumented migrants, said regularisation programmes had been criticised by EU governments for the last two decades. “If an EU country offers regularisation possibilities, other member states believe that it may invite secondary movement [onward movement to other EU member states] – that is what EU member states don’t want and that explains most likely the harsh response.”

Amid widespread suspicion that Morocco helped fuel the crisis, von der Leyen described the country as “an important strategic partner”, especially in combating “illegal migration”.

EU chief Ursula von der Leyen said Morocco was “an important strategic partner” in stopping illegal migration as Europe looks to tighten its borders.

BRUSSELS: European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen proposed on Monday (Aug 3) that the European Union further strengthen its borders and increase support for Morocco to help prevent a repeat of the chaotic influx of 50,000 migrants into the Spanish enclave of Ceuta last week.

The surge, which began on Thursday at one of the EU's only two land borders with Africa - the other being Morocco's frontier with the Spanish enclave of Melilla - triggered alarm across the bloc.

Migration has been one of the European Union's most divisive political issues since the 2015-16 migration crisis, when more than a million refugees and migrants, many fleeing war in Syria, arrived in Europe.

The influx fuelled support for anti-immigration and far-right parties across the bloc and intensified years of disputes over border controls, asylum rules and burden-sharing.

Increasingly, the EU relies on migration agreements with countries in North Africa to curb irregular arrivals.

"Morocco is an important strategic partner, particularly in our efforts to combat migrant smuggling and illegal migration," von der Leyen wrote.

"In cooperation with Spain, especially on anything related to Ceuta and Melilla, we could enhance border management early-warning systems and improve our technical and financial support to Morocco."

Morocco said on Sunday that recent mass crossings to Ceuta and Melilla had been fuelled by misinformation on social media, human trafficking networks and misinterpretation of a Spanish court ruling.

In the event, most of those who breached the border barrier found no food or welcome, and returned to Moroccan soil.

Nevertheless, Italy suspended passport-free Schengen travel arrangements with Spain for a month, even though they do not apply to Ceuta, while 22 of the EU's 27 members urged coordinated action to strengthen the bloc's external borders.

The Ceuta incident is the first major test of the EU's Migration Pact, which entered into force in June after years of negotiations aimed at creating common migration rules and stronger solidarity among member states.

"The idea of the pact is control and solidarity between EU member states. Yet we've seen the opposite," said Bram Frouws, director of the Mixed Migration Centre.

"If the first major incident shows that the pact isn't doing much differently, that's politically risky," Frouws said, arguing that it could undermine confidence in European leaders and provide "fuel for the far right".

With elections approaching in several EU countries, migration remains among the bloc's most politically sensitive issues.

Critics argue the strategy of relying on transit countries leaves Europe vulnerable to pressure if these relax migration controls, or threaten to do so, in pursuit of political or economic concessions.

"Europe's increasing reliance on neighbouring countries for migration control may reduce arrivals in the short term, but it can also provide those countries with significant political leverage," said Alberto-Horst Neidhardt, senior policy analyst at the European Policy Centre.

The toll in rain-related incidents in Kerala has risen to 15, PTI quoted Chief Minister VD Satheesan as saying on Monday.

While he did not specify the period in which the deaths took place, the state has been receiving heavy and incessant rainfall since Friday, triggering flash floods and landslides.

Satheesan said on Monday that seven persons were missing amid the rains. Of these, two persons each were missing from Kollam and Kannur, and one each in Malappuram, Idukki and Muvattupuzha.

Malappuram had reported four deaths, while three persons died in Kottayam district. Two deaths each were reported in Ernakulam and Kollam, and one person each died in Idukki, Muvattupuzha and Kannur.

The chief minister announced that the state government will provide Rs 10,000 in funeral expenses to the families of those who died. Persons returning home from relief camps would receive Rs 10,000 as an immediate measure.

Additionally, an ex gratia of Rs 8 lakh was announced for the families of those who died. A compensation of Rs 12 lakh will be given to those who lost their homes and land due to landslides. Rs 6 lakh will be provided for families to rebuild their homes, said Satheesan.

As of 9 am on Monday, 11,018 persons had been displaced by the rains and flooding and 316 relief camps were functioning in the state, he said at a press conference.

Around 165 hectares of farmland had been damaged, affecting 3,596 farmers, ANI quoted Satheesan as saying.

The India Meteorological Department has warned of rain in all 14 districts of Kerala on Monday.

It has issued an orange alert, signalling a need to prepare for action, has been issued for 12 districts: Palakkad, Thrissur, Kasaragod, Kannur, Kozhikode, Wayanad, Malappuram, Ernakulam, Idukki, Kottayam, Alappuzha and Pathanamthitta. The toll in flood-related incidents in Assam rose to 85, the State Disaster Management Authority said on Monday.

The flood situation has improved since Sunday, with the number of affected persons falling to around 1.3 lakh from 1.9 lakh. The number of affected districts has also declined to five.

Since April 29, floods have affected 25 districts and impacted a total of 11.4 lakh persons in the state.

The Manikandanchal causeway across the Pooyamkutty River near Kothamangalam was submerged on Monday morning after heavy rain. The water receded by afternoon, officials said. | Photo Credit: Special Arrangement

A total of 110 people remained in relief camps across the district on Monday (August 3) following heavy downpours over the past few days. The day passed without much rain-related trouble.

Eight camps remained active in the district—four in Muvattupuzha taluk and two each in Kunnathunad and Kothamangalam—while four had been closed. Those who have taken shelter in camps include 51 women, 40 men, and 19 children.

A cyclist wearing a raincoat rides through Kochi city on August 3. | Photo Credit: H. VIBHU

Though the district did not witness incessant rain on Monday, the authorities advised people residing along the Muvattupuzha and Thodupuzha rivers, as well as in low-lying areas, to remain alert as the water levels in the rivers remained above the warning marks.

Authorities said the water level at the Malankara dam had touched the red-alert mark of 41.30 metres in the evening as inflow from the catchment area remained heavy. Excess water had been discharged from the dam by gradually raising its shutters since Sunday night (August 2), they said.

Though a flood-like situation prevailed along the Pooyamkutty river in Kothamangalam following heavy rain on Sunday night, it eased later as weather conditions improved. The Manikandanchal causeway across the river, which serves as a critical link between interior tribal hamlets and villages such as Manikandanchal and Kallelimedu and the mainland, was submerged in the morning. However, the water receded by afternoon, officials said.

Higher Education Minister Roji M. John, who is in charge of the district, visited different wards of the Chellanam grama panchayat that had been severely affected by heavy rain and sea surge. Stating that the situation in the region required a permanent solution, the Minister said the government would shortly initiate steps to complete the construction of a seawall. Mr. John was accompanied by Kochi MLA Muhammed Shiyas.

All educational institutions in the district will remain closed on Tuesday (August 4) in view of the heavy rainfall the district has been receiving, according to a communication issued by the Ernakulam District Collector.

Heavy rainfall continued to lash Kozhikode for the third consecutive day on Monday (August 3, 2026). Low-lying areas in many parts of the district are still under water and families were shifted to safer locations. A couple of instances of partial collapse of houses too were reported on the day.

The India Meteorological department has issued an orange alert forecasting very heavy rainfall accompanied by gusty winds on Tuesday (August 4, 2026). The district administration has declared a holiday for the second consecutive day for educational institutions in view of the incessant rain in the upland areas and coastal regions. However, exams scheduled earlier and online classes will go on as usual.

The house of K.K. Noushad of Kuyyalil Kattil in Velliparamba, Kozhikode, that collapsed in heavy rain. | Photo Credit: K Ragesh

As on Monday evening, 964 people belonging to 365 families are at present staying in 32 relief camps set up across the district. There are 376 men, 418 women, and 170 kids. The highest number of people are in Kozhikode taluk. As many as 701 people from 260 families have been put up at 15 camps. There are 165 people in Thamarassery taluk, 80 in Vadakara taluk, and 18 people in Koyilandy taluk.

Meanwhile, around 200 families were affected after many parts of Kannadikkal-Parambil Bazar area were inundated. This followed the rising of water levels in the Poonoor River. The residents were shifted to safer places after low-lying areas went under water. Power connection was switched off in the area. The IMD, which had issued a yellow alert predicting isolated heavy rainfall for Kozhikode district on Monday, had later revised it to an orange alert.

Autorickshaw driver Noushad and family from Velliparamba near the Government Medical College Hospital had a narrow escape in the early hours of Monday when his house partially collapsed in the heavy rain. A tragedy was averted when Mr. Noushad, his wife, and two children ran outside the house after hearing a big sound around 3 a.m. The kitchen, one of the washrooms, and work area were completely damaged. Some household utensils too were destroyed. Both Mr. Noushad and his wife were sleeping next to the kitchen, it is learnt.

Around 20 families have been shifted to a relief camp at Moozhikkal in Kozhikode. | Photo Credit: K. Ragesh

It was a similar experience for Ajayakumar of Nadakkavu in Kozhikode city when a wall inside his house collapsed on Sunday night. The incident happened when Mr. Ajayakumar, his wife, and kids were watching television. They have been shifted to another house at present.

Wells reportedly caved in at Odumbra and Chalappuram areas. Low-lying areas Mavoor are facing water-logging. People staying on the banks of the Kuttiyadi River have been asked to be on alert as two spillway gates of the Kakkayam Dam have been opened to safely discharge water.
