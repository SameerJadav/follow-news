On February 17, 2025, the Central Board of Secondary Education (CBSE) denied claims of 2025 exam paper leaks, calling them "baseless and intended to create unnecessary panic among students and parents". The CBSE stated that it had made comprehensive arrangements for the smooth and fair conduct of the examinations, which began on February 15, 2025, for Classes 10 and 12, involving at least 4.4 million students. The board also warned that it was actively monitoring social media platforms like YouTube, Facebook, and X (formerly Twitter) for such rumors and was taking strict action against those spreading misinformation, working with law enforcement to identify and prosecute offenders. Students found involved in such activities could face penalties under CBSE's "UNFAIR MEANS" rules and relevant sections of the Indian Penal Code (IPC), including cancellation of exams for the current and next three years. Parents were also advised to guide their children to rely only on official communications from the CBSE website.

Separately, in February 2025, the Odisha government's "The Odisha Public Examinations (Prevention Of Unfair Means) Act, 2024" received the Governor's assent. This new law expanded the scope of an earlier 1988 law by redefining offenders and unfair means, and introduced more stringent punishments for examination malpractices.
