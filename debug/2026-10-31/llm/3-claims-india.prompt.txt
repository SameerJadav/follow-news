=== STORY 5 [india] — Six killed as helicopter carrying pilgrims crashes near Joshimath in Uttarakhand ===

[a0] Hindustan Times <https://www.hindustantimes.com/india-news/audits-sops-after-stormy-2025-season-preceded-chopper-crash-near-joshimath-101793388149892.html> (FULL TEXT)
Six people were killed after a helicopter crashed near Joshimath in Chamoli district on Friday, adding to a series of accidents in Uttarakhand’s pilgrimage helicopter sector despite successive rounds of safety measures by the aviation regulator.

The crash occurred weeks after the Aircraft Accident Investigation Bureau (AAIB), in its final report on a fatal 2025 crash in the Kedarnath valley, recommended that pilots complete 50 hours of supervised hill flying before operating alone in the region. The bureau recorded eight accidents and one serious incident in the Char Dham sector between 2015 and 2025.

The accident is the first in Uttarakhand since the civil aviation ministry introduced sweeping safety measures for the 2026 pilgrimage season. These included air traffic control at Sahastradhara in Dehradun and Sersi in Rudraprayag, command centres monitoring helipads and weather, tracking devices on all helicopters, and ceilometers — instruments that measure cloud-base height — at five locations including Kedarnath and Badrinath. Pilots were required to have at least 750 hours of hill flying, with 100 in the preceding year and 10 take-offs and landings at Kedarnath. Helicopters were barred from filling more than 70% of their passenger capacity.

The ministry had said the first phase of the 2026 season passed without incident.

The Char Dham sector’s safety record has drawn repeated regulatory intervention. Data the ministry presented in the Rajya Sabha on July 21 last year showed six helicopter accidents during the Char Dham Yatra between 2021 and June 2025, four of them in the first six months of 2025 alone. One accident each was recorded in 2022 and 2023; none in 2021 and 2024.

The deadliest recent cluster came in the 2025 season. On May 8, an Aero Trans Services Bell 407 crashed near Gangnani in Uttarkashi after its main rotor struck an overhead fibre-optic cable during a landing attempt, killing six people and seriously injuring another. On May 17, a Pinnacle Air helicopter was involved in an accident near Kedarnath, followed by a Kestrel Aviation helicopter accident at Sersi on June 7. On June 15, an Aryan Aviation Bell 407 flying from Kedarnath to Guptkashi crashed near Gaurikund, killing all seven people on board.

The AAIB’s final report on the Gaurikund crash, issued on October 13 this year, identified the pilot’s limited experience of flying in the valley, rapidly descending clouds and the failure to turn back or change course in time among the factors relevant to the accident. It recommended that pilots complete at least 50 hours of supervised dual hill-flying sorties with an experienced pilot before undertaking single-pilot operations in the Kedarnath valley for the first time. It also recommended that the Directorate General of Civil Aviation (DGCA) exercise oversight of the Sitapur control centre and that radio communications between pilots and ground controllers be recorded.

In an interim report on the same crash, published in November 2025, investigators had recorded that the Kedarnath valley had no meteorological facility. A weather instrument at the helipad reported wind, visibility and temperature, but provided no information about clouds. Its readings were not stored; a UCADA security staffer shared screenshots on an operators’ WhatsApp group. On the morning of the crash, no weather information had been posted by the time of the first flight.

The 2022 Kedarnath crash showed a similar pattern. On October 18 that year, an Aryan Aviation Bell 407 crashed into a mountain ridge near Garud Chatti shortly after taking off from Kedarnath, killing all seven people on board. The AAIB attributed the accident to the crew’s failure to adequately assess deteriorating weather conditions, with fast-descending clouds obscuring visibility.

DGCA has responded to accidents with audits, inspections, fines and suspensions. After a hard landing at Kedarnath in June 2022, it audited shuttle operators and fined five of them ₹5 lakh each over irregularities in their flying records. After the April 2023 tail-rotor death, it ordered a second audit of all operators before clearing them for the season and announced specialised training for Himalayan flying.

Following the 2025 accidents, the regulator ordered special audits, familiarisation flights for pilots new to the sector or returning after more than a year, and deployed air traffic control and meteorological personnel at control rooms in Sahastradhara and Sitapur. The ministry suspended one operator’s Char Dham operations and suspended two pilots’ licences for six months for flying in unsuitable weather.

The main rules for pilgrimage flights are set out in an operations circular issued by DGCA in February 2023. For the Kedarnath shuttle, it fixes the route and the altitude at each point: helicopters must climb to 8,500 feet by the valley’s entry and leave it at 9,000 feet on the way back. No more than four helicopters may be in the valley at once.

Five pilgrims and a pilot have died after their helicopter crashed in the northern Indian state of Uttarakhand, officials say.

The aircraft reportedly took off from the Hindu pilgrimage site of Badrinath on Friday afternoon before losing contact.

The cause of the crash is not yet known but there had been reports of heavy rain and poor visibility in the region.

Uttarakhand, a Himalayan state which borders China and Nepal, is home to some of Hinduism's holiest shrines and attracts millions of pilgrims every year.

Badrinath is one of four sacred sites on the Char Dham pilgrimage circuit, alongside Kedarnath, Gangotri and Yamunotri. Many devotees travel through steep Himalayan valleys to reach the temples, which are open for only part of the year because of harsh winter conditions.

Uttarakhand's Chief Minister Pushkar Singh Dhami said the helicopter came down near a village close to Joshimath in Chamoli district.

The local civil aviation authority later confirmed to Indian media that six bodies had been located.

Helicopter services have become increasingly popular among pilgrims seeking to avoid difficult mountain journeys, but their safety has come under scrutiny following a series of accidents.

Before Friday's crash, India's weather department had issued an orange alert for the area, warning of thunderstorms, hail and strong winds.

In June 2025, seven people were killed when a helicopter carrying pilgrims crashed near Kedarnath. Another seven died in a crash on the same pilgrimage route in October 2022.

Several other helicopter accidents were reported during the 2025 pilgrimage season, prompting aviation authorities to tighten safety checks and restrict some operations.

[a4] Livemint <https://www.livemint.com/news/india/gslvf18nvs03-mission-launch-today-check-time-how-to-watch-live-all-about-2nd-gen-navic-series-20th-gslv-flight-11793340303371.html> (FULL TEXT)
India is set to launch its next-generation navigation satellite, NVS-03, aboard the GSLV-F18 rocket today, Friday, October 30, at 11:51 pm IST, in a mission aimed at strengthening the country’s indigenous satellite navigation capabilities.

The launch from Sriharikota will be the GSLV rocket's 20th flight and marks another attempt by India to strengthen its own satellite navigation system, NavIC, which helps people and services determine their location and track time accurately.

The launch comes at a challenging time for NavIC, as several of its existing satellites have stopped working properly, while attempts to replace them have also faced setbacks.

The Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO) has scheduled the GSLV-F18/NVS-03 mission for October 30, 2026, at 11:51 pm Indian Standard Time.

The 51.7-metre-tall GSLV Mk II rocket will lift off from the Second Launch Pad at the Satish Dhawan Space Centre, Sriharikota.

Its primary task will be to place the 2,232 kg NVS-03 satellite into a Geosynchronous Transfer Orbit (GTO).

Space enthusiasts can follow the launch through ISRO's official digital platforms.

Viewers should check ISRO's official website and YouTube channel for the live broadcast and mission updates.

NVS-03 is the third satellite in ISRO's new-generation NavIC programme, which aims to improve and strengthen India's home-grown navigation system.

The satellite will carry an Indian-made atomic clock along with other clocks sourced from outside the country. It will also introduce support for an additional signal, which could help more smartphones and navigation devices use NavIC.

With these improvements, India hopes to make its navigation system more reliable and useful for everyday needs. NavIC is designed to cover the whole of India and areas extending up to 1,500 km beyond its borders.

Atomic clocks play a crucial role in helping navigation satellites pinpoint locations accurately. Even a tiny error in timekeeping can affect how accurately a phone or other device identifies where someone is.

By developing its own atomic clocks, ISRO hopes to rely less on imported technology and give India greater control over its satellite navigation system.

NVS-03 will also support an additional signal, known as the L1 band, alongside the existing signals. This is expected to make NavIC more compatible with smartphones and other navigation devices, while allowing it to work more effectively alongside systems such as the US-based GPS.

NavIC has been facing several problems in recent years, with a number of its older satellites developing faults or becoming unusable. Some stopped working because of failures in their onboard clocks, while others could not reach the right orbit.

The system suffered another setback in January 2025, when ISRO's NVS-02 satellite failed to move into its intended orbit because of a problem with its thrusters. As a result, it could not begin providing navigation services as planned.

The situation became more difficult in March 2026, when the last working atomic clock aboard another satellite, IRNSS-1F, also stopped functioning. This left NavIC with only three satellites capable of supporting navigation services, fewer than the four needed for the system to work fully.

ISRO is now looking to rebuild the network with NVS-03, followed by two more satellites, NVS-04 and NVS-05. Together, these launches are expected to help make India's navigation system more reliable.

ISRO has also made changes to avoid a repeat of the problem that affected NVS-02. For the new mission, the space agency has decided to keep certain valves open before launch to prevent a similar technical fault from occurring.

The successful launch of NVS-03 would be an important step towards making India's own navigation system stronger and more dependable. It could also reduce the country's reliance on foreign systems such as GPS, which are widely used for navigation and other essential services, including banking, transport and telecommunications.
