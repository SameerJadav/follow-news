Confrontations between Indian and Chinese troops have been taking place at several locations along the Arunachal Pradesh border, The Hindu quoted Indo-Tibetan Border Police Director General Shatrujeet Singh Kapur as saying on Thursday.

“In the past few days, a situation arose where face-off happened at several locations in Arunachal and it still continues,” Kapur said at an event in Alwar, Rajasthan.

The Indo-Tibetan Border Police, a central force, guards the 3,488-km Line of Actual Control with China.

The force is stepping up preparations for modern warfare with drones, specialised commandos and new underground shelters, PTI quoted Kapur as further saying.

Reports of a stand-off between Indian and Chinese soldiers near Taksing in Arunachal Pradesh’s Upper Subansiri district emerged in July, with several confrontations reportedly taking place along the boundary.

“Sometime in July, during routine patrolling, Indian and Chinese troops came face to face near Maja,” an unidentified official told The Hindu. “After [a] brief confrontation, both sides returned but the Chinese PLA [People’s Liberation Army] came back in larger number few days later and pitched tents, around 6-7 kms within India’s perception of the LAC.”

The Indian military and the Union government had not commented on the reports at the time.

In August, the Ministry of External Affairs said that maintaining peace and tranquillity along the Line of Actual Control was of “utmost importance” and warned that the “state of the border affairs” will affect bilateral relations in general.

In June, The Arunachal Times quoted the Nah Welfare Society as having alleged that Chinese soldiers were occupying areas near the Line of Actual Control, building roads and military camps, and taking over grazing grounds.

On Friday, the Congress accused the Bharatiya Janata Party-led government of “suppressing” reports of skirmishes along the border since July.

The Opposition said that Prime Minister Narendra Modi “was seen joking around” with Chinese President Xi Jinping at the BRICS summit in September instead of taking action.

“Modi has abandoned our soldiers every time, betrayed the country and shirked his responsibility,” the Opposition party said on social media. “This weak prime minister has caused immense harm to the country.”

Xi visited India for the summit between September 11 and September 13. The visit came amid a thaw since 2024 in India-China relations, following years of tensions over a military standoff in Ladakh.

The two countries have held three rounds of high-level border talks since July, The Hindu reported.

China has for long sought to rename places in Arunachal Pradesh. Beijing lays territorial claims over a large portion of Arunachal Pradesh, claiming that it is “South Tibet”. China refers to Arunachal Pradesh as Zangnan. India has rejected China’s claims.

Ministry of External Affairs spokekesperson Randhir Jaiswal said on Friday that the situation along the India-China border areas “gets disturbed from time-to-time when Chinese forces diverge from established patrolling patterns”.

Answering a question about Kapur’s remarks, Jaiswal said during the ministry’s weekly media briefing that New Delhi and Beijing continue to use “existing diplomatic and military mechanisms” to resolve outstanding issues and “avoid misunderstanding and miscalculation along the Line of Actual Control”.

He added that the two sides have agreed to discuss ways to “improve understanding of LAC in appropriate areas” for better border management.
