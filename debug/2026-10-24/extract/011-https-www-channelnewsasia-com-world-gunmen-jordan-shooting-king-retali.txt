The attack happened during a funeral procession in Karak, and police launched a search for the gunmen.

Two gunmen are seen dressed in casual clothes and brandishing pistols, with a third carrying a long gun, before they open fire on the church from an entrance on the street and charge forwards."A terrorist group has carried out a horrific act, one that is condemned in every respect, and the security services are working round the clock to track them down," Interior Minister Mazen Al-Faraya told a press conference."Jordan has been targeted by a terrorist attack carried out by a group of extremists."He said the perpetrators had "absolutely no connection whatsoever to human values" and it "only serves to strengthen the Jordanians' resolve ... to combat and fight terrorism in all its forms".Mohammed Al-Khalayleh, minister of awqaf, Islamic affairs and holy sites, said "the sanctity of places of worship is inviolable and any violation of this is unacceptable on religious, moral and national grounds", calling national unity "a red line that must not be crossed".Prime Minister Jafar Hassan ordered a three-day period of national mourning from Friday, with flags to be flown at half-mast over government and public institutions.
