Kushner predicts Hamas disarmament progress in 'as little as 30 days'

More now from Jared Kushner’s interview with Fox News in Tel Aviv, where Donald Trump’s special envoy and son-in-law said that progress in getting Hamas to give up its weapons and move forward with a Gaza peace plan could materialize in about a month.

“We could be seeing progress ... in as little as 30 days, hopefully starting to take some of the weapons out,” Kushner said.

He said he met for nearly four hours with Israeli prime minister Benjamin Netanyahu and reassured him on several sticking points. “There were some valid concerns” on the Israeli side, Kushner said.

For example, he said, the Trump administration is committed to helping to rebuild Gaza. “But we will not allow Gaza to be rebuilt until the demilitarization occurs. Nobody wants to put more money into a place ... if it’s just going to be taken over by terrorists,” he said.

“We’re also not going to restrict Israel’s right to defend itself if there are any imminent threats and so we had to clarify what that means,” Kushner said. “For Israel we think it’s a win-win situation.”

Either Hamas will go ahead in disarming “willingly over the next 60 to 90 days” or “Israel will have a lot more support from the US and others to go and finish the job in the appropriate way,” Kushner added.

He said he had had a “very cordial” meeting with Hamas officials, but added that “it’s very, very hard to trust a terrorist organization.”

Construction on a US-Mexico border project in Big Bend national park has halted, the Trump administration said today, as Customs and Border Protection (CBP) evaluates the controversial project in Texas.

“CBP is pausing all construction activity in Big Bend National Park while I visit and conduct a personal, on-the-ground evaluation,” CBP’s head, Rodney Scott, wrote in a social media post.

Footage of bulldozers ploughing through the 800,000-acre (325,000-hectare) Chihuahuan desert national park emerged earlier this month, after the Trump administration did not announce and initially denied construction on a multibillion-dollar “smart wall” expansion in the park.

Last year, Congress approved $46.5bn for a border wall expansion in the “big, beautiful” spending bill Donald Trump signed into law on 4 July 2025.

Although many Texas Republicans have remained silent about the construction, outgoing senator John Cornyn sent a letter to Markwayne Mullin, Trump’s homeland security secretary, asking him to halt construction on the wall.

“My constituents believe that the 1,000-foot riverbank cliffs and remote Big Bend terrain offers its own sources of deterrence for any illegal migrants crossing into the United States,” Cornyn wrote.

Scott, the CBP chief, has said that he expects the agency will build the first layer of the wall by the end of next year.

Texas parents ask US supreme court to hear Ten Commandments in classrooms case

A group of Texas parents have asked the US supreme court to block a state law that requires the Ten Commandments to be displayed in public schools.

The American Civil Liberties Union (ACLU) and other groups joined the parents of diverse faiths with children in Texas public schools in asking the high court to hear the case.

“The US Supreme Court has rejected this kind of government-imposed religion before, and it should do so again,” Chloe Kempf, an attorney at the ACLU of Texas, said in a statement.

“Our nation’s bedrock principle of separating church and state means that families and faith communities – not politicians – get to decide what role religion plays in children’s lives,” she said.

In a similar case in 1980, the Supreme Court ruled that the display of the Ten Commandments in schools in Kentucky was unconstitutional.

However in April, a conservative-dominated appeals court ruled in favor of the Texas law, which requires the scripture to be posted in every classroom.

The judges ruled that the bill did not violate the establishment clause of the US Constitution, the part of the first amendment that prohibits the government from establishing a national religion or showing preference to one over another.

Conservatives enjoy a 6-3 majority on the supreme court and the justices have sided with religious rights claims in a number of recent high-profile cases.

Along with Texas, Louisiana, Arkansas and Alabama have all passed laws requiring the posters of the commandments in public schools. South Carolina, West Virginia and Missouri are also considering similar bills.

House ethics committee investigating Jimmy Gomez over sexual misconduct allegations

The House ethics committee has announced that it is investigating Democratic representative Jimmy Gomez over allegations of sexual misconduct.

The committee said in a statement that it is reviewing allegations that Gomez “engaged in sexual misconduct in violation of the Code of Official Conduct or any other applicable standard of conduct, including engaging in inappropriate sexual contact with a House staffer.”

It added that that existence of an investigation and public disclosure of the investigation “does not itself indicate that any violation has occurred”.

CNN reported back in June that the panel was investigating 51-year-old Gomez, of California, including over an alleged romantic relationship he had in 2023 with an aide who worked for another member of Congress. He’s been married since 2011.

According to CNN’s sources, the committee made early contacts in an attempt to follow up on a New York Post story, which alleged that Gomez had been spotted kissing a much younger aide, who worked for another member of Congress, outside a backyard party in 2023. A Gomez spokesperson told The Post at the time that the accounts were “not true” and the kiss “didn’t happen”.

While reaching out to look into that story, one of the sources told CNN that the committee had learned of other allegations of sexual misconduct against Gomez that it is now also investigating.

House rules don’t currently prohibit members of Congress from having relationships with staffers outside their own offices, but the bipartisan sexual misconduct task force is weighing whether to recommend changing this.

It comes not long after two other representatives, Democrat Eric Swalwell and Republican Tony Gonzales, recently resigned amid allegations of sexual misconduct with staffers.

The San Francisco Chronicle reported that an unnamed former staffer had accused Swalwell, of California, of sexually assaulting her on two occasions. CNN later published a similar account, as well as allegations from three other women of him sending them unwanted sexual messages.

In the case of Gonzales, of Texas, he weathered for weeks calls to resign after admitting to an affair with an aide who later died by suicide, before eventually caving to pressure to not run for re-election.

Republican Chuck Edwards also recently dropped his bid for re-election after the committee determined that he “fostered a hostile work environment and engaged in sexual harassment” by giving gifts and lavishing attention on two young female staffers.

The committee has also opened an investigation into Republican Cory Mills regarding campaign financial misconduct and domestic abuse allegations.

Trump blames 'vandals' for damaged grass in the spot where he held his big July 4 celebration

Donald Trump has blamed “vandals” for large swaths of dead grass on the National Mall near the World War II Memorial — in the very spot where he had a stage built and hosted crowds for his 4 July celebration.

Trump made the accusation in a social media post yesterday without saying why he believes it’s the work of vandals instead of trampled turf from the Independence Day festivities. He posted a photo showing a once-grassy area by the World War II Memorial, now patchy and brown across much of the sprawling turf.

“Look what VANDALS did to the grass connecting the vandalized World War II Monument and the vandalized Reflecting Pool, which will be opened again, and better than ever, shortly,” Trump said on his Truth Social platform. “Anybody who thinks the Reflecting Pool wasn’t vandalized should go back to Law School!”

Trump has repeatedly blamed “vandals” for damage to his renovation projects in the nation’s capital, often without evidence and sometimes despite opposing accounts from his officials.

Trump lashed out at US attorney Jeanine Pirro this month after her office concluded that damage to the Lincoln Memorial Reflecting Pool was the result of poor construction and not the work of vandals.

Authorities on Friday announced an arrest in connection with vandalism at the World War II Memorial , after the words “Clean hands dirty $” were found painted on the monument and bubbles filled a fountain. Pirro said felony charges were being brought against Melissa L Farris, who was in custody and couldn’t be reached for comment by the Associated Press.

A woman identified on her Facebook page as Melissa Lovewell on Thursday posted a video in which she is seen taking cans of spray paint from her bag and then painting the monument. “I will wait here to be arrested for spraying our veterans’ fountain,” she says. “It was me. I’m doing it. It’s on camera.”

The woman was identified by her Kentucky driver’s license as Melissa L Farris, of Elizabethtown, Kentucky, an arrest warrant cited by the AP says.

Charging documents in that case make no mention of the grassy area Trump is highlighting in his new claims. Authorities said Farris had previously been arrested for camping on federal property a few blocks from the World War II Memorial, but not the area cited by Trump.

That stretch of lawn is instead where a large stage was built and crowds gathered to hear from Trump and watch fireworks at a 4 July event celebrating the nation’s 250th anniversary. Trump claimed without providing evidence that more than 400,000 people attended the event before a storm hit the area and delayed his remarks.

Photos from the time, taken by a streaming webcam atop the Washington Monument, show a large bandstand and seating areas covering nearly the entire lawn Trump now says has been vandalized. Associated Press photos from the days that followed showed workers removing the structures, revealing brown, damaged grass underneath.

On 1 August, Trump posted a different photo of the same area that appears to show the numbers “86 47” – which federal officials have said constitutes a threat against Trump, the 47th president – emblazoned into the grass.

He cited it as evidence refuting Pirro’s conclusion, calling it “a pure case of VANDALISM, that included the grass.” Trump wrote: “There may have been some contractor difficulty, but the major damage was caused by VANDALS!”

Those numbers are not visible in the photo Trump posted yesterday.

US gas prices this month are so far the highest ever recorded for August as peace talks between the US and Iran have stalled and Donald Trump launched fresh threats against Oman.

The national average price of gas was $4.06 per gallon on Monday, according to data from AAA, $0.05 higher than last week and $1 more expensive than a year ago. In California and Hawaii, averages hit about $5.50 a gallon.

Energy prices have been high since the US-Israel war with Iran began at the end of February and the strait of Hormuz, a crucial waterway through which a fifth of the oil passes, was blocked.

Brent crude, the global oil benchmark, reached $112 a barrel in March, a high not seen since 2022. While prices have since come down closer to $85 a barrel, they are still about 30% higher than a year ago.

Attorney General Todd Blanche on Monday announced changes to the process for restoring federal gun possession rights to those who lost them as a consequence of criminal convictions.

“Restoration of firearm rights is neither automatic nor guaranteed,” Blanche said in a published statement on X.

Under the new rules, each applicant must prove to the attorney general’s satisfaction that their record, reputation and conduct since the prohibition show they are not likely to act in a manner dangerous to public safety; and that granting relief is not contrary to the public interest.

Violent felons, registered sex offenders, undocumented immigrants, fugitives, habitual drug users and people under domestic-violence restraining orders are presumed ineligible for gun rights restoration.

Previously, the restoration of federal gun rights effectively required a presidential pardon. The DoJ is launching a web-based portal for applications.

Supreme court rejects Trump's bid to overturn E Jean Carroll verdict for second time

The US supreme court has rejected Donald Trump’s latest attempt to overturn a jury judgment that found him liable for sexually abusing and then defaming writer E Jean Carroll.

The high court did not give any reason for its decision not to rehear the president’s challenge. In late June it also refused to hear his appeal against the original May 2023 verdict, leaving intact the $5m that Carroll was awarded in damages.

Carroll, 82, a former journalist and columnist, accused Trump of assaulting her in ​a Bergdorf Goodman dressing room in Manhattan in 1996.

When the allegations were published in an excerpt from her 2019 memoir, Trump called Carroll a “nut job” and accused her of lying.

Trump paid $5.6m in damages and post-judgement interest to Carroll last month after a civil jury found him liable for sexual abuse and defamation.

In a separate defamation case in New York, Trump was ordered to pay $83.3m to her. Trump has also appealed that judgment to the supreme court.
