Qld parliamentary review of liability bill chooses not to hear from sexual abuse victims

A Queensland parliamentary committee reviewing a bill to overturn a ban on lawsuits by child sexual abuse victims against some employers has chosen not to hear from any victim-survivors.

The state Labor MP Meaghan Scanlon tabled the bill last year. It reverses a 2024 high court judgment ruling that the Catholic church was not legally responsible for the actions of a priest, because they were not a direct employee. The judgment also applies to all other employers.

Similar legislation has already passed parliaments in Victoria and the ACT.

The Queensland parliament’s justice, integrity and community safety committee held a single two-hour hearing into the bill this morning. The committee, which has a government chair, chose only to hear from lawyers.

Child abuse survivor Diane Carpenter said she was “devastated” not to be allowed to be heard by the inquiry and called for the bill to be passed.

If the bill is not passed, many adults who, as children, were abused in institutional care, will have no pathway to justice. Currently, we have two classes of victims: those who can access justice and those who can’t.

Retrial date for UK rapper accused of raping Australian fan

A new trial date has been set for a British musician and media personality accused of violently raping a fan in a hotel, after a jury was unable to reach a verdict, AAP reports.

Rapper Yung Filly, who is legally known as Andres Felipe Valencia Barrientos, faced trial in July over his 2024 encounter with a 20-year-old woman after a Perth nightclub gig.

The 31-year-old was found guilty of two assault offences and acquitted of three sexual penetration charges following an eight-day trial in Western Australia’s district court in July.

The jury was hung on three other sexual penetration counts and Barrientos is set to be retried for the charges.

He was not required to appear when the case returned to court on Friday and a fresh trial was listed for 2027.

The trial, beginning on 15 June in the same court, is expected to run for eight days.

Judge Linda Black previously permitted Barrientos to vary his bail so he could travel home to the UK while he awaits retrial. He is understood to have departed Perth in late August.

Australian shares on track for fourth straight losing week

The Australian share market has fallen to a three-month low and is headed for its fourth straight week of losses, as oil prices and bond yields both moved higher, AAP reports.

Near noon on Friday, the benchmark S&P/ASX200 index had fallen 0.43% to 8,664, its lowest level since mid-June, while the broader All Ordinaries had dropped 0.55%, to 8,848.2.

With a few hours of trading left, the ASX200 was on pace to finish the week down 0.8%, and was also 0.6% lower since the start of the year.

The Australian dollar was changing hands for 70.12 US cents, from 70.35 US cents at 5pm on Thursday.

Albanese said AI breach information was released as soon as possible

The prime minister, Anthony Albanese, says it’s “nonsense” to suggest the government may have released details about the OpenAI breach when it did because it suited him politically.

He needs to act more like a responsible leader of an alternative government if he’s going to be taken seriously.

More Queensland social housing dwellings vacant for longer

More social housing dwellings in Queensland are sitting vacant for longer, according to new data released by the state government.

The average downtime for a social housing dwelling has increased from 25.1 days last June to 42.7 days in July. Vacancy rates are slightly up from about 0.56% to 0.6%.

The data was released yesterday in response to a parliamentary question.

The number of people in the state’s emergency homelessness assistance scheme – which pays hotels and motels to accommodate them – has declined from 3,623 to 2,707. The state made many homeless people ineligible for the system last year.

There has been a slight increase in the number of Queenslanders in the state’s public housing system, from 55,913 to 55,998, but a decline in the number on the social housing waiting list, from 53,874 last September to 46,037 in July.
