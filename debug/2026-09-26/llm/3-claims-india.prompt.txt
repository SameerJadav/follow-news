=== STORY 8 [india] — Protests and political row intensify over electoral roll revision and demands for CEC Gyanesh Kumar's resignation ===

[a0] Times of India <https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/india/3-bjp-allies-say-ec-must-clear-air-cant-lose-peoples-trust/articleshow/134495064.cms> (FULL TEXT)
Three NDA partners - Chirag Paswan's LJP(RV), Upendra Kushwaha's RLM, and Chandrababu Naidu's TDP - Friday urged the Election Commission (EC) to explain its position on the political controversy surrounding the electoral process and implementation of the special intensive revision (SIR) of electoral rolls, saying this was necessary to dispel doubts among the public.Paswan, LJP(RV) chief and Union food processing industries minister, told reporters in Patna he did not intend, as part of govt, to "defend what is a constitutional body", but wanted EC to address concerns among people. "If questions are being raised about its conduct, it is the responsibility of EC, especially the chief election commissioner (Gyanesh Kumar), to present its side firmly," Paswan said. He added that he had nothing to do with what the opposition said, as their job was to level allegations and politicise the issue, but the common people needed to be taken into confidence."If people start losing trust in the electoral system, that will not augur well for democracy and, therefore, I think the EC must come out with its clarification more forcefully. It need not respond to what Rahul Gandhi and his associates are saying. But it must dispel misgivings in the minds of the people," he said.RLM president Kushwaha echoed Paswan, writing in a social media post, "There is considerable confusion among the public regarding the controversy surrounding SIR. This issue needs to be addressed. The EC should immediately clarify the situation to dispel this confusion."Transparency to help boost trust in EC: TDPChandrababu Naidu's Telugu Desam Party joined two other NDA allies - LJP(RV) and RLM - in urging the Election Commission to issue a clarification amid reports about internal differences and protests by the opposition.Reports in The Indian Express that two top election officials had objected multiple times to decisions made without their knowledge or consent have led to opposition demands that CEC Gyanesh Kumar be removed from office and SIR process halted. EC had said Wednesday that differing views among members were normal and that its final decisions had been unanimous.TDP parliamentary party neta Lavu Sri Krishna Devarayalu, while saying SIR had been a success in Andhra, where the party is in office, also sought clarification from the EC and greater transparency. "The EC is a constitutional institution entrusted with a crucial responsibility in our democracy. Therefore, whenever questions are raised by anyone, providing appropriate clarification and maintaining transparency will only help strengthen public confidence in the institution," Devarayalu said. He added that in Andhra Pradesh, SIR was conducted transparently and voters were given adequate opportunities and time to raise concerns.Bihar BJP spokesperson Manoj Sharma said, "They (Chirag and Kushwaha) head their own parties and hold the right to talk about this issue." Another BJP functionary, who did not wish to be named, said the party's alliance with LJP(RV) and RLM was "limited only to Bihar and was not on the all-India level".Get the latest India News and Live updates. Maharashtra Navnirman Sena chief Raj Thackeray on Friday (September 25, 2026) sought the scrapping of the Special Intensive Revision (SIR) process. He termed it a “new weapon in the hands of the Bharatiya Janata Party”.

“It has become evident how the Bharatiya Janata Party [BJP] leveraged individuals like Gyanesh Kumar to secure an endless grip on power,” Mr. Thackeray said in a post on social media platform X.

The demand comes amid the Opposition’s chorus seeking the removal of India’s Chief Election Commissioner, Gyanesh Kumar and the scrapping of SIR. This was after media reports of differences flagged by the two Election Commissioners.

Echoing the demand made by his cousin Uddhav Thackeray, Mr. Raj also sought elections on the ballot papers. He also supported the call given by several other opposition parties to seek unity of all the parties to fight against it.

“Observing the irregularities within the SIR and noting that even Election Commissioners are speaking out about them, only one conclusion remains: the entire SIR process must be scrapped. All opposition parties in the country need to unite for this cause. Maharashtra Navnirman Sena [MNS] demands the complete cancellation of the recent SIR exercise, an impartial inquiry into the irregularities, and a start the process anew,” Mr Thackeray said in his post.

Speaking to the media this week, Shiv Sena (UBT) chief Uddhav Thackeray had said that some States should have re-elections in light of the fresh allegations. He said that the opposition parties had been raising the issue of anomalies in the voters’ lists for some time.

Meanwhile, protests were held at several places in the country by Congress on Friday (September 25), seeking the resignation of Gyanesh Kumar.

Opposition leaders and legal observers have raised questions about alleged judicial impropriety by Supreme Court Justice Satish Chandra Sharma for hearing petitions regarding the constitutionality of a 2023 law that governs the appointment of members of the Election Commission, despite his son being the standing advocate for the poll panel in Madhya Pradesh.

It was not clear if the judge had disclosed this information to the petitioners against the 2023 Chief Election Commissioner and Other Election Commissioners Appointment, Conditions of Service and Term of Office Act before the hearing, Live Law reported on Friday.

On Wednesday, he had disagreed with Justice Dipankar Datta, who was part of the bench hearing the six petitions against the Act, when the latter held that the law “does not appear to ensure an independent selection process”.

The Act states that the head of the poll panel and the two other top members are to be appointed based on the recommendations of a selection committee comprising the prime minister, a Union Cabinet minister and the leader of the Opposition in the Lok Sabha.

The composition of the panel means that the government enjoys a 2:1 majority over the leader of the Opposition, if there is a difference of opinion.

Sharma on Wednesday also headed a bench that overturned an Allahabad High Court order directing Gautam Buddha Nagar District Magistrate Medha Roopam to pay Rs 5 lakh in compensation from her salary to 25-year-old Delhi University graduate Akriti Chaudhary, who had been detained under the National Security Act after the workers’ protest in Noida.

Roopam is the daughter of Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar.

The matter was listed before Sharma’s bench, despite it not being assigned to handle preventive detention cases according to the Supreme Court roster, reported Live Law.

According to the Madhya Pradesh High Court’s website, Sharma’s son is the standing counsel for the Election Commission since 2024. He is also the standing counsel for the Central Board of Direct Taxes and the Income Tax Department since 2023.

Pointing this out, Saurav Das, a convenor of the Cockroach Janta Party political campaign, asked how a judge can be expected to deliver “impartial and fair” justice in politically sensitive cases when his family members “serve at the pleasure of the government”.
