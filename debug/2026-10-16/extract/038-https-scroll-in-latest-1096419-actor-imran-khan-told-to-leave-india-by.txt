The Mumbai Police has directed actor Imran Khan to leave India by October 28 after he joined a protest demanding the resignation of Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar, ANI reported on Thursday.

The police issued a Leave India Notice after Khan had taken part in the agitation at August Kranti Maidan in Mumbai on Saturday “despite not being an Indian citizen”, the news agency reported.

A Leave India Notice is a directive issued by the authorities requiring a foreign citizen to exit the country within a specified time period. Failing to comply with the order could lead to detention, deportation or blacklisting.

Khan is a citizen of the United States and holds an Overseas Citizen of India card, Hindustan Times reported.

Overseas Citizen of India is an immigration status that allows foreign citizens of Indian origin to live and work in India indefinitely.

The police have initiated action against him under the Immigration Act, the newspaper reported.

Khan’s lawyer said that rules do not prohibit Overseas Citizen of India cardholders from participating in a protest, PTI reported. The actor will move the Bombay High Court against the notice, Bar and Bench reported.

Bharatiya Janata Party leader Kirit Somaiya welcomed the action by Mumbai Police. “All such foreign citizens, persons using foreign fund to create anarchy in India should face such action,” he said.

Before the notice was issued, Somaiya had written to the Mumbai Police seeking an inquiry into Khan’s participation in the protest.

Speaking at the protest, the actor had said that he was attending in his capacity as a resident of Mumbai and was not affiliated with any organisation, the Hindustan Times reported.

“I am here as a citizen, as a person who lives in this city,” he had said. “I am here to see why freedom of movement is being restricted. Why are people’s voices being suppressed?”

Protests are being held in several parts of the country calling for Kumar to resign over concerns about the functioning of the Election Commission and its handling of the special intensive revision exercise.

The trigger for this round of protests was a September 23 investigation by The Indian Express, which revealed that Election Commissioners Sukhbir Singh Sandhu and Vivek Joshi had formally objected 14 times in 10 months to decisions by the poll panel related to the special intensive revision, saying some had been taken without their knowledge.

Since the exercise began in June 2025, it has faced questions about the manner and pace of its implementation, amid concerns that it could deny genuine electors their right to vote and shape election outcomes, as Scroll has reported.

In May, the Supreme Court upheld the legality of the exercise, but said that it does not mean that the Election Commission can decide on whether the person is an Indian citizen.

Despite this, BJP-ruled states have cited the SIR to deny ration cards to those who have been eliminated from the rolls. In some cases, the police have denied permission for their passports to be renewed.
