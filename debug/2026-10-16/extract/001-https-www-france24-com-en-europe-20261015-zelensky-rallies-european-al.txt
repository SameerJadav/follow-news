Ukraine's President [Volodymyr Zelensky](https://www.france24.com/en/tag/volodymyr-zelensky/) sought Thursday to rally [European allies](https://www.france24.com/en/europe/20261014-kyiv-power-plant-hit-by-russian-missile-without-warning-from-allies) after saying [Kyiv](https://www.france24.com/en/tv-shows/focus/20261015-war-in-ukraine-kyiv-residents-decry-shortage-of-bomb-shelters) would no longer abide by US President Donald Trump's demands [not to strike Russian oil refineries](https://www.france24.com/en/video/20261015-we-re-done-ukraine-resumes-strikes-on-russian-oil-refineries-as-only-leverage-for-peace-talks-1).

Relations between Kyiv and [Washington](https://www.france24.com/en/tag/washington-d-c/) have soured since last week, when Trump eased sanctions on Russia in exchange for Moscow releasing large amounts of diesel onto global markets.

Anger in Ukraine has been further heightened by outages to a critical US-provided satellite system that has hampered its military's ability to counter [Russia](https://www.france24.com/en/tag/russia/)'s intense bombardments.

Against the backdrop of his latest falling-out with Trump, Zelensky met [NATO](https://www.france24.com/en/tag/nato/) chief [Mark Rutte](https://www.france24.com/en/tag/mark-rutte/) in Brussels and held a string of bilateral meetings with European leaders before he was set to join an EU summit on Friday.

"All of us in Europe want this war to end. And for that to happen, strong signals are needed – more real pressure on Russia and more defence support for [Ukraine](https://www.france24.com/en/tag/ukraine/)," Zelensky wrote online.

"It is especially important now to act together to protect lives."

European leaders have broadly thrown their support behind Zelensky, as they look to weather the latest storm in Trump's volatile relations with Kyiv.

French President [Emmanuel Macron](https://www.france24.com/en/tag/emmanuel-macron/) on Thursday said it was "crucial" that Ukraine receive "all the means necessary to protect itself" after meeting Zelensky.

"I can reassure you, dear Ukrainian friends and President Zelensky: YOU ARE NOT ALONE," Polish Prime Minister [Donald Tusk](https://www.france24.com/en/tag/donald-tusk/) posted online.

## 'Maximum destruction'

Zelensky has had to endure a rollercoaster relationship with [Trump](https://www.france24.com/en/tag/donald-trump/) since the mercurial US leader returned to office last year.

In a move that risked infuriating Trump further, Zelensky told the Financial Times that Ukraine would no longer hold off targeting Russian refineries as Moscow was not abiding by a ceasefire on hitting energy infrastructure announced by the US leader.

As he met with Macron in [Brussels](https://www.france24.com/en/tag/belgium/), Zelensky said that the Kremlin had "launched yet another vile drone strike on Ukrainian energy infrastructure" in the city of Lutsk, causing blackouts.

"Russia has demonstratively disregarded the American president's proposal for an energy [ceasefire](https://www.france24.com/en/tag/ceasefire/). And now it is taking advantage of problems with Starlink operations to cause maximum destruction to everything that sustains everyday life," he wrote online.

The Kremlin meanwhile accused Ukraine of derailing US peace efforts by resuming its strikes on refineries, with Russian authorities reporting a [drone](https://www.france24.com/en/tag/drones/) attack on an oil refinery south of [Moscow](https://www.france24.com/en/tag/moscow/) that caused a fire.

Kyiv "continues to launch strikes against these facilities," Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov told reporters.

"It is coming into serious conflict with the peace efforts of the United States and American negotiators, who are making vigorous efforts to initiate the peace process," he added.

In [Kyiv](https://www.france24.com/en/tag/kyiv/), residents told AFP they sensed a change in Washington's attitude towards Ukraine.

"Let them live here for two to three days. All these nighttime attacks, what people here are going through – blackouts, no electricity. And then maybe he'll see all this differently," 30-year-old Alyona Nikitina said.

Russia has intensified its bombardment of Ukraine and its capital in recent months, leading to mounting civilian casualties and disrupting daily life for citizens.
