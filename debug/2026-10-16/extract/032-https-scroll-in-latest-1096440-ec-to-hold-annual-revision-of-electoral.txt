The Election Commission on Thursday said that it would conduct the special summary revision in 13 states and three Union Territories where the special intensive revision of electoral rolls has been conducted, as well as in Assam, where the poll panel decided not to undertake the exercise.

The poll panel had been conducting a special revision of the voter list in Assam, which is similar to the usual updates to the electoral roll.

Writing to the chief electoral officers of Andaman and Nicobar Islands, Assam, Bihar, Chhattisgarh, Goa, Gujarat, Kerala, Lakshadweep, Madhya Pradesh, Manipur, Puducherry, Punjab, Rajasthan, Tamil Nadu, Uttar Pradesh, Uttarakhand and West Bengal, the poll panel said that the special summary revision with the qualifying date of January 1 would be conducted from November.

The draft rolls after the revision will be published on November 12. Following this, claims and objections will be invited till December 11 and the final rolls will be published on January 4.

The poll panel’s announcement came days after media reports said that the freezing of the annual revision of electoral rolls after 2024 due to the special intensive revision could affect the enrolment of voters who have recently turned 18.

On September 24, The Economic Times reported that the poll panel had overshot its May-June announcement timeline for the annual revision due to a lack of consensus between Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar and Election Commissioners Sukhbir Singh Sandhu and Vivek Joshi.

The newspaper reported that Sandhu and Joshi had been advocating for resuming the exercise, while Kumar sought legal opinion on it.

A day later, the Congress accused Prime Minister Narendra Modi of stopping the special summary revisions of electoral rolls “to ensure that Gen Z voters are not enrolled”.

“This is a PM [prime minister] who fears the youth having dashed their hopes and aspirations,” said party leader Jairam Ramesh.

“Gen Z” refers to persons born between the late 1990s and 2010.
