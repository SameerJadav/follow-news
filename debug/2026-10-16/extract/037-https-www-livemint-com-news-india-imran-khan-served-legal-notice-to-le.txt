Bollywood actor Imran Khan will challenge the legal notice issued to him to leave the country by October 28. As per PTI, Khan is likely to file the plea in the Bombay High Court by Friday or Monday over alleged violation of the Immigration and Foreigners Act. The controversy stems from Khan's participation in protests against Chief Election Commissioner (CEC) Gyanesh Kumar in Mumbai.

Khan, a US national and Overseas Citizen of India (OCI) cardholder, received a notice from the Foreigners Regional Registration Office (FRRO) after his detention at the demonstration.

According to an MHA notification dated March 4, 2021, OCI cardholders are entitled to a multiple-entry, lifelong visa to visit India and are exempt from registration with FRRO, irrespective of the duration of their stay.

The same notification also specifies parity with Non-Resident Indians (NRIs) in certain economic, financial and educational matters, subject to applicable conditions. However, OCI cardholders must obtain special permission for specified activities such as research, missionary or Tabligh work, mountaineering, journalism, selected internships or employment at foreign diplomatic missions in India, and visits to protected, restricted or prohibited areas.

Under Section 7B of the Citizenship Act, 1955, OCI cardholders are not entitled to all rights reserved for Indian citizens. OCT holders do not have rights like the right to vote or contest parliamentary and state legislative elections, according to the Ministry of Home Affairs.

Article 19 of the Constitution, which guarantees freedom of speech and expression and the right to assemble peacefully, applies specifically to citizens.

This is where Imran Khan's rights as an OCI cardholder may remain controversial. The notification from the MHA does not particularly list participation in political protests or demonstrations among the activities for which an individual under OCI status requires prior permission.

The notice was sent to Imran Khan following a complaint against him by former BJP MP Kirit Somaiya.
