Ousted Venezuelan president [Nicolas Maduro](https://www.france24.com/en/tag/nicolas-maduro/) and his wife Cilia Flores pleaded not guilty on Thursday to [new torture charges](https://www.france24.com/en/americas/20261008-us-files-new-torture-charges-venezuela-nicolas-maduro-cilia-flores) brought against the pair held in US custody since their [extraordinary capture in January](https://www.france24.com/en/video/20260103-replay-us-president-donald-trump-press-conference-on-venezuela-s-attack-and-capture-of-maduro).

They appeared at a [New York](https://www.france24.com/en/tag/new-york/) court after prosecutors alleged they conspired to commit acts including waterboarding, rape and beatings against victims– including American citizens– in Venezuelan detention.

Maduro, who has previously called himself a "prisoner of war," slammed the latest charges as "false accusations" during a 35-minute hearing.

Asked by the judge how he pleaded to the indictment against him, the 63-year-old responded, "I reject this document in its entirety."

Flores, who wore the same brown prison jumpsuit as Maduro, said: "I am completely innocent, I am not guilty."

According to prosecutors, the couple used torture to "intimidate, harass, and neutralise their opponents, including through kidnappings."

Maduro "presided over" a system of repression and torture, while Flores helped him to develop it, according to a charge document.

In one instance in 2022, Venezuelan authorities allegedly tortured an American who they accused of plotting to assassinate Maduro.

## Immunity plea

Maduro and Flores already face [drug trafficking](https://www.france24.com/en/tag/drug-trafficking/) and firearms charges in the United States after they were snatched from their Caracas compound in a [US military](https://www.france24.com/en/tag/us-military/) raid.

US prosecutors claim that Maduro used state power to protect and promote illegal drug smuggling, and that he allied himself with cartels to ship tons of [cocaine](https://www.france24.com/en/tag/cocaine/) into the United States.

Both are slated for a trial in [New York](https://www.france24.com/en/tag/new-york/) next June and are being held in a Brooklyn jail.

The pair have appealed to have the charges against them dismissed, claiming they have immunity as president and first lady of a sovereign country.

Since [Maduro's capture](https://www.france24.com/en/tag/nicolas-maduro-s-capture/), former Executive Vice President [Delcy Rodriguez](https://www.france24.com/en/tag/delcy-rodriguez/) has governed [Venezuela](https://www.france24.com/en/tag/venezuela/)[as interim leader](https://www.france24.com/en/americas/20260106-delcy-rodriguez-profile-maduro-deputy-acting-president-venezuela)under intense pressure from US President [Donald Trump](https://www.france24.com/en/tag/donald-trump/)'s administration.

She has notably ushered through reforms to open Venezuela's prized oil sector to international investment, and signed a massive deal providing [US control over a large portion of the country's reserves](https://www.france24.com/en/tv-shows/people-profit/20260108-venezuela-s-crude-concessions-us-vows-to-exert-control-over-world-s-largest-oil-reserves).
