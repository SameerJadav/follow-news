The White House’s unilateral effort to negotiate an energy ceasefire between Russia and Ukraine has collapsed as Moscow sent a record swarm of drones overnight to attack infrastructure in central Ukraine and Kyiv appeared to resume strikes on Russian oil refineries despite warnings from Donald Trump.

Moscow accused Ukraine of launching a strike against a Russian oil refinery in the Ryazan region, its first since Trump abruptly declared an “energy ceasefire” that neither Russia nor Ukraine said they had agreed to.

Trump has demanded that Volodymyr Zelenskyy stop attacking Russian oil refineries, claiming it is the cause of a global rise in the price of diesel fuel.

An attack on two neutral ships in Romanian territorial waters at the mouth of the Danube River indicated that strikes against vulnerable commercial targets are set to expand.

“These very warplanes were ready to launch new attacks on Ukraine and were loaded with aerial bombs,” Zelenskyy said, publishing a satellite photo of the damaged airfield.

In Ukraine, more than 360 Russian drones targeted Kirovohrad, a central region that has been hit less hard than others closer to Russia, in attacks that killed three people and injured six.

“The enemy deliberately hit critical infrastructure, transport enterprises, logistics ⁠facilities and residential homes,” the regional governor, Andriy Raikovych, said in a statement.

“It is obvious that Russia deliberately chose to escalate its attacks after receiving sanctions relief and seeing attempts to pressure Ukraine,” Zelenskyy said in a social media post on X, referring to a growing quarrel with the Trump administration.

“Ukraine needs steady support, above all in defending our skies. We need HONESTY from our partners about this war: It is important that everyone in America stand not with the aggressor, but with our efforts to protect lives, end this war, and guarantee security,” he said.

# 'It is clear that sooner or later we will have European casualties', Romanian MEP

 Europe 

Issued on: 15/10/2026 - 22:03

Rescuers searched on Thursday for seven missing sailors after drones struck two chemical tankers near Romania's Black Sea coast, setting them ablaze and raising fresh security concerns along a vital shipping route used by Ukraine. Speaking with FRANCE 24's Europe editor Armen Georgian, Nicolae Stefanuta, Romanian MEP and Co-Chair of the Group of the Greens, says that the Danube delta region is facing a more intense threat from Russia than Baltic States and that "it is clear that sooner or later we will have European casualties".

Seven of 12 crew members are missing after a drone attack left a chemical tanker ablaze off Romania's coast on Thursday morning, naval authorities say.

The San Marino-flagged Wine Trader was 13 nautical miles (24km) east of the mouth of the River Danube at Sulina, when it reported blasts and a fire.

Romanian President NicuÈor Dan confirmed that the Wine Trader and another chemical tanker in the area, the Yara J, had both been struck by drones - the Yara J on Wednesday.

Moscow said two cargo ships with military cargo heading to Ukraine's Odesa port had been hit. It did not name the vessels.

Overnight, a swarm of Russian drones was detected not far from the scene, near Ukraine's Snake Island.

The west coast of the Black Sea has recently seen a spate of drone attacks.

On Tuesday, two merchant ships were struck by drones further south along the coast of Bulgaria, Romania's neighbour, and one of the vessels sank, leaving 11 crew members missing.

Bulgaria said it suspected Russia was behind that attack, summoning the country's envoy.

A search and rescue operation for the missing members of the Wine Trader continues, Romania's authorities have said. Two Black Hawk helicopters and two rescue boats have been deployed.

The Romanian interior ministry has released a video showing one of the helicopters hovering above the vessel.

The priority is now to reach the four crew members confirmed to be alive, while ensuring the safety of rescue teams with very high waves reported in the area.

The Wine Trader tanker, which was sailing from Tunisia to Romania, reported several explosions on board at about 07:00 (05:00 GMT).

Romanian media cited crews on nearby ships as saying the Wine Trader had been hit by two drones.

The leaders of Romania and Bulgaria are due to discuss shortly whether Article 4 of the Nato treaty should be invoked.

This allows any member of the military alliance to request consultations with its allies if it believes its territorial integrity or security is threatened.

The Romanian president said: "We want to have all the information about what happened, but there will be a response from the Romanian state."

Dan is due to meet Nato Secretary General Mark Rutte in Brussels alongside Bulgarian Prime Minister Rumen Radev to discuss growing security threats in the Black Sea region.

The Romanian president said discussions would focus on operational requirements, including additional equipment to protect shipping in the area.

Ukraine has directly blamed Russia for earlier attacks on civilian vessels in the western side of the Black Sea - a key route for Ukrainian exports and imports.

On Tuesday, European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen spoke of the Black Sea becoming "another front of escalation of the Russian war in Ukraine. This is unacceptable".

Russia's military has recently intensified its strikes on vessels in the area, as part of its war against Ukraine.

A fire broke out on the Comoros-flagged Yara J late on Wednesday after being struck by a drone, but its crew - who are all reportedly safe - were able to douse the flames themselves.

Last week, another two ships were struck off Bulgaria, and on the same day Ukraine hit an oil tanker in the Russian Black Sea port of Sochi.

Moscow says it is targeting ships delivering weapons and dual-use cargo to Ukrainian ports.

Kyiv and its European allies say merchant vessels carrying grain and other civilian cargo are being attacked.

Both Russia and Ukraine are major grain-exporting countries and Turkey has called on both countries to halt attacks in the Black Sea.

Moscow has repeatedly rejected calls by the US and Europe for a naval ceasefire in the Black Sea, blaming Ukraine for attacking Russian merchant ships in the area.

Russian President Vladimir Putin launched a full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022, and Moscow currently controls about 20% of Ukrainian territory.

"This is a matter of significant gravity, given that such incidents are occurring in Romanian territorial waters," he said.

The other fire, which broke out earlier, occurred onboard the Comoros-flagged chemical and oil tanker Yara J.

Ships often use the canal as a place to anchor in the case of delays caused by weather or congestion.

Ukrainian ships are also choosing routes in the Black Sea close to the coasts of Bulgaria and Romania.

This comes as shipping in the Black Sea has come under increasing attacks ​⁠in recent months that have drastically reduced grain transports from both Ukraine and Russia, both major cereal exporters.
