A Delhi court on Monday extended by two days the judicial custody of Cockroach Janta Party leaders Ashutosh Ranka and Saurav Das, Bar and Bench reported.

Ranka and Das, along with another campaign leader, Ajinkya Shinde, were detained on October 13 after they trespassed on Jantar Mantar and waved the national flag. They had “sneaked into” the Jantar Mantar to oppose the authorities’ decision to denotify the area as a designated protest site.

On October 14, Ranka and Das were sent to judicial custody for five days. Shinde was released on a personal bond.

After the custody period ended on Monday, they were produced before a special executive magistrate, where the Delhi Police sought an extension, Bar and Bench reported.

The magistrate allowed the request, extending their judicial custody by two more days.

Cockroach Janta Party leader Ratna Singh described the decision as a “mockery of the justice system”.

Abhik Chimni, the lawyer representing the CJP leaders, said that the proceedings were preventive in nature and that the court had itself observed that there was no need to send them to judicial custody.

The Cockroach Janta Party had earlier alleged that there was a “deliberate delay” in the release of Ranka and Das, accusing the authorities of prolonging their bail proceedings and rejecting bail sureties.

The allegations came after the Delhi Police registered a first information report against unidentified persons for allegedly threatening the special executive magistrate scheduled to hear the leaders’ bail applications.

On October 11, Jantar Mantar was denotified as a designated protest site in the national capital, based on recommendations from a committee constituted by the Union home ministry. The police order was reported on October 12.

The police order came against the backdrop of protests in the national capital by the Cockroach Janta Party, the Opposition parties and student groups to demand the resignation of Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar over concerns about the functioning of the Election Commission and its handling of the special intensive revision of electoral rolls.
