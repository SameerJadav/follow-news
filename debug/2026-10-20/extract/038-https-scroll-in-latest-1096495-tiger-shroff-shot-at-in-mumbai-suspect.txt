Actor Tiger Shroff escaped an assassination attempt in Mumbai on Sunday as unidentified miscreants shot at him.

Two persons, including Shroff’s driver, were injured in the attack that occurred at about 7 pm when the actor was leaving a football ground in Juhu, The Hindu reported.

The other person injured in the incident was a bystander, the police said. Both were being treated in hospital.

Videos of the incident widely shared online show several gun shots being fired at Shroff’s car as it left the venue.

Shroff was seen with bandages in videos of him meeting Deputy Chief Minister Eknath Shinde after the incident. However, the police said that the actor was not hit by any bullet and was safe.

The Rajasthan Police on Monday arrested three men in connection with the firing, ANI reported.

The suspects were travelling on the Pune-Jaipur Express when they were apprehended at Kota railway station following inputs from the Mumbai Police.

Two of the three suspects allegedly opened fire, while the third was a bike taxi driver, Kota Superintendent of Police Tejasvini Gautam said.

The Rajasthan Police had initially reported that a minor was among those detained, but the Mumbai Police later clarified that all three suspects were adults, The New Indian Express reported.

The assailants fired five to six rounds at Shroff’s car and the motive behind the attack remained under investigation, Mumbai Deputy Commissioner of Police Raj Tilak Roshan said. No weapon was recovered from the suspects, he added.

Earlier in the day, the Mumbai Police had detained a separate person in the matter and registered a first information report against three unidentified persons.

In a purported audio clip shared online, an alleged member of the Lawrence Bishnoi gang claimed responsibility for the attack, NDTV reported.

A man who identifies himself as Anil Pandit and claiming to be a member of the Lawrence Bishnoi gang said that Shroff was on the group’s target list as “he was not heeding our directions” and “was working with” actor Salman Khan, NDTV reported.

Bishnoi, who is in jail in Gujarat, has repeatedly threatened to kill Khan.

Deputy Commissioner of Police Mohit Garg declined to comment on the social media post by the Lawrence Bishnoi gang claiming responsibility for the attack, The Hindu reported.

Shinde condemned the incident and said that the police have formed several teams to track the suspects. He said that the state government will assess threats to other celebrities as well.

“…they [suspects] will be apprehended and given a response in their own language,” ANI quoted him as saying. “Our current priority is to keep Mumbai and its citizens safe.”

He added that the city has seen “large-scale gang wars” in the 1990s and the Mumbai Police handled the situation effectively by dealing with the perpetrators “in their own language”.

The All Indian Cine Workers Association said that gangsters were deliberately targeting persons related to the film industry to create an “atmosphere of fear” and “extort money” from celebrities.

The association added that the government and the police “have failed to stop these attacks and to protect the people of Bollywood”.

Since January 2018, Khan has been on the radar of the Lawrence Bishnoi gang. Khan is accused of hunting an endangered blackbuck in Kankani village near Jodhpur during the shooting of the film Hum Saath Saath Hain in 1998. The prosecution relied on the statements of the Bishnoi community, which lives in the area and protects the blackbuck.
