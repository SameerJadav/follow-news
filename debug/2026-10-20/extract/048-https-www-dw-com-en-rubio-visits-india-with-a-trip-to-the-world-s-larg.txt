US Secretary of State Marco Rubio kicks off a three-day visit to India on Monday in the finance hub of Mumbai, before heading to Jamnagar, home to a major oil refinery owned by industrial titan Reliance Industries.

Rubio's business-oriented visit comes as trade talks remain stalled amid continued ambiguity on punitive measures from the US over India's purchases of Russian oil.

Reliance is a top buyer of Venezuelan oil. The refinery can process 1.4 million barrels of crude oil a day, so it's a valuable exporter of diesel fuel for global markets. The refinery has also been a major buyer of Russian Urals crude, making it a significant part of India's energy relationship with Russia.

In March, Washington unveiled plans for a new oil refinery in Texas, backed by Reliance.

US President Donald Trump has put the value of the project at $300 billion (€258 billion) and presented it as a way to help reduce the US trade deficit with India. Whether the project moves forward, and on what terms, will be closely watched.

Since taking office for a second time in 2025, Trump has gone back and forth on tariff threats with India over what he considers to be unfair trade practices, along with India's continued purchases of Russian oil amid the war in Ukraine.

In February 2026, Trump announced a "trade deal" that slashed US tariffs on Indian goods, in exchange for India lowering trade barriers and halting purchases of Russian oil. Trump had claimed that Indian Prime Minister Narendra Modi promised to stop buying Russian oil. However, this has never been confirmed by New Delhi.

While maritime data from Kpler, a commodities and energy-market intelligence company, showed Indian refiners briefly diversified supply away from Russia to avoid tightening sanctions, India's deep reliance on discounted Russian crude continues to be a cornerstone of its energy strategy. Russia remains India's top supplier of crude oil.

In mid-September, Trump signed the "Lindsey O. Graham Sanctioning Russia and Iran Act," imposing 100% tariffs on top buyers of Russian oil and gas, which includes India.

However, last week, just days before the legislation was due to go into effect, Trump signed an executive order pausing the sanctions.

Before traveling to India, Rubio was in Uzbekistan for a meeting of the "C5+1" diplomatic platform comprising the US and five Central Asian countries. In remarks on the sidelines, Rubio said trade talks with India are taking place with the sanctions waivers in mind.

"We have had to waive sanctions over the past few months, looking at the global supplies, looking at global prices, and the impact it was having on other countries," he said.

"We're trying to finalize a trade deal with India right now. We're very close to doing that," Rubio added.

In an interview with Indian broadcaster NDTV, US Ambassador to India Sergio Gor said that Jamnagar could be a valuable source of diesel for the US.

Foreign policy analyst C Raja Mohan of Jindal Global University in India told DW that Rubio skipping a visit to the Indian capital in favor of Mumbai and Jamnagar should not be considered a diplomatic snub, as the refinery has international importance amid the global energy crunch brought on by the wars in Ukraine and the Middle East.

He said Jamnagar is an "important node in global trade" because it processes Russian oil and serves as a conduit for refined Russian petroleum products reaching Western markets.

Although Trump's sanctions reprieve offers New Delhi temporary relief, the underlying threat remains. Washington retains the option of imposing punitive tariffs, leaving Russian oil as a potential source of leverage in wider trade negotiations.

Former Indian ambassador to the US, Meera Shankar, cautioned against reading too much into Rubio's decision to skip Delhi amid political criticism in India.

"The focus of his current trip is on energy, and he is visiting the Reliance refinery in Jamnagar," she told DW. "The high price of fuel, particularly diesel, has become a political liability for the Trump administration, which wants to be seen taking action on this issue."

Diesel prices have skyrocketed around the world following the Iran war and the ensuing disruption of supply from the Middle East. In waiving Russia sanctions, Trump has tried to blame Ukrainian attacks on Russian oil facilities as being the cause of high prices.

Shankar pointed to Reliance's proposed Texas refinery, noting that the state is "one of the states in play" in the upcoming midterm elections in the US.

"Some forward movement on the investment would be welcome," she said.
