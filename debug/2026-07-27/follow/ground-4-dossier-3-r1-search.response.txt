The "Cockroach Janta Party" (CJP) initially called off their protests after the Indian government accepted their primary demand for the resignation of Education Minister Dharmendra Pradhan. Pradhan announced his resignation on Saturday, July 25, 2026.

In addition to Pradhan's resignation, the CJP's demands, established on May 16, included compensation of 10 million rupees ($104,000) for each student who died by suicide following the exam paper leak. They also sought greater legislative oversight of the educational system, gender equity in government roles, a fully independent media, and reforms to the electoral and political system, such as a 20-year ban on politicians switching parties before seeking election.

At a joint press conference on Saturday, July 25, 2026, both the CJP and cabinet members stated that the government had agreed to drop all legal measures, including lawsuits, against the student demonstrators. Health Minister JP Nadda also said that the central government would soon fix the compensation amount for the families of the children who died by suicide after question leaks.

However, by Monday, July 27, 2026, the CJP warned of resuming protests, stating there was a "breach of agreement" regarding no police action against protesters. They alleged that hundreds of students had been arrested or harassed in various states, and demanded the immediate withdrawal of all First Information Reports (FIRs) against protesters and the release of detained students, threatening renewed agitation if these conditions were not met by Tuesday.
