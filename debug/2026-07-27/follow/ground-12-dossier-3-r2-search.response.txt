At least 20 students have died by suicide following the discrediting of the medical school entrance exam, according to Agence France-Presse. The Cockroach Janta Party (CJP), a student movement, claims 21 student suicides are linked to the exam controversy, though Reuters could not independently verify this figure. As of June 19, 2026, the CJP founder Abhijeet Dipke stated that at least 11 NEET aspirants had died by suicide in the preceding week, with five deaths occurring within 48 hours. The Indian Express reported at least a dozen student suicides following the announcement of the exam's invalidation.

Regarding compensation, the Cockroach Janta Party (CJP) has demanded 10 million rupees (approximately $104,000) for each student who died by suicide. Union Minister JP Nadda announced that the government has agreed to provide suitable compensation for the families of the NEET aspirants who died by suicide. On July 22, 2026, activist Sonam Wangchuk stated that the government assured him it would "positively consider adequate compensation" for the affected families. However, the government has not yet commented on the deaths of the exam candidates.

The current status of legal measures and FIRs against student protesters across India is complex and evolving. The Cockroach Janta Party (CJP) suspended its protests after reaching an understanding with the government that all legal measures, including lawsuits against student demonstrators, would be dropped. Union Health Minister JP Nadda stated that the central government agreed not to pursue legal action against any of the protesters. Activist Sonam Wangchuk ended his 26-day hunger strike on July 23, 2026, after receiving a written assurance from the government that no cases or FIRs would be registered against those who protested peacefully.

However, on July 27, 2026, the CJP accused the Centre of violating this assurance, alleging that hundreds of students had been arrested or were facing police action in several states, particularly Assam, West Bengal, and Bihar, despite the agreement. In response, the Bihar government announced on July 27, 2026, that it would withdraw all FIRs and legal actions against individuals who participated in protests in Bihar prior to 6:00 PM on July 26, 2026, and release those arrested or detained. Rajya Sabha MP Kapil Sibal has offered legal support to students facing police action and pledged 1 crore rupees to establish a legal assistance platform. The Delhi High Court declined a Public Interest Litigation (PIL) seeking criminal proceedings against CJP protesters for alleged arson, assault, and property damage, instead permitting the petitioner to make a representation to the authorities. The Supreme Court reaffirmed on July 27, 2026, that the right to peaceful protest is "absolutely guaranteed" under the Constitution and that police excesses cannot be justified by the mere existence of an agitation.

Regarding the promised written agreement, Sonam Wangchuk stated that he ended his fast after the Indian government provided a written assurance that no cases would be filed against peaceful protesters. However, on July 27, 2026, CJP national spokesperson Ashutosh Ranka stated that the CJP was still awaiting the government's promised written agreement on the withdrawal of criminal cases against protesters and organizers. The CJP had submitted a draft agreement to Union ministers J P Nadda and Jitendra Singh, and it was agreed that the government would share a written agreement by Tuesday (July 28, 2026) after legal consultations. The CJP warned that if this written agreement was not received by Tuesday, they would be forced to resume their agitation.

The government's official response to the allegations of pellet gun use by Rapid Action Force (RAF) personnel on July 20, 2026, has been characterized by initial denials followed by internal investigations. The Delhi Police initially stated that claims of pellet gun use were "completely false and misleading" and that neither the police nor the government used such weapons during the protest.

However, reports and internal inquiries have contradicted these initial denials. On July 24, 2026, the Rapid Action Force (RAF), a specialized riot police force under the Central Reserve Police Force (CRPF), admitted to the "excessive use of force" following an internal investigation. The CRPF has since launched a detailed review and internal professional assessment into reports that RAF personnel fired pellet rounds during the July 20 'Sansad Chalo' march. Sources indicate that pellets were indeed used, and an internal inquiry reportedly found that an RAF personnel fired seven rounds from a pellet gun, with five rounds hitting protesters and two striking the ground.

As of July 27, 2026, the CRPF inquiry commission is expected to submit its report within two days, and the RAF personnel involved in deploying pellet guns and electric shock weapons have been identified. RAF Inspector General Seema Dhundhia reportedly directed personnel to avoid excessive force and strictly adhere to "Sensitive Policing" principles, criticizing actions that did not conform to training protocols. The Ministry of Home Affairs (MHA) has not yet issued an official statement or clarified its position on the use of disproportionate force. Congress leader Rahul Gandhi has questioned Union Home Minister Amit Shah regarding who ordered the use of "lethal force including pellet guns" on the protesting students.
