Beyond Sahil Lochab, other named student victims of the paper leaks and their specific circumstances include:

Pradeep Kumar: A 21-year-old who died by suicide after the NEET exam held on May 3, 2026, was voided due to allegations of a paper leak.

Sheikh Sana: A 19-year-old who died by suicide a day before the re-examination was to be held, writing "I am sorry" on a sheet of paper. Her family attributed her death to the fear and pressure of retaking the NEET exam after the initial test was voided.

Ritik Mishra: A 21-year-old who took his own life a day after news of the exam leak surfaced. It was his third attempt at the exam, and his father stated he believed he had performed well this time.

Begum: A student whose death occurred days after news of the medical entrance examination paper leak and the order for a retest. Her father, Rajaul Islam, believes the retest announcement changed everything for his daughter, who was confident she would clear the exam initially.

Chaitanya Aggarwal: A 19-year-old student who described the experience of being forced to retake the exam as "very demoralising mentally."

In addition to these named individuals, reports indicate that at least 12 prospective students, and later at least 14 students, died by suicide due to the mental stress and financial strain caused by the leak and subsequent re-examination. The Cockroach Janta Party (CJP) claims 21 student suicides were linked to the exam fiasco, though this number could not be independently verified by Reuters.

Regarding police action, while there are reports of clashes during protests where police used batons and tear gas, leaving several students injured, specific named student victims of police action beyond Sahil Lochab have not been identified in the provided information. The Supreme Court is scheduled to hear petitions alleging excessive police action against students during protests.
