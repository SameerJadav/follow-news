The identified leaders and prominent members of the youth activist group "Cockroach Janta Party" (CJP) include its founder, Abhijeet Dipke, and spokespersons Saurav Das, Ashutosh Ranka, Vaishnavi Gaur, Aafreen Nawaz, Deepak Baliyan, and Ratna Singh. Vijeta Dahiya was initially appointed as a spokesperson but was later removed.

Abhijeet Dipke, 30, is the founder and National Convenor of the CJP. He is a Boston University graduate and a former social media volunteer for the Aam Aadmi Party. Saurav Das is an investigative journalist and serves as the chief spokesperson. Ashutosh Ranka is an alumnus of IIT Kanpur and the London School of Economics, and a former consultant at McKinsey & Company.

Several Members of Parliament have also publicly supported the CJP movement, including Mahua Moitra (Trinamool Congress MP), Kirti Azad (Independent MP and former BJP member), and Akhilesh Yadav (former Chief Minister of Uttar Pradesh and Samajwadi Party leader).

Specific individuals within the National Testing Agency (NTA) or other relevant government bodies alleged to be involved in the NEET UG 2026 paper leak include Pune-based chemistry professor P. V. Kulkarni and biology professor Manisha Gurunath Mandhare, who were arrested by the Central Bureau of Investigation (CBI) on May 12, 2026. Kulkarni reportedly had access to question papers through his work as a translator for the NTA. Manisha Sanjay Havaldar, the headmistress of a Pune-based school, was arrested by the CBI on May 22, 2026, for allegedly leaking the physics question paper, having access to it as a subject expert appointed by the NTA.

The CBI also alleged that the leaked paper was circulated through a chain involving Shubham Khairnar, Yash Yadav, Mangilal Biwal, Vikas Biwal, and Dinesh Biwal. Mangilal Biwal allegedly obtained the paper from Yash Yadav for Rs 10 lakh and then sold it to several candidates for Rs 12 lakh each. Vikas Biwal, Mangilal's son, allegedly came into contact with Yash Yadav during coaching classes in Sikar, Rajasthan. Dinesh Biwal and Vikas Biwal are among 13 accused arrested by the CBI in connection with the leak.

The NTA has terminated the services of 47 officials following the paper leak controversies, with legal and criminal proceedings likely to be initiated against some, though their names, ranks, or specific reasons for termination have not been disclosed by the NTA. Union Education Minister Dharmendra Pradhan resigned from his post on July 25, 2026, following the controversy.

=== BLOCK q2 ===
The administration and security protocols for the NEET UG 2026 exam involved a multi-layered system with various officials and agencies. The National Testing Agency (NTA) is responsible for conducting the examination.

For the re-test on June 21, 2026, more than two lakh personnel were mobilized, including city coordinators, observers, centre superintendents, invigilators, district administration officials, and police personnel. Security personnel, local administration officials, and examination authorities jointly oversaw the three-tier security system at examination centers.

Union Home Secretary Govind Mohan reviewed the preparedness of state governments for the re-examination on June 15, 2026, with a focus on security arrangements and exam integrity. Directors General of the Central Reserve Police Force (CRPF), Central Industrial Security Force (CISF), Bureau of Civil Aviation Security, and the Director General of the National Testing Agency were present at this meeting. CRPF and CISF personnel were deployed to provide security for the exam papers during transportation to examination centers. The question papers were transported under high-security protocols, including GPS-enabled vehicles with police escorts, and digital locks on boxes.

Technology-driven monitoring included biometric attendance systems, facial recognition tools, and CCTV cameras for continuous surveillance, with live footage monitored by officials through a central control mechanism.
