The NEET UG 2026 paper leak first came to public light after the examination was conducted on May 3, 2026. A teacher from a coaching center in Sikar, Rajasthan, discovered a handwritten "guess paper" given to him by his landlord that was identical to the actual exam paper. He noticed this on the evening of May 3, 2026. The teacher initially approached the Udyog Nagar police station on May 4, 2026, but did not file a written complaint at that time. He later officially lodged a complaint with the National Testing Agency (NTA) on May 7, 2026, providing a 60-page handwritten PDF containing chemistry and biology questions that matched the actual exam. Initial reports and social media virality regarding alleged paper leaks from specific examination centers in various states began between May 6-8, 2026. The NTA initially denied a widespread leak on May 9, 2026. However, the exam was officially cancelled on May 12, 2026, following investigations that revealed significant overlaps between a pre-circulated guess paper and the actual question paper. The Director General of the NTA, Abhishek Singh, stated on May 12, 2026, that while the "entire paper was never available on Telegram or any other channel," some questions did match. The investigation was subsequently transferred to the Central Bureau of Investigation (CBI).

=== BLOCK q1 ===
The NEET UG 2026 paper leak was facilitated by several vulnerabilities and mechanisms within the examination system. Investigations revealed that the breach originated from the question paper setter, rather than the distribution chain, suggesting that the integrity of individuals involved in setting the papers is a major vulnerability. Before printing, question papers pass through stages of question setting, moderation, translation, and digitization, during which a small group of individuals has privileged access.

Specific vulnerabilities and mechanisms identified include:
  * Insider Vulnerability: The primary vulnerability is believed to lie with individuals who have privileged access to question papers before printing. IIT Madras Director V Kamakoti, a member of the task force on NTA examination reforms, stated that the biggest security risk is now the integrity of the people who set the question papers. Manisha Sanjay Havaldar, a school headmistress and NTA subject expert, was arrested for leaking the physics question paper by recalling it from memory and sharing it with a student.
  * Digital Circulation: A handwritten copy of the NEET UG 2026 question paper was scanned and converted into PDF files in Rajasthan by the father of an aspirant and then circulated to students in coaching centers. Digital distribution, using scanning and messaging applications, has become a prominent method for wide and rapid circulation.
  * Cybersecurity and Operational Blind Spots: The NEET-2026 leak exposed weaknesses beyond printed papers, including a teenager reportedly accessing candidate accounts to divert refund payments and an ethical hacker gaining access to super-administrative functions. AI-enabled surveillance and monitoring systems reportedly failed to detect warning signals related to widely circulated "guided papers."
  * Structural and Institutional Weaknesses of NTA: The National Testing Agency (NTA) functions as a society rather than a constitutional body, raising concerns about its autonomy, accountability, and administrative authority during crises. Its heavy reliance on contractual staff and officers on deputation affects long-term institutional expertise, secrecy management, and examination security systems.
  * Logistical and Security Vulnerabilities: Critical functions such as printing, transportation, and storage of question papers are often outsourced to private vendors. Each stage involving human handling increases the possibility of leaks. Conducting large-scale examinations for over 20 lakh candidates on a single day creates enormous operational risks, where a breach at one stage can compromise the entire national examination process. Weak examination centers and dependence on private testing centers also contribute to technical failures and organized cheating networks.

Union Education Minister Dharmendra Pradhan admitted there was a "breach in the command chain."
