​The Domino Effect: How the NEET crisis unfolded into a ministers resignationNew Delhi: For nearly two months, the Narendra Modi government stood firmly behind Union Education Minister Dharmendra Pradhan despite mounting criticism over the NEET-UG 2026 paper leak. Even as protests intensified, Parliament's Monsoon Session witnessed repeated disruptions and the Centre carried out a sweeping overhaul of the National Testing Agency (NTA), the official line remained unchanged: Pradhan would stay.As late as July 24, government sources insisted his resignation would amount to "running away" from the problem rather than fixing it.Less than 24 hours later, Pradhan resigned, becoming the highest-profile political casualty of India's biggest examination controversy in recent years.Timeline: How the crisis unfoldedDateDevelopmentMay 3NTA conducts NEET-UG 2026 for over 20 lakh candidates; irregularities surface almost immediately.Early MayCentre hands the matter to the CBI, cancels the examination, announces a re-test and says NEET will shift to computer-based testing from next year.May 15-16Chief Justice of India Surya Kant's remarks trigger the formation of the Cockroach Janta Party (CJP) as a satirical movement.June 6CJP holds its first protest at Jantar Mantar demanding Pradhan's resignation.June 21NEET-UG re-examination conducted.June 28Sonam Wangchuk begins an indefinite hunger strike at Jantar Mantar.July 16Re-examination results declared.July 18Wangchuk is shifted to Safdarjung Hospital ahead of the planned Parliament march.July 20'Sansad Chalo' march coincides with the opening of Parliament's Monsoon Session; clashes erupt with Delhi Police.July 21Centre announces fast-track examination reforms as the restructuring of the NTA gathers pace.July 22Union Ministers J.P. Government publicly rules out Pradhan's resignation.July 25Pradhan submits his resignation to Prime Minister Narendra Modi.From examination controversy to political crisisThe crisis traces back to May 3, when the NTA conducted NEET-UG 2026 for more than 20 lakh medical aspirants. Allegations of a paper leak surfaced almost immediately. The Centre responded within days by ordering a CBI investigation, cancelling the examination and announcing a nationwide re-test. It also unveiled a structural reform by shifting NEET to computer-based testing from next year to reduce the scope for future leaks.The re-examination was held on June 21 and results were declared on July 16. By then, the controversy had moved well beyond allegations of a paper leak. Questions over the NTA's credibility, administrative accountability and the integrity of India's examination system had transformed what began as an examination controversy into one of the biggest governance challenges faced by the Education Ministry in recent years.This was not new terrain for Pradhan. During the 2024 NEET-UG controversy, he had maintained there was "no evidence of a paper leak" and described the NTA as "very credible". The position came under intense scrutiny then as well, eventually leading to a Supreme Court-directed re-test for more than 1,500 candidates. Unlike 2024, however, the 2026 crisis ultimately claimed the minister's office.From satire to street movementThe protest movement that eventually forced Pradhan's exit did not begin as one. On May 15, Chief Justice of India Surya Kant made remarks widely interpreted as comparing unemployed youth to "cockroaches". The following day, political communications strategist Abhijeet Dipke, formerly associated with the Aam Aadmi Party, founded the Cockroach Janta Party (CJP) as a satirical response.As anger over NEET and separate CBSE on-screen marking issues spread among students, CJP gradually evolved from an online campaign into an organised protest platform. Its first demonstration at Jantar Mantar was held on June 6.The movement gained national traction on June 28 when education reformer Sonam Wangchuk began an indefinite hunger strike at the same venue, drawing opposition leaders, public figures and sustained media attention.On July 18, with Wangchuk 20 days into his fast, Delhi Police shifted him to Safdarjung Hospital, a move protest organisers described as an attempt to weaken the agitation ahead of the planned Parliament march.The crackdown and a parallel reckoning inside the NTAThe 'Sansad Chalo' march on July 20, the opening day of Parliament's Monsoon Session, became the largest mobilisation of the agitation. Thousands of protesters marched towards Parliament under prohibitory orders imposed under Section 163 of the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita.Delhi Police used tear gas and baton charges after alleging protesters breached barricades and pelted stones.
