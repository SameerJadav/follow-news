# ‘Cockroaches’ call off protests after talks with India govt, minister’s resignation

CJP spokespersons and Indian ministers address a joint press conference held at the Constitution Club of India in New Delhi on Saturday. Photo: Collected

A  A

The Cockroach Janta Party (CJP) has called off protests “in good faith” and the government has agreed to drop all legal measures including lawsuits against the student demonstrators, both sides said at a joint press conference on Saturday afternoon.

At the press conference, cabinet members also said they would work on the students’ demand for Tk1 crore compensation for the families of those who took their leaves in anguish over the medical entry question paper leak – a grave tragedy that started off the protests.

The government has agreed to consider the remaining demands of the movement after the resignation of Dharmendra Pradhan as education minister, CJP Spokesperson Saurav Das said at the presser held at the Constitution Club of India in New Delhi.

The government will formally announce the withdrawal of legal action in writing on Tuesday, Saurav Das said, adding another round of talks will be held with the government in four weeks.

At the presser, Health Minister JP Nadda said that the central government has agreed not to take legal action against any of the protestors. Nadda also said that the Centre will soon fix the compensation amount for the families of the children who died by suicide after question leaks.

Shortly earlier, Dharmendra Pradhan submitted his resignation letter to Prime Minister Narendra Modi. In a letter, shared on X, Pradhan said he was saddened by the sequence of events in the last ten days and that the decision was taken in interest of the unity of the country.

In their first reaction after Pradhan’s resignation, the Cockroach Janta Party posted on social media: “Cockroaches won, Democracy won”.

**Candle light march planned Sunday**

Earlier, the Cockroach Janta Party called for a candle light march on Sunday in solidarity with the victims of police brutality. It was not clear whether the march would continue following Saturday’s presser and talks between the protesters and the government.

While the third round of talks were ongoing, stone-pelting was reported in at least three Bihar districts on Sunday, as protestors clashed with the police over the NEET paper leak. JJD chief and Lalu Yadav’s elder son Tej Pratap Yadav was detained by the police as he participated in a protest.

Prime Minister Narendra Modi late on Friday thanked everyone for the responses and positive suggestions received to his earlier video message, where he announced the government’s decision to bring a strict law to punish those involved in paper leaks. In the video message, Modi had said that a new bill incorporating provisions for strong action against paper leaks will be introduced in the ongoing session of Parliament next week.

This comes after the Union Cabinet approved a draft bill to ensure stricter punishments – 10 years’ jail for paper leaks, fine up to 10 crore rupees. The draft bill was approved in a cabinet meeting chaired by the prime minister at the Parliament House complex. The bill seeks an amendment to the Public Examinations (Prevention of Unfair Means) Act, 2024 and proposes a minimum punishment of five years’ imprisonment for individuals involved in paper leaks and/or a fine.
