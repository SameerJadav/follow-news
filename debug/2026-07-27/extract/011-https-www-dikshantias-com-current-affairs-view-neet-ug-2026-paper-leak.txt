**_Why in News?_**

The National Testing Agency (NTA) cancelled the NEET-UG 2026 examination after allegations of a major paper leak and circulation of a highly accurate “guess paper.” The investigation has now been transferred to the Central Bureau of Investigation (CBI).

The incident has once again raised serious concerns regarding transparency, accountability, and institutional weaknesses within India’s examination system. It has also renewed attention on the incomplete implementation of the recommendations made by the Dr. K. Radhakrishnan Committee (2024).

**_Key Challenges in India’s Examination Ecosystem_**

**_1. Structural and Institutional Weaknesses_**

**_NTA’s Limited Institutional Status_**

Unlike the UPSC, which is a constitutional body, the NTA functions as a society registered under the Societies Registration Act, 1860. This creates concerns regarding autonomy, accountability, and administrative authority during major crises.

**_Absence of Permanent Staff Structure_**

The NTA depends heavily on contractual staff and officers on deputation. The lack of a permanent professional cadre affects long-term institutional expertise, secrecy management, and examination security systems.

**_Risk of Single-Day Mega Exams_**

Conducting large-scale examinations for over 20 lakh candidates on a single day creates enormous operational risks. Any breach at one stage can compromise the entire national examination process.

Although the NTA introduced measures such as GPS-tracked transportation, sealed paper handling, police escorts, and CCTV monitoring under a “Zero Error, Zero Tolerance” policy, repeated paper leak incidents exposed major gaps in implementation.

**_2. Logistical and Security Vulnerabilities_**

**_Outsourcing of Sensitive Processes_**

Critical functions like printing, transportation, and storage of question papers are often outsourced to private vendors. Each stage involving human handling increases the possibility of leaks.

**_Weak Examination Centres_**

Several exams are conducted in private schools and institutions lacking proper security infrastructure such as functioning CCTV systems, signal jammers, and trained invigilators.

**_Risks Associated with OMR-Based Tests_**

While OMR-based exams are economical for large-scale testing, the physical transportation of answer sheets creates additional opportunities for tampering and manipulation.

**_3. Technological Challenges_**

**_Digital Security Risks_**

Computer-Based Testing (CBT) reduces the risk of physical paper leaks but introduces cybersecurity threats such as hacking, remote access manipulation, and digital fraud.

**_Inadequate Digital Infrastructure_**

India still lacks sufficient government-owned high-capacity digital examination centres. Dependence on private testing centres often results in technical failures and organized cheating networks.

The NTA currently has the capacity to conduct computer-based tests for only around 1.5 lakh candidates in a single shift. Multi-shift exams create additional concerns regarding fairness and score normalization.

**_Advanced Malpractice Techniques_**

Cheating networks increasingly use encrypted communication platforms, dark web channels, and advanced electronic devices such as Bluetooth-enabled gadgets, making detection difficult.

**_4. Socio-Economic Pressures_**

**_Coaching Industry Influence_**

The expanding coaching industry has created a highly competitive “win-at-all-costs” environment. This increases incentives for unethical practices and paper leak syndicates.

**_Extreme Competition_**

With nearly 23 lakh students competing for around 1 lakh MBBS seats, examinations have become extremely high-stakes events. This desperation fuels demand for illegal shortcuts.

**_Financial Inequality_**

The huge cost difference between government and private medical colleges turns NEET into a make-or-break examination for many middle-class and economically weaker families.

**_Decline in Ethical Standards_**

Growing acceptance of corruption and willingness to pay large sums for leaked papers weakens meritocracy and public faith in institutions.

**_Ethical Concerns_**

**_Impact on Women and First-Generation Learners_**

Repeated cancellations and delays disproportionately affect women and first-generation learners, especially those facing social pressure, financial limitations, or restrictions on mobility.

**_Loss of Public Trust_**

The credibility of public institutions depends on fairness and merit-based opportunities. Examination failures erode trust in the state and democratic governance.

**_Violation of Social Justice_**

Paper leaks unfairly disadvantage rural and economically weaker students who lack access to illegal networks or financial resources.

**_Psychological Burden_**

Continuous cycles of preparation, cancellation, and uncertainty cause severe mental stress among students, affecting the country’s demographic potential.
