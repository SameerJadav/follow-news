The Cockroach Janta Party (CJP; lit. 'Cockroach People's Party'), also known as the Cockroach movement,[1][2] is an Indian youth-based frivolous political party founded on 16 May 2026 by Abhijeet Dipke, a political communications strategist and activist.

It was created in response to remarks made on 15 May 2026 by Surya Kant, the Chief Justice of India, in which he referred to certain activists and unemployed youth as "cockroaches" and "parasites of society", sparking widespread public reaction.[3] Kant later clarified that his remarks referred to people obtaining fraudulent degrees and did not target India's youth, whom he called "the pillars of a developed India".[4] The movement's name is a wordplay parodying the ruling party in India, the Bharatiya Janata Party.[5]

The party achieved significant popularity online, rapidly gaining a large following across social media platforms shortly after its formation in May 2026.[6] The party issued a five-point manifesto relating to the conduct of parliament members and of election commissioners, reservation of seats for women in politics, and media licensing. Beginning in June, the party led protests in Delhi and across the country over the fallout from irregularities in that year's entrance examinations for undergraduate medical programs. The protests culminated in talks with the central government and the resignation of Dharmendra Pradhan, the Indian Minister of Education, on 25 July 2026.[7] The education minister's resignation was considered a major victory for the Indian youth movement.[8]

Alongside its online presence, the movement organises protests and community service activities across India.[5][9][10] The movement campaigns on broader societal, economic, and political issues affecting Indian youth. It is not registered as a political party with the Election Commission of India.[11]

The Ministry of External Affairs (MEA) has clarified and dismissed allegations regarding foreign funding related to the CJP. On 25 July 2026, the MEA stated that neither the Indian government nor its intelligence agencies have any information regarding foreign funds linked to the CJP and its protests.[12][13][14]

The origin of the movement are tied to a contempt plea filed in the Supreme Court of India against concerning a delay by the Delhi High Court in conferring a senior advocate designation. A two-judge bench led by the Chief Justice of India (CJI), Surya Kant, and Justice Joymalya Bagchi refused to hear the case, remarking that the court was waiting for an appropriate case to direct a Central Bureau of Investigation investigation into lawyers practising under fake degrees.[15]

While reprimanding the petitioner's lawyer for unprofessional conduct regarding the language used on social media and the use of frivolous litigation targeting the judiciary system,[15][16] the CJI drew parallels to unemployed individuals who turn to social media, media, and public activism to attack government institutions. His comparison of such individuals to "cockroaches" and "parasites" drew widespread backlash.[17][18]

There are already parasites of society who attack the system, and you want to join hands with them? There are youngsters like cockroaches; they don't get any employment, and they don't have any place in a profession. Some of them become media, some of them become social media, some of them become RTI activists, some of them become other activists, and they start attacking everyone... And you people file contempt petitions.[19]

The CJI later issued a clarification stating that his remarks had been misquoted and that he was specifically criticising individuals who had entered "legal, media, social media, and other noble professions" using fake degrees, and added that he held India's youth in high regard.[20][21] Despite the clarification, the controversial "cockroach" label stuck with youth commentators.[22]

Abhijeet Dipke, a political communications strategist, launched the "Cockroach Janta Party" (CJP) online on 16 May 2026 as a satire of the political establishment. The party's name is a references to the CJI's remarks, as well as a parody of the ruling Bharatiya Janata Party. The CJP's name roughly translates to "Cockroach People's Party", paralleling the BJP's "Indian People's Party".[9][23] The CJP's website went live under the tagline "Voice of the Lazy & Unemployed", describing itself as a "platform for all the 'cockroaches' out there".[24]

Dipke said that the CJP is not affiliated with any political organisation and intends to serve as a platform for young Indians who feel left out of mainstream politics.[25] He stated in an interview with India Today that the CJI's comments had been particularly hurtful because they came from the custodian of the Constitution, who is tasked with protecting freedom of expression.[26]
