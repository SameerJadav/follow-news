=== STORY 4 [india] — US suspends PERM green card program for major IT firms, prompting concern and official reaction in India ===

[a0] Channel News Asia <https://www.channelnewsasia.com/asia/us-green-card-crackdown-clouds-indian-tech-workers-american-dream-6446536> (FULL TEXT)
Four Indian IT giants are among eight tech firms suspended from a key US permanent residency programme. Analysts tell CNA the immediate business impact may be limited, but workers face greater uncertainty.

BENGALURU: The Trump administration on Thursday (Oct 8) suspended eight major technology companies from a key United States programme that allows employers to sponsor foreign workers for permanent residency, citing alleged abuse of the immigration system.

The companies include four Indian IT giants - Tata Consultancy Services (TCS), Infosys, Wipro and HCLTech - as well as Microsoft, Adobe, Cognizant and Capgemini.

US Labor Secretary Keith Sonderling said authorities would stop accepting new applications and processing pending ones under the Permanent Labor Certification (PERM) programme.

Under PERM, employers must first demonstrate that qualified American workers are unavailable for the jobs, according to the US Department of Labor.

India's Ministry of External Affairs said in a statement on Friday that the restrictions did not “advance the shared ambitions” of the two countries. It added that the move could affect eligible workers' green card applications but not existing H-1B visa holders.

Indian IT industry body NASSCOM stressed that the programme concerns permanent immigration and is distinct from the H-1B visa programme for temporary workers, which is unaffected by the suspension.

Analysts told CNA the immediate impact on Indian IT firms would be limited, but prolonged restrictions could raise staffing costs and disrupt workers' plans to settle permanently in the US.

“I see this as a bigger talent retention concern than an immediate business disruption (for Indian IT firms),” said Achyuta Ghosh, executive research leader at analyst firm HFS Research.

In a statement filed with the local stock exchange on Friday, TCS said that it had submitted fewer than 10 PERM applications over the past two years and did not expect the suspension to affect its workforce strategy or client engagements.

It reiterated its plans to hire 15,000 additional employees in the US over five years.

Ghosh said Indian IT firms would likely focus their US hiring on client-facing consulting, architecture and specialised engineering, while subcontractors in the country offered “more immediate flexibility”.

He expects companies to use more subcontractors and selectively hire locally in the US over the next six to 12 months, while reassessing how much US-based work can be shifted offshore.

Pareek Jain, CEO at EIIR Trend, a research firm that tracks the global engineering and IT services industry, estimated that hiring in the US was 25 to 50 per cent more expensive than sending Indian employees there, depending on the role.

Sarbojit Mallick, chief business officer at recruitment platform Instahyre, said the biggest impact on Indian workers was the loss of a "predictable path to permanent residency" in the US, rather than immediate visa cancellations.

“I don't expect a mass return (to India) over the next six to 12 months,” he said, adding that prolonged uncertainty could gradually redirect experienced Indian tech workers towards opportunities at home and other global hubs.

Overseas postings have long helped Indian IT companies attract and retain talent despite relatively low entry-level salaries, said Jain of EIIR Trend.

“If the option of global (job) mobility is gone, it will be difficult for them to attract good talent (at home),” he added.

The companies may have to develop opportunities in other overseas markets instead, he said.

Ghosh said Singapore and the United Arab Emirates could attract some experienced Indian professionals, but their tech job markets were “considerably smaller” than India's.

Mallick said that Singapore could attract experienced Indian professionals in AI, fintech and tech leadership, while Malaysia could benefit from talent in cloud, cybersecurity and engineering.

He said returning professionals should not assume that years of US-based work experience will translate into an equivalent job in India.

“India can absorb specialised talent, but not necessarily every returnee at comparable seniority and pay.”

Mallick said opportunities were strongest in global capability centres (GCCs), product engineering, AI infrastructure, cybersecurity and cloud computing.

The rapid adoption of AI is widening the divide between demand for specialists and generalist developers, Mallick said.

He cited industry data showing recruitment for AI and machine-learning roles rose 45 per cent in the financial year 2025-26, while overall IT hiring remained flat.

“Demand for specialists does not guarantee enough equivalent roles for generalist developers and traditional IT services professionals,” Mallick said.

India on Friday said that the United States’ decision to suspend several information technology companies from the permanent labour certification programme goes against advancing the shared ambitions of the two countries.

Permanent labour certification is a process that employers must complete before filing petitions for most employment-based green cards. Under the programme, employers must establish that there are no qualified, willing and available US workers for a particular job.

Cognizant, Capgemini, Microsoft, Adobe and India-based information technology companies Infosys, Tata Consultancy Services, Wipro and HCL are expected to be affected by the Donald Trump administration’s decision.

The Indian external affairs ministry said that the permanent labour certification programme is different from the H-1B visa programme, which allows companies in the US to temporarily employ foreign workers for special occupations.

The suspension of permanent labour certification applications does not affect the validity of existing H-1B visas or the persons who hold the visa and their dependents, it said.

However, it said that there could be “some impact” on the Green Card process of eligible employees of the affected companies. A green card allows a foreign citizen to live and work permanently in the US.

Over the past few years, Indians have constituted the majority of H-1B visa holders. Indians comprised 71% of all H-1B visas issued by the US in the financial year 2023-’24.

The ministry further described US Vice President JD Vance’s remarks on the decision as “unwarranted”.

Vance, who announced the new measures with US Labor Secretary Keith Sonderling, said that the H-1B programme was meant to allow US companies to hire “the best of the best from outside the United States of America for positions that are completely impossible to fill with American workers”.

He added that companies should hire “great American workers” instead of “foreign indentured servants”.

New Delhi responded by saying that “such descriptions ignore the fact” that Indian professionals in the US are “highly educated and skilled contributors” to its economy and innovation ecosystem.

“Indeed, the history of the United States itself has been shaped by generations of immigrants whose labour, enterprise and innovation have contributed to its growth and prosperity,” the ministry said. “Resorting to terminology that carries painful historical and colonial legacy connotations is deeply offensive.”

The ministry also said that talent mobility “adds value” to both economies.

“While it creates opportunities for Indians, it equally helps US companies with cutting-edge talent, innovation, research, productivity, competitiveness and job creation, besides creating shareholder wealth in the US,” it added.

The BJP won the Nagaon Lok Sabha by-election in Assam, with the Congress being pushed to the third place. The result has drawn sharp criticism from Raijor Dal chief Akhil Gogoi, a former ally of the Congress, who called the defeat a “tragedy for Assam's anti-BJP forces”.

Akhil Gogoi also blamed Assam Congress president Gaurav Gogoi for the party's poor performance and called for his immediate resignation. His remarks come as the Congress struggles in a constituency where Muslims account for more than 55% of voters.

Reacting to the by-election results, Raijor Dal MLA Akhil Gogoi pointed to the sharp fall in the Congress's vote count. He noted that the constituency had previously been held by the Congress, but a candidate's move to the BJP led to the by-election.

“Regarding the by-election in Nagaon—it was originally a Congress seat, but the candidate switched to the BJP, which is why this by-election is taking place,” he was quoted as saying by news agency ANI.

Akhil Gogoi said the Congress had won the seat by a margin of 2.5 lakh votes in the previous election but was now struggling to reach even two lakh votes.

“This is essentially a minority-dominated seat,” he said, adding that the Congress's minority vote share had fallen sharply.

“The most significant development this time is that the minority vote share for Congress has plummeted from 25% to just 5%. The BJP is poised to win this minority-dominated seat,” he said.

Calling the outcome a historic setback for the Congress, Akhil Gogoi said Gaurav Gogoi had failed in his role as state party president.

“This is a historic defeat for the Congress,” he said. “I believe the Congress President, Gaurav Gogoi, has failed completely. In my opinion, Gaurav Gogoi should resign from the post of State Congress President this very day.”

Union Home Minister Amit Shah welcomed the BJP-led NDA's performance in the by-elections in West Bengal, Assam and Puducherry, as well as recent local body polls in Rajasthan and Tripura.

In a post shared on X, Shah said the results reflected continued public support for the BJP under Prime Minister Narendra Modi's leadership.

“The BJP, under the leadership of PM Shri Narendra Modi Ji, continues to win elections one after another despite the lies and disinformation campaigns run by Rahul Gandhi and the INDI Alliance,” Shah said.

He claimed that the results showed voters had rejected the opposition alliance.

“The NDA’s victories in the by-elections in West Bengal, Assam and Puducherry, and the recently held local body polls in Rajasthan and Tripura, demonstrate that the people have completely rejected the INDI Alliance,” he said.

Shah also accused opposition parties of questioning institutions after electoral defeats, saying voters had recognised their “true character”.

On Thursday night, Meta took down a viral video that purported to show Bharatiya Janata Party candidate Shankharav Sarkar going behind the screen of a polling booth where an electronic voting machine was placed during the bye-election for West Bengal’s Rejinagar Assembly constituency on October 6.

The video, posted by Golam Nabi Azad, the BJP’s principal opponent in the seat, was removed in compliance with a legal request from Indian authorities, according to a message that Meta overlaid on the post. The Aam Janata Unnayan Party leader in his Facebook post had described the bye-poll as an “unprecedented farce in the name of democracy”.

On Tuesday, Scroll reporters in Rejinagar witnessed the police turning away Muslim voters from some polling stations. Opposition workers alleged that they were intimidated in the lead-up to the vote and some independent journalists said that the social media pages through which they disseminated their videos had also been blocked.

When the results of the bye-election in Rejinagar were announced on Friday, Sarkar was declared the winner with 1,29,914 votes, while Azad received 54,098 votes. In Nandigram, the other constituency in West Bengal in which a bye-poll was held, Hasirani Rath of the BJP beat Milan Pradhan of the Congress by 1,22,753 votes.

The video posted by Azad purports to show BJP candidate Sarkar entering a library in Rejinagar’s Majlishpur village, where a polling station was set up on October 6. The clip shows him greeting voters in the queue with “Jai Shri Ram” and telling paramilitary personnel stationed there that he is “the BJP candidate”.

The video purports to show Sarkar going behind the screen of the booth, after which a beep is heard. The screen behind which the machine is placed identifies the polling station as “12 Majlishpur Rural Library” and the Assembly seat as “70 Rejinagar”.

According to the Election Commission’s model code of conduct, candidates can visit polling stations and inspect the process of voting if they have a valid pass. It is unclear from the video if the BJP leader was doing that on the day of the bye-election in Rejinagar.

On Friday afternoon, the West Bengal unit of the Congress party also posted the video on its X account, alleging that it depicted “vote chori” or vote stealing.

Former Chief Election Commissioner SY Quraishi told Scroll that candidates can, at best, look at the electronic voting machine from a distance, but cannot go up to it.

“Nobody can touch the machine except the voter,” he added. “If a candidate has any concerns about the machine, he can complain to the presiding officer. He can’t go to the machine and ‘test’ it himself.”

OP Rawat, another former chief election commissioner, shared his view.

Sarkar, the BJP candidate, did not respond to calls and messages seeking a clarification. Neither did Harshal Pancholi, the Madhya Pradesh cadre Indian Administrative Officer whom the Election Commission appointed as the general observer for the Rejinagar bye-poll. The article will be updated if they respond.

The controversy could have been settled if Sarkar had gone up to the electronic voting machine to cast his own vote.

In his election affidavit, Sarkar had stated that he was registered as a voter in the Baharampur Assembly constituency, about 31 km away from Majlishpur. Scroll cross-checked this with the final voter list for that seat published by the Election Commission in April, before the Assembly elections, and found his name on it.

Technically, it is possible that the BJP candidate shifted his vote from Baharampur to Rejinagar between April and October. But a search with his electors photo identity card number on the Election Commission’s portal for voters to find their names on the electoral roll shows that Sarkar is still a voter in Baharampur.
