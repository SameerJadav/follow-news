Yes, there were specific prior exam paper leaks in India that directly contributed to the current widespread student unrest and demands for reform. The current protests, primarily triggered by the National Eligibility cum Entrance Test (NEET-UG) 2026 paper leak, are fueled by a history of such incidents.

Notably, the NEET exam paper itself was leaked in both 2024 and 2025, according to a spokesperson for the Cockroach Janata Party (CJP), a movement leading the current protests. These repeated leaks of the same high-stakes medical entrance exam contributed to the "Molotov cocktail of frustration" among young people.

Furthermore, the Public Examinations Act, a law governing public examinations, was initially enacted in 2024 following "similar NEET exam leaks". This indicates that prior leaks directly led to legislative action aimed at strengthening the examination system, a form of reform. The current government has now proposed amendments to this 2024 law to increase penalties for those involved in exam irregularities.

Other significant past leaks that have undermined the integrity of India's examination system and likely contributed to the broader sentiment of unrest include:
*   The All India Pre-Medical Test (AIPMT) in 2015, which was entirely cancelled by the Supreme Court due to a large-scale leak.
*   The Staff Selection Commission (SSC) Combined Graduate Level (CGL) examination in 2017, which was marred by leak allegations and sparked massive protests and a CBI inquiry.
*   The Central Board of Secondary Education (CBSE) Class 12 Economics and Class 10 Mathematics papers in 2018.

The persistence of these paper leaks is attributed to high-stakes competition for limited seats and jobs, making aspirants vulnerable to an "education mafia". The current movement demands accountability, education reforms, and political action, with the resignation of Education Minister Dharmendra Pradhan being a key demand, which he fulfilled on July 25, 2026.
