The investigation into the NEET UG 2025 question paper compromise is ongoing. During the NEET-UG 2026 investigation, the Central Bureau of Investigation (CBI) found evidence that the same racket responsible for the 2026 leak had also compromised the 2025 question paper. This information comes from investigative and agency-linked reporting during the 2026 CBI probe.

On May 18, 2026, the CBI arrested Shivraj Motegaonkar, owner of Renukai Chemistry Classes Coaching Institute in Latur, Maharashtra, in connection with the paper leak. The CBI informed a New Delhi court that Motegaonkar had received the chemistry questions on April 23, 2026. Manisha Sanjay Havaldar, a headmistress from Pune, was arrested by the CBI on May 22, 2026, for allegedly leaking the physics question paper. She reportedly recalled the questions from memory and shared them with a student, who then circulated them via a messaging app.

The National Testing Agency (NTA) launched a platform on April 23, 2025, for students and the public to report suspicious activities and claims related to the NEET UG 2025 exam, following issues during the NEET UG 2024 exam. The NTA warned against fake claims by individuals or groups promising leaked question papers or special access to exam materials.

=== BLOCK q1 ===
The Radhakrishnan Committee, formed in June 2024 after the NEET-UG 2024 paper leak, submitted a report in October 2024 with 101 recommendations aimed at transforming the National Testing Agency (NTA) into a "nimble, zero-error, adaptive and integrative process". The committee's vision was to address vulnerabilities across the entire examination ecosystem, rather than treating paper leaks as isolated incidents.

Specific findings and recommendations included:
  *   **Overhaul of NTA:** The report argued that technology alone would not solve NTA's problems and called for stronger institutional capacity, reduced dependence on outsourced agencies, and a recast Governing Body as an "empowered and accountable Apex Body" headed by a Director General. It also proposed creating 10 dedicated verticals within the agency, including Nation-wide Testing Centre Infrastructure, Information Security, Vigilance and Forensics, and Research and Development.
  *   **Technological Reforms:** A central recommendation was Computer-assisted Secure Pen-and-Paper Testing (CPPT) to eliminate breaches during printing, storage, and transportation of question papers. This involved transmitting encrypted question papers electronically to secure servers at examination centers for printing shortly before the exam. Another model suggested delivering the question paper via computer to each candidate, with responses recorded on an OMR sheet. The committee also proposed a digital authentication framework called DIGI-EXAM for candidate verification.
  *   **Security Measures:** Recommendations covered question paper preparation, printing and transportation, candidate authentication, CCTV monitoring, invigilation, and custody of answer sheets. It also suggested better center allocation policies to detect suspicious patterns. The committee recommended that NTA seal test centers in the presence of district administration and police before the exam and guard them until de-sealed, with CCTV monitoring at both center and district levels.
  *   **Examination System Reforms (General):** The committee advocated for multi-session testing over several days or weeks, and multi-stage testing (similar to JEE Main) for NEET UG. It also suggested introducing objective-type exams alongside essay-type examinations, reserving one-third of marks for classroom work, dividing courses into self-contained sections, abolishing grace marks, and incorporating viva-voce exams for professional courses.
  *   **Coordination and Infrastructure:** The panel proposed closer coordination with state governments, recommending the creation of state- and district-level committees to identify secure examination centers, coordinate with police, supervise paper transportation and storage, inspect centers, and respond to security incidents. Government institutions were to be preferred over private centers. It also suggested setting up a testing center in each district and mobile testing centers for rural or remote areas.

The Parliamentary Standing Committee on Education, in a report made public in June 2026, found that the NTA had accumulated a surplus of approximately Rs 448 crore over six years, recommending these funds be used to strengthen NTA's capacity and oversight of vendors. The committee noted a striking contrast between this surplus and NTA's overwhelmingly non-permanent workforce. It also found that NTA had not been submitting annual reports to Parliament despite requests and recommended a nationwide blacklist of exam vendors responsible for irregularities. In December 2025, the Parliamentary Standing Committee stated that NTA's performance had "not inspired much confidence". The committee also firmly backed pen-and-paper exams over computer-based testing in December 2025, directly opposing the Radhakrishnan Committee's recommendation to shift towards CBT, citing UPSC's leak-proof record.

Regarding the adoption of these recommendations, a member of the Radhakrishnan Committee, Pankaj Bansal, stated in May 2026 that "all 95 recommendations have to be implemented" and that computer-based testing "should eliminate 95 per cent of the problems". However, as of July 2026, NTA had not publicly stated that the recommendation to physically seal examination centers had been implemented. Multi-session and multi-stage testing for NEET UG were also not implemented by NTA until the NEET UG 2026 was leaked, after which the Education Ministry announced NEET UG would transition to online (CBT) mode from 2027. IIT Madras Director V Kamakoti, a member of the new task force on NTA reforms, stated in July 2026 that many of the Radhakrishnan Committee's recommendations focused on strengthening security during printing, transportation, and distribution of question papers were implemented before NEET-UG 2026, and that the leak did not happen in distribution.

=== BLOCK q2 ===
Sheikh Irshad Mansoori, a 25-year-old protester, was discharged from Lady Hardinge Hospital on July 24, 2026, four days after undergoing surgery to remove pellets from his face and neck. He still had a bloodshot left eye and healing wounds on his forehead, cheeks, and nose. Doctors informed him that three pellets had lodged close to sensitive areas: one near a major nerve and blood vessel in his neck, another near the muscle controlling movement of his left eye, and a third inside the facial cavity near his nose. The surgery removed pellets from his face and neck, but he still has stitches in those areas and inside his mouth. Mansoori stated he can see properly now, though his eye remains red from the surgery. Earlier reports indicated that surgeons were unable to extract a pellet close to a major blood vessel in his neck due to the risk involved, and that he might require another surgery. Mansoori also claimed some pellets are still lodged in his body, posing a risk of infection, and he is undergoing treatment at a private hospital in Delhi.

An unnamed 28-year-old journalist with Outlook magazine was also injured during the July 20 protest. He sustained at least 30 small, round wounds on his back, elbow, and arm, which his medical report from Safdarjung Hospital described as "suggestive of pellet entrance wounds". Doctors told him that removing the pellets was not necessary as none appeared to be lodged near vital organs, but he is seeking a second opinion. The journalist narrowly avoided being hit in the chest and face by turning away from the firing officer, resulting in severe injuries to his arm. Medical records documented multiple puncture wounds on his arm.
