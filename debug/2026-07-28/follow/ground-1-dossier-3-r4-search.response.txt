Differing perspectives and criticisms regarding the effectiveness, scope, and leadership of the proposed exam reform task force have been voiced primarily by opposition parties and leaders.

The Congress party has strongly criticized the formation of the high-powered task force, calling it an "attempt to repair a compromised image" of Prime Minister Narendra Modi. They argue that the government has failed to implement the 101 recommendations submitted by the Dr. K. Radhakrishnan Committee, which was formed in June 2024 after previous examination irregularities, including the NEET-UG. Congress MP Jairam Ramesh highlighted that for most of the two years between the 2024 and 2026 paper leaks, the government did not even appoint a full-time Director General for the National Testing Agency (NTA), questioning the credibility and effectiveness of setting up another task force.

Rajya Sabha MP Kapil Sibal also criticized the Centre's handling of the NEET paper leak issue, accusing the Modi government of initially denying the problem, then cracking down on protesters, and only later announcing exam reforms. Communist Party of India (Marxist) (CPI(M)) MP John Brittas similarly questioned the status of the K. Radhakrishnan Committee and its 184-page report, asking why the nation was not apprised of its implementation.

Critics suggest that the new task force, led by Infosys co-founder Nandan Nilekani, is a diversionary tactic rather than a genuine commitment to systemic reform, given the alleged neglect of previous recommendations. While the government emphasizes the task force's multidisciplinary nature and its focus on technology-driven reforms to ensure a trustworthy, transparent, and tech-savvy examination system, the opposition views it as an insufficient response to deeper structural problems and a way to deflect accountability.
