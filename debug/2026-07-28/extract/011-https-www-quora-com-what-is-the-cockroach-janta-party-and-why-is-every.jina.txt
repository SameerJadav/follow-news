Most "comparison" sites are really lead collectors — the required phone number field is the tell. Enter it, and your info gets sold to a dozen agents who call for weeks. The fix: use a site that doesn't take your phone number at all.

They exist. They're just outnumbered, because selling your number pays better than showing you rates.

I use [ComparisonAdviser](https://trk.heysaver.com/db4301e6-3645-4952-abf2-de4632f5f8cf?utm_campaign=AT3_Desk&utm_medium=AT3_Desk_Q&utm_content=Where%20can%20I%20actually%20check%20car%20insurance%20rates%20without%20being%20spammed%20by%20phone%20calls%3F&qclid=1660690450940993472%7C211106242949473%7C0 "trk.heysaver.com") — enter your car and coverage details, and it shows what a range of companies would charge. No phone number field, nothing to sell to call centers, no way for the calls to start.

Two habits for any rate-check:

Optional phone field? Leave it empty. Required? Close the tab. The calls are the business model.

And be skeptical of sites promising prices without asking anything real. A site that asks almost nothing isn't quoting you — it's harvesting you.

The shame is that comparing genuinely pays — the same driver and coverage can price wildly differently across companies. The spam-call industry just made everyone afraid of the one habit that saves money.
