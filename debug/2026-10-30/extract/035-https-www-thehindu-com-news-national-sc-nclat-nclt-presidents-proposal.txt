The Supreme Court of India on Thursday (October 29, 2026) orally expressed concern that the Ministry of Corporate Affairs has “hijacked” staff appointments at the National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT).

A Bench headed by Chief Justice of India Surya Kant said the power to make staff appointments must remain with the NCLT president, and an employer-employee relationship must prevail between the staff members and the tribunal.

Senior Advocate Meenakshi Arora said the process of appointing staff members has been outsourced.

“I know how contractors play their hands. They don’t care about judicial/technical members. Complete autonomy must be with presidents. They can take UPSC/SSC. The tribunal has been completely hijacked by the Ministry of Corporate Affairs. That is impinging on the autonomy of the institution. That is the problem. The Ministry wants to have a complete grip. That is most unfortunate,” the Chief Justice orally remarked.

The observations were made while hearing a plea by contractual employees working in the NCLT and the National Company Law Appellate Tribunal (NCLAT).

The court directed the presidents of the two tribunals to submit within four weeks comprehensive proposals on infrastructure, buildings and staffing required to bolster the institutions’ day-to-day functioning.

The directions came after the court was informed that infrastructure at the NCLT Principal Bench was “collapsing”, with two floors shut and courts functioning at half strength.

The court had earlier directed the Centre to identify infrastructural amenities urgently required by the National Company Law Tribunal Benches in consultation with the NCLT president.

Observing that tribunals were the government’s lifeline, it also ordered the NCLT Principal Bench Bar Association to collect data on infrastructure at all regional benches in a tabulated format.
