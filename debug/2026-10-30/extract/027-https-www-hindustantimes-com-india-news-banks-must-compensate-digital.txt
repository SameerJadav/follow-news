The Supreme Court on Thursday said banks and other financial institutions must compensate victims if the fraud is found to have resulted from a failure of due diligence, and called for preventive safeguards to stop suspicious transactions before money is transferred.

A bench comprising Chief Justice of India Surya Kant and Justices Joymalya Bagchi and V Mohana said it would examine the Reserve Bank of India's proposed Know Your Customer (KYC) Amendment Directions, 2026, which are expected to be notified by November 30 and operationalised by December 31, news agency PTI reported.

The court stressed that the framework must provide for suitable compensation to victims where financial institutions fail to meet their due diligence obligations.

During the hearing, the CJI also expressed frustration over people continuing to fall prey to digital arrest scams despite public awareness campaigns and media coverage.

"What kind of person you are in a democratic country if you think you are getting arrested on mobile? Hit the mobile on the wall, and the arrest goes," he remarked, as reported by LiveLaw.

The amicus curiae, senior advocate NS Nappinai, told the bench that such scams continued despite earlier interventions and cited a recent case in which a woman lost ₹2.5 crore, the report further added.

She submitted that fear of arrest often drives victims to comply with the demands of fraudsters, with some psychologists describing the phenomenon as a form of hypnosis.

The Attorney General, R Venkataramani, appearing for the Centre and other authorities, told the court that reported digital arrest cases had declined by around 50% compared with the previous year.

The CJI, however, pointed out that losses from such offences had still reached nearly ₹800 crore this year, according to news agency PTI .

The bench stressed the need for preventive measures, including temporarily suspending unusual transactions that deviate from an account holder's established transaction pattern, automated reporting of suspicious activity and physical verification to establish the genuineness of transactions.

"Report will only be post-mortem. What about prevention? Moment a transaction comes, why can't it be withheld?" the CJI asked.

The court also emphasised the need for banks to alert neighbouring police stations about unusual transactions involving customers who may be victims of digital arrest scams.

The bench asked the RBI to submit a list of all authorised financial institutions by the next date of hearing, indicating that it could issue directions applicable to all such institutions without requiring individual banks to be impleaded in the proceedings.

It also sought details of the institutions that would be covered by the proposed guidelines and the measures being considered to compensate victims for their losses.

The court said it would examine the RBI's proposed KYC Amendment Directions, 2026, before issuing further directions.

Recent cases reported across India in October show how digital arrest scams have cost victims crores of rupees, with fraudsters posing as law enforcement officials to coerce them into transferring money.

In Delhi, police arrested two men for allegedly operating a mule bank account used to route proceeds linked to a ₹2.10-crore digital arrest scam targeting a Janakpuri resident. The account, registered in Yamuna Vihar in northeast Delhi, received ₹98,592 linked to the case on October 12. Police recovered mobile phones, debit cards and chequebooks from the accused.

In Hyderabad, cybercrime police arrested two men for allegedly defrauding a 63-year-old retired employee and his daughter of ₹29.17 lakh. The suspects allegedly laundered the money through mule accounts, cash deposit machines and cryptocurrency conversions. A third accused, a Bengaluru resident, remains absconding.

In Kozhikode, a 57-year-old woman allegedly lost ₹1.35 crore after fraudsters posing as Mumbai Crime Branch officers threatened her with criminal cases and asked her to transfer money to the Reserve Bank of India for verification. The Cyber Crime police registered a case after she received WhatsApp voice and video calls between September 22 and October 15.

In Karnataka's Dakshina Kannada district, a 62-year-old farmer from Puttur taluk allegedly lost ₹1.31 crore after fraudsters posing as Mumbai police and Anti-Terrorism Squad officers accused him of transferring money to a suspect in the Pahalgam terror attack. Police said he was kept under digital arrest from September 2 to October 6, 2026, and coerced into transferring money to multiple bank accounts.

A status report filed by the Indian Cybercrime Coordination Centre (I4C) and the Ministry of Home Affairs (MHA) before the Supreme Court said more than 21,900 complaints of digital arrest fraud, involving over ₹647 crore, were registered between January 1 and August 31, 2026.
