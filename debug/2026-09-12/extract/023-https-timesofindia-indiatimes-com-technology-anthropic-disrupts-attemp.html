AI company Anthropic said it has disrupted several attempts to use its Claude models for research that could potentially support the development of biological weapons, amid rising concerns over the risks posed by increasingly capable artificial intelligence.In its latest threat intelligence report released Thursday, the US-based company said it identified five cases involving users who were using its AI models in ways that could support biological weapons development.Anthropic said it could not determine with certainty whether the research was intended for malicious purposes, but chose to intervene because the same scientific knowledge can have both medical and military applications.

AI used to explore dangerous biological research

In one case, reported in May, a scientist sought help with a grant application to engineer mutations in chikungunya, a mosquito-borne virus, with the aim of making it more harmful in live animals, according to Anthropic.The company said the case raised additional concerns because it determined that the research was intended for a military research institute. Claude used in cyber, weapons and espionage operations

The report also detailed other attempts to misuse Anthropic's AI models, ranging from cyberattacks and scams to surveillance and the development of conventional weapons.Anthropic said Claude had been used in efforts linked to missile projects, armed drones, bombs and other weapons, as well as systems used for targeting and control.The report also identified suspected state-backed hackers and criminal groups that used Claude in cyber operations. In one case, a group allegedly linked to Russia used AI to develop malware that could detect when it was being flagged by security systems and modify its code to evade detection.Anthropic said its models had also been used in espionage and influence operations, including activity linked to Russia and Iran.The company said the cases detected between December 2025 and August 2026 involved its Claude Haiku, Sonnet and Opus models. It said it had disrupted the activity and incorporated its findings into systems designed to better detect and prevent similar misuse.
