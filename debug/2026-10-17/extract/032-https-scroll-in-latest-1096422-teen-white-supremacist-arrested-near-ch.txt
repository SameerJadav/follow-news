A 19-year-old man was arrested for plotting an attack “potentially targeting” a Hindu place of worship near Chicago, with the authorities recovering a document expressing white supremacist views and containing references to violence against people of “Indian descent”.

India on Thursday raised concern about the increasing “racist hostility and anti-India hate in the US”.

The teen, Alexandros Paporakis was arrested on Sunday after police in Palatine, a suburb near Chicago, received information about a potential mass shooting.

The information came from a person who had received a “concerning” document from Paporakis that outlined the alleged plan to carry out a mass shooting, the police said. The suspect had allegedly shared images depicting a rifle, rifle magazines, body armour and other tactical equipment.

Paporakis allegedly drove to a Hindu temple in Aurora, about 30 km west of Chicago, on Sunday and attempted to open fire, but his rifle jammed, according to court records cited by NBC Chicago.

He then returned home, searched online for instructions on how to unjam the weapon and called a colleague about his alleged plan to carry out a mass shooting.

The colleague alerted the police, who arrested him before he could return to the temple, NBC Chicago.

During searches at Paporakis’ home, the police recovered a rifle, several loaded rifle magazines, hundreds of rounds of additional rifle ammunition, tactical equipment and other items believed to be relevant to the investigation.

The recovered items included a tactical vest with body armour and a patch displaying “imagery associated with white supremacist extremism”, the police said.

The officers also found a document attributed to Paporakis that was titled “Why I Fought: A.K.A.: (Aurora Shooter’s Manifesto)”.

The document contained statements “expressing white supremacist and extremist ideologies, including references to violence directed toward individuals of South Asian heritage, particularly those of Indian descent,” the police said.

Paporakis has been charged with making a terror threat and for unlawful possession of weapons.

Raja Krishnamoorthi, a US congressman of Indian origin from the Chicago region, said that he was “horrified that a white supremacist allegedly attempted to massacre Indian Americans at a Hindu temple” in Aurora.

“This is where the poison of anti-Indian hatred and white supremacy can lead, and we cannot wait until innocent people are killed to take it seriously,” Krishnamoorthi said on social media. “I am grateful to the coworkers who sounded the alarm and the law enforcement officers who intervened.”

Every American deserves to live and worship without fear, and we must stand together against hatred in all its forms, the congressman said.

The Indian consulate in Chicago said that it appreciated the “timely action” by the US authorities. The diplomatic mission added that it was in touch with members of the Indian community and was closely monitoring the situation.

The incident comes at a time when online hate in the US against South Asians has surged 240% between January 2024 and June 2026, according to a recent study by Washington-based think tank Center for the Study of Organized Hate.

More than half a million posts with racist, dehumanising and xenophobic slurs and narratives targeting South Asians appeared on social media platform X between January 2025 and June 2026, the study found last month.

In September 2025, the Center for the Study of Organized Hate said that the tariff dispute between India and the US and the outrage based on specific incidents such as an accident by a Sikh truck driver that left three dead in Florida had coincided with a spike in racist social media posts against Indians.

The Ministry of External Affairs said that India was “deeply concerned” by the incident.
