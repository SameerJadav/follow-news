Trump's UN remarks - in summary

  • Trump opened his speech to the UN by commemorating the nation’s 250th anniversary and focusing not on international issues, but touting his domestic record. Tax cuts, stock market highs, crime rates, and immigration enforcement, to name a few.

  • He once again framed the war he launched on Iran earlier this year with Israel the same way he has to his domestic audience — as a necessary conflict to addressing a long-ignored threat. The United States, Trump said, had acted to prevent Iran from obtaining a nuclear weapon, while other countries ignored the issue.

  • Trump then re-issued his threat to “annihilate” Iran and “drive them into Hell” if the regime didn’t capitulate and make a deal.” He touted his version of the war: one in which the US had defanged Iran militarily and driven the country to the brink of economic collapse. And hedged by saying he was confident Iran would make a deal after the US midterm elections – an argument he’s made to US voters.

  • Trump said he was “working closely with the leaders of Russia and Ukraine” to end the disastrous war. “It’s going to happen, I think, more quickly than people understand,” he said. The US president said he had signed into law “a powerful new law” on sanctions, and “if necessary, I will have to use them.” He added: “It’s time to stop the killing.”

  • Trump defended his administration’s actions in Latin America – including the midnight raid to arrest former Venezuelan president Nicólas Maduro, and the oil deal he struck with his more US-friendly successor, Delcy Rodriguez. “To the victor belong the spoils,” Trump declared, justifying the deal that will funnel a share of the nation’s immense oil wealth into the US treasury.

  • Defending his drone strikes on boats in the Caribbean and the Pacific, Trump equated the cartels he claims to be fighting with Islamist terrorist organizations. “The cartels are the ISIS of the western Hemisphere – not good people,” he said. “Like ISIS, they should be killed, exiled or detained as enemy combatants without the possibility of release, which is what we’re doing.”

  • He accused Mexico of being effectively controlled by the cartels, and said it was “unacceptable” for the US to share a border with a nation under such conditions. “So Mexico must reclaim control of its soil from the enemies of humanity,” Trump said.

  • Trump also pointed to regime change in Cuba. “My administration is also seeking a fundamental change in the situation in Cuba, where the communist regime is under great pressure, the biggest pressure they’ve ever been under,” he said. “It’s an absolutely failed state. It’s failing.”

  • Trump said the US has reached “an unprecedented agreement” on Greenland, with the Kingdom of Denmark. He said the deal – to be formally signed shortly after his speech – “gives the US permanent control over security and all of the needs on that territory” and that the US will build two “very major” military bases in Greenland.

  • Trump said he would reject any calls for a “globalist scheme” to control artificial intelligence, and made an impromptu pitch to world leaders to call the technology “super intelligence” or “SI”. Amid mounting fears over the dangers of AI as some, including leaders in the industry, call for international regulation on the technology, Trump said he would not hinder its growth. He said he believes will be “bigger than the industrial revolution,” and vowed the US would lead on AI. “We’re only going to encourage superintelligence.”

60 Minutes’ blockbuster interview with Patrick Clancy, the ex-husband of Lindsay Clancy, failed to provide a major ratings boost for the Sunday show, according to audience figures released Tuesday.

Sunday night’s episode of the newsmagazine show drew an average of 6.77m total viewers, the third-smallest audience in the past 25 years for the second episode of a season, adding to a disappointing debut for Bari Weiss, who, as CBS editor-in-chief, rewired the show, firing several high-profile correspondents in the run-up to the new season.

The 59th season debuted on 13 September with an audience down sharply from the 58th season debut, drawing 7.94 million viewers compared with 10 million for last season’s first episode.

Republican National Committee sues New Jersey election officials for refusal to turn over voter records

The Republican National Committee has sued top election officials in New Jersey, alleging they failed to produce public voter roll records, including the addresses of noncitizen voters.

The lawsuit follows New Jersey governor Mikie Sherril’s announcement in July that “a software error” in the state’s motor vehicle registration system added 6,600 noncitizens to the state’s voter registration list beginning in 2023. According to the state, 400 votes were cast by noncitizens – although Donald Trump claimed that “thousands of votes” were cast by noncitizens in the state, in an effort to drum up support for his SAVE America Act.

In 2024, Kamala Harris won New Jersey by more than 252,000 votes – meaning that even if all voters were registered as Democrats, which they were not, their votes would not have swung the presidential election.

In Tuesday’s lawsuit, the RNC states that, New Jersey “objected to or denied” several of its records requests “including the request for records of address confirmation notices”.

In July, assistant attorney general for civil rights Harmeet Dhillon wrote to Sherril requesting the “full names, dates of birth, nationalities, residential addresses, and registration dates and locations” of all 6,600 noncitizen voters.

Sherrill has said she will not provide that information. “We now have certain people that, through no fault of their own, have been registered to vote,” she said at a press conference. “It may imperil their immigration status.”

3h ago17.21 EDT
Tiago Rogero

US president Donald Trump called on all Latin American and Caribbean countries aligned with his administration to designate cartels as narco-terrorists and “apply crushing sanctions” against them.

During a meeting of the Shield of the Americas – an anti-drug cartel coalition formed by 14 Latin American and Caribbean countries governed by rightwing or far-right presidents – Trump said that “for years, cartels have waged war on the people of this hemisphere. Now, we’re waging war on them”.

He added: “So, today, I’m asking all nations in the Shield of the Americas to formally designate narco-terrorists as common threats to stability and apply crushing sanctions against all 24 U.S.-designated foreign terrorist organizations.”

The group, which includes countries such as Argentina, El Salvador and the Dominican Republic, met in New York after Trump said in his speech to the UN General Assembly that “the cartels are the ISIS of the Western Hemisphere” and that “like ISIS, they should be killed, exiled or detained as enemy combatants without the possibility of release”.

In a speech lasting just over 10 minutes to members of the coalition, Trump added: “You probably noticed that the drugs, the fentanyl being delivered by sea, by ocean and sea, it’s down 97 per cent coming into the US” – although many experts have repeatedly pointed out that there is no evidence of fentanyl being produced in or trafficked by sea to the US from South America.

The US president also used the meeting to boast that candidates he had backed had won every election in the region since he took office for a second term.

“There’s been a big shift in your countries, a big shift to the right. That’s a good thing,” said Trump, who particularly celebrated the victory in Colombia of the far-right millionaire former lawyer and businessman Abelardo de la Espriella – who was not present and sent his vice-president instead because he pledged not to leave the country during his four-year term.

“He was a big Trump fan,” said Trump of De la Espriella’s campaign. “All he did was talk about Trump, Trump, Trump. And I said, I like that guy. Let’s endorse him.”

Although the US president said that, after his endorsement, De la Espriella “went from number 10 to number one and won in a landslide”, Trump only endorsed him in the second round, after the Colombian had already won the first round – in which Trump had not backed any candidate.

Trump's FBI killed corruption probe into Susan Collins' campaign: report

The FBI under Trump killed an anti-corruption probe into Susan Collins, the Republican senator from Maine, stemming from a convicted former defense contracting executive’s claims that he’d paid off Collins’ campaign in exchange for government contracts, ProPublica reported Tuesday.

The report by ProPublica presents evidence that Kao contributed campaign funds in return for Collins steering funds toward Kao’s company, Navatek. Trump strikes warm tone in meeting with UK prime minister

Andrew Sparrow

Trump also met earlier this afternoon with the the United Kingdom’s new prime minister, Andy Burnham, with whom he appeared to enjoy a solid rapport – a divergence from his at-times testy relationship with Burnham’s predecessor in the role, Keir Starmer.

“I think he’s going to be a great prime minister,” Trump said of Burnham. “I think he’s got a lot of assets on his side.”

Trump added: “We talked about everything from Rolls-Royce to energy, immigration. We covered a lot.”

One point of tension in an otherwise cordial exchange: the Chagos Islands deal, a proposal by Keir Starmer’s goverment to give Mauritius sovereignty over the Chagos Islands, on condition that the US and the UK can continue to operate the Diego Garcia military base there for at least another 99 years.

“I don’t support it,” Trump said. “I think it’s a terrible deal.”

At one point the relationship between Trump and Starmer was good. But it worsened as Starmer started to criticize Trump publicly on issues like his proposal to annex Greenland, and his claim British troops avoided frontline action in Afghanistan. And the full rupture came when Keir Starmer refused to allow the US to use British airbases for attacks against Iran at the start of the war.

Members of US delegation met Iranian leadership at UN, Trump says

During a press conference alongside Volodymyr Zelenskyy, Trump said some members of his team met today with the Iranian delegation, a meeting that he said lasted three hours and “went very well.”

Trump left the exact nature of the exchange – what was discussed, and what progress, if any, was made toward a deal – unclear, but said it was a “a very good meeting.” He then repeated his threat delivered to the assembled world leaders in an address earlier that morning that Iran had to choose between “potential greatness or obliteration.”

“They had a very good meeting, and they have another one scheduled in the near future,” Trump said, teasing future conversations with Iran.

Steve Witkoff, Trump’s special envoy to the Middle East, and Jared Kushner, his son-in-law-turned-Middle East diplomat, sat alongside the president. Both declined to share specifics of the meeting.

“I feel very good right now,” Witkoff said when asked how he felt after the initial meeting.

“How will you feel tomorrow?” a reporter pressed him.

“I think very good,” he said.

Trump endorses a US embargo on diesel exports in conversation with Zelenskyy

President Trump appeared to support the increasingly loud calls from some members of his party to halt the export of US diesel fuel, whose skyrocketing price has begun to ripple out through the American economy and stung Republicans ahead of the upcoming midterm elections.

“I’ve called for that too, I’ve said, ‘let’s not send out the diesel’,” Trump said, when asked about the outcry from his fellow Republicans.

He then turned to Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent, sitting at his side, who said the Trump administration is evaluating the feasibility of a full or partial plan. Trump declined to share a timeline for when such a ban could come down.

The subject was raised after a reporter mentioned Ukraine’s attacks on Russian oil infrastructure, which Trump was quick to point out had damaged global diesel supplies. (The conflict between the US and Iran, which Trump launched in February alongside Israel, has also strangled global energy markets.)

“It is a serious hit on the Russians,” Trump said. “It’s also a very serious hit on the price of diesel.”
