Almost two million people are under evacuation orders in Japan as Typhoon Dujuan batters a number of the country's prefectures.

The country's public broadcaster, NHK, reported that two people had died and four others were missing as a result of the typhoon. It also reported four were missing in Kanagawa prefecture.

Japan's Meteorological Agency had warned of an "imminent threat to life", but several weather warnings have now been downgraded.

Strong winds caused power cuts for more than 50,000 people, while hundreds of flights have been cancelled and rail services have been suspended.

Dujuan has had sustained winds of 130km/h (80.8mph) and gusts of 194 km/h.

Authorities have advised the public to pay attention to weather information.

Dujuan, which is Japan's 25th typhoon of the year, has hit in the midst of a five-day holiday and forced the cancellation of several events.

Concerts scheduled for Monday at the Rock in Japan music festival have been cancelled, while the Tokyo Game Show, a video game convention, has also cancelled Monday's programme.

Japan's Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi offered her condolences in a post on X to those who lost thier lives in the typhoon, before saying she would be continuing with her scheduled trip the United States as planned.

"With a sense of urgency, I instructed Chief Cabinet Secretary Takehara and Minister Hosono to respond with utmost preparedness," she wrote, before saying she had boarded a flight to the US.

She will be speaking at the UN General Assembly in New York on Tuesday, and will meet with US President Donald Trump.

The Yokosuka City Fire Department told the BBC that a woman who lived in the house, aged in her 60s, may have been swept away and is believed to have been buried alive.

Following a search by rescue crews, she was found inside the home and pronounced dead, NHK reported.

Separately, Sodegaura City Fire Department in Chiba prefecture told the BBC a man in his 50s was rescued after getting trapped while working to clear debris at a landslide site.

The department said the worker fell into a bamboo thicket, along with some heavy machinery. He was then taken to hospital before his death was confirmed, according to local reports. Police are investigating the details of the incident.

Firefighters in Kanagawa prefecture said they received a call from a man who reported his house had been crushed by a landslide.

Some research suggests that Japan has become more exposed to destructive storms, but climate change is not thought to be increasing the number of these storms globally, and could even make them less common in some regions.

As temperatures rise, typhoons that do form are likely to become more intense on average, however.

Warmer ocean waters mean storms can pick up more energy, leading to higher wind speeds.

And because a warmer atmosphere can hold more moisture, these storms could also bring heavier rainfall.
