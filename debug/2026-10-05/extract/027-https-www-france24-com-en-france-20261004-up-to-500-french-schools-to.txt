Up to 500 schools across [France](https://www.france24.com/en/tag/france/) will be totally or partly closed on Monday due to the [protests by teenage pupils](https://www.france24.com/en/france/20261002-hundreds-of-french-schools-remain-closed-as-student-protests-turn-violent) that have shaken the country, the education minister said.

Lessons will be "totally or partly suspended" in some "400-500" high schools as the security conditions "have not been fulfilled", said Education Minister Edouard Geffray on Sunday, adding that 24 schools had been "burned or ransacked" since the start of the movement.

The teens at French high schools, known as lycées, say that their studies are being ruined by high teacher absences, overlong school days and dilapidated facilities, as well as other grievances.

Geffray said the number of schools affected on Monday would be comparable to the figure from Friday but vowed there would be a "partial, progressive return to lessons".

More than a third of the 468 schools in the wider Ile-de-France region surrounding the capital will be shut on Monday as a result of the protests, Valerie Pecresse, the right-wing president of the region, told broadcaster Franceinfo.

The movement began at a [Paris](https://www.france24.com/en/tag/paris/) school on September 17 but grew last week into nationwide protests marked in places by outbursts of violence involving teenagers and security forces, which sometimes used tear gas and batons to disperse the children.

## 'Deliberately targeted'

At Saint-Denis, just north of Paris, students took turns expressing their grievances at an open-air meeting Sunday.

When you are a student from one of the deprived districts, said one student, "The violence is unleashed."

She referred in particular to one student who had lost his teeth to a police rubber bullet and another who had been hit in the eye.

One 16-year-old boy, Campion, held a placard that read: "They say they are listening to us but prefer to gas us".

"I don't understand why the government deploys so much force against a case that is valid: our education, our future," he told AFP.

Geffray, in his statement, spoke of far-left infiltrators who had "hijacked' the movement "and engaged in extreme violence".

"Clearly, this poses a risk to both our staff and our students," he alleged, adding that 78 education staff members had been wounded – "something we have never seen before".

"They were deliberately targeted because they were employees of the national education system," Geffray claimed.

Organisers have called for the protests to continue on Tuesday, citing a lack of "concrete measures" from the government.

In a bid to address the protesters' concerns, Prime Minister [Sébastien Lecornu](https://www.france24.com/en/tag/s%C3%A9bastien-lecornu/) set out a timetable on Sunday urging local authorities to prioritise filling gaps left by long-term absences and unfilled posts.

He also called a meeting for Tuesday with regional presidents to discuss overhauling the country's often-dilapidated school buildings.

## Violent incidents

More than 5,000 people, 85 percent of them minors, have been arrested since the start of the movement.

A 16-year-old was detained on Sunday over the assault of a teacher in the southern town of La Ciotat. The teacher, 59, was struck and doused with gasoline on the sidelines of a high school student protest on Friday.

A group of youths were last week filmed beating and kicking a policeman on the ground in Belfort in eastern France in images that caused an outcry among officials.
