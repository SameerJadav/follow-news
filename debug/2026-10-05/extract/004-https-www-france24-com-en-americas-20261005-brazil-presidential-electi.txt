Brazilians voted Sunday in a high-stakes presidential election pitting incumbent Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva against Sen. Flávio Bolsonaro at a moment when the region is shifting to the right in line with the Trump administration.

Bolsonaro, 45, is the son of far-right former President [Jair Bolsonaro](https://www.france24.com/en/tag/jair-bolsonaro/) who is serving a 27-year sentence for a coup attempt and an ally of US President [Donald Trump](https://www.france24.com/en/tag/donald-trump/). Lula, who at 80 is one of the world’s oldest heads of state, is running in his seventh presidential election and has strengthened [Brazil](https://www.france24.com/en/tag/brazil/)'s ties with [Beijing](https://www.france24.com/en/tag/beijing/).

Trump allies have fared well in recent elections in [Latin America](https://www.france24.com/en/tag/latin-america/), including in [Argentina](https://www.france24.com/en/tag/argentina/), [Colombia](https://www.france24.com/en/tag/colombia/), [Honduras](https://www.france24.com/en/tag/honduras/) and [Peru](https://www.france24.com/en/tag/peru/).

If none of the 12 presidential candidates gets more than 50% of the vote, the top two will head to a runoff on Oct. 25 in Latin America’s economic powerhouse.

Brazil's nearly 159 million eligible voters will also elect 27 state governors, members of state legislatures, all 513 federal members of the lower house of congress and 54 of the country’s 81 senators in Sunday’s vote.

Polls closed at 5 p.m. Sunday with those still in line able to vote and tallies started rolling in within the hour. In the last presidential election, the two candidates who advanced out of the first round were mathematically determined at about 8 p.m. Brasilia time with nearly 99% of votes counted.

While the once widely popular Lula saw his approval ratings plummet during his third term, standing up to the US has given him a boost. And he still enjoys a strong base of supporters who credit his social policies for improving millions of Brazilians’ lives.

The incumbent president has often spoken about risks of US intervention in the [Brazilian elections](https://www.france24.com/en/tag/brazilian-elections/) after the Trump administration imposed high [tariffs](https://www.france24.com/en/tag/tariffs/) against Brazilian exports, revoked the visa of the country’s ambassador and sanctioned a [Supreme Court](https://www.france24.com/en/tag/supreme-court/)[justice](https://www.france24.com/en/tag/justice/) who chaired the [investigation](https://www.france24.com/en/tag/investigation/) that eventually sentenced Jair Bolsonaro, Lula's predecessor.

Lula told journalists after voting Sunday that he needs “to do double of what we have done” if reelected, and expressed confidence in a first-round win.

But the incumbent faces a tough battle because of an affordability crisis and a [corruption](https://www.france24.com/en/tag/corruption/) scandal that ensnared the same justice who led the investigation into Bolsonaro.

“Many in the Lula campaign were not expecting such a difficult election a few weeks ago, and it is clear to them that the present election is a tough battle and the next ones, without Lula, could be even more so,” said Carlos Melo, a political [science](https://www.france24.com/en/tag/science/) professor at Insper University in Sao Paulo.

Flávio Bolsonaro has touted his proximity to the [White House](https://www.france24.com/en/tag/white-house/), although he denies his campaign received help from the US [government](https://www.france24.com/en/tag/government/).

He has promised to crack down on Brazilian criminal groups and insisted that Brazil needs to strike better deals with the Trump administration, including in the rare [minerals](https://www.france24.com/en/tag/minerals/)[economy](https://www.france24.com/en/tag/economy/), second only to China. Bolsonaro also has campaigned on releasing his father and bringing him back to politics.

“Brazil needs change. Brazil can’t stand so much suffering,” he told reporters at the [Rio de Janeiro](https://www.france24.com/en/tag/rio-de-janeiro/) voting center, where he cast his vote Sunday.

The senator faces his own challenges. He has seen his political career tainted by several corruption accusations, including receiving millions of dollars from a disgraced banker to allegedly produce a movie on his father’s life.
