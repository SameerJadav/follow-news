Amid large-scale detentions across the national capital on Saturday (October 10, 2026), as police sought to thwart a youth protest demanding the resignation of Chief Election Commissioner (CEC) Gyanesh Kumar over deletions of names from electoral rolls, legal experts questioned the basis on which Delhi Police detained protesters beyond the New Delhi district, where prohibitory orders under Section 163 of the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS) are in force.

Among those detained on Saturday were Cockroach Janta Party (CJP) representatives Abhijeet Dipke, Saurav Das and Ashutosh Ranka, who were taken into custody after police boarded their Air India flight upon its arrival in Delhi. CJP legal wing head Ratna Singh alleged that police prevented her from leaving her residence in Jangpura, while YouTuber Dhruv Rathee was detained at a residential flat in Gurugram.

Several instances of protesters being picked up and taken away in buses were also reported through the day, with some alleging that they were not informed of the grounds for their detention, raising concerns over compliance with constitutional safeguards governing deprivation of personal liberty.

Section 163 of the BNSS, which replaced Section 144 of the Code of Criminal Procedure (CrPC), empowers an executive magistrate to issue prohibitory orders where “immediate prevention or speedy remedy is desirable”.

Delhi Police has maintained that detentions in the capital followed violations of prohibitory orders imposed under Section 163 of the BNSS in the New Delhi district, which bar unauthorised public assemblies, demonstrations and protests. Late on Saturday evening, police said women protesters and senior citizens were being released from detention centres, while “due legal process” would be followed in the case of the CJP leaders.

However, there was no clarity on the legal authority invoked to detain protesters from areas where such orders were not in force, or whether any other preventive powers, including those under special laws, had been invoked.

“The detention of CJP representatives from areas in Delhi-NCR where prohibitory orders are not in force is wholly illegal,” advocate Nipun Saxena said. He also pointed to what he described as the increasing use of prohibitory orders to quell even peaceful protests.

“Intended as an exceptional measure to deal with emergencies and maintain public peace, such orders are often reissued by the authorities in a cyclostyled manner, turning an exceptional power into a routine tool to curb protests,” Mr. Saxena said.

He also pointed to the practice of resorting to detention rather than formal arrest, which, he said, enabled the police to avoid some of the procedural safeguards that accompany an arrest.

“The police know that the power to arrest cannot be exercised casually when protesters are not committing a cognisable offence punishable with imprisonment of more than seven years. If they make arrests in such cases, they will have to justify them before the courts,” he said. “That is why they choose to detain people and release them within 24 hours, so that they do not have to be produced before a magistrate.”

The safeguards applicable to such brief detentions during protests, however, remain a grey area. The extent to which safeguards governing formal arrest apply to temporary restraints on personal liberty, and the consequences of their breach, have not been conclusively settled by the Supreme Court.

Referring to instances of protesters being picked up and taken to buses and stadiums across the capital, former Supreme Court judge Abhay S. Oka said authorities were obligated under Article 22 of the Constitution to communicate the grounds for depriving a person of their liberty “as soon as may be”. He said this protection could not be circumvented merely by describing the restraint as detention rather than a formal arrest.

Mr. Saxena agreed, saying that even where the police claim to be exercising preventive powers rather than arresting a person for an offence, the grounds for detention must be communicated at the earliest opportunity.

“There are a host of Supreme Court judgments which hold that even in cases of preventive detention under stringent laws such as the National Security Act, 1980 (NSA), communication of the grounds of detention is a sine qua non,” he said.

Chaotic scenes were witnessed in Delhi as the Cockroach Janta Party's supporters attempted to congregate at Delhi's Jantar Mantar to protest on Saturday. ## **Delhi Chief Minister's 'B-Team' Swipe**

Gupta accused the two leaders of venting their frustration over repeated electoral defeats on the people of Delhi.

"Rejected by the people of the country, Rahul Gandhi and Arvind Kejriwal, along with their B-team CJP, want to turn Delhi into an arena," she said in a post on X.

"Why are these leaders hell-bent on taking out their frustration over non-stop defeats on the public? What is the fault of Delhiites troubled by chaos on the streets?" she asked.

"Are your political ambitions bigger than the livelihoods, safety and peace of lakhs of families in Delhi?" she asked.

Gupta also attacked her predecessor, Arvind Kejriwal, calling him a "seeker of anarchy" and accusing him of putting Delhi's peace and security at risk for his own political gain.

"Be reminded, holding Delhi hostage will not be permitted," she warned.

Talking about Prime Minister Narendra Modi's third consecutive term,Gupta accused Opposition parties of refusing to accept the voters' mandate.

**Detained On Plane, Dharna At Bus Stop**

CJP founder Abhijeet Dipke and two other leaders, Saurav Das and Ashutosh Ranka, were detained from inside an aircraft shortly after their flight from Mumbai landed at Delhi airport.

A video showed police personnel entering the aircraft as Dipke questioned why they could not wait for passengers to disembark.

"I have come here to be arrested. Arrest me," he told the officers, who responded that he was being detained, not arrested.

Former Bengal chief minister Mamata Banerjee staged a sit-in at a bus stop in South Avenue with a band-aid over her mouth before being detained along with several Trinamool Congress MPs.

Kejriwal was detained as he attempted to leave his residence for the protest. Former Kerala chief minister Pinarayi Vijayan and former Tripura chief minister Manik Sarkar were also among those detained.

Protesters trying to reach Jantar Mantar were stopped at several locations and taken to temporary detention centres, including Chhatrasal Stadium.

With Dipke in detention, CJP handed over responsibility for continuing the agitation to Ajinkya Shinde through a pre-recorded video.

**Metro Curbs, Top Court's Questions**

Even as the Chief Minister blamed Opposition leaders for disrupting public life, restrictions imposed by the authorities also affected commuters, resulting in traffic jams across Delhi.

Entry and exit gates at 45 Delhi Metro stations were shut, more than 250 trains were cancelled, and internet services were suspended in parts of central Delhi. Barricades, security checks and traffic diversions added to the inconvenience.

The slew of restrictions had come under scrutiny in the Supreme Court on Friday, a day before the planned protest.

"You can't use a sledgehammer to kill a fly," the court observed while questioning the scale of the curbs on Metro and railway services.

The court asked the Centre to reconsider the restrictions, saying authorities could regulate movement for security reasons but could not completely shut down essential public transport.

Delhi Police had denied permission for the demonstration, citing the organisers' failure to provide the mandatory 10-day advance intimation and concerns over law and order.

Nine persons were killed between Friday and Saturday in Manipur’s Kamjong, Kangpokpi and Ukhrul districts, the Hindustan Times reported, quoting unidentified police officials.

At least 14 homes were burnt in Kamjong and the district authorities have imposed prohibitory orders within a 400-metre radius of Chassad police station.

The violence followed the death of a 42-year-old woman, who was killed in the Kuki-dominated Chassad village in Naga-majority Kamjong district, reported PTI. A mother of five, the woman was shot near her neck on Friday night during a gunfight between armed groups and security forces.

The gunfight had started after security forces arrested some village volunteers, reported Hindustan Times.

The term “village volunteers” has been used for armed civilians guarding villages since ethnic clashes broke out in the state between the Kuki and Meitei groups in May 2023.

The women’s death on Friday triggered an exchange of fire between Kuki and Naga armed groups, reported PTI. A 22-year-old man was killed in the clashes and at least 14 homes belonging to members of the Kuki and Naga communities were burnt.

Separately, suspected militants opened fire in the Ningchou village, a Tangkhul Naga settlement near the Myanmar border, in Kamjong, reported PTI.

Two persons, identified as 35-year-old Ngachanso Ningshen and 48-year-old Leishishan Leishadang, were shot dead, the news agency quoted unidentified officials as saying.

Four persons were killed in Kangpokpi on Saturday, the police confirmed to Scroll.

At around 6 am, suspected Kuki militants allegedly opened fire at four Naga village volunteers between Makui and Tapok villages along the Imphal-Tamenglong road, reported Hindustan Times.

While one person, identified as 35-year-old Machungwangbou was killed, two others were injured.

In another attack on Makui at around 5 am, three Naga village guards were killed, according to the newspaper. They were identified as Machungammamg, Ajinpou Ringkangmai and Robart.

In Ukhrul district, a 40-year-old man, Sandeep Rai, was shot dead near Chepu Yaolen by suspected militants, reported PTI. The man died inside the four-wheeler he was driving.

Tensions between the Kukis and the Nagas escalated in Manipur in February after an alleged assault involving members of the Tangkhul Naga and the Kuki-Zo communities in the Ukhrul district.

This followed the ethnic clashes that broke out in Manipur in May 2023 between the Meitei and Kuki-Zo-Hmar communities. More than 300 persons have been killed and more than 59,000 persons displaced since then in the conflict.

### **Houses, Shops Set On Fire**

At least 10 houses and 20 shops were also set ablaze in and around Chassad on Saturday, though the extent of the damage and the number of people displaced remain unclear.

The Kamjong district administration has imposed prohibitory orders around Chassad Police Gate Junction. In an order dated October 9, District Magistrate L. N. Kashung invoked Section 163 of the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS), 2023, prohibiting the assembly of five or more persons, the carrying of weapons and movement outside homes within a 400-metre radius of the junction.

The restrictions will remain in force until further notice, with exemptions for security personnel, magistrates on duty, essential-service personnel and individuals holding special permits.

Meanwhile, CoTU, Sadar Hills, announced a 24-hour total shutdown across Kangpokpi district from 6 pm on Saturday, citing the killings in Chassad and alleged coordinated attacks in Chassad, Yaolen in Ukhrul district and along the Imphal-Tengnoupal Road. Emergency medical services have been exempted from the shutdown.

The Kuki Workers' Union (KWU) condemned the killings of Domkhonei Touthang and Michael Ngamminlen Haokip, demanding registration of a case under Sections 103 and 105 of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS), removal of the IRB post and an independent inquiry.

### **Former Chief Minister Condemns Deaths**

Meanwhile, former Manipur chief minister N Biren Singh expressed concern over the deteriorating security situation, claiming in a social media post that seven people had lost their lives in violent incidents across the state in a single day.

Singh referred to reported attacks in Ningchou village in Kamjong district, violence along the Makui-Imphal-Tamenglong Road and in Chassad, and the reported burning of a police station in Kamjong. He also said two Naga residents of Makui Ashang village in Kangpokpi district had been reported missing.
