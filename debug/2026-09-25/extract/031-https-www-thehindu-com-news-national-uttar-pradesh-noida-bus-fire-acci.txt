Nine people were killed after a tourist bus travelling from Delhi to Mahoba caught fire near the 33-km milestone of the Yamuna Expressway in Jewar, Gautam Buddh Nagar, on Wednesday (September 23, 2026) night.

The bus, with around 35 passengers on board, was about 5 km from the Noida International Airport when the fire broke out, triggering panic and traffic congestion in the area.

The first information report (FIR), registered at the Jewar police station following a complaint by a survivor from Hamirpur, said the passengers noticed a burning smell from the driver’s cabin around 11.30 p.m. and repeatedly asked the crew to stop. But the driver dismissed it as a common occurrence on the bus and did not stop to inspect the vehicle.

The FIR alleged that the driver and conductor smoked in the cabin before a fire broke out near the driver’s seat. When passengers raised the alarm again, the two refused to stop, telling them, “If you want to die, then die. We won’t stop the bus.”

By the time the bus stopped, smoke had filled the passenger compartment, and the fire had engulfed the bus. Police have detained the drivers, Pavan Kumar and Yogesh Dixit, and the conductor, Rohit. All are residents of Kanpur. Police have registered a case and slapped charges of culpable homicide.

Preliminary investigation suggests that a spark or blast in the air-conditioning system may have ignited the engine oil, causing the fire to spread rapidly, police said. The exact cause remains under investigation.

Jewar Station House Officer Ajay Kumar Singh said six bodies were handed over to their families after post-mortem examination. The post-mortem of three unidentified bodies will be done on Friday. Police said the nine victims died from burns and asphyxiation.

Police identified the deceased as Shruti Rawat from Chhatarpur, Madhya Pradesh; Saroj Gupta, 52, and Mayank Gupta, 28, from Hamirpur, Uttar Pradesh; Devendra Sahay Bhardwaj, 60, from Hisar, Haryana; Har Sharan Gupta, 55, from Jalaun in Uttar Pradesh; and Usha Devi, 65, from Hamirpur.

One passenger, Rekha Bhardwaj, sustained injuries after jumping out of the bus and is currently stable.

Rinki Rajput, who was asleep, said she woke after someone banged on the bus door. “I did not pick up any of my belongings. I did not even put on my shoes. I just grabbed my phone and stepped out,” she said.

Ms. Rajput said visibility inside the bus was poor, and passengers pushed and shoved as they tried to escape. She said someone pulled her out, while several passengers behind her were unable to escape.

Another survivor said he woke up when the fire had already spread inside the bus. “It was smoke everywhere. We all scrambled to get out,” he said.

The Hindu has found that the bus, a sleeper coach registered in Bihar and operated by Raj Kalpana Travels, had 17 pending challans amounting to ₹1.59 lakh at the time of the incident. It had accumulated 55 challans in Delhi and Uttar Pradesh between April 2023 and August 2026, according to records on the Parivahan platform. The violations included driving without a licence, breaches of permit conditions, failure to carry requisite documents, overspeeding, carrying goods on the roof and violations of safety norms.

Police said the bus operates from Madhya Pradesh and is registered in the Madhubani district of Bihar. Its fitness certificate was issued in Rajasthan and its pollution certificate in Haryana.

Among the challans was an ₹18,500 fine slapped on August 19, 2026, for “carriage of goods on top in stage and contract carriage”, indicating that goods were being transported in a passenger bus in violation of the stated carriage rules.

Two other challans, for ₹21,000 and ₹26,000, cited violations including “vehicle not plying for specified purpose (tourist vehicle)” and “vehicle driven in contravention of permit conditions relating to route, area or purpose”. The violations indicate that the vehicle had also been booked for use in ways contrary to the conditions of its permit.

The Motor Vehicle Inspector’s (MVI) report following the fire found large quantities of cosmetic products stored in the bus. Sanjay Kumar Gupta, MVI at the Gautam Buddh Nagar RTO office, said the bus measured 4.1 metres in height against the prescribed 3.8 metres under AIS-119, while its other specifications largely met the standard. The bus had an emergency exit, though the upper sleeper berth above it may have made evacuation difficult, he said. The bus had an emergency gate but Mr. Gupta said the upper sleeper tier above it may have made evacuation difficult.
