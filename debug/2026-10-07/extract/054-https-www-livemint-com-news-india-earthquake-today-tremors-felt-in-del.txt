A 4.9-magnitude earthquake hit Uttarakhand's Chamoli on Tuesday night, with tremors felt in the national capital and nearby areas. Residents of Delhi took to social media to share that they were left shaken.

According to the National Centre for Seismology, the quake occurred at around 10:15 pm. Tremors were also felt in Delhi-NCR, Himachal Pradesh, Punjab and Haryana.

The NCS said an earthquake of magnitude 4.9 struck Chamoli in Uttarakhand at 10:22 pm IST.

Further details regarding any damage or casualties are awaited.

As #earthquake made it to the top of the trending lists, social media users turned to humour, sharing hilarious memes to discuss the unexpected shake-up.

A user wrote: “Felt my entire couch move, the entire building move - Safdurjung Enclave, Delhi! Never experienced anything close!”

Meanwhile, earlier in the day, an earthquake of magnitude 4.5 struck Assam's Nagaon district in the early hours of Tuesday, according to the National Centre for Seismology (NCS).

The earthquake occurred at 4:23:47 am IST at a depth of 33 kilometres, the NCS said in a post on X.

The epicentre of the earthquake was located at latitude 26.428 North and longitude 93.060 East, according to the NCS.

Delhi-NCR has felt tremors from several earthquakes so far this year, ranging from mild local quakes to stronger ones originating in Afghanistan.

On January 19, 2026, a 2.8-magnitude earthquake was recorded in New Delhi. The epicentre was in North Delhi, at a shallow depth of around five kilometres. The tremors were briefly felt in parts of the capital, although there were no reports of damage.

Delhi-NCR experienced another round of tremors on April 3 after a 5.9-magnitude earthquake struck Afghanistan. The epicentre was located near Darawan. Mild shaking was also reported in Punjab, Haryana, Jammu and Kashmir and Kargil.

No casualties or property damage were reported immediately after the earthquake.

On June 27, a stronger 6.2-magnitude earthquake struck Afghanistan, with tremors reaching Delhi-NCR and other parts of northern India.

The National Centre for Seismology said the earthquake occurred at a depth of approximately 215 kilometres.
