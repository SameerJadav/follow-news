Discovery, which Hollywood spent most of 2026 treating as either inevitable or illegal, depending on who was paying for the lunch, is done. The parent company will be called Skydance, and David Ellison will run it alongside his incoming co-CEO Ynon Kreiz. Ellison simply reused the name of Skydance Media, his original film production company, as the umbrella for everything being folded into the new conglomerate. Paramount and Warner Bros. themselves survive as brands under that umbrella, keeping their logos on their films, much as Universal sits under Comcast.The road here was not short. Paramount offered $31 a share in cash, valuing Warner's equity at $81 billion and the whole transaction at $110 billion once debt is counted. Netflix had got there first, with a $27.75-a-share agreement for the studio and streaming assets, but when the price rose its co-CEOs Ted Sarandos and Greg Peters told Warner the deal was no longer financially attractive and walked away. Then came a lawsuit from twelve states.
