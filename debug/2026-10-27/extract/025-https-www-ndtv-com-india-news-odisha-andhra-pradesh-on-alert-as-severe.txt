**New Delhi:**

The eastern coastal states of Odisha and Andhra Pradesh closed schools, prohibited sea fishing and kept rescue teams on standby, stepping up precautionary measures ahead of severe Cyclone Arnab's expected landfall on Wednesday to ensure "zero loss of life and minimal damage to infrastructure".

The India Meteorological Department (IMD) said the deep depression over east-central and southeast Bay of Bengal has moved westward and has intensified into cyclonic storm 'Arnab'.

It is likely to cross Andhra Pradesh and Yanam coasts between Bapatla and Visakhapatnam as a very severe cyclonic storm, with maximum sustained winds of 115-125 kmph gusting up to 140 kmph by the forenoon of October 28, it said.

In the national capital, the National Crisis Management Committee reviewed cyclone preparedness, with Cabinet Secretary T V Somanathan saying that besides Andhra Pradesh, Odisha, Telangana, Chhattisgarh and Puducherry, the MHA and the IMD should alert eastern Uttar Pradesh and Bihar, since high rainfall was also expected there.

Somanathan asked agencies to ensure zero loss of life and minimal damage to property and infrastructure and directed them that, in the event of any damage, essential services be restored at the earliest.

The National Disaster Response Force (NDRF) informed the meeting that they have deployed teams, with additional teams on standby.

"Rescue and relief teams of the Army, Navy, Air Force and Indian Coast Guard, along with their ships and aircraft, have been kept ready. Indian Coast Guard has so far shepherded multiple ships/boats to jetties/shore, and the remaining ships have been alerted to return to the coast," a government statement said.

While the Andaman and Nicobar Islands have ordered the closure of all schools till Tuesday, the Odisha government shuttered schools, colleges, and Anganwadi centres in six southern Odisha districts of Ganjam, Gajapati, Rayagada, Malkangiri, Koraput and Nabarangpur on October 28 and 29.

In view of inclement weather conditions, the state prohibited fishing activities in the sea, stopped entry of tourists in the hills, waterfalls and dams in the six districts and started moving people from vulnerable areas to safety.

Chief Minister Mohan Charan Majhi deputed eight ministers at separate places to monitor pre-cyclone activities.

He said that though the severe cyclonic storm is likely to make landfall between Andhra Pradesh and Yanam in Puducherry, Odisha would be heavily impacted, witnessing very heavy rainfall and winds gusting up to 80 kmph.

Rescue teams, comprising personnel of the NDRF, Odisha Disaster Rapid Action Force (ODRAF), the Fire and Emergency Services and the police, have been pre-positioned at vulnerable areas.

"People living in thatched houses and low-lying areas are being shifted to shelters. Pregnant women are being taken to health centres in advance. Special arrangements are also in place for senior citizens, children, and persons with disabilities," Odisha's Disaster Management Minister Suresh Pujari said.

He said all cyclone centres, along with some temporary ones, have been readied. The state government has cancelled leave of its employees in the identified districts, and control rooms have been opened to coordinate relief and rescue operations.

The agriculture department has advised farmers to harvest and safely store crops that are 80-85 per cent mature. Dedicated agricultural helpline numbers have been opened for assistance, the minister added.

The Andhra Pradesh government released Rs 27 crore to 20 districts for relief operations. Rs 2 crore each have been released to Bapatla, Krishna, Konaseema, East and West Godavari, Kakinada and Anakapalli districts for relief and response measures, a release from Andhra Pradesh State Disaster Management Authority said.

Chief Minister N Chandrababu Naidu reviewed cyclone preparedness with officials and instructed them to alert people according to the likely impact of the weather system and take all necessary steps to minimise damage.

He directed them to analyse information from previous cyclones and issue timely warnings to farmers and the public to help them take necessary precautions.

Educational institutions in cyclone-affected areas have been declared closed. Beaches have been closed to the public, while movement on coastal roads is scheduled to be suspended from Tuesday afternoon to ensure public safety.

Officials informed the chief minister that arrangements were underway to shift 4,711 identified pregnant women from vulnerable areas to primary health centres, community health centres and hospitals, while all fishermen who had ventured into the sea had returned safely.

As many as 15 NDRF teams have been stationed across Kakinada, Nadipudi in Amalapuram, Konaseema, Rajahmundry in East Godavari, Eluru, Narsapuram in West Godavari, Machilipatnam in Krishna district and Nakkapalli in Anakapalli district.
