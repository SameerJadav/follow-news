[Jordan](https://www.france24.com/en/tag/jordan/)said it had struck "terrorist organisations" in neighbouring [Syria](https://www.france24.com/en/tag/syria/) on Monday in retaliation for an [attack](https://www.france24.com/en/tag/attacks/)[on a Christian church last week](https://www.france24.com/en/middle-east/20261023-several-killed-dozens-wounded-in-jordan-church-shooting) that killed nine people–just hours after confirming they had arrested the three assailants responsible, wounding them in a dawn raid and shootout.

Jordan's Public Security Directorate said the assailant group were cornered at an abandoned house where they had barricaded themselves inside and opened fire on security forces.

"All three[terrorists](https://www.france24.com/en/tag/terrorism/) have been injured. They have been taken to hospital and are still receiving treatment," Director of Public Security Major General Abdullah Al-Maaytah told a press conference.

Later Monday, the Jordanian military announced retaliatory strikes in southern Syria, saying air force jets had "destroyed several terrorist positions" and killed and wounded "numerous members" of groups linked to the perpetrators of the attack.

Syrian state media reported that civilians had been killed in the strikes on the southern Syrian province of Daraa. The director of health for the region told Syria's state-run SANA news agency that at least eight civilians had been killed, including women, children and a 65-year-old man.

On Friday, the gunmen stormed the compound of the Greek [Orthodox](https://www.france24.com/en/tag/orthodox-church/) St George's Church in the village of Hammoud in Karak governorate during a funeral procession, authorities said.

Karak is home to a significant [Christian](https://www.france24.com/en/tag/christians/) community in Muslim-majority Jordan, where Christians make up between two and three percent of the population.

The arrested suspects– two brothers and another man related by marriage–are [Islamic extremists](https://www.france24.com/en/tag/islamist-militants/), Maaytah said, adding they will be investigated and brought before the courts.

"This country must remain an oasis of security and stability, and we affirm that Jordan will remain impervious to anyone who dares to challenge it," he said.

One of the suspects was in critical condition, Jordan's state broadcaster Al-Mamlaka reported. There were no casualties among security forces, it added.

Under Jordanian law, murder is [punishable by death](https://www.france24.com/en/tag/death-penalty/) by hanging.

Attacks on houses of worship in Jordan are rare. In 2016 an attack in Karak targeted a security centre and police patrols, leaving 10 people dead.

A number of [weapons](https://www.france24.com/en/tag/weapons/) and a large quantity of ammunition were seized from the three suspects, the Public Security Directorate said.

## 'We will show them'

Jordan's [King Abdullah II](https://www.france24.com/en/tag/king-abdullah-ii-of-jordan/) said in a post on X that security forces "chased them to the very end".

Before news of the strikes on Syria, he wrote, "We vowed the terrorists would not escape punishment, and no proponent of dark ideologies that divide Jordanians will be tolerated."

"We will show them," he added.

Earlier, the king visited wounded victims of the church attack in hospital, dressed in military fatigues and carrying a pistol on his hip.

Interior Minister Mazen al-Faraya told Al-Mamlaka on Monday that the attackers were all Jordanians and "had been holed up in a building in one of the villages near the site of the attack".

"There is no indication of any external contacts or collaboration with foreign parties," he said.

Government communications minister Mohammad al-Momani said in response to the church attack "we stand ready and will strike with an iron fist, without mercy or leniency, against anyone who attempts to undermine Jordan's security".

On Friday, Prime Minister Jafar Hassan ordered a three-day period of national mourning for the victims of the church attack, with flags to be flown at half-mast over government and public institutions.

Eight of the nine victims were laid to rest on Sunday next to the church in a funeral that drew a huge crowd amid a large security presence, AFP images showed.

The ninth person, Mohammed al-Qasous, was buried in south Amman on Monday.
