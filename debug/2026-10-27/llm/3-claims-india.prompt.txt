**New Delhi:**

The Election Commission of India (ECI) has clarified to the Supreme Court that it has not made changes to Form 6, a document all first-time voters are required to duly fill in.

In an affidavit filed to counter petitions against Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar and the Special Intensive Revision exercise, the poll body submitted to the court that a declaration form was added to the formalities under the SIR exercise. The form requires voters to link their details with those of their parents and grandparents in previous electoral rolls.

"On a review of the manner in which Form 6 and the Declaration were displayed on the IT platform, some concerns arose for the reason that the manner in which the Declaration was presented in the online system was such that it could be construed as though an amendment of Form 6 had been made," the Election Commission wrote in the affidavit.

It wrote in the court document that only the central government can make changes to the format of Form 6.

"The amended Declaration was a measure that would enable the person seeking registration as a voter to dispense with the need for filing documents during the claims and objections period," it said.

The poll body clarified that Form 6 is the mandatory document for new voters. The declaration form serves a different purpose, it added.

"In the SIR Orders, issued by the ECI for the voters whose names did not appear in the draft Electoral Roll, one of the documents stipulated for meeting the eligibility criteria is the additional Declaration Form. The Declaration Form is one of the documents which is required to be submitted by any Applicant/voter along with Form 6 for his/her inclusion in the Electoral Roll during the SIR exercise. The Declaration is a document provided by the SIR Orders and is NOT a part of Form 6, which is the statutory Form for inclusion in the Electoral Roll," it added.

It said the said declaration was amended to introduce a concept called "mapping".

The concept was introduced "to connect the details of the Applicants with the earlier SIR of 2002/2003 data, by requesting the Applicants to provide the details of the electoral roll/entry in the earlier electoral roll of self/parents/grandparents".

The poll body explained that after providing the data, applicants would eliminate the need for them to submit further documents for verification. "It was a measure for the assistance of voters," it said.

The poll body told the court that the declaration would only be required for the SIR process.

This mechanism facilitates/expedites the further progress of the SIR towards its conclusion," the Election Commission noted in the affidavit.

Karnataka had 5.54 crore voters when the exercise began. | Photo Credit: File photo

After more than three months of a clean-up drive under the Special Intensive Revision (SIR) of electoral rolls, Karnataka’s final electoral roll will be published on October 27. The list will be uploaded on the website of the Chief Electoral Officer, Karnataka.

The exercise, which began with the stated aim of ensuring that no eligible voter was excluded and no ineligible voter remained on the rolls, led to the deletion of 1.07 crore names across the State. Karnataka had 5.54 crore voters when the exercise began. The final roll will have around 4.54 crore voters, with one crore names struck off the rolls. Details on the number of ineligible voters identified, or the count of foreign citizens — another stated aim of the SIR — remain unknown so far.

During the draft roll stage, 16.39 lakh names were deleted on the ground that the voters were dead, while over seven lakh were identified as duplicate entries. Another 3.83 lakh names were deleted under the “Others” category, which is not seen in many other States that conducted the SIR. Even as the exercise comes to an end, the CEO’s office has not clearly explained what the “Others” category comprises, apart from stating that it includes people who refused to sign.

However, the largest category of deletions — 80.62 lakh names — comprised voters who had shifted residence or were not found at their addresses during the enumeration phase. Unlike those identified as dead or duplicate, these voters were not necessarily ineligible to vote. They would have had to apply through Form 6 to have their names restored to the rolls. Yet, only 7,87,534 applications for inclusion were received, a figure that also includes applications from new voters.

The scale of deletions under the SIR has not been offset by additions in any constituency across the State, with applications for inclusion falling far short of the number of names struck off the draft roll.
