The civic body has separately identified 26 framed structures above G+4 floors on Wednesday for close monitoring as they may be vulnerable.

The survey of PG buildings will continue on Thursday, even as the civic body seeks to further intensify action against unauthorised construction, misuse of properties and dangerous structures. Several such properties have already been identified for major action.

But by then videos alleging that the government had enforced an internet shutdown in Satya Niketan had gone viral on Instagram. Some of these videos went so far as to say that those trapped under the debris of the collapse were unable to contact their families and friends because of the purported shutdown.

Journalist Nikhil Pahwa, who founded MediaNama, a website focused on technology policy, argued that the Modi government and the Delhi Police had only themselves to blame for this. Their use of internet shutdowns during the recent youth protests at Jantar Mantar, he said, had led to a loss of credibility.

“It is very, very natural that people jump to conclusions because they [the government] have done their best when it comes to losing the trust of people,” Pahwa added.

According to the Telecommunications (Temporary Suspension of Services) Rules, which the Modi government notified in November 2024, officials have the power to suspend “any class of telecommunication services” for up to 15 days. This means that they can halt high-speed mobile phone internet services without putting in place a complete shutdown.

Pahwa pointed out that the government has done this before in Jammu and Kashmir. As recently as March, the home department of Jammu and Kashmir had asked internet service providers to reduce data services to 2G speeds for four days in the “entire Kashmir valley” after the assassination of Ali Khamenei, the supreme leader of Iran.

The order capped 2G speeds at 128 kilobits per second, which made it impossible to share videos.

The same rules also require officials issuing internet suspension orders to make them public. However, the rules do not specify when that must be done.

In practice, this has meant that the orders are sometimes released with a lag. During the July youth protests at Jantar Mantar in Delhi, for example, the Union home ministry faced criticism from activists for the delay in making its suspension orders public.

The grouping has since undergone a transformative expansion, welcoming Egypt, Ethiopia, Iran, Saudi Arabia, and the UAE as full members in January 2024, followed by Indonesia in January 2025.

Today, featuring 11 full member states alongside an expanding array of partner nations, the alliance commands significant global economic and diplomatic influence.

Chinese President Xi Jinping is expected to attend, though China has not formally confirmed his participation.

If Xi visits, it would be his first trip to India since the 2019 informal summit in Mamallapuram with Modi. A Modi-Xi bilateral meetingon the sidelines is widely anticipated.

The Chinese leader's delegation could run to around 400 officials, roughly double the 2019 contingent, according to some media reports.

Delhi Traffic Police has issued a public advisory ahead of the BRICS Summit 2026, warning commuters of diversions and regulated traffic movement across the city during the summit period.

The advisory also covers a motorcade rehearsal scheduled for September 9 from 7 pm to 10 pm.

The summit will be held at Bharat Mandapam, Pragati Maidan, from September 11 to 13. The traffic advisory covers 22 diversion points and more than 35 roads.
