RNC chair Joe Gruters kicks off midterm convention: 'This is history in the making'

Joe Gruters, the Republican National Committee (RNC) chair, has kicked of the GOP midterm convention. Expected to be full of party pageantry, Gruters said the two-day event in Dallas would be about “defying history”.

Wielding a super-sized gavel, Gruters said that Republicans didn’t come to the American Airlines Center to “to talk about winning. We came here to do it.”

“This isn’t just another convention; this is history in the making,” Gruters added.

The RNC chair also flagged that tonight’s programming will include everyday Americans and several GOP candidates in midterm races this November.

A reminder that both nights of the convention will end with speeches from Donald Trump.

Missouri’s Trump-backed voting maps approved by federal panel challenged in supreme court

Lucy Campbell

Opponents of congressional redistricting in Missouri appealed to the US supreme court on Wednesday after a federal panel let stand a ruling allowing the state to use a new map backed by Donald Trump in November’s midterms.

The map’s challengers, including a group ‌opposed to the Republican-drawn map called ​People Not Politicians, made the request in an emergency ⁠filing, moving swiftly ​after Stephen Clark, a St ​Louis-based chief ​US district ​judge, ‌issued his ​decision on Tuesday.

Clashing rulings had plunged Missouri’s congressional elections into uncertainty, less than two months out from the crucial midterms that will decide which party controls a closely divided congress – and will impact the fate of Trump’s agenda for the rest of his term.

First, the conservative supreme court justice Brett Kavanaugh had denied an emergency request from Missouri officials to use a congressional map in November that was redrawn with Trump’s support to give the GOP an edge in the midterms. That decision effectively allowed a Missouri supreme court ruling to revert back to the 2022 map to stand.

Not long after, a ruling by Clark, who was appointed by Trump, issued a contradictory decision, temporarily preventing the state from using its old congressional maps in the November elections. The temporary restraining order, or TRO, ordered Missouri to use the newly drawn districts that favor Republicans, which the state used in its August primary elections.

Clark’s order was then swiftly appealed to the eighth US circuit court of appeals.

Several Republicans in competitive races stay away from GOP midterm convention

While there are dozens of Republican lawmakers and candidates who are facing tough races this November at the convention in Dallas over the next two days, there are several who have chosen not to attend the event that has been hastily thrown together at the behest of Donald Trump.

Many of these Republicans are up for election in competitive districts and states where the administration’s policies have proven unpopular. This includes Susan Collins, the five-term incumbent senator from Maine, who is contending with a deluge of anti-Trump animus in her purple state ahead of her re-election race, and has frequently bucked her party to pushback against the president. Her campaign has told various outlets that she would be constituents back in Maine during the convention.

Similarly, Dan Sullivan – the Republican senator from Alaska who is facing a tough contest against former Democratic congresswoman Mary Peltola – is opting to skip the event. Sullivan has spent Wednesday on the campaign trail in Alaska, posting videos from various stops. This comes after the US president said last week that he would travel to the state to campaign on behalf of Sullivan ahead of the general election.

Meanwhile, Tom Barrett, a Michigan representative, and Tom Tiffany, the congressman and Republican nominee for Wisconsin governor, are some of the House Republicans – seen as vulnerable in their respective races – who will also not attend the GOP convention in Dallas.

Earlier, Trump snapped at an ABC News reporter who asked about the absences.

“We have room for the ones that have a close race, if they’re slightly behind or slightly ahead or tied,” the president said. “But we don’t have room for people that are 35 points up and don’t need to be there … Every politician that we’ve wanted to be there is there.”

David Smith

Reporting from Dallas

The midterm convention is under way. Each speaker stands at a lectern on a shiny black stage with big blue star and gold trim. There are four US national flags and five giant video screens behind them. Earlier today, the screens showed dark, spooky images of Democrats Elizabeth Warren, Bernie Sanders, Kamala Harris, Joe Biden, Nancy Pelosi and Tim Walz.

The red-carpeted convention floor looks full and has the familiar vertical signs with the names of individual states, even though state delegates have not been elected this time. There is also a flavour of a Donald Trump campaign rally: the so-called “Front row Joes” are here, as are the “Trump Tribe of Texas” with “Lady Liberty” (a woman dressed in a Statue of Liberty costume).

Blake Marnell, wearing his customary brick patterned suit and tie and red “Make America great again” cap, told me: “There’s probably no administration in history that you can look at two years in and say we got everything we wanted but I’m overall extremely happy. My number one issue of course is the border. There are a lot of other issues that are important, but I think that most of those are downstream from illegal immigration.”

But the regular seating at the American Airlines Center is a different story: some sections are quite full, others have lots of empty seats (some occupied only by Stars and Stripes signs). The “nosebleeds” at the top seem completely unfilled so far. Of course, it’s still early days: Trump and other big name speakers are yet to appear. The stadium may well be much nearer capacity when they do.

Chris Daniel, 44, from Houston, Texas, who has been a delegate to every Republican convention since 2004, said: “It feels different only because this is the midterms and we haven’t seen anything remotely like this since the 1800s. In this case, it is to rally the troops and get everyone on the same page so that we move forward towards November.”

Trump merchandise is on sale in the square outside. It includes “Trump 2028” baseball caps.

Ray Washburne, co-chair of the Republican midterm convention, has acknowledged that Donald Trump will be “front and center” of the event and denied the US president will be a liability for the party’s candidates.

“Absolutely, he’s an asset – just look at the turnouts he gets,” Washburne told a roundtable at Bloomberg News in Dallas. “The ratings for listening to him speak will be fantastic because people want to hear what he’s got to say and he’s gonna set the tone for the rest of this fall. People are gonna look at that and his tone towards people like Ken Paxton and others to get the enthusiasm behind their campaigns.”

Washburne said the two main contenders to host Republicans’ first midterm convention had been Dallas and Las Vegas. After Dallas got the nod in mid-June, the host committee raised $45m in three weeks as it raced to meet the September deadline. He expects an attendance of 18,000 to 20,000 each night in the American Airlines Center but is not aware of how many members of Congress will be among them. Washburne, chair of the gas company Sunoco and vice-chair of Gillon Capital, said the idea of a midterm convention appealed to Trump. “He’s an entertainer. He obviously loves big, big events and things and his career has been built around that. I think when the idea was broached to him about doing it he thought about it for a while and was like, that makes a lot of sense to do it because it’s going to get a lot of attention, we can showcase people we need to showcase, and make the argument for another two years of Republican-held House and Senate to go along with the administration’s goals to get things done.”

I think once that was really talked through this spring, it was realised, yeah, let’s do something. I don’t think the Democrats – who would they put up? AOC, Bernie Sanders, Mamdani. Trump signals openness to pardoning Michael Cohen, his former fixer

Trump on Wednesday said he was open to pardoning Michael Cohen, his former lawyer and fixer who once testified against him in court before again seeking his favor in his second term.

“If everything is checking out, it’s something I would consider,” Trump said of a possible pardon for Cohen. He was asked about the subject on the tarmac before departing to the GOP midterm convention in Dallas.

It might seem like ages ago, so in case you forgot, Cohen testified in Trump’s 2024 criminal trial in Manhattan that the then-former President had directed him to illegally misclassify hush money payments to a porn star as legal fees. Trump’s conviction on 34 counts in that case could’ve landed him in prison, if not for his 2024 presidential election victory.

Trump’s remarks on the tarmac come a week after he took to social media to claim that Cohen had fully recanted his testimony against him. In actuality, Cohen has so far stopped short of such a step, saying only that he felt pressured by the prosecution in Trump’s cases without disputing the accuracy of his testimony.

Over the course of a decade, Cohen has traced a winding path from the President’s former fixer, to a key witness in legal proceedings against him, before finding his way back into Trump’s good graces in his second term. Along the way, he served a three-year prison sentence in connection with the hush money payments he later said he made at Trump’s behest, along with other offenses.

“What they did to Michael Cohen was a disgrace,” Trump continued on the tarmac. “They forced him to say things that he didn’t want to say, and that he didn’t believe. And I appreciate that at least he came out, although late, he came out and rectified the situation.”

Emergency slide deployed on Qatari Air Force One delays Trump en route to Dallas

Donald Trump boarded a flight to Dallas for the GOP’s midterm convention, after being delayed as an emergency slide on his Qatari-gifted replacement for Air Force One was unfurled on the tarmac.

Asked he was sure the jet was now safe to fly, Trump responded: “Yeah, I am, or I wouldn’t be on it.”

Texas Republican in race for key House seat under fire for derogatory comments

Oliver Milman

A Republican candidate for a key congressional district in Texas has come under scrutiny over a series of derogatory comments made about women in his social media posts and videos.

Brandon Herrera, who is the GOP candidate for Texas’s 23rd congressional district, has made a string of insults while fashioning a rightwing online persona as the “AK Guy”, and hosts a YouTube channel with more than 4 million subscribers that tests and reviews firearms.

The comments, first reported by CNN, include Herrera taking aim at people he opposes politically, and women. In a 2023 video, he said that a gun range dummy’s empty head was “full of mush – much like the average Austin feminist”, and in a separate 2024 video, he likened the weight of “angry blue-haired” feminists to that of orca whales and elephants.

In 2020, Herrera filmed himself firing a gun at bottles of ranch dressing and, after they exploded, rubbing the condiment on to himself and declaring that this would “make me irresistible to the fat bitches”. While discussing the merits of a car on X in 2023, Herrera opined that “4 doors means more whores”.

Other groups have also been the focus of Herrera’s online content. In 2020, while firing a gun at a ham, Herrera said that while purchasing a ham for target practice was expensive, it was “still cheaper than a homeless person”.

This isn’t the first time that Herrera has come under scrutiny. In 2024, during a run for Congress, Herrera said: “I am not, nor have I never [sic] been, a neo-Nazi” after describing a Nazi-era gun as “the original ghetto blaster”. The Republican Jewish Coalition condemned him at the time as “a goose-stepping extremist”.

Herrera said that all these comments were jokes, and took aim at his opponent in this November’s midterm election, Democrat Katy Padilla Stout. “It’s disappointing that CNN would allow someone to allege that jokes about feminism lead to violence when Stout is fighting to return children to the parent convicted of abusing them,” Herrera said in a statement to CNN, referring to a case in which Stout was appointed as a defense attorney for a father seeking to restore parental rights after a domestic abuse allegation.

Asked to comment on his previous statements made online, Herrera said via his campaign that it was “disappointing” that “alleged journalists” would “ignore” the Stout case. He did not reference his own past statements.

The past comments could add to Republicans’ challenge of holding on to the US House of Representatives seat. Herrera is attempting to replace Tony Gonzales, a Republican lawmaker who resigned in April amid a House ethics committee investigation into his relationship with a former aide.

While Democrats have not prevailed in this district since 2015, the party has recently added it to its target list and the Democratic Congressional Campaign Committee has sent funding to Stout, a former teacher and attorney, in what is expected to be a strong year for Democrats amid rising voter dissatisfaction with Donald Trump.
