## 2. Headline verdict

**The digest's trust machinery is sound and the pipeline is healthy.** 216
stories, 1,823 claims, 2,134 markers, **zero** structural violations: every
claim resolves to a listed source, every marker resolves to a real claim with
matching attribution, no marker span falls outside its body, no two distinct
spans overlap, and `unanchored_share` recomputes to the recorded value on all
216. `docs/` re-renders **byte-for-byte identical** from `data/` + `followed/`.

visible in the record.

Three things are wrong that the 5-day window could not have shown:

- **Two published stories carry figures that trace to no claim** — including a

## 3. High findings

### H1 — Unanchored figures reach the page, and the `Subject:` line is the route · **High**

`CLAUDE.md`: *"The write pass sees claims only, never article text, so a fact
that is not an anchored claim has no way into the prose."* Over 216 stories
there are two counter-examples, and one of them has a named mechanism.

**The route.** `llm.py:568` builds each story's block in the write prompt as:

```python
f"Subject: {cluster.headline_hint}",
```

`headline_hint` is written by the *select* pass, which reads RSS titles and
summaries — not claims. So any figure select puts in a hint is handed to the
write pass with no claim behind it. Measured across all 212 clusters that
reached the claims pass:

**9 of 212 clusters (4.2%) arrived at the write pass with a `Subject:` line
carrying a figure that appears in none of that cluster's claims.**

| day | figure | Subject line |
|---|---|---|
| 07-30 | 18 | Powerful earthquake in Japan **kills at least 18** and triggers massive search… |
| 08-08 | 4,000 | Ebola outbreak in DR Congo **passes 4,000 cases** |
| 08-11 | 100 | 7.4-magnitude earthquake strikes Colombia, **killing over 100 people**… |
| 08-11 | 1 | Typhoon Dolphin forces **over 1 million** evacuations in eastern China |
| 08-14 | 260 | Colombia earthquake… **death toll rises past 260** |
| 08-16 | 100 | Moroccan security forces intercept **over 100 migrants**… |
| 08-19 | 8,452 | PM CARES Fund audit report shows **₹8,452 crore** corpus… |
| 08-20 | 5 | Israeli military opens criminal probe after troops fired on car carrying **5**… |
| 08-21 | 35 | Mirza Fakhrul Islam Alamgir elected President of Bangladesh… **35** |

**One of the nine reached the published page — in the headline.**
`data/2026-07-30.json`:

> headline: *"Powerful earthquake in Japan **leaves 18 dead** as search operations continue"*
> body: *"A powerful earthquake struck the island of Kyushu in southwestern Japan, **leaving at least 18 people dead**…"*

No claim in that story carries `18`, in digits or spelled out. The only claim
mentioning deaths is BBC's *"three employees were killed and four were
hospitalized after an explosion struck the Aeon Mall in Kashima."* The `18` came
from the Subject line. The clause sits inside marker span → claim 25, which is
about **magnitude 7.1 vs 6.8** — so the page attributes the death toll to the
Guardian, which did not supply it through any claim.

**The second case has no Subject-line origin at all.** `data/2026-08-16.json`
publishes:

> *"Local authorities reported the fire **expanded from 80 hectares on Friday to
> over 2,700 hectares by late Saturday**, scorching at least 16 square
> kilometres."*

Only the final clause is a claim (Channel News Asia: *"The wildfire in Belgium's
High Fens park had scorched at least 16 square kilometres by Saturday
afternoon."*). The Subject line for that cluster carries no figure at all. And
the invented numbers **contradict the claim they are anchored to**: 2,700
hectares is 27 km², not 16. This is precisely the never-blend-numbers failure
`product.md` forbids, produced inside a marker span that makes it look sourced.

**The existing detector sees both and is designed not to act.**
`anchor.unsourced_figures` is documented as *"Logged and recorded in signals but
never used to drop a story — spelled-out numbers and rephrasings make this too
noisy to gate on."* The 27-day record now lets that judgement be tested. It
fired **9 times across 7 stories**:

| classification | n | examples |
|---|---|---|
| False positive — spelled out in a claim | 5 | body "17 people", claim "Seventeen people"; body "19-year-old", claim "nineteen" |
| False positive — not a quantity | 1 | `24` matched from the outlet name **France 24** |
| **Real** | **3** (2 stories) | 07-30 `18`; 08-16 `80` and `2700` |

So the noise is real (6 of 9) — **and every source of it is mechanical**. Strip
outlet names from the body before scanning, and normalise number-words in claims
before differencing, and the detector's precision over this record goes to
**3/3**. At that precision it is gate-able.

> Evidence: `llm.py:568`; `debug/2026-07-30/llm/3-write.prompt.txt:32`;
> `debug/2026-08-16/llm/3-write.prompt.txt:66`; `data/2026-07-30.json`,
> `data/2026-08-16.json` (`signals.unsourced_figures`); `anchor.py:310-320`;
> per-cluster figure diff over all 27 `debug/*/claims.json`.

**Fix:** two independent changes. (a) Strip figures from the `Subject:` line, or
drop the line entirely — the claims already carry the story. (b) Make
`unsourced_figures` precise (outlet-name mask + numeral-word normalisation) and
then gate on it, since at 3/3 precision it stops exactly the two stories above.

### H2 — A transient 503 aborts the whole run; no retry anywhere · **High**

`ratelimit.call_with_resume` is the project's only resilience around a Gemini
call, and `ratelimit.is_rate_limited` matches **only** 429 / `RESOURCE_EXHAUSTED`:

```python
code = getattr(exc, "code", None)
if code == 429: return True
blob = _blob(exc)
return "429" in blob or "RESOURCE_EXHAUSTED" in blob
```

Anything else re-raises on the first attempt. A `503 UNAVAILABLE` — *"This model
is currently experiencing high demand. Spikes in demand are usually temporary.
Please try again later."* — is a server's explicit request to retry, and gets
zero.

**Measured over 41 captured digest runs:**

| aborting stage | runs | what was already spent |
|---|---|---|
| `claims` | 7 | 1 select call + the full extract stage |
| `select` | 4 | nothing |
| `write` | 3 | 1 select + 1 claims call |
| `claims` (RemoteProtocolError) | 1 | 1 select + extract |
| **total** | **15 of 41 (37%)** | |

On 13 of those days a later staggered cron rescued the digest. **On 2026-08-07
all three fires drew a 503 and the day was lost** — the only gap in 27 days.
`data/2026-08-07.json` has never existed in any commit.

The stagger is doing its job. But the *cost accounting* runs the opposite way to
the one recorded in `calibration.md` (2026-08-23), which declined a retry
because *"a retry loop around `select` would spend the schema pool to buy it
back."* It is the **absence** of a retry that spends the pool:

| day | successful LLM calls | invariant |
|---|---|---|
| 08-02, 08-04, 08-06 | **5** each | 3 |
| 08-01, 08-08, 08-13, 08-14, 08-15, 08-19, 08-21 | **4** each | 3 |

**13 successful calls beyond the 3-per-morning invariant over 27 days**, every
one of them a re-do of work a failed run had already paid for. A single in-run
retry would have cost at most 1 call on each occasion and saved the re-run's
select (and, at `write`, its claims too).

This also invalidates the schema-pool arithmetic. `dossier.py:102` sizes the
pool as *"20 measured − 4 for the digest at its worst − 2 margin = 14"*. The
digest's worst is **5**, not 4, on three days. 14 + 5 = 19 against RPD 20 —
one call of headroom, not two.

**Diagnostics gap on the same day.** `CLAUDE.md` says *"`run.json`'s
`stopped_at` names the stage that emptied the pipeline"*. On 2026-08-07 —
the only day it was ever needed — `stopped_at` is absent, because a raised
exception bypasses `digest.stopped()` entirely; only `stages[-1].error` carries
it. The stale-page fallback did fire correctly and the archive skips the day
cleanly, so readers saw yesterday's digest honestly labelled.

**Window recovery worked.** 2026-08-08 widened its window to **47.6 h** —
just inside `WINDOW_CAP_H = 48` — and caught up. A two-day outage would have
been clipped.

> Evidence: `ratelimit.py:78-86`, `ratelimit.py:196-198`;
> `debug/2026-08-07/run.json` (all three commits `944440f`, `b9d226f`,
> `1fa67b0`); failed-stage scan over all 28 `debug/*/trace.jsonl`;
> `debug/*/llm/*.meta.json`; `dossier.py:102-107`; `calibration.md`
> §2026-08-23 "Not fixed, recorded".

**Fix:** classify 5xx and connection-reset as retryable in `ratelimit.py`, with
a short bounded backoff (2–3 attempts) distinct from the 429 wait budget.

### H3 — The claims pass decays with batch position; India pays for it · **High**

`llm.extract_claims` sends every cluster in one prompt, and its system prompt
says *"Aim for 8-20 claims per story."* Claims produced, by position in that
prompt, over all 212 clusters:

| position | n | mean claims | share below the prompt's own floor of 8 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 26 | **12.96** | 0% |
| 1 | 26 | 10.27 | 15% |
| 2 | 26 | 8.54 | 23% |
| 3 | 26 | 8.23 | 31% |
| 4 | 26 | 7.62 | 42% |
| 5 | 22 | 6.95 | 68% |
| 6 | 20 | 6.85 | 55% |
| 7 | 17 | 6.59 | 65% |
| 8 | 12 | 6.58 | 75% |
| 9 | 7 | 5.43 | **100%** |
| 10 | 3 | 5.67 | 67% |
| 11 | 1 | 4.00 | 100% |

Monotone, 3.2× from first to last, and **85 of 212 clusters (40%) land below the
floor the prompt itself sets.**

**It is not a confound.** Cluster order is rank order, so later clusters have
fewer outlets — that alone would produce a slope. Controlling for it, paired
within a single day, same section, identical distinct-outlet count:

```
n = 124 pairs
earlier cluster produced MORE claims : 70
later cluster produced more          : 21
tie                                  : 33
mean (later − earlier)               : −1.14 claims
one-sided sign test                  : p = 1.3e-07
```

Worked pairs: 2026-08-12, world, 4 outlets — position 0 got **18** claims,
position 3 got **10**. 2026-08-14, world, 3 outlets — position 2 got **10**,
position 5 got **5**.

**The write pass is innocent.** Words-per-claim is flat across every position
(20.6–26.4), and the same paired test on word count is a wash (51 vs 74, mean
−0.6). The loss is entirely in `extract_claims`.

**India is structurally penalised.** Every day's prompt runs World first, then
India (`WWWWWIIII`, 27 of 27 days). Result: **world clusters average 9.33 claims,
India clusters 6.92.** Not because Indian coverage is thinner — Indian clusters
carry the same outlet counts — but because they are always at the tail.

**This produced the only two anchor-gate drops in the record.** 2026-08-14, a
12-cluster day; clusters 9 and 10 were both India:

| | Badal kirpan attack | Chamoli tunnel flood |
|---|---|---|
| batch position | 9 of 12 | 10 of 12 |
| articles / distinct outlets | 5 / 4 | 5 / 4 |
| extracted article text | **20,808 chars** | **17,649 chars** |
| extraction failures | 0 | 0 |
| **claims produced** | **3** | **3** |
| body words | 73 | 68 |
| verdict | `dropped:body_too_short` | `dropped:body_too_short` |

The extraction was excellent and the writing was faithful. The claims pass
returned 3 claims from 20 KB of good text, and two real Indian news stories were
thrown away. `finish_reason: STOP`, 4,813 of 32,768 output tokens used — no
truncation. The model simply attends less at the tail.

> Evidence: per-cluster counts from all 27 `debug/*/claims.json`;
> `debug/2026-08-14/anchor/index.json` (clusters 9, 10);
> `debug/2026-08-14/extract/index.json`; `debug/2026-08-14/llm/2-claims.meta.json`;
> `llm.py:193` (the 8-20 instruction), `llm.py:421-445`.

**Fix (a dial first, per `CLAUDE.md`):** the cheapest correction that needs no
prompt change is to **split the claims call in two** — one per section — which
resets position for India and costs one extra call (4/morning, still inside the
stated invariant). Alternatively interleave sections so neither is
systematically last. Either way, log an observation in `calibration.md` first.
Changing the *wording* of the 8-20 instruction would be an editorial change and
needs owner sign-off per `decisions.md`.

### M1 — Follow can never close its own GitHub issue

Live check during this analysis:

```
gh issue list --repo SameerJadav/follow-news --state all
  #3  OPEN    label: follow    Follow: PM Modi creates exam reform task force…
```

`followed/3/record.json` says `status: "closed"`, `close_reason:
"no_development"`, `closed_at: "2026-08-12T23:12:20Z"`. **Eleven days of
divergence.** The trace for that day says why:

```
follow: could not comment on #3 (HTTPError('403 … /issues/3/comments'))
follow: could not close #3   (HTTPError('403 … /issues/3'))
follow: #3 -> closed, no development for 14+ days
```

The cause is a workflow permission, not Python. `follow.yml` declares
`issues: write`. **`digest.yml` — the daily path, which is where the auto-close
actually fires — declares only `contents`, `pages` and `id-token`.** With an
explicit `permissions:` block every unlisted scope is `none`, so on a public
repo the anonymous-equivalent `GET` in `fetch_issues()` still works (the trace
reads "3 owner issue(s) in scope") while `POST` and `PATCH` 403.

`comment()` swallowing a failure is right — *"the published page is the product;
the comment is a courtesy."* But `close_issue()` inherits that best-effort
handling and the record is marked closed regardless, so the close is **never
retried**. It is a permanent one-way divergence, and it defeats the direction of
the kill switch `CLAUDE.md` describes.

Not currently harmful — `_new_follows` skips an issue that already has a record,
so the sweep is stable — but the owner's tracker now shows an open follow for a
story the site says is finished, and the closing comment carrying the final-update
link was never posted.

> Evidence: `gh issue list`; `debug/2026-08-13/trace.jsonl`;
> `.github/workflows/digest.yml` `permissions:`; `follow.py:296-324`,
> `follow.py:431-459`.

### M2 — `MAX_ROUNDS` exhaustion still reports `complete`, silently

Unchanged from the last analysis. `dossier.py:1616-1622`:

```python
        if saturated(dsr):
            dbg(f"dossier: #{issue} saturated after {dsr['rounds']} round(s)")
            dsr["research_state"] = "complete"
            break
    else:
        dsr["research_state"] = "complete"
```

The saturation branch logs; the round-ceiling branch writes the identical
verdict with no `dbg()` and no tracer event. `_research_follow` then treats the
two as interchangeable (`if state in ("complete", "capped")`), so a dossier that
stopped because it ran out of rounds renders "The full picture" exactly like one
that genuinely ran dry.

The *daily-delta* path is safe — `_update_follow` computes `settled` itself — so
this only bites the initial deep-research burst. That path has not run since
2026-07-30, which is why the record cannot show it firing again. It fired before:
commit `4f7120d` left `rounds=8, calls=44, state=complete, open=648`.

It is now **more** reachable, not less: with the gap detector bounded (F2, fixed),
a dossier can plausibly work through 8 rounds without saturating.

> Evidence: `dossier.py:1616-1626`; `follow.py:517-522`.
